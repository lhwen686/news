启动社区急救员未能改善心脏骤停后的短期存活率

Activation of community first responders did not improve short-term survival after cardiac arrest

深度解析

这篇新闻报道揭示了HeartRunner随机对照试验的结果，该试验评估了社区急救员对院外心脏骤停（OHCA）患者30天生存率的影响。院外心脏骤停是一种危及生命的急症，其预后与早期识别、心肺复苏（CPR）和除颤紧密相关，形成“生存链”。研究发现，尽管社区急救员的介入显著提高了旁观者CPR和旁观者除颤的比例，但并未在救护车响应时间较短的区域（约7分钟）改善患者的30天生存率或良好神经功能结局。 这项研究的核心专业概念在于对OHCA管理策略的再思考。早期高质量CPR能维持重要脏器灌注，而早期除颤是终止心室颤动、恢复自主循环（ROSC）的关键。社区急救员系统旨在通过缩短从发病到首次干预的时间窗，补充专业紧急医疗服务（EMS）的不足。然而，此项研究结果表明，在EMS响应迅速的城市环境中，社区急救员的“增量效益”可能受限。这提示我们，其在不同地理和EMS响应时长的区域可能发挥不同作用，例如在农村或偏远地区，其潜在价值可能更大。这对于公共卫生策略制定、EMS资源配置及未来相关研究具有重要指导意义，强调了在推广此类系统时需充分考虑地区差异和EMS体系的成熟度。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain Community first responders did not improve 30-day survival in patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, according to results presented in a Hot Line session today at ESC Congress 2026. Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest is a time-critical emergency in which survival depends on early recognition, early cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and early defibrillation. Many countries have developed smartphone-based systems in which volunteer first responders are mobilized to perform CPR and defibrillation before emergency medical services arrive. "Although community first responders are becoming more widespread, we only have observational studies to support their use," said the principal investigator of the HeartRunner trial, professor Fredrik Folke from Copenhagen University Hospital, Denmark. "We conducted a randomized study first to evaluate the impact of community responders on patient outcomes and second to answer the question of whether using untrained volunteers is safe." In the HeartRunner trial, emergency calls for suspected out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in the Capital Region of Denmark were randomized (1:1) to community first responder activation plus standard response or standard response alone. In total, 2,060 suspected cardiac arrests were analyzed in two strata based on the estimated time from community first responder activation to arrival at the patient's side: less than three minutes (0–358 meters [0–1,175 feet] away) and three to nine minutes (360–1,080 meters [1,180–3,540 feet] away). The researchers found that the primary endpoint of 30-day survival was the same whether first responders were present or not. When estimated responder arrival time was less than three minutes, 30-day survival was 15% in patients with community first responder activation compared with 17% with no community responder activation (p = 0.42). When estimated responder arrival time was three to nine minutes, 30-day survival was 13% in patients with community first responder activation compared with 14% with no community first responder activation (p = 0.43). However, community first responder activation did significantly increase the rate of bystander CPR and doubled the rate of bystander defibrillation. For example, when responder arrival time was three to nine minutes, bystander CPR increased from 75% to 86% (p<0.0001), and bystander defibrillation increased from 6% to 14% (p<0.0001). Return of spontaneous circulation and survival with a favorable neurological outcome were similar between the two groups. Explaining why he thought the primary results were neutral, Folke said, "The trial was conducted in an area where ambulance response times were very short, around seven minutes." He continued, "Importantly, the trial showed that community first responder systems may significantly improve bystander CPR and defibrillation, but their effect on survival is highly dependent on the time available for responders to intervene before the ambulance arrives. When this interval is short, the incremental benefit of responder-initiated defibrillation may be limited. It would be interesting to assess their impact in regions with slower ambulance responses, including more rural areas." Key medical concepts Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest
