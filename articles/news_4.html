较高的ADK水平与曲西立滨在白血病细胞中的更强效果相关，提示其可能成为潜在的生物标志物

深度解析

本研究揭示了急性淋巴细胞白血病（ALL）治疗中曲西立滨作用机制的关键环节，并提出了一种潜在的预测生物标志物——ADK酶。核心医学机制在于，曲西立滨本身是一种前药，需要细胞内的ADK酶将其磷酸化，转化为活性形式。这种活性代谢物随后作为一种抗代谢剂，干扰ALL细胞增殖所必需的DNA和RNA核苷酸合成，从而抑制癌细胞生长。研究发现ADK酶水平与白血病细胞对曲西立滨的敏感性呈正相关，ADK水平越高，药物效果越显著。 这一发现对肿瘤治疗领域具有深远影响。鉴于曲西立滨此前在临床试验中因治疗反应有限而受挫，ADK作为生物标志物的识别，为其实施精准肿瘤学策略提供了新契机。这意味着未来可以通过检测患者ALL细胞中的ADK水平，筛选出对曲西立滨治疗可能响应良好的特定患者亚群。这不仅能提高治疗的有效性和安全性，避免不必要的副作用，还能重新评估和优化现有药物的临床应用价值，推动个体化治疗方案的发展。进一步的临床验证将至关重要，以确立ADK在患者治疗决策中的指导作用。

DOI: 10.1038/s41698-026-01736-z In a comprehensive study of acute lymphoblastic leukemia, researchers at Karolinska Institutet have shown that the enzyme ADK plays an important role in determining how leukemia cells respond to triciribine. Published in npj Precision Oncology, the study also provides new insights into how the compound works and identifies ADK as a potential biomarker for predicting its effects on leukemia cells. Acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) is a blood cancer that can affect people of all ages. Triciribine is a compound that has previously been investigated as a cancer treatment, but its mechanism of action in ALL cells has not been fully understood. The researchers therefore set out to investigate how triciribine acts in leukemia cells and why some cells are more susceptible to its effects than others. To better understand how triciribine works, the researchers used several methods to study a large number of proteins in leukemia cells. This allowed them to map the cellular processes affected by the compound. Their findings suggest that triciribine disrupts several processes that leukemia cells need to grow and divide, including the production of building blocks for DNA and RNA. One important factor was the enzyme ADK, which is required to convert triciribine into its active form inside the cell. The researchers found that triciribine had a greater effect on laboratory-grown leukemia cells with higher levels of ADK. A similar pattern was observed in 10 patient samples containing leukemia cells exposed to triciribine outside the body. "We found that ADK levels were associated with the effect of triciribine on leukemia cells. This makes ADK an interesting candidate as a potential biomarker," says Xuekang Qi, a researcher at the Department of Oncology-Pathology and first author of the study. Previously tested in clinical trials Triciribine has previously been tested in clinical trials involving patients with blood cancers, but treatment responses have been limited. The new study is based on experiments using laboratory-grown leukemia cells and patient-derived leukemia cells studied outside the body. It therefore remains to be established whether ADK can also predict treatment response in patients. "If ADK proves able to predict treatment response, it could improve our ability to identify patients who may benefit from triciribine. This is particularly relevant for a compound that has produced limited treatment responses in previous clinical trials," says Rozbeh Jafari, a researcher at the same department and senior author of the study.
