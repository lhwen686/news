放射学人才储备增速不及整体医学领域

Radiology pipeline expanding slower than medicine as a whole

深度解析

本文揭示了美国医疗保健领域一个日益严峻的挑战：医学影像服务需求不断攀升与合格放射科医师供应停滞之间的鸿沟。核心专业概念在于“放射科医师人才输送管道”，这是一个多阶段过程，涵盖医学院教育、住院医师培训和临床执业。尽管住院医师职位和受训人员数量有所增加，但过去15年间，人均执业放射科医师的实际数量几乎没有变化。这一现象主要源于人口结构变化，特别是人口老龄化，其固有地对慢性病管理和预防性筛查需要更多的诊断影像。此外，人员流失率（包括退休、职业变更或职业倦怠）也削减了劳动力。 其行业影响深远。放射科医师的持续短缺导致影像解读周转时间延长，可能延误患者的诊断及后续治疗启动。在临床上，这可能损害患者预后，尤其是在卒中或急性创伤等时间敏感性疾病中。从医疗系统角度看，瓶颈加剧了现有诊断基础设施的压力，可能导致更长的等待名单和医疗服务可及性下降，尤其是在服务不足的地区。解决这一问题需要多管齐下的策略，包括优化培训路径、通过改善工作条件加强劳动力保留，并可能整合人工智能辅助诊断工具以增强人类能力并提高影像分析效率，尽管这些工具仍需专家监督。这一系统性问题凸显了战略规划的紧迫性，以确保及时、准确的诊断服务，这对于现代医学至关重要。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain Demand for medical imaging in the U.S. keeps rising, but the number of radiologists available to read those images has barely budged relative to the population, according to a study published in the Journal of the American College of Radiology. The researchers tracked the radiologist pipeline over roughly the past 15 years using four national datasets covering the residency match, accredited training programs, graduate medical education and the practicing physician workforce. They found that radiology residency positions grew 33% between 2010 and 2025, and the total number of radiology residents grew 23% through 2024. Yet the number of radiologists actually in practice rose only 12% between 2010 and 2022, translating to an increase from 11.1 to 11.5 radiologists per 100,000 people. According to the authors, radiology also expanded more slowly than medicine as a whole: Positions grew 66% across the specialty from 2002 to 2025, compared with 89% across all specialties. According to Ajay Malhotra, MBBS, MD, MMM, professor of radiology and biomedical imaging, neurology and neurosurgery at Yale School of Medicine, more trainees are entering radiology, but attrition and population growth are absorbing nearly all that gain, and the aging population means per capita imaging demand is climbing at the same time. "The radiologist pipeline has grown, but almost none of that growth has reached patients," said Malhotra. "After accounting for population growth, the number of radiologists per person is essentially where it was 15 years ago, and that is before you factor in an aging population that uses more imaging per capita. For anyone waiting on a scan result, that is the number that matters." Publication details Ajay Malhotra et al, The Evolving US Radiologist Pipeline: Trends in Residency Positions, Resident Workforce, and Practicing Radiologists per Unit Population, Journal of the American College of Radiology (2026).
