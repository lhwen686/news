Immune cell defects may drive celiac disease development

Original Text
New research from the Snow Centre for Immune Health is challenging long-held assumptions about autoimmune disease, revealing that celiac disease may be driven not just by an overactive immune system, but by subtle defects in how immune cells function.

Published in Immunology & Cell Biology, the study found consistent shifts in immune cell behaviour in people with celiac disease – differences that may appear long before symptoms develop.

Researchers identified distinct patterns in early immune responses that could help predict autoimmune risk and support more personalised monitoring and care in future.

CD4 helper T cells displayed weaker signalling, slower activation and reduced survival – challenging the idea that autoimmunity is simply an overactive immune response.

These findings may extend beyond celiac disease, offering insight into a broader range of autoimmune and inflammatory conditions.

The new study was led by Dr Vanessa Bryant, Professor Phil Hodgkin and Dr Susanne Heinzel with clinical expertise from Snow Centre Director, Professor Jason Tye-Din.

The research examined a type of immune cell known as CD4 helper T cells, which coordinate immune responses, fight infection and support antibody production.

"Our assay is a bit like winding up a toy and letting it go to see how long it runs and what tricks it performs.

"Our method reveals how well T cells read and store their activation signals to maintain their 'momentum' once the original stimulus is gone. and whether people with autoimmune disease have built-in differences in how their cells function."

Measuring immune 'momentum'

To explore this, researchers used the centre's pioneering Cyton2 Cell Timer model, which tracks immune cell activity over time.

The aim was to track the T‑cell "momentum". Rather than continuously stimulating cells, as most laboratory tests do, the team briefly activated them, then removed all signals to observe what happened next.

This allowed us to see how cells behave once the initial push has stopped. It reveals properties that are harder to detect under constant stimulation." Dr. Vanessa Bryant, Walter and Eliza Hall Institute

The team measured how cells divided, survived and produced key signalling molecules, building a detailed picture of early immune behavior.

Dr Bryant said this approach is an entirely unique way to measure immune responses, giving researchers new insights into how they can go awry.

"Understanding these early defects could lead to better ways to predict risk, monitor disease, and eventually develop treatments that restore healthy immune balance, not just manage symptoms through a gluten‑free diet."

A surprising result

Contrary to expectations, immune cells from people with celiac disease were not simply overactive. Instead, they showed weaker responses.

The study found CD4 helper T cells from people with celiac disease:

produced less interleukin-2, a key immune signalling molecule

entered cell division more slowly

were less likely to survive

"These differences were subtle but remarkably consistent," said Dr Bryant.

Notably, the same pattern appeared regardless of sex or whether individuals were newly diagnosed or managing the condition with a gluten-free diet.

"This tells us the effect isn't simply driven by inflammation or diet," said Dr Bryant. "It suggests an underlying difference that may be linked to genetic risk."

Potential beyond one disease

Although the study focused on celiac disease, the findings may have broader relevance.

Autoimmune diseases affect around five per cent of the population, and many share overlapping genetic risk factors.

"If autoimmune risk is partly built into how immune cells behave from the start, this could change how we think about early detection," said Dr Bryant.

While this approach is a research tool rather than a clinical test, it opens the door to combining genetic information with functional immune measurements to better predict disease risk.

A shift in how immune health is viewed

Professor Tye-Din said the study highlights the power of the Snow Centre's Cyton 'cell-timer' model to uncover hidden patterns in immune function.

"This gives us a new way to understand immune behaviour in greater detail. My hope is that these fundamental insights will eventually translate into clinically useful tools to inform assessments of disease risk."

Rather than focusing only on immune cells that directly damage tissues, the study highlights the importance of cells that regulate and sustain immune responses.

"Although these helper cells aren't always the main focus in autoimmune research, they may hold important clues to why disease develops in some people and not others," said Professor Tye‑Din.

The team is now investigating whether similar immune patterns exist in other autoimmune conditions, and how early in life these differences appear.

"Our long‑term goal is to understand autoimmune risk well enough that we can act earlier – potentially even before disease begins," said Professor Tye-Din.

这些发现可能超越乳糜泻本身，为更广泛的自身免疫和炎症性疾病提供见解。

这项新研究由瓦尔特和伊丽莎霍尔医学研究所的瓦妮莎·布莱恩特博士、菲尔·霍奇金教授和苏珊娜·海因策尔博士领导，并获得了雪中心主任贾森·泰迪恩教授的临床专业知识支持。

该研究检查了一种被称为CD4辅助性T细胞的免疫细胞，这些细胞负责协调免疫反应、对抗感染并支持抗体生成。

“我们的检测方法有点像给玩具上发条然后松手，看看它能运行多久、能表演什么花样。”

“我们的方法揭示了T细胞如何有效地读取和储存其激活信号，以便在原始刺激消失后仍能保持其‘动量’。它还揭示了患有自身免疫性疾病的人其细胞功能是否存在内在差异。”

测量免疫“动量”

为了探索这一点，研究人员使用了该中心开创性的Cyton2细胞计时器模型，该模型能够随时间追踪免疫细胞的活动。

目的是追踪T细胞的“动量”。与大多数实验室测试持续刺激细胞不同，该团队短暂激活它们，然后移除所有信号，以观察接下来发生什么。

“这让我们能够看到细胞在最初的推动停止后如何表现。它揭示了在持续刺激下难以检测到的特性。”瓦妮莎·布莱恩特博士，瓦尔特和伊丽莎霍尔医学研究所。

该团队测量了细胞如何分裂、存活以及产生关键信号分子，从而描绘出早期免疫行为的详细图景。

布莱恩特博士表示，这种方法是一种测量免疫反应的全新独特方式，为研究人员提供了关于免疫反应如何出错的新见解。

“理解这些早期缺陷可能为预测风险、监测疾病带来更好的方法，并最终开发出能够恢复健康免疫平衡的治疗方法，而不仅仅是通过无麸质饮食来管理症状。”

一个出人意料的结果

与预期相反，乳糜泻患者的免疫细胞并非仅仅是过度活跃。相反，它们表现出较弱的反应。

研究发现乳糜泻患者的CD4辅助性T细胞：

产生的白细胞介素-2较少，白细胞介素-2是一种关键的免疫信号分子

进入细胞分裂的速度更慢

存活的可能性更低

“这些差异虽然微妙，但异常一致，”布莱恩特博士说。

值得注意的是，无论性别如何，也无论个体是新诊断的患者，还是通过无麸质饮食控制病情的患者，都出现了相同的模式。

“这告诉我们，这种效应并非仅仅由炎症或饮食驱动，”布莱恩特博士说。“它表明可能与遗传风险相关的潜在差异。”

超越单一疾病的潜力

尽管这项研究专注于乳糜泻，但其发现可能具有更广泛的意义。

自身免疫性疾病影响大约百分之五的人口，其中许多疾病共享重叠的遗传风险因素。

“如果自身免疫风险部分地内置于免疫细胞从一开始的行为方式中，这可能会改变我们对早期检测的看法，”布莱恩特博士说。

虽然这种方法是一种研究工具而非临床测试，但它为结合基因信息与功能性免疫测量打开了大门，以更好地预测疾病风险。

免疫健康观念的转变

泰迪恩教授表示，这项研究强调了雪中心的Cyton“细胞计时器”模型在揭示免疫功能隐藏模式方面的强大能力。

“这为我们提供了一种以更详细的方式理解免疫行为的新途径。我希望这些基本见解最终能转化为具有临床实用价值的工具，为疾病风险评估提供信息。”

该研究并未仅仅关注直接损伤组织的免疫细胞，而是强调了调节和维持免疫反应的细胞的重要性。

“尽管这些辅助细胞并非自身免疫研究中的主要焦点，但它们可能掌握着关于疾病为何在某些人中发生而在另一些人中不发生的关键线索，”泰迪恩教授说。

该团队目前正在调查其他自身免疫疾病中是否存在类似的免疫模式，以及这些差异在生命的哪个阶段出现。

“我们的长期目标是充分理解自身免疫风险，以便我们能够更早地采取行动——甚至可能在疾病开始之前，”泰迪恩教授说。
