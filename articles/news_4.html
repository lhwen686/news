Night owls eat later, choose less nutritious food, carry more belly fat and show higher metabolic risk

本文深入揭示了昼夜节律类型（Chronotype）这一内在生物时钟对代谢健康的关键影响。研究表明，夜型人（晚睡晚起者）由于其独特的进食模式——即白天进食量少而夜晚进食量大，即便总能量摄入相似，仍面临显著的代谢劣势。这主要源于进食时间与身体自然代谢节律的错位，导致一系列不利的生物标志物变化：胰岛素和甘油三酯水平升高预示着胰岛素抵抗和脂质代谢紊乱风险增加；保护性高密度脂蛋白（HDL）胆固醇水平降低则加剧了心血管疾病风险；同时，瘦素（leptin）水平升高和胃饥饿素（ghrelin）水平降低反映了体脂调控与食欲信号的失衡，可能促使脂肪堆积并影响饱腹感。这些机制共同解释了夜型人更高的体脂百分比和中心性肥胖风险。

这项发现对医疗健康行业具有重要启示，强调了“昼夜节律营养”（Chrono-nutrition）——即饮食时间与生物钟协调性——的重要性。未来的健康管理将超越简单的“吃什么”和“吃多少”，更需关注“何时吃”。医生和营养师在制定个性化干预方案时，应将患者的昼夜节律类型纳入考量，提供定制化的膳食时间建议。这有助于更精准地预防和管理肥胖、代谢综合征及相关心血管疾病，推动医疗实践向更精细化、个体化的方向发展，并为不同族裔人群的健康指南提供科学依据。

For generations, early to bed and early to rise was seen as the blueprint for a healthy life, and any departure from it was often considered unhealthy. Scientists, however, have discovered that whether someone is an early bird who wakes up early and starts the day with energy or a night owl who naturally stays up late and wakes up later is far more than a lifestyle choice. This pattern reflects the body's natural preference for the timing of sleep and wake cycles within a 24-hour day.

A recent study, however, suggests that being a night owl might come with its own set of metabolic disadvantages.

Meals are also timing cues for the body's internal clock, and when we eat can influence metabolism. In a recent paper, researchers explored whether a person's chronotype is linked to eating habits, body composition and overall metabolic health.

The study followed a group of healthy women living in Auckland, New Zealand, and found that night owls consumed less energy and fewer micronutrient-dense foods than those with a morning-type chronotype. Higher calorie intake in the evening was associated with a higher body fat percentage and a greater risk of obesity.

Because most night owls ate very little in the morning and consumed a large portion of their food late at night, they tended to have a higher body mass index (BMI) and body fat percentage than early birds.

The findings were published in Frontiers in Nutrition.

Sleep, food and biomarkers

Studies suggest that chronotype influences more than when we sleep—it also shapes our eating habits. While most studies have focused on total food intake, they have paid less attention to the timing of calories and nutrients, even though both what we eat and when we eat can vary by chronotype.

Evening types are more likely to eat later in the day, make less healthy food choices and have poorer eating habits overall. They have also been associated with a higher BMI. However, BMI alone cannot distinguish between muscle mass and body fat. This is particularly relevant in New Zealand, where Pacific women typically have a larger body build and greater muscle mass than those of European descent.

In this study, the researchers went beyond BMI. The team applied the Munich Chronotype Questionnaire (MCTQ) to determine the chronotypes of 130 New Zealand Pacific women and 157 New Zealand European women ages 18 to 45. Based on their sleep-wake patterns, participants were classified as morning, intermediate or evening types. Women who typically went to sleep at 11 p.m. and woke up at 8 a.m. were categorized as intermediate types.

Participants then recorded everything they ate and drank over five nonconsecutive days—three weekdays and two weekend days. Dietitians later interviewed each participant to verify portion sizes and ensure the dietary records were accurate.

Instead of relying solely on body weight, the researchers used DXA scans, a type of X-ray, to measure how much body fat the women had and where it was stored, including around the belly and hips. They also measured blood biomarkers, including blood sugar, cholesterol and fat-related hormone levels.

Night owls had poorer results across nearly every health marker researchers measured, compared with morning and intermediate types. They had a higher average BMI and carried more body fat overall, with a higher android-to-gynoid ratio, indicating more weight around the midsection rather than the hips—a pattern known to raise the risk of health problems. They ate less healthy food, consumed less fiber and fell short on essential vitamins and minerals.

Blood work revealed that evening types had higher insulin and triglyceride levels and lower levels of protective HDL cholesterol. They also showed increased levels of leptin, a hormone linked to body fat, and lower levels of ghrelin, the hormone that signals hunger, compared with other chronotypes.

The findings show that although all chronotype groups consumed similar amounts of calories and macronutrients, evening-type women had less favorable metabolic biomarker, body fat and nutrient profiles.

The researchers say more research is needed on this link between chrono-nutrition and obesity, especially in ethnically diverse populations. This will help scientists understand chronotype's true impact and eventually build personalized health interventions.

世代以来，早睡早起一直被视为健康生活的蓝图，任何偏离都常被认为是不健康的。然而，科学家们发现，一个人是早起并精力充沛地开始一天事务的“早鸟”，还是自然晚睡晚起的“夜猫子”，远不止是一种生活方式选择。这种模式反映了身体在24小时周期内对睡眠和觉醒时间节律的自然偏好。

然而，最近一项研究表明，作为夜型人可能伴随着一系列代谢劣势。

膳食也是身体内部时钟的时间提示，我们进食的时间会影响新陈代谢。在最近的一篇论文中，研究人员探讨了一个人的昼夜节律类型是否与饮食习惯、身体成分和整体代谢健康相关。

这项研究追踪了一群居住在新西兰奥克兰的健康女性，发现夜型人摄入的能量和微量营养素密度高的食物少于早型人。晚上较高的卡路里摄入量与较高的体脂百分比和更大的肥胖风险相关。

由于大多数夜型人在早上吃得很少，并在深夜摄入大部分食物，他们往往比早型人拥有更高的体重指数（BMI）和体脂百分比。

研究结果发表在《营养前沿》（Frontiers in Nutrition）杂志上。

睡眠、食物与生物标志物

研究表明，昼夜节律类型不仅影响我们的睡眠时间，还塑造我们的饮食习惯。虽然大多数研究关注总食物摄入量，但对卡路里和营养素的摄入时间关注较少，尽管我们吃什么和何时吃都可能因昼夜节律类型而异。

夜型人更倾向于在一天中较晚的时间进食，做出不健康的食物选择，并且整体饮食习惯较差。他们也与较高的BMI相关。然而，单独的BMI无法区分肌肉量和体脂肪。这在新西兰尤为重要，因为太平洋裔女性通常比欧洲裔女性拥有更大的体格和更多的肌肉量。

在这项研究中，研究人员超越了BMI的局限。研究团队应用慕尼黑昼夜节律问卷（MCTQ）来确定130名18至45岁的新西兰太平洋裔女性和157名新西兰欧洲裔女性的昼夜节律类型。根据她们的睡眠-觉醒模式，参与者被归类为早型人、中间型人或夜型人。通常在晚上11点睡觉、早上8点醒来的女性被归类为中间型人。

随后，参与者记录了他们在五个非连续日（三个工作日和两个周末日）内所吃所喝的一切。营养师随后采访每位参与者，以核实份量并确保膳食记录的准确性。

研究人员没有仅仅依赖体重，而是使用了双能X射线吸收法（DXA）扫描，这是一种X射线技术，用于测量女性的体脂肪量及其储存位置，包括腹部和臀部。他们还测量了血液生物标志物，包括血糖、胆固醇和脂肪相关激素水平。

与早型人和中间型人相比，夜型人在研究人员测量的几乎所有健康指标上都表现更差。他们平均BMI更高，整体体脂量更大，并且雄性脂肪分布（android-to-gynoid ratio）更高，表明体重更多集中在腰腹部而非臀部——这种模式已知会增加健康问题的风险。他们摄入的健康食物更少，纤维摄入量不足，且缺乏必需的维生素和矿物质。

血液检查显示，夜型人胰岛素和甘油三酯水平更高，而保护性高密度脂蛋白（HDL）胆固醇水平更低。与其他昼夜节律类型相比，他们还表现出瘦素（一种与体脂肪相关的激素）水平升高，以及胃饥饿素（ghrelin，一种发出饥饿信号的激素）水平降低。

研究结果表明，尽管所有昼夜节律类型组摄入的卡路里和宏量营养素总量相似，但夜型女性的代谢生物标志物、体脂肪和营养素状况均较不理想。

研究人员表示，需要更多研究来探讨昼夜节律营养（chrono-nutrition）与肥胖之间的这种联系，尤其是在不同族裔人群中。这将帮助科学家理解昼夜节律类型的真正影响，并最终构建个性化的健康干预措施。
