Original Text
Children recover significantly faster from concussion after receiving early, multidisciplinary care designed to treat persistent symptoms, according to a new study. And the model will provide a blueprint for future child-specific concussion clinics across Australia.

The results, published in the British Journal of Sports Medicine, reveal at three months post‑injury, 62.5 per cent of children who accessed treatment from the Murdoch Children's Research Institute (MCRI) Concussion Essentials intervention fully recovered, compared with 37 per cent receiving standard care. Additionally, early intervention greatly reduced the risk of physical and mental symptoms from becoming entrenched with 94 per cent of children experiencing improvements.

MCRI researcher Vanessa Rausa said the findings addressed a major gap in concussion care for children whose symptoms don't resolve quickly.

"While most children recover well after concussion, almost a third continue to experience symptoms that disrupt school, sport and everyday life," she said. "This study shows that if we identify those children early and provide targeted, coordinated care, we can significantly speed up recovery."

Former Victorian Minister Danny Pearson visited MCRI last April to announce the launch of a study that would explore the impact of this intervention in a dedicated children's concussion clinic. The study is supported by a Victorian Medical Research Acceleration Fund (VMRAF) grant.

The randomized clinical trial involved 158 children, aged eight to 18 years, with ongoing post‑concussion symptoms, recruited within weeks of their head injury from The Royal Children's Hospital (RCH) emergency department and GP referrals. Treatment began four weeks post injury and was delivered weekly by an integrated multidisciplinary team for up to eight weeks, with their care adjusted as symptoms evolved.

The study, led by MCRI, in partnership with the Neurological Rehabilitation Group, found that participants who received tailored care combining education, physiotherapy and psychological support were substantially more likely to fully recover than those receiving usual care alone. The program was feasible and acceptable for families despite the commitment required to complete the treatment.

Children in the intervention arm also experienced significantly fewer and less severe symptoms across sleep and their physical, cognitive and mental health.

Concussion is one of the most common reasons children present to hospital with a head injury. While most recover within a few weeks, about 30 per cent develop persisting symptoms. Current care often focuses on single treatments or involves delays between referrals, which can allow symptoms to worsen.

MCRI Professor Vicki Anderson said the results supported incorporating this intervention into routine clinical practice.

"Concussion is a growing health concern, with diagnosis and management poorly understood," she said. "Children don't experience concussion in a single way. By bringing education, physiotherapy and psychological care together and tailoring treatment to each child, we can respond to those different needs. We think early targeted treatment may also reduces the need for repeated healthcare visits and specialist referrals."

Professor Anderson said further research would focus on how the model could be expanded beyond hospital settings.

"We now need to look at how this kind of care can be scaled to community clinics and delivered through options like telehealth so more families outside of metropolitan areas can access effective treatment earlier," she said.

Macy, 15, suffered a concussion during a surfing lesson last year while on a school excursion.

After nose-diving off her surfboard, Macy, scraped her head on the ocean floor, leaving her with sand burn above her right eyebrow.

Mum Jo said Macy seemed fine at first, but 18 hours later it was clear that something wasn't right.

"Macy was not herself at all," she said. "She was absent minded, emotional, tired and easily overwhelmed, had headaches and kept bumping into things. Three days after her wipeout, Macy was back at school, but she was overwhelmed by all the classroom noise, couldn't follow what the teacher was saying and ended up leaving in tears. I picked her up early and made a GP appointment that day."

A GP diagnosed Macy with a concussion and when her symptoms didn't improve, she was referred to a sports doctor and physiotherapist.

But the turning point came when the family was alerted to a news story about MCRI's new concussion clinic.

"It was amazing that we found the clinic, a relief and a blessing," Jo said. "There was hope that Macy could recover and receive all the specialist care she needed in the one location."

Jo said the clinic was a turning point for Macy's recovery.

"We didn't have to convince the clinic staff of anything, they understood concussion immediately," she said. "Macy finally had the practical support she needed, and the team liaised with her school, recommending tailored adjustments. This helped teachers understand what Macy was going through and eased her return to learning."

Jo said while attending eight sessions at the clinic, Macy gradually improved.

"Her recovery took about five months, which left her frustrated and frightened at times," she said. "But the girl who walked into the clinic and the one who walked out were completely different."

To also help recognise concussion early, the Concussion Essentials (HeadCheck) app has been designed by leading child concussion experts at MCRI in collaboration with the Australian Football League. The free assessment tool educates parents, coaches and players to understand when medical attention is needed and how to manage recovery safely.

Researchers from the University of Melbourne, University of Queensland, Austin Hospital, Cabrini Hospital, University of Canterbury in New Zealand, Deakin University and Monash University also contributed to the study.

一项新研究显示，儿童在接受旨在治疗持续性症状的早期多学科综合护理后，脑震荡康复速度显著加快。这一模式将为澳大利亚未来针对儿童的脑震荡诊所提供蓝图。

发表在《英国运动医学杂志》上的研究结果显示，在受伤后三个月，接受墨道克儿童研究所（MCRI）“脑震荡要点”干预治疗的儿童中，有62.5%完全康复，而接受标准护理的儿童康复比例为37%。此外，早期干预大大降低了身体和精神症状固化的风险，94%的儿童症状有所改善。

MCRI研究员瓦妮莎·劳萨表示，这些发现弥补了对症状未能迅速缓解的儿童进行脑震荡护理方面的一个主要空白。

她说：“虽然大多数儿童在脑震荡后恢复良好，但仍有近三分之一的儿童持续经历影响学习、运动和日常生活症状。这项研究表明，如果我们能及早识别出这些儿童并提供有针对性、协调一致的护理，我们就能显著加快康复速度。”

去年四月，前维多利亚州部长丹尼·皮尔森访问了MCRI，宣布启动一项研究，旨在专门的儿童脑震荡诊所中探讨这项干预措施的影响。这项研究获得了维多利亚医学研究加速基金（VMRAF）的资助。

这项随机临床试验共纳入了158名8至18岁、持续存在脑震荡后症状的儿童，他们是在头部受伤数周内从皇家儿童医院（RCH）急诊科和全科医生转诊中招募的。治疗于伤后四周开始，由一个综合多学科团队每周提供，持续长达八周，并根据症状发展调整护理方案。

由MCRI与神经康复小组合作牵头进行的这项研究发现，接受结合了教育、物理治疗和心理支持的个性化护理的参与者，比仅接受常规护理的参与者更有可能完全康复。尽管完成治疗需要投入时间和精力，但该方案对家庭而言是可行且可接受的。

干预组的儿童在睡眠以及身体、认知和心理健康方面，症状的发生率和严重程度也显著降低。

脑震荡是儿童因头部受伤就诊的最常见原因之一。尽管大多数儿童在几周内康复，但约有30%的儿童会出现持续性症状。目前的护理通常侧重于单一治疗，或在转诊之间存在延迟，这可能导致症状恶化。

MCRI教授维姬·安德森表示，研究结果支持将这项干预措施纳入常规临床实践。

她说：“脑震荡是一个日益增长的健康问题，其诊断和管理往往知之甚少。儿童经历脑震荡的方式不尽相同。通过将教育、物理治疗和心理护理结合起来，并为每个孩子量身定制治疗方案，我们可以应对这些不同的需求。我们认为早期有针对性的治疗也可能减少重复就诊和专科转诊的需要。”

安德森教授表示，未来的研究将重点关注如何将这一模式扩展到医院以外的环境。

她说：“我们现在需要研究如何将这种护理推广到社区诊所，并通过远程医疗等方式提供，以便大都市以外的更多家庭能够更早地获得有效治疗。”

15岁的梅西去年在一次学校郊游中上冲浪课时，遭遇了脑震荡。

梅西从冲浪板上栽倒，头部擦伤了海底，右眉上方留下了沙子擦伤。

妈妈乔说，梅西起初看起来没事，但18小时后，情况明显不对劲。

她说：“梅西完全不像她自己了。她心不在焉、情绪化、疲惫，很容易感到不知所措，还头痛并经常撞到东西。摔倒三天后，梅西回到了学校，但她被课堂上的所有噪音压垮了，听不懂老师在说什么，最后哭着离开了。那天我提前接她回家，并预约了全科医生。”

全科医生诊断梅西为脑震荡，当她的症状没有改善时，她被转诊给运动医生和物理治疗师。

但转折点出现在家人得知有关MCRI新脑震荡诊所的新闻报道之后。

乔说：“我们找到了这个诊所，真是太棒了，令人宽慰又是一种福气。这让我们看到了梅西可以康复的希望，她可以在一个地方获得所有需要的专科护理。”

乔说，这个诊所是梅西康复的转折点。

她说：“我们不需要向诊所工作人员解释什么，他们立刻就理解了脑震荡的情况。梅西终于获得了她所需的实际支持，团队还与她的学校联系，建议进行个性化调整。这帮助老师们理解了梅西的经历，并缓解了她重返学习的难度。”

乔说，在诊所接受了八次治疗后，梅西逐渐好转。

她说：“她的康复花了大约五个月时间，这有时让她感到沮丧和害怕。但走进诊所和走出诊所的女孩判若两人。”

为了帮助早期识别脑震荡，MCRI的顶尖儿童脑震荡专家与澳大利亚澳式足球联盟合作设计了“脑震荡要点”（HeadCheck）应用程序。这个免费的评估工具旨在教育家长、教练和运动员了解何时需要医疗护理以及如何安全管理康复。
