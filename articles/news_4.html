处罚医院的医疗厌女症并不会帮助女性，反而会伤害她们

Fining hospitals for medical misogyny won't help women, it will hurt them

这篇文章揭示了英国国家医疗服务体系中根深蒂固的医疗性别歧视问题及其潜在的政策应对偏差。核心概念包括“医疗性别歧视”（medical misogyny），它源于现代医学实践长期以男性身体为参照，导致女性在药物剂量、疼痛研究和疾病诊断标准方面被忽视，进而造成子宫内膜异位症等疾病的诊断延误和心肌梗死等急症的误诊。

文章还探讨了“按绩效付费计划”（pay-for-performance schemes）的商业逻辑：通过经济激励提高服务质量。然而，其行业影响却可能适得其反，加剧“逆向护理定律”（inverse care law）所述的困境——即医疗资源最匮乏的医院反而因评分不佳而进一步被削减资金，导致最需要帮助的患者（特别是弱势女性）的护理质量下降，形成“厄运循环”。

这种政策可能非但不能解决问题，反而加速医护人员流失。深度解析表明，真正有效的解决方案应聚焦于从根本上解决医学研究和临床实践中的性别偏见，投资于女性健康中心等预防和早期干预服务，并确保稳定的临床人员配置和专业培训，而非简单地通过预算削减来惩罚已然不堪重负的医疗机构。

Hospitals that score poorly on feedback from female patients could soon see their budget cut under a plan unveiled in April by Wes Streeting, the UK's health secretary. Branded "patient power payments," the scheme would tie a slice of hospital income to women's experiences of care, a measure designed to end what Streeting himself has called an "appalling culture of medical misogyny" in England's National Health Service.

The instinct behind the policy is understandable. Women's anger is real, well founded and widely overdue for a serious answer.

The current backlog experienced by women is the clearest summary of the problem. Nearly a quarter of a million women are on waiting lists for gynecological care in England. This number has roughly doubled since 2018 and grown faster than any other clinical specialty's waiting list.

In a survey of more than 100,000 women, half said their pain had been disregarded and overlooked. In the UK today, obtaining a diagnosis of endometriosis (a painful condition affecting roughly one in ten women) now takes an average of nearly nine years and roughly 10 visits to a GP.

This is a cultural sickness. Whether the right response is to dock money from overburdened hospitals is a different question.

Pay-for-performance schemes for hospitals have a long and somewhat checkered history abroad. A US review found that American hospitals serving the poorest patients paid roughly 10% of all penalty dollars under Medicare's quality programs while taking home only 5% of the bonuses. Research has also found that such programs risk widening inequality by diverting funds from hospitals that care for the sickest patients.

The surveys themselves are not without bias. Female, Asian and black doctors score lower on patient experience ratings than their white male peers, even when the care is identical. These scores often pick up charm, confidence and continuity rather than clinical quality.

The problem with importing this logic into England is that the hospitals most likely to score the worst are already in the worst shape.

Patients in deprived areas tend to be sicker and develop multiple long-term conditions up to seven years earlier than those in wealthier neighborhoods—factors that drag satisfaction scores down without necessarily reflecting poor clinical care.

Hospitals serving patients in the most deprived areas recorded the steepest fall in finances last year, and the NHS as a whole is carrying a deficit above £1 billion, with more than 20,000 roles needing to be cut just to balance the books.

The inverse care law

Strip cash from hospitals with the longest waits, the hardest caseloads and the smallest budgets, and the women least well served to start with will find their care worse, not better. That is what the inverse care law, first set out in 1971, predicts: good health care is scarcest where it is needed most, and scarcer still where market pressures dominate.

The doom loop is easy to foresee: a struggling hospital loses funding because its ratings are poor; it cannot adequately recruit or retain gynecologists; waiting times lengthen; ratings drop further; more money disappears.

The question, then, is what would actually work.

Do other policies show more promise? Between 2023 and 2025, England funded women's health hubs—one-stop community clinics offering contraception, menopause care and help with period problems and pelvic pain under one roof. Evaluations led by Rand Europe reported shorter waits, fewer hospital referrals and better patient experience. Dedicated funding for the hubs has not been renewed and many face being scaled back or closed in 2026.

A deeper fix might be found within medicine itself. Much of modern practice was built around male bodies, and women were largely excluded from clinical trials until the 1990s, leaving drug doses, pain research and disease criteria mostly calibrated around men.

The textbook heart attack symptom of crushing chest pain is one more likely to be experienced by men. This is one reason why women are around 50% more likely than men to be given the wrong diagnosis when having a heart attack.

Women reporting abdominal pain are routinely sent through slow general clinics rather than dedicated gynecology services. Resetting those defaults costs relatively little and tackles the bias at its source.

The reality is that this problem is bigger than any one solution can deliver. Women are dismissed in consultations, wait years for routine gynecology and are misdiagnosed for conditions from endometriosis to heart attacks—issues that need clinical time, training and steady staffing, not budget cuts.

Obstetrics and gynecology have among the worst vacancy rates in the English health service. One in five obstetricians and gynecologists plans to leave the NHS within five years and nurses are quitting the profession at record rates. A scheme that cuts the budgets of struggling hospitals threatens to speed the exodus driving the problem in the first place.

"Patient power payments" has the appeal of borrowing the language of consumer choice. It reframes our cultural failure as a marketplace one. But a marketplace will always reward customers with the most power to walk away. And punish the hospitals left to look after the women with the least.

根据英国卫生大臣韦斯·斯特里廷（Wes Streeting）四月份公布的一项计划，那些在女性患者反馈中得分较低的医院，可能很快就会面临预算削减。“患者赋能支付”计划将把医院收入的一部分与女性患者的护理体验挂钩，此举旨在终结斯特里廷本人所称的英格兰国家医疗服务体系中“骇人听闻的医疗性别歧视文化”。

这项政策背后的初衷是可以理解的。女性的愤怒是真实存在的，有充分依据的，而且早该得到认真回应。

女性目前经历的积压问题，是这个问题的最清晰概括。在英格兰，将近二十五万名女性正在等待妇科护理。自2018年以来，这一数字大约翻了一番，并且比任何其他临床专科的候诊名单增长得更快。

在一项对超过十万名女性的调查中，一半受访者表示她们的疼痛被忽视和漠视。在今天的英国，诊断子宫内膜异位症（一种影响大约十分之一女性的痛苦疾病）平均需要将近九年时间，并要看大约十次全科医生。

这是一种文化病态。然而，对不堪重负的医院进行资金削减是否是正确的应对方式，则是一个不同的问题。

针对医院的按绩效付费计划在国外有着漫长且不无争议的历史。美国的一项审查发现，服务最贫困患者的美国医院在医疗保险质量计划下支付了大约10%的罚款，而获得的奖金仅为5%。研究还发现，此类计划可能通过将资金从那些护理病情最重患者的医院分流出去，从而加剧不平等。

调查本身并非没有偏见。女性、亚裔和黑人医生在患者体验评分上普遍低于白人男性同行，即使提供的护理相同。这些评分往往更侧重于魅力、自信和连续性，而非临床质量。

将这种逻辑引入英格兰的问题在于，那些最有可能得分最低的医院，其现状也通常是最糟糕的。

贫困地区的患者往往病情更重，并且比富裕社区的患者提前多达七年患上多种慢性病——这些因素会拉低满意度评分，而不一定反映临床护理质量差。

去年，服务最贫困地区患者的医院财务状况跌幅最大，而整个国家医疗服务体系的赤字超过10亿英镑，仅为收支平衡就需要削减2万多个职位。

逆向护理定律

从那些候诊时间最长、病例负担最重、预算最小的医院削减资金，只会让那些原本就得不到良好服务的女性的护理状况变得更糟，而非更好。这就是1971年首次提出的“逆向护理定律”所预测的：优质医疗保健在最需要的地方最为稀缺，而在市场压力主导的地方则更加稀缺。

这种“厄运循环”很容易预见：一家陷入困境的医院因评分不佳而失去资金；它无法充分招聘或留住妇科医生；候诊时间延长；评分进一步下降；更多资金流失。

那么，问题在于什么才是真正有效的。

其他政策是否更有前景？在2023年至2025年期间，英格兰资助了女性健康中心——这些一站式社区诊所提供避孕、更年期护理以及月经问题和盆腔疼痛的帮助。由兰德欧洲（Rand Europe）主导的评估报告显示，候诊时间缩短、转诊医院次数减少以及患者体验改善。然而，这些中心的专项资金尚未续期，许多中心面临在2026年被缩减规模或关闭的风险。

更深层次的解决方案可能存在于医学本身。现代医学实践的很大一部分是围绕男性身体建立的，直到1990年代，女性在临床试验中基本上都被排除在外，导致药物剂量、疼痛研究和疾病诊断标准主要以男性为参照进行校准。

教科书上描述的心脏病发作症状——压迫性胸痛——更常出现在男性身上。这也是女性在心脏病发作时被误诊的可能性比男性高约50%的原因之一。

报告腹痛的女性通常被送往缓慢的普通门诊，而不是专门的妇科服务。重新设定这些默认程序成本相对较低，并且能从源头上解决偏见问题。

现实是，这个问题比任何单一解决方案所能解决的范围都要大。女性在诊疗中被轻视，为常规妇科检查等待数年，从子宫内膜异位症到心脏病发作等疾病被误诊——这些问题需要临床时间、培训和稳定的医护人员配置，而不是预算削减。

妇产科在英格兰卫生服务体系中是空缺率最高的专科之一。五分之一的妇产科医生计划在五年内离开国家医疗服务体系，护士辞职率也创历史新高。一项削减困境医院预算的计划，恐将加速这种最初导致问题加剧的人员流失。

“患者赋能支付”方案具有借用消费者选择语言的吸引力。它将我们的文化失败重新定义为市场问题。但一个市场总是会奖励那些最有能力离开的客户，并惩罚那些留下照顾最弱势女性的医院。
