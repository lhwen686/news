Standard breast cancer classification may overlook important differences

深度解析

这篇医学新闻的核心洞见在于挑战了乳腺癌雌激素受体（ER）分类的传统二元模式，即简单分为ER阳性或ER阴性。传统的ER阳性乳腺癌通常被视为激素敏感型，主要通过阻断雌激素作用的内分泌治疗。然而，文章引入了“光谱”概念，强调ER表达水平并非简单的有无，而是连续变化的。研究通过对大量HER2阴性乳腺癌患者的数据分析发现，即使在ER阳性范围内，不同比例的肿瘤细胞表达ER，其生物学特性、临床预后及对治疗的反应也存在显著差异。具体而言，ER表达水平较低或中等（低于60%）的患者生存率明显低于高表达患者，并且这些肿瘤在生物学上更接近ER阴性肿瘤。研究甚至观察到，ER中低水平的患者可能从化疗与激素治疗的联合方案中获益。这一发现深刻影响了肿瘤学领域的精准医疗实践。它提示临床医生在诊断和制定治疗方案时，应超越简单的ER阳性/阴性判断，更精细地量化ER表达百分比。这有助于更准确地识别预后较差的患者亚群，并可能引导开发或优化针对特定ER表达水平的个体化治疗策略，从而提高治疗效果和患者生存率，推动乳腺癌诊疗进入一个更精细、更个体化的新时代。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain Breast cancer is often classified as either estrogen receptor-positive (hormone-sensitive) or not. However, a new study from Karolinska Institutet suggests that this simplified classification may overlook important biological differences between patients that could affect treatment decisions. The results are published in The Lancet Regional Health – Europe. Breast cancer tumors that express estrogen receptors are known as estrogen receptor-positive (ER-positive) or hormone-sensitive breast cancer and, on average, have a better prognosis than ER-negative breast cancer. ER-positive tumors can grow in response to estrogen and can therefore be treated with drugs that block the effects of this hormone. It is a spectrum Internationally, tumors in which at least 1% of the tumor cells express the estrogen receptor are classified as ER-positive, whereas in Sweden the threshold is 10%. "Rather than talking about ER-positive or ER-negative breast cancer, we need to view it as a spectrum," says Balazs Acs, associate professor at the Department of Oncology-Pathology, Karolinska Institutet. "Our study shows that a more detailed classification can provide important information about the tumor's biological characteristics and the patient's prognosis." The researchers analyzed data from more than 75,000 women in Sweden who, between 2007 and 2023, had been diagnosed with HER2-negative breast cancer, a type of cancer that does not have elevated levels of the HER2 protein. The patients were divided into five groups based on the percentage of tumor cells expressing the estrogen receptor: 0%, 1%–9%, 10%–29%, 30%–59% or 60%–100%. The tumor characteristics, treatments and survival rates were then compared across the groups. The researchers also analyzed molecular data from almost 4,800 tumors to study biological differences. May guide treatment Women whose tumors had low or moderate levels of estrogen receptors (up to 59%) had poorer survival rates than those whose tumors had high levels (more than 60%). These differences remained even when the researchers took into account other important factors such as tumor stage, age and treatment. Tumors in which 10% to 29% of the tumor cells expressed estrogen receptors were biologically and clinically similar to tumors with very low levels or no estrogen receptors at all. "We also observed that patients with mild to moderate levels of estrogen receptors appear to benefit from a combination of chemotherapy and hormone therapy," Acs says. "This suggests that the exact level of estrogen receptors may be important when choosing a treatment." The researchers emphasize that the results come from an observational study and that treatments were not randomly allocated among patients. Further studies are needed to investigate how the findings can best be translated into clinical practice.
