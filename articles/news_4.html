深度解析

野火烟雾中的PM 2.5微粒是核心致病因素。这些直径小于2.5微米的颗粒能够深达肺泡并穿透肺部屏障进入血液循环，诱发全身炎症反应和氧化应激。医学机制上，PM 2.5直接刺激气道，加剧哮喘和慢性阻塞性肺疾病（COPD）患者的气道炎症、支气管痉挛，导致呼吸困难、咳嗽等症状恶化。对于儿童，其未成熟的呼吸系统更易受损。心血管系统方面，PM 2.5可引发内皮功能障碍、血栓形成风险增加，并可能诱发心肌梗死、中风或心律失常，尤其对已有心脏病或糖尿病的患者构成严重威胁。 行业影响层面，野火空气污染对公共卫生系统造成巨大压力，表现为急诊和紧急护理就诊量激增，凸显了医疗机构应对突发公共卫生事件准备的重要性。这推动了公众健康教育，强调预防措施，如室内空气净化（HEPA设备）、正确佩戴N95/KN95呼吸器，并刺激了相关医疗设备和防护用品的市场需求。此类事件也深刻揭示了气候变化对人类健康的直接威胁，促使医疗健康领域与环境科学、政策制定者之间进行更紧密的跨学科合作，以构建更具韧性的公共卫生防御体系。

This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies . Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Days of poor air quality from thick wildfire smoke are pushing more Oregonians into emergency rooms and urgent care clinics, state health officials say. Respiratory complaints have climbed past what the state normally sees this time of year in regions blanketed by smoke, the Oregon Health Authority (OHA) reported Wednesday, according to The Oregonian, the state's largest newspaper. Much of Oregon is under an air quality advisory, including Portland and long stretches of the central and eastern parts of the state. The demographics of those seeking care have surprised experts. Adults ages 18–44 made up the largest share of patients seeking care, rather than older adults, as expected. Gabriela Goldfarb, environmental health manager for OHA's Public Health Division, said older adults and people with chronic illness may already be taking precautions, while younger adults are shrugging off the risk. "Folks who are in that younger cohort may feel a little bit less vulnerable," Goldfarb told The Oregonian. "But it turns out it impacts everybody's health as well." When trees and plants burn, the smoke carries tiny particles. The smallest are called PM 2.5 and are about 30 times thinner than a human hair. They are small enough to sink deep into the lungs and pass into the bloodstream, Goldfarb said. Those particles are especially risky for people with heart disease, asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) or diabetes, she said. Kids are vulnerable too because their respiratory systems are still maturing. Officials urge people to stay indoors when air quality turns unhealthy and to skip strenuous outdoor activity such as running or heavy yard work. Those without air conditioning were also urged to spend part of the day in a library, community center or other public building with filtered air. Indoors, OHA points to portable HEPA units or a do-it-yourself version rigged from a box fan and furnace filters to help reduce smoke particles. Outdoors, only a snug N95 or KN95 respirator will do. Bandanas, cloth face coverings and surgical masks let smoke particles slip through, Goldfarb said. Burning eyes, coughing and a sore throat are the most common symptoms of smoke exposure. Anyone with painful or difficult breathing or whose preexisting health conditions worsen should seek medical advice. Smoke is expected to thin around Portland by Friday and across eastern Oregon by Saturday. Nationally, crews are fighting 86 uncontained large fires, roughly half of them in Oregon and Washington, the National Interagency Fire Center said. Fourteen states are currently reporting large fires. Firefighters have responded to 45,287 wildfires so far this year, burning more than 5.5 million acres.
