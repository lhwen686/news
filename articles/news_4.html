保护性药物地拉佐生可减少儿童癌症幸存者在治疗结束后多年出现的心脏损伤

Protective drug dexrazoxane reduces heart damage in pediatric cancer survivors years after treatment ends

深度解析

这篇文章揭示了心脏保护药物右雷佐生（dexrazoxane）在减轻蒽环类化疗药物（如多柔比星doxorubicin）对儿科癌症患者心脏长期损害方面的显著作用。多柔比星作为一种广泛使用的蒽环类化疗药，通过嵌入DNA并抑制拓扑异构酶II来杀死癌细胞，但其主要的剂量限制性毒性是心肌细胞损害，主要机制涉及自由基的产生和氧化应激，导致线粒体功能障碍、心肌细胞凋亡及坏死，最终表现为心腔扩大和心力衰竭。右雷佐生作为一种环磷酰胺衍生物，其核心机制是铁螯合作用。它能与铁离子结合，形成一个不易被多柔比星还原的复合物，从而阻止多柔比星诱导的自由基形成，保护心肌细胞免受氧化损伤。这项长期研究证实了右雷佐生的临床疗效，不仅能提高患儿的长期生存质量，也可能改变对这些幸存者的临床随访策略，例如减少超声心动图的检查频率，从而减轻患者和家庭的经济及心理负担。这对于优化儿科肿瘤治疗方案、制定更精细的长期管理指南具有重要的行业影响，并为患者带来了重要的安心。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: CP Khanal from Pexels A new study reveals that the cardioprotective drug dexrazoxane significantly reduces heart damage caused by chemotherapy in pediatric cancer patients 15 years or more after treatment ends. The research, which builds on previous studies at earlier follow-up times, is the first large, long-term study suggesting that receiving dexrazoxane may change how doctors follow survivors for decades after treatment. The findings could also help guide prescribing recommendations while providing families and patients with reassurance about the long-term effects of cancer treatment on young patients. "When people receive chemotherapy it goes all over the body; it doesn't just go to one location. Chemotherapy does a lot of good in killing cancer cells, but it also harms our healthy cells," said Erin Mobley, an assistant professor of surgery at the University of Florida College of Medicine in Jacksonville and the first author of the new study. "Especially in young, growing people, those healthy cells include the heart." Mobley collaborated with the original leaders of the clinical trial, including Eric Chow, M.D., at the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center and Steven Lipshultz, M.D., at the University at Buffalo. The researchers published their findings, which were supported in part by the National Institutes of Health and the Children's Oncology Group, on Aug. 6 in the Journal of Clinical Oncology. The study followed nearly 900 childhood cancer survivors into young adulthood, including 230 survivors who had been followed for at least a decade, with almost 200 participants tracked for more than 15 years. The researchers focused on pediatric patients who received doxorubicin, a type of chemotherapy known as an anthracycline, to treat different types of leukemia, lymphoma and sarcoma. Despite advances in cancer treatment, more than half of treatment regimens still include anthracyclines—the class of drugs most strongly associated with heart damage. Half of the patients in the study received dexrazoxane, which was originally approved to help protect heart tissue in adults undergoing chemotherapy and is now commonly prescribed to children as well. Chemotherapy can expand the size of the heart's chambers, weakening the organ and potentially leading to heart failure. In echocardiogram tests years after treatment, patients who received dexrazoxane had significantly healthier hearts. Many survivors who received the drug were classified as at moderate risk of heart damage rather than high risk. This classification could mean that survivors who received dexrazoxane during chemotherapy require less intensive screening after finishing treatment, although any potential changes to screening recommendations would require additional studies. "Now that we've established there's this benefit, and the majority of cancer patients do receive this drug, we will hopefully be able to decrease the frequency of their echocardiograms as they age, although we need to keep studying these survivors to better understand how their cardiovascular health changes as they enter their 50s and 60s," Mobley said. Mobley, a pediatric cancer survivor who received anthracyclines herself, said that in addition to updating screening guidelines for survivors, one of the biggest benefits of this kind of long-term study could be helping families imagine brighter futures after treatment ends. "It could give patients and their families a little bit of peace of mind," she said. "It's what makes me want to get out of bed and do cancer research every day. The opportunity to make the cancer survivorship experience better for people, whether they're diagnosed at 5 or 95." Publication details Erin M. Mobley et al, Longitudinal Change in Cardiac Function After Doxorubicin and Dexrazoxane: A Report From Children's Oncology Group ALTE11C2, Journal of Clinical Oncology (2026).
