生长追赶不佳的早产儿可能出现脑部异常

Premature babies who do not catch up well on growth may suffer brain abnormalities

深度解析

本研究深入探讨了宫外生长受限（EUGR）对早产儿脑发育的长期影响及其临床意义。EUGR 是指早产儿出生后未能达到其在宫内应有的生长轨迹，普遍影响约 37% 的早产儿。由于早产中断了胎儿在子宫内持续且充足的营养供应，若出生后未能及时、充分地进行追赶性生长，将导致持续性营养不足，进而对神经系统产生深远影响。研究核心机制在于，未能实现充分的追赶性生长与大脑结构，特别是额叶的持续性异常密切相关。额叶是高级认知功能和情绪调节的关键区域，其结构损伤可能导致情绪调节障碍及其他神经发育问题。这些发现对儿科和新生儿学领域具有重大影响。它强调了在生命早期阶段，特别是新生儿期后，优化营养策略和实施早期、积极的营养干预至关重要，例如个体化的肠内或肠外营养支持。同时，研究呼吁临床实践应制定标准化协议，不仅关注体重改善，更要通过长期监测生长发育来促进发育中器官的结构和功能成熟。这为高风险早产儿人群的干预和监测策略提供了新的方向，有望显著改善他们的长期神经认知和情绪发展预后。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: University of Granada Nutrition and growth during the first years of life are critical to the development of children born prematurely. A new study from the University of Granada analyzes the long-term consequences of extrauterine growth restriction (EUGR), which affects 37% of premature infants. The researchers identified possible persistent structural brain abnormalities. The study, published in the journal Early Human Development, demonstrates that failure to achieve adequate catch-up growth following extrauterine growth restriction has long-term consequences for the brain regions responsible for emotional regulation, particularly certain areas of the frontal lobe. The scientists evaluated children with a history of very low birth weight prematurity who had experienced EUGR and analyzed their development up to 2 years of age, focusing particularly on those who had not achieved optimal growth. The results indicate that a lack of weight catch-up during this critical period is associated with a lasting impact on development. The finding reinforces the importance of optimizing nutritional strategies from the earliest stages of life. "Prematurity interrupts the continuous supply of nutrients that occurs during gestation, making early and appropriate nutritional intervention essential to prevent deficits that could have long-term consequences," said José Uberos Fernández, professor in the Department of Pediatrics at the University of Granada (UGR) and author of the study. Furthermore, the study provides relevant evidence in a clinical context where nutritional practices for preterm infants still vary. The results underscore the need for protocols that promote proper growth in the early years, with the goal of improving not only weight but also the structure and function of developing organs. Overall, this research highlights that monitoring growth beyond the neonatal period is essential and that the absence of catch-up growth can have persistent consequences, opening the door to new intervention and monitoring strategies for this high-risk population. Publication details J. Uberos et al, Impact of catch-up growth on brain structures involved in emotional regulation in preterm children at 2 years of age, Early Human Development (2026).
