Bile acid buildup linked to aggressive breast cancer progression

深度解析

这项研究揭示了肠道微生物组失调与激素受体阳性（HR+）乳腺癌转移之间一种此前未被充分认识的关键医学机制。核心概念在于，当肠道微生物环境失衡，即发生菌群失调时，其对胆汁酸组成的调节能力受损，导致某些胆汁酸在体内积聚。胆汁酸是重要的信号分子，其异常升高会引发全身性炎症反应，特别是乳腺微环境的炎症，从而创建了一个有利于乳腺癌细胞增殖和向肺部等远处器官转移的生态位。 这一发现对临床和行业产生了深远影响。首先，它为识别高危HR+乳腺癌患者提供了潜在的生物标志物，即通过检测胆汁酸水平来评估转移风险。其次，研究提出了两种极具前景的干预策略：一是通过调节肠道微生物组，例如补充特定益生菌或进行粪菌移植，以恢复胆汁酸的正常代谢；二是对患者使用已获FDA批准用于治疗代谢疾病的胆汁酸螯合剂。后一种方法尤为引人注目，因为现有药物的再利用可以显著缩短新药开发的周期和成本，加速其在肿瘤治疗中的应用。这开辟了预防转移性乳腺癌的新路径，有望改善患者的长期预后，体现了精准医学和微生物组疗法在肿瘤领域的巨大潜力。

英文原文

Original
A buildup of bile acids caused by an unhealthy gut can drive breast cancer to other parts of the body, new University of Virginia Comprehensive Cancer Center research reveals. The findings could open the door to new ways to prevent breast cancer metastasis (spread), improve patient outcomes and save lives. The research from UVA's Melanie Rutkowski, PhD, and colleagues helps explain how the gut microbiome – the collection of microorganisms that live in and on our intestines – plays a role in the spread of hormone receptor-positive (HR+) breast cancer. The work adds to a growing understanding of the far-reaching influence of the microbiome in both disease and in maintaining good health. "We found that an unhealthy gut microbiome loses the ability to regulate the composition of bile acids, potent signaling molecules that when not properly regulated can cause havoc on your health, metabolism and the immune system. This creates an environment throughout the body that favors breast tumor metastasis," Rutkowski said. "Our findings suggest that we could reduce breast cancer metastasis either by replenishing the bacteria that can modify the composition of bile acids, or by treating breast cancer patients with bile acid sequestrants, drugs that are already FDA-approved for treating metabolic diseases." Targeting HR+ breast cancer HR+ breast cancer is by far the most common form of metastatic breast cancer, with 225,000 diagnoses each year in American women. HR+ breast cancer tends to spread early, with tumors often forming in the lymph nodes and lungs. Once the cancer has spread, it is much more difficult to treat, and the patient's risk of death goes up. Prior studies had linked gut microbiome imbalance – known as gut "dysbiosis" – with poor patient outcomes, but Rutkowski's research identifies a specific mechanism responsible for this. The study found that gut dysbiosis led to a buildup of bile acids, which play important roles in digestion and metabolism. They further found that bile acid buildup triggers harmful breast inflammation that ultimately fuels the spread of breast cancer cells to other parts of the body, especially the lungs. "We found that certain bile acids produced when the gut microbiome is disrupted can create conditions that make it easier for breast cancer cells to spread," said Audrey Putelo, PhD, co-author of the study. "Because bile acids can be measured and modified, this finding opens exciting possibilities for developing new approaches to identify high-risk patients and reduce the likelihood of metastatic disease." Effects in human patients While the scientists' work was in lab mice, they went on to look at human patients with HR+ breast cancer. They found that elevated bile acids and insulin resistance were associated with reduced survival. Further, patients with metastatic disease who received bile acid-suppressing medications tended to live longer. (These medications, known as bile-acid sequestrants, have already been approved by the federal Food and Drug Administration, but the researchers caution that more studies are needed to determine if the drugs are directly causing the benefits seen in patients.) Rutkowski's promising results suggest that targeting bile acids, or the underlying dysbiosis, could eventually improve outcomes for patients with metastatic HR+ cancer. "Because many of the patients who were on bile-acid sequestrants have health issues that tend to favor a worse prognosis for breast cancer, the survival of patients who were prescribed bile-acid sequestrants and who had metastatic breast cancer are extremely encouraging," Rutkowski said. "Now we want to know if these existing, FDA-approved drugs can prevent the cancer from spreading in the first place. If that proves true, this could support the use of a well-tolerated class of drugs for the prevention of metastatic breast cancer." Finding new ways to improve patient outcomes and battle cancer more effectively are the primary missions of UVA Comprehensive Cancer Center, one of only 57 cancer centers in the nation to earn the prestigious "comprehensive" designation from the National Cancer Institute. The designation is awarded only to elite cancer centers with the most outstanding cancer care and research programs in the country. The UVA Comprehensive Cancer Center's efforts are complemented by UVA's Paul and Diane Manning Institute of Biotechnology, which works to accelerate how quickly discoveries such as Rutkowski's can be turned into new drugs and treatments to benefit patients around the world. Findings published Rutkowski and her collaborators have published their findings in the scientific journal Cancer Research. The research team consisted of Audrey M. Putelo, Simona Bajgai, Mika K. Poblete, Gabrielle Guido, Mirna Perusina Lanfranca, Qingyi He, Tajbir Raihan, Cara N. Hatzinger, Akshita Mirani, Sree H. Kolli, Daniel S. Lank, Tzu-Yu Feng, Mitchell T. McGinty, Una Miagkov, Asal Pilehvari, Wen You, Thurl E. Harris and Rutkowski. The scientists have no financial interest in the work. The research was supported by the National Institutes of Health's National Cancer Institute, grant 1R01CA253285. Additional personnel support came from NCI grants 5R01CA262634 and P30CA044579, as well as American Cancer Society grant IRG 81-001-26.

AI Translation
弗吉尼亚大学综合癌症中心最新研究显示，不健康的肠道导致的胆汁酸积聚可促进乳腺癌向身体其他部位转移。这一发现有望开辟预防乳腺癌转移、改善患者预后并挽救生命的新途径。 弗吉尼亚大学的梅拉妮·鲁特科夫斯基博士及其同事的研究有助于解释肠道微生物组——生活在我们肠道内外的微生物群落——如何在激素受体阳性（HR+）乳腺癌的扩散中发挥作用。这项工作进一步加深了对微生物组在疾病发生发展和维持健康方面广泛影响的理解。 “我们发现，不健康的肠道微生物组失去了调节胆汁酸组成的能力。胆汁酸是强效的信号分子，若调节不当，可能对健康、新陈代谢和免疫系统造成严重破坏。这在全身范围内创造了一个有利于乳腺肿瘤转移的环境，”鲁特科夫斯基说。“我们的研究结果表明，我们可以通过补充能够改变胆汁酸组成的细菌，或者通过使用胆汁酸螯合剂治疗乳腺癌患者来减少乳腺癌转移，胆汁酸螯合剂是已经获得FDA批准用于治疗代谢疾病的药物。” 靶向HR+乳腺癌 HR+乳腺癌是迄今为止最常见的转移性乳腺癌类型，每年在美国女性中约有22.5万例诊断。HR+乳腺癌往往早期扩散，肿瘤常形成于淋巴结和肺部。一旦癌症扩散，治疗难度将大大增加，患者的死亡风险也会随之升高。 先前的研究已将肠道微生物组失衡——即肠道‘菌群失调’——与不良患者预后联系起来，但鲁特科夫斯基的研究明确了造成这一现象的具体机制。该研究发现，肠道菌群失调导致胆汁酸积聚，而胆汁酸在消化和新陈代谢中发挥着重要作用。他们进一步发现，胆汁酸积聚会引发有害的乳腺炎症，最终助长乳腺癌细胞向身体其他部位，特别是肺部的扩散。 该研究的合著者奥黛丽·普特洛博士说：“我们发现，当肠道微生物组紊乱时产生的某些胆汁酸，可以创造条件，使乳腺癌细胞更容易扩散。”“由于胆汁酸可以被测量和调节，这一发现为开发识别高危患者和降低转移性疾病发生率的新方法开辟了令人振奋的可能性。” 对人类患者的影响 尽管科学家们的研究是在实验小鼠身上进行的，但他们随后也观察了HR+乳腺癌患者。他们发现，胆汁酸升高和胰岛素抵抗与生存率降低相关。此外，接受胆汁酸抑制药物治疗的转移性疾病患者往往寿命更长。（这些药物，即胆汁酸螯合剂，已获得美国联邦食品和药物管理局的批准，但研究人员提醒，仍需要更多研究来确定这些药物是否直接导致了患者观察到的益处。） 鲁特科夫斯基的这项有前景的研究结果表明，靶向胆汁酸或潜在的菌群失调，最终有望改善转移性HR+癌症患者的预后。 鲁特科夫斯基说：“由于许多服用胆汁酸螯合剂的患者本身存在可能导致乳腺癌预后更差的健康问题，因此这些被处方胆汁酸螯合剂且患有转移性乳腺癌的患者的生存情况，是极其令人鼓舞的。”“现在我们想知道这些现有的、FDA批准的药物是否能从一开始就阻止癌症扩散。如果这一点得到证实，这将支持使用一类耐受性良好的药物来预防转移性乳腺癌。” 寻找改善患者预后和更有效抗癌的新方法，是弗吉尼亚大学综合癌症中心的首要任务。该中心是全国仅有的57家获得国家癌症研究所“综合”称号的癌症中心之一。这一称号只授予拥有全国最杰出癌症护理和研究项目的顶尖癌症中心。 弗吉尼亚大学综合癌症中心的努力得到了弗吉尼亚大学保罗和黛安·曼宁生物技术研究所的补充，该研究所致力于加速将鲁特科夫斯基等人的发现转化为造福全球患者的新药和新疗法。 研究成果发表 鲁特科夫斯基及其合作者已在科学期刊《癌症研究》上发表了他们的研究成果。研究团队成员包括奥黛丽·M·普特洛、西蒙娜·巴杰盖、米卡·K·波布莱特、加布里埃尔·吉多、米尔纳·佩鲁西纳·兰弗兰卡、何清怡、塔吉比尔·赖汉、卡拉·N·哈青格、阿克希塔·米拉尼、斯里·H·科利、丹尼尔·S·兰克、冯子玉、米切尔·T·麦金蒂、乌娜·米亚格科夫、阿萨尔·皮莱瓦里、尤文、瑟尔·E·哈里斯和鲁特科夫斯基。研究人员在这项工作中没有经济利益。 该研究获得了美国国立卫生研究院国家癌症研究所（资助号1R01CA253285）的支持。额外的人员支持来自NCI资助号5R01CA262634和P30CA044579，以及美国癌症协会资助号IRG 81-001-26。
