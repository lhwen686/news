人工智能模型在识别心房组织中心律失常相关异常方面达到89%的准确率

AI model achieves 89% accuracy in identifying arrhythmia-related abnormalities in atrial tissue

深度解析

这篇文章揭示了心房心肌病（ACM）无创表征技术的一项突破性进展。其核心技术原理在于利用图神经网络（GNNs）对体表电位图（BSPM）数据进行深度分析。心房心肌病是指心房组织出现的电学和结构异常，如纤维化，这些异常是导致最常见心律失常——心房颤动（AF）发生和进展的关键因素。传统上，识别这些组织变化需依赖侵入性心内电解剖标测或昂贵的磁共振成像。而这项新的人工智能模型，则通过分析简单的体表电极记录的心脏电信号，利用GNNs处理复杂且相互关联的时空电信号数据，以高精度定位并量化异常心房组织。这种非侵入性方法对行业具有变革性影响，有望使先进的心脏组织表征技术更易普及，减少对专业医疗中心的依赖。通过早期提供ACM的精确洞察，它有望显著改善AF的风险分层和治疗规划，例如优化消融手术策略。尽管目前仍处于概念验证阶段，主要基于模拟数据，但其对不同解剖结构和信号噪声的鲁棒性及高准确率，预示着巨大的临床应用潜力。未来的临床验证是其迈向实际应用的关键一步，有望为心律失常的管理带来更可及、高效且以患者为中心的范式转变。

DOI: 10.1007/s10791-026-10439-9 Researchers from the COR group at the Institute of Information and Communications Technologies (ITACA) of the Universitat Politècnica de València (UPV) have developed an artificial intelligence model capable of locating and quantifying tissue abnormalities associated with atrial cardiomyopathy using electrical recordings from the body's surface. The system, based on graph neural networks (Graph Neural Networks, GNNs), achieved 89% accuracy in locating affected areas and 84% accuracy in determining the extent of damage to atrial tissue. It maintained this capability when analyzing anatomical structures not used during its training. The next step: Validating it in patients The study is still a proof of concept developed using simulated data, so its results do not imply immediate clinical application. The next step will be to validate the model using records from real patients. As the study itself points out, "prospective validation using clinical records is required before this approach can be transferred to treatment planning applications." If the results are confirmed in clinical trials, this technology could contribute to new tools for characterizing atrial tissue without invasive procedures and provide further information for the study and treatment of conditions associated with atrial cardiomyopathy. The study, published in the journal Discover Computing, is led by María Macarulla-Rodríguez, a researcher at COR-ITACA. It also involves Jorge Sánchez, Andreu M. Climent and María S. Guillem, researchers at ITACA and members of Corify Care S.L.; Cristian Barrios Espinosa and Axel Loewe (Karlsruhe Institute of Technology); and Ernesto Zacur (Corify Care S.L.). "The main conclusion of the study is that the combination of body surface electrical maps, spatiotemporal analysis and graph neural networks shows great potential for the noninvasive characterization of atrial cardiomyopathy," said Macarulla-Rodríguez, lead author of the paper. A new approach to studying atrial tissue Atrial cardiomyopathy encompasses electrical and structural abnormalities in atrial tissue, such as fibrosis, that are linked to the onset and progression of atrial fibrillation, one of the most common cardiac arrhythmias. "Knowing where the altered tissue is located and how extensive it is can help us better understand the disease and, potentially, plan treatments such as ablation," says Guillem, director of ITACA and a participant in the study. Currently, techniques such as invasive intracardiac electroanatomical mapping or certain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans can be used to characterize these abnormalities. The new approach proposes using body surface potential maps (BSPM), obtained via electrodes placed across the torso to record the heart's electrical activity. "Artificial intelligence then analyzes how these signals are distributed in space and how they evolve over time to identify patterns related to the location and extent of the affected tissue," explains the director of ITACA. 89% accuracy in localization To develop and evaluate the model, the team worked with 14,400 simulated body surface potential maps, generated from different atrial and torso anatomies and with varying degrees and locations of atrial cardiomyopathy. "In the baseline configuration, with 128 electrodes, the model achieved 89% accuracy in locating the affected tissue, with mean values of 89% sensitivity and 90% specificity," Macarulla-Rodríguez said. One of the most significant findings is that the system maintained good performance when analyzing anatomies different from those used to train it, a crucial consideration for future applications in people with different anatomical characteristics. "The model also obtained an overall accuracy of 84% in determining the extent of the affected tissue. Furthermore, we found that its performance remains relatively stable when signal quality decreases, which suggests a certain robustness against noise," Macarulla-Rodríguez said. The results also show that the anatomical diversity of the training data is a key factor: Incorporating a wider variety of atrial and torso models improved the system's classification ability. More information María Macarulla-Rodríguez et al, A graph neural network framework for characterizing atrial cardiomyopathy from body surface potential maps, Discover Computing (2026).
