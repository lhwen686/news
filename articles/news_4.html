Researchers at NYU Abu Dhabi have developed soft, flexible sensors that help restore a surgeon's sense of touch during minimally invasive (keyhole) surgery. These procedures allow for faster recovery and less pain, but surgeons lose the ability to feel how much force they are applying through long instruments. This makes it harder to safely handle delicate tissue.

In this study, published in Microsystems & Nanoengineering, the team, led by NYU Abu Dhabi Associate Professor of Mechanical Engineering and Bioengineering Mohammad A. Qasaimeh, designed multichannel soft sensors that can measure a wide range of forces, from very light contact to stronger gripping, in real time. The sensors are made from soft silicone with tiny channels filled with liquid metal. When pressure is applied, the channels deform slightly, allowing the system to detect and measure force.

To demonstrate the technology, the researchers integrated the sensors into a laparoscopic grasper. One sensor was placed on the handle to measure the force applied by the surgeon, while another was placed on the tool's jaw to measure how it interacts with tissue.

"Minimally invasive surgery benefits patients, but it also removes a key human capability: the surgeon's sense of touch," said Mohammad A. Qasaimeh, Associate Professor of Mechanical Engineering and Bioengineering at NYU Abu Dhabi and senior author of the study. "In this work, we developed soft sensors that can capture a wide range of forces while remaining easy to integrate with existing tools, moving us closer to smarter and safer surgical instruments."

NYU Abu Dhabi researchers develop smart soft sensors to restore surgeons' sense of touch in minimally invasive procedures. Credit: NYUAD

"Our goal was to create sensors that are both sensitive and practical for real surgical environments," said Wael Othman, first author of the study and a former postdoctoral associate at NYU Abu Dhabi (now Assistant Professor at Khalifa University). "This design allows us to measure both gentle and strong forces within the same small device, and to place sensors where they are most useful on surgical tools."

The study also shows how sensor design, including size and material, can be adjusted for different uses. The sensors can be placed on the handle to keep the tool tip clear and easy to sterilize, or directly on the jaw when a precise measurement is needed.

Beyond surgery, this technology could be used in robotics, wearable devices, and other systems that require accurate force sensing.

纽约大学阿布扎比分校的研究人员开发出柔软、柔性传感器，有助于在微创（腔镜）手术中恢复外科医生的触觉感知。这些手术能加快康复并减轻疼痛，但外科医生会失去通过长器械感知所施加力度的能力。这使得安全处理脆弱组织变得更加困难。

这项研究发表在《微系统与纳米工程》期刊上，由纽约大学阿布扎比分校机械工程与生物工程副教授穆罕默德·A·卡萨伊梅（Mohammad A. Qasaimeh）领导的团队，设计了一种多通道软传感器，能够实时测量从极轻接触到强力抓握的广泛力值。这些传感器由软硅胶制成，内部填充有液态金属的微小通道。当施加压力时，通道会轻微变形，从而使系统能够检测并测量力。

为了演示这项技术，研究人员将传感器集成到腹腔镜抓钳中。一个传感器放置在手柄上以测量外科医生施加的力，而另一个传感器放置在工具的钳口上以测量其与组织的相互作用方式。

“微创手术造福患者，但它也剥夺了一项关键的人类能力：外科医生的触觉，”纽约大学阿布扎比分校机械工程与生物工程副教授兼该研究的资深作者穆罕默德·A·卡萨伊梅说。“在这项工作中，我们开发了能够捕捉广泛力值且易于与现有工具集成的软传感器，这使我们更接近于实现更智能、更安全的手术器械。”

“我们的目标是创造出既灵敏又实用于真实手术环境的传感器，”该研究的第一作者、纽约大学阿布扎比分校前博士后研究员（现哈利法大学助理教授）瓦埃勒·奥斯曼（Wael Othman）说。“这种设计使我们能够在同一个小型设备中测量轻柔和强劲的力，并将传感器放置在手术工具上最有效的位置。”

该研究还展示了传感器设计（包括尺寸和材料）如何针对不同用途进行调整。传感器可以放置在手柄上，以保持工具尖端清晰且易于消毒，或者在需要精确测量时，直接放置在钳口上。

除了手术领域，这项技术还可用于机器人技术、可穿戴设备以及其他需要精确力传感的系统。
