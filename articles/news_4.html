Each year, more than 6,000 Swedes are diagnosed with melanoma. Melanoma is a form of skin cancer that, if detected late, can be fatal. Not all melanomas behave in the same way. A new doctoral thesis from Karolinska Institutet investigates why some patients have poorer outcomes than others and whether certain markers in tumor tissue can help predict who is at risk.

We asked Sofi Vikström, a doctoral student at the Department of Oncology-Pathology, to tell us more about her thesis.

"In my thesis, I examined survival among people with cutaneous melanoma, both over time and compared with people without the disease. I used a measure that shows how many life years are lost because of the disease. I also investigated whether the level of a specific protein (SAMHD1) in the tumor is associated with how long patients survive," Vikström said.

What are the most important findings?

We found that survival improved during 2011–2020 compared with earlier years, 1990–2010. We also showed that people with stage II and III melanoma lose life years compared with the general population. In addition, we observed that patients whose tumors had high levels of the protein SAMHD1 were more likely to live longer than those with low or no levels, particularly when the melanoma had spread to the brain.

How can this knowledge help improve people's health?

The improved survival in recent years may be due to new treatments, but also to better early detection of the disease. By showing how many life years are actually lost, it can become clearer why preventive efforts and treatments are important.

In the future, the protein SAMHD1 could help doctors identify which patients are at greatest risk and therefore need closer follow-up or different treatments. Overall, the thesis provides a clearer picture of how the disease develops and what influences survival.

What are your future ambitions?

I want to continue working at the intersection of clinical pathology and translational research. In the short term, I want to learn more about how melanoma works, for example, how the immune system responds to tumors in relation to SAMHD1.

In the long term, I hope to contribute to developing new ways to predict disease progression and improve treatment, especially for patients with advanced melanoma that has spread to the brain.

我们请肿瘤病理学系的博士生索菲·维克斯特罗姆（Sofi Vikström）向我们详细介绍了她的论文。

维克斯特罗姆说：“在我的论文中，我研究了皮肤黑色素瘤患者的生存情况，包括随时间的变化以及与未患该病的人群相比。我使用了一种衡量方法来显示因疾病损失了多少生命年。我还调查了肿瘤中特定蛋白质（SAMHD1）的水平是否与患者的生存期相关。”

最重要的发现是什么？

我们发现，与早期（1990年至2010年）相比，2011年至2020年期间的生存率有所提高。我们还发现，II期和III期黑色素瘤患者与普通人群相比损失了生命年。此外，我们观察到，肿瘤中SAMHD1蛋白质水平较高的患者比水平较低或没有该蛋白的患者更有可能活得更久，特别是当黑色素瘤已扩散至脑部时。

这些知识如何帮助改善人们的健康？

近年来生存率的提高可能归因于新疗法，但也可能归因于疾病更好的早期发现。通过展示实际损失了多少生命年，可以更清楚地说明预防工作和治疗的重要性。

未来，SAMHD1蛋白质可以帮助医生识别哪些患者风险最高，从而需要更密切的随访或不同的治疗。总体而言，这篇论文为疾病发展方式以及影响生存的因素提供了更清晰的图景。

我希望继续在临床病理学和转化研究的交叉领域工作。短期内，我想更多地了解黑色素瘤的作用机制，例如，免疫系统如何响应肿瘤与SAMHD1的关系。

长期而言，我希望为开发预测疾病进展和改进治疗的新方法做出贡献，特别是针对已扩散至脑部的晚期黑色素瘤患者。
