深度解析

本研究揭示了气候变化对原住民儿童健康的复杂且多维度影响。从医学机制角度看，气候变化通过多种途径损害健康。例如，水文气候变异性（如干旱、洪水）和季节模式改变直接影响农业生产和食物获取，导致营养不良，进而削弱儿童免疫系统，增加对传染病（如腹泻病、呼吸道感染）的易感性。热应激则可引发脱水、热射病等急性健康问题，并加剧慢性病负担。更深层次的影响体现在心理社会和文化福祉上。原住民文化与土地、社区紧密相连，气候变化导致的生态破坏、资源流失甚至被迫迁徙，会切断这些基本联系，诱发儿童和家庭的压力、焦虑、抑郁、创伤和悲伤等精神健康问题，长远影响其发展轨迹和生活质量。围产期健康受损则可能涉及母体营养不良、环境毒素暴露增加及心理压力，进而影响胎儿发育和新生儿结局。 从行业影响来看，这项研究凸显了公共卫生、儿科医学与环境科学交叉研究的迫切性。它要求医疗卫生体系在制定干预策略时，必须采纳整体健康观，将原住民知识和文化实践纳入气候适应和健康促进的核心。这意味着政策制定需从自上而下的模式转向原住民主导、以儿童为中心的行动，强调社区韧性建设和代际知识传承。此外，研究呼吁全球加强对原住民儿童在气候健康研究中的代表性，确保数据和证据的全面性与地理多样性，从而推动更公平、有效的全球卫生政策和气候行动，以应对这一日益严峻的全球性挑战。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain Indigenous children across the globe are experiencing significant climate-related health impacts, ranging from malnutrition and infectious diseases to effects on mental health and cultural well-being and problems before birth, according to a new study. Researchers from The Kids Research Institute Australia, The University of Western Australia and Flinders University reviewed evidence from 53 studies worldwide and found that Indigenous children were already experiencing a broad range of climate-related health impacts across diverse environmental, cultural and geographic settings. Published in Globalization and Health, the study is the first to synthesize global evidence on how climate change is affecting Indigenous children's health and well-being. Lead author Jaime Turnbull from The Kids said the review highlighted climate change as both an environmental and child health issue, with Indigenous children facing unique and overlapping vulnerabilities. "Importantly, these impacts do not occur in isolation. Climate change affects Indigenous children's relationships with Country, culture, family and community, which are fundamental to their health and well-being," Turnbull said. The review identified six major climate-related exposure themes—heat stress, changing seasonal and oceanic patterns, hydroclimatic variability including floods and droughts, air pollution, climate-related disasters and other climate-related environmental risks. These exposures were linked to five main health outcomes—psychosocial and cultural well-being, malnutrition, infectious diseases, perinatal health, and morbidity and mortality. The strongest and most consistent finding was the effect of climate change on psychosocial and cultural well-being. Across studies, environmental changes disrupted connections to land, cultural identity, language, and traditional knowledges and cultural practices, while contributing to stress, anxiety, depression, trauma and grief. Co-author and Yamatji elder Aunty Dr. Mara West from The Kids said the findings reinforced the importance of understanding Indigenous health through a holistic lens. "For Indigenous communities, health is deeply connected to culture, Country, family and community. When climate change affects the environment, it can also affect the cultural practices and relationships that help keep children strong and healthy," West said. "The findings underscore why Indigenous knowledges and Indigenous leadership must be central to climate responses." Rather than viewing Indigenous children solely as vulnerable to climate change, the researchers say young people should be recognized as important contributors to solutions. The review found meaningful involvement of younger generations in Indigenous-led climate action can strengthen cultural identity, self-determination, intergenerational knowledge transfer and community resilience. "Better outcomes start with Indigenous-led, child-centered action," West said. "Indigenous children and young people have an important role to play in shaping climate responses. Supporting their participation helps strengthen resilience today and for future generations." Despite growing global concern about climate-related health impacts, co-authors UWA pediatrician professor Peter Le Souef and Flinders ecologist professor Corey Bradshaw said Indigenous children were underrepresented in climate-health research and almost half of the studies reviewed did not report findings specifically for Indigenous children. Existing evidence was concentrated in a few countries and communities. Co-author Dr. Melinda Judge from The Kids and UWA's Department of Mathematics and Statistics said addressing these gaps would require more geographically diverse research with meaningful involvement of Indigenous youth to support climate action that could strengthen cultural identity, self-determination, knowledge sharing and community resilience. Turnbull added that climate-health research and policy responses needed to reflect the realities, priorities and strengths of Indigenous children, families and communities. "Recognizing Indigenous peoples' sovereignty, knowledges and lived experiences, and ensuring Indigenous communities genuinely lead and shape solutions, are essential if we are to achieve equitable and effective outcomes for children and families." More information Jaime J. Turnbull et al, Global systematic review of how climate change degrades the wellbeing and health of Indigenous children, Globalization and Health (2026). 该文章已根据Science X的编辑流程和政策进行审阅。编辑在确保内容可信度的同时，强调了以下特点： 图片来源：Unsplash/CC0公共领域 一项新研究显示，全球各地的原住民儿童正在经历重大的气候相关健康影响，范围从营养不良和传染病，到对心理健康和文化福祉的影响，以及出生前的问题。 澳大利亚儿童研究所、西澳大利亚大学和弗林德斯大学的研究人员审查了来自全球53项研究的证据，发现原住民儿童已经在各种环境、文化和地理背景下经历了广泛的气候相关健康影响。 该研究发表在《全球化与健康》杂志上，是首次综合全球证据，阐明气候变化如何影响原住民儿童的健康与福祉。 来自The Kids机构的首席作者杰米·特恩布尔表示，此次审查强调气候变化既是一个环境问题，也是一个儿童健康问题，原住民儿童面临着独特且相互重叠的脆弱性。 特恩布尔说：“重要的是，这些影响并非孤立发生。气候变化影响着原住民儿童与国家（Country）、文化、家庭和社区的关系，而这些关系对他们的健康与福祉至关重要。” 审查确定了六个主要的气候相关暴露主题：热应激、季节和海洋模式变化、包括洪水和干旱在内的水文气候变异性、空气污染、气候相关灾害以及其他气候相关环境风险。 这些暴露与五个主要的健康结果相关联：心理社会和文化福祉、营养不良、传染病、围产期健康以及发病率和死亡率。 最强烈和最一致的发现是气候变化对心理社会和文化福祉的影响。各项研究表明，环境变化破坏了与土地、文化认同、语言以及传统知识和文化习俗的联系，同时导致压力、焦虑、抑郁、创伤和悲伤。 来自The Kids机构的合著者兼亚马吉长者玛拉·韦斯特博士（Aunty Dr. Mara West）表示，这些发现强化了通过整体视角理解原住民健康的重要性。 韦斯特说：“对于原住民社区来说，健康与文化、国家（Country）、家庭和社区紧密相连。当气候变化影响环境时，它也会影响有助于儿童保持强壮和健康的文化习俗和关系。” “这些发现强调了为什么原住民知识和原住民领导力必须成为气候应对措施的核心。” 研究人员表示，不应仅仅将原住民儿童视为气候变化的脆弱群体，而应将年轻人视为解决方案的重要贡献者。 审查发现，年轻一代有意义地参与原住民主导的气候行动可以增强文化认同、自决、代际知识传承和社区韧性。 韦斯特说：“更好的成果始于原住民主导、以儿童为中心的行动。原住民儿童和年轻人可以在塑造气候应对措施方面发挥重要作用。支持他们的参与有助于增强当前和未来世代的韧性。” 尽管全球对气候相关健康影响的担忧日益增加，但合著者西澳大利亚大学儿科教授彼得·勒苏埃夫和弗林德斯大学生态学家科里·布拉德肖教授表示，原住民儿童在气候健康研究中的代表性不足，近一半的受审查研究没有专门针对原住民儿童报告发现。现有证据集中在少数国家和社区。 来自The Kids机构和西澳大利亚大学数学与统计系的合著者梅琳达·贾奇博士表示，解决这些差距需要开展更多地理上多样化的研究，并让原住民青年有意义地参与，以支持能够增强文化认同、自决、知识共享和社区韧性的气候行动。 特恩布尔补充说，气候健康研究和政策应对措施需要反映原住民儿童、家庭和社区的现实、优先事项和优势。 “承认原住民的主权、知识和生活经验，并确保原住民社区真正主导和塑造解决方案，对于我们为儿童和家庭实现公平有效的成果至关重要。” 更多信息：Jaime J.
