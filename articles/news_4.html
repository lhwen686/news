iRBD患者对心跳的脑部反应发生改变

Brain responses to heartbeat are altered in patients with iRBD

深度解析

这篇医学新闻深入探讨了孤立性快动眼睡眠行为障碍（iRBD）与大脑对心跳的反应（即心跳诱发电位，HEP）之间的关联，揭示了HEP作为神经退行性疾病早期生物标志物的巨大潜力。iRBD是一种典型的突触核蛋白病（如帕金森病和路易体痴呆）的临床前兆，其特征是患者在快动眼睡眠期间将梦境付诸行动。本研究的核心机制在于考察大脑如何处理内脏感觉信息，特别是心脏跳动所产生的生理信号，并将其转化为可测量的电生理活动（HEP）。研究发现，iRBD患者在清醒状态下表现出异常的HEP，且这些异常随症状持续时间而增强，这表明患者的内感受性加工能力可能受损。这一发现对医学领域具有深远影响。当前，帕金森病等神经退行性疾病往往在脑部损伤显著后才被诊断。如果HEP能被确认为可靠的早期生物标志物，将有助于在疾病早期阶段识别高风险个体，从而争取更早的干预窗口。早期诊断不仅能为患者提供更及时的治疗选择，延缓疾病进展，还有望加速针对神经保护性疗法的药物研发和临床试验。此外，HEP作为一种非侵入性、客观的电生理指标，具有良好的临床可行性和可重复性，有望成为疾病筛查、诊断辅助及疗效评估的重要工具，推动精准医疗的发展。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: EPFL Isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD), a condition characterized by strong physical reactions to vivid, often violent dreams, is one of the earliest warning signs of brain changes seen in Parkinson's disease or dementia with Lewy bodies, sometimes appearing years before diagnosis. In a new study led by Fosco Bernasconi and published in npj Parkinson's Disease, researchers explored brain responses to the heartbeat, known as heartbeat-evoked potential (HEP), and how they differ between patients with iRBD and healthy individuals. Using overnight recordings of brain activity and heartbeat in 13 patients with iRBD and 23 healthy individuals, they showed that patients with iRBD exhibit altered HEP while awake, and these alterations tend to be more pronounced the longer an individual has had RBD symptoms. In both patients and healthy individuals, the team also found that HEP is weaker during sleep than during wakefulness and that heart rate itself changes across different sleep stages. Taken together, these findings show that the brain's responses to the heart change in iRBD, and these changes grow stronger with symptom duration, underscoring the potential of HEP as a biomarker for the disorder. Publication details Fosco Bernasconi et al, Altered heart-brain coupling in awake patients with isolated REM sleep behaviour disorder, npj Parkinson's Disease (2026).
