A fast-rising strep bacterium has become increasingly notorious for causing serious infections in humans, including complications that can lead to muscle damage and patient death. New Houston Methodist research published in The American Journal of Pathology sheds light on how Streptococcus dysgalactiae subspecies equisimilis (SDSE) causes disease and may provide insights to aid vaccine development.

To better understand how SDSE causes disease compared with its better-known close relative, Strep A, Houston Methodist scientists, led by James Musser, MD, Ph.D., chair of the Department of Pathology and Genomic Medicine and director of the Center for Infectious Diseases at Houston Methodist, used a genomic screening method known as transposon-directed insertion-site sequencing (TraDIS) to test gene function in SDSE. The study examined two closely related SDSE strains, which helped detect subtle strain-specific differences that influence infection behavior.

"We identified several genes that were essential for bacterial survival in both laboratory and infection conditions," Musser said. "The study also identified various genes in each strain that influenced the bacteria's ability to grow and persist during muscle infection."

Unexpectedly, several genes traditionally associated with Strep A infection were found to reduce survival and growth in SDSE when active, suggesting fundamental differences in how the two closely related bacteria cause disease.

"By defining which genes are truly required for growth and survival of the bacteria during infection, this work provides a foundation for understanding how SDSE causes severe disease and provides important information that may help in creating a SDSE vaccine," Musser said.

The researchers said that as SDSE continues to be a threat, the findings provide a critical resource for future studies aimed at developing novel strategies to prevent and treat invasive SDSE infections.

一种快速崛起的链球菌已因其在人类中引起严重感染而日益臭名昭著，包括可能导致肌肉损伤和患者死亡的并发症。休斯顿卫理公会医院发表在《美国病理学杂志》上的新研究揭示了马型链球菌兽疫亚种（Streptococcus dysgalactiae subspecies equisimilis，简称SDSE）致病的机制，并可能为疫苗开发提供启示。

为了更好地理解SDSE与其更广为人知的近亲A族链球菌相比，是如何致病的，休斯顿卫理公会医院的科学家们在病理学和基因组医学系主任兼感染病中心主任詹姆斯·马瑟（James Musser）医学博士、哲学博士的带领下，使用了一种名为转座子定向插入位点测序（transposon-directed insertion-site sequencing，简称TraDIS）的基因组筛选方法来测试SDSE的基因功能。该研究考察了两个密切相关的SDSE菌株，这有助于检测影响感染行为的微妙菌株特异性差异。

马瑟表示：“我们确定了几个对细菌在实验室和感染条件下生存至关重要的基因。研究还识别出每个菌株中影响细菌在肌肉感染期间生长和存活的各种基因。”

出乎意料的是，一些传统上与A族链球菌感染相关的基因在SDSE中活跃时，反而会降低其生存和生长能力，这表明这两种密切相关的细菌在致病方式上存在根本性差异。

马瑟说：“通过明确在感染过程中细菌生长和生存真正需要的基因，这项工作为理解SDSE如何引起严重疾病奠定了基础，并提供了可能有助于开发SDSE疫苗的重要信息。”

研究人员表示，鉴于SDSE持续构成威胁，这些发现为未来旨在开发预防和治疗侵袭性SDSE感染新策略的研究提供了关键资源。
