深度解析

这篇文章通过结合单细胞RNA测序和空间转录组学技术，深入剖析了乳腺肿瘤内的复杂微环境。核心概念是“休眠或静止癌细胞”（quiescent cells），它们与快速增殖细胞不同，对多数靶向分裂细胞的化疗药物具有天然耐受性，是肿瘤复发和转移的关键驱动因素。研究首次详细绘制了这些静止细胞在肿瘤内的空间分布，发现它们常被CXCL10阳性巨噬细胞和肌成纤维细胞样肿瘤相关成纤维细胞等免疫及支持细胞形成的“保护性微环境”所环绕，形成所谓的“封闭群落”。 这种“内置”的耐药机制对肿瘤治疗策略具有重大指导意义。传统的单一疗法往往忽视了静止细胞，导致治疗后复发。未来癌症治疗必须从多维度考量，设计能够同时靶向增殖细胞和静止细胞的联合疗法。这包括开发能穿透或破坏支持细胞屏障的药物，以及针对静止微环境中特定通路（如补体通路）的干预措施。深入理解肿瘤细胞与微环境的协同进化，将推动肿瘤学研究进入精准靶向治疗的新阶段，旨在实现更持久的肿瘤控制并有效预防癌症复发。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain Researchers at the MRC Laboratory of Medical Sciences (LMS), Imperial College London and UCL Genetics Institute have created a detailed map of cells within breast tumors and identified distinct neighborhoods of proliferating and dormant cells. The study, published in Genome Medicine, reveals that protective immune and connective cells surround the hibernating cancer cells, potentially shielding them from treatments. It suggests future therapies may need to target not only different cancer cell states but also the local environments that help them survive, to both halt tumor growth and prevent cancer from coming back later. The landscape of a tumor is diverse. Nestled among the frantically dividing cells are immune cells, newly formed blood vessels and a class of suspiciously quiet cancer cells that can have devastating effects. These dormant, or 'quiescent,' cells can evade treatment and subsequently lead to cancer spread or resurgence. Researchers at the LMS, Imperial and UCL are hunting these cells, asking where they sit within untreated tumors, what characterizes them and what surrounds them. The team has used publicly available data to create a map of breast cancer tumors and found distinct pockets of quiescent cells, surrounded by cells that may act as a protective guard. Silent but deadly "Quiescent cancer cells are very dangerous," explains Dr. Alexis Barr, co-lead author and head of the Cell Cycle Control group at the LMS. "These cells can hide from chemotherapy and then remain in this dormant quiescent state in the tumor, and then later reactivate to drive proliferation." Some cancer cells take up this state, essentially pausing their progress, in response to the stress of tumor life, where nutrients and blood flow can take time to keep up with rapid tumor expansion. Like a bear hibernating through harsh conditions, they wait until more favorable conditions return, potentially after any treatment is finished. Barr adds, "If we want to achieve long-term control of people's tumors and prevent tumor relapse, we have to focus on these dormant quiescent cancer cells, and have to understand more about them." To do this, Barr worked with Dr. Maria Secrier's computational biology team at UCL to establish a detailed map of the tumor and its surrounding immune and support cells. They combined single-cell RNA sequencing, which showed which genes individual cells were using, with spatial transcriptomics, which then mapped the cells and their neighbors within the tumor. "We found cells that resemble therapy-resistant cells already residing in the tumor before we give any treatment," says Secrier, which suggests that features linked to treatment resistance can be built into the tumor rather than only developing in response to treatment. This was the case both in aggressive tumor types and slower-developing classes—a surprising finding because quiescence had previously been associated with slower-growing disease. Gated communities The team's tumor analysis mapped not just cancer cells but also the wide variety of support cells that are drawn into tumor growth. They found a repeating pattern: Dormant cells were commonly found near CXCL10-positive macrophages (a type of immune cell) and myofibroblastic cancer-associated fibroblasts (a type of tumor-supporting cell). These cells appear to have been co-opted to protect the cancer cells, possibly forming a barrier to keep cancer-killing immune cells or cancer treatments from disturbing the sleepy neighborhood. "The cancer cells are really encapsulated within these areas of macrophages and fibroblasts that we think act as shields for these dormant cancer cells," said Secrier. "But we don't yet know the direction of cause and effect: whether the surrounding cells push cancer cells into dormancy or if the cancer cells attract or alter their surroundings. It's very likely coming from both sides." Treating every part of a tumor Many chemotherapy drugs are most effective against rapidly dividing cells. But as any teacher knows, you can't ignore the quiet ones. This study suggests proliferating and dormant regions may have different drug sensitivities. The analysis found increased activity of the complement pathway (a part of the immune system) within dormant niches, which may make these regions vulnerable to treatments targeting this pathway. The surrounding support cells may provide another potential target, although their role in maintaining dormancy still needs to be tested. "Different parts of the tumor will likely respond to different drugs," said Secrier. "If we understand what drug combinations we can use to target both the proliferative and the dormant areas, potentially that could be more successful than current therapies. This is giving us a first insight into how we can then intervene with different therapeutics that specifically target different areas of the tumor where the cells have adapted and have evolved differently." Barr said, "It is clearly important to focus on proliferative cancer cells, but we also need to understand this population of quiescent dormant cancer cells. And that's been less studied." The predictions of this research will now need to be tested experimentally, but identifying built-in treatment-resistant regions in tumors and deciphering their support systems may ultimately offer a way to design more effective treatment combinations. Putting quiescent cells on the map will lead toward better cancer treatments that last. Medicine）上的研究揭示，保护性免疫细胞和结缔细胞围绕着“冬眠”状态的癌细胞，可能使其免受治疗影响。这表明未来的疗法可能不仅需要针对不同的癌细胞状态，还需要针对帮助它们存活的局部微环境，以同时阻止肿瘤生长并防止癌症后期复发。 肿瘤的微环境是多样化的。在疯狂分裂的细胞中，夹杂着免疫细胞、新形成的血管以及一类“可疑地”安静的癌细胞，它们可能产生毁灭性的影响。这些休眠的或“静止的”细胞可以逃避治疗，并随后导致癌症扩散或复发。LMS、帝国理工学院和伦敦大学学院的研究人员正在寻找这些细胞，探究它们在未经治疗的肿瘤中位于何处、有哪些特征以及被什么细胞包围。该团队利用公开数据绘制了乳腺癌肿瘤的图谱，发现了独特的静止细胞“口袋”，这些细胞被可能充当保护性“卫士”的细胞所包围。 沉默却致命 “静止癌细胞非常危险，”该研究的共同主要作者、LMS细胞周期控制组负责人Alexis Barr博士解释说。“这些细胞可以躲避化疗，然后以这种休眠静止状态停留在肿瘤中，之后再重新激活以驱动增殖。” 一些癌细胞在肿瘤生存压力下进入这种状态，本质上暂停了它们的进程，因为营养和血流可能难以跟上肿瘤的快速扩张。它们就像熊在恶劣条件下冬眠一样，等待更有利的环境恢复，可能是在任何治疗结束后。Barr补充说：“如果我们要实现对患者肿瘤的长期控制并防止肿瘤复发，我们就必须关注这些休眠静止癌细胞，并且必须对它们有更多的了解。” 为此，Barr与伦敦大学学院Maria Secrier博士的计算生物学团队合作，建立了肿瘤及其周围免疫细胞和支持细胞的详细图谱。他们结合了单细胞RNA测序技术（显示单个细胞正在使用哪些基因）和空间转录组学技术（随后绘制细胞及其在肿瘤内的邻近关系）。 Secrier表示：“我们发现，在给予任何治疗之前，肿瘤中就已经存在类似耐药细胞的细胞。”这表明与治疗耐药性相关的特征可能已经内置于肿瘤中，而不仅仅是作为对治疗的反应而发展出来。无论是侵袭性肿瘤类型还是生长缓慢的类型，都存在这种情况——这是一个令人惊讶的发现，因为静止状态此前一直与生长缓慢的疾病相关。 封闭群落 该团队的肿瘤分析不仅绘制了癌细胞的图谱，还绘制了被吸引到肿瘤生长中的各种支持细胞的图谱。他们发现了一个重复的模式：休眠细胞通常发现于CXCL10阳性巨噬细胞（一种免疫细胞）和肌成纤维细胞样肿瘤相关成纤维细胞（一种肿瘤支持细胞）附近。这些细胞似乎已被肿瘤“征用”来保护癌细胞，可能形成一道屏障，阻止杀伤癌细胞的免疫细胞或癌症治疗干扰这些“沉睡”的区域。 Secrier说：“癌细胞确实被包裹在这些巨噬细胞和成纤维细胞区域内，我们认为它们是休眠癌细胞的‘盾牌’。但我们尚不清楚因果关系的方向：是周围细胞促使癌细胞进入休眠状态，还是癌细胞吸引或改变了周围环境。很可能两者兼而有之。” 治疗肿瘤的每一个部分 许多化疗药物对快速分裂的细胞最有效。但正如任何老师都知道，你不能忽视那些“安静的”学生。这项研究表明，增殖区域和休眠区域可能具有不同的药物敏感性。分析发现，在休眠微环境中补体通路（免疫系统的一部分）的活性增加，这可能使这些区域易受靶向该通路的治疗影响。周围的支持细胞可能提供另一个潜在靶点，尽管它们在维持休眠状态中的作用仍需验证。 Secrier说：“肿瘤的不同部分可能对不同的药物产生反应。如果我们能了解可以使用哪些药物组合来同时靶向增殖区域和休眠区域，这可能比目前的疗法更成功。这为我们提供了初步的见解，说明我们如何能够用不同的疗法进行干预，专门针对肿瘤中细胞已经适应和以不同方式进化的不同区域。” Barr表示：“关注增殖性癌细胞显然很重要，但我们也需要了解这群静止休眠的癌细胞。而这方面的研究较少。” 这项研究的预测现在需要通过实验进行验证，但识别肿瘤中内置的耐药区域并破译其支持系统，最终可能为设计更有效的治疗组合提供途径。将静止细胞纳入研究视野，将有助于开发更持久的癌症治疗方法。 发表详情：Cenk Celik 等人，《乳腺癌的空间生态学揭示增殖和休眠微环境的协同进化》，《基因组医学》（2026年）。DOI: 10.1186/s13073-026-01711-0 期刊信息：《基因组医学》
