Same duty cycle, very different outcomes: What tough materials taught us about histotripsy

深度解析

这篇研究深入探讨了聚焦超声组织碎裂术（histotripsy）治疗的复杂性，特别是针对纤维化和坚韧组织时，脉冲参数对治疗效果的关键影响。核心专业概念在于揭示，即使总超声能量（占空比）相同，通过调整脉冲持续时间（pulse duration）和脉冲重复频率（pulse repetition frequency），仍能显著改变空化气泡的动力学行为及其对组织的机械破坏模式。文章强调，较长脉冲配合较低重复频率，能通过“重复机械载荷”机制更有效地诱导纤维组织的连续液化，同时给予空化气泡再成核的时间，避免重复作用于同一气泡群，从而促进更均匀的治疗效果。相比之下，短脉冲、高重复频率可能仅造成局部损伤而非广泛液化。这种理解通过多模态成像技术得以验证，包括B模式超声、相位差显微镜和开放式光片显微镜，它们提供了从宏观到微观、从动态到三维的损伤评估。对业界而言，这项发现具有深远影响，它提示临床实践中，尤其是处理良性前列腺增生（BPH）等含有纤维肌性成分的异质组织时，需要超越简单的能量考量，转向更精细的“脉冲时序与组织力学及气泡群体相互作用”的治疗设计，以期提高治疗的可预测性和疗效，为更广泛的组织类型提供精准有效的组织碎裂术治疗方案。

Different imaging methods captured different aspects of the same treatment process, from cavitation activity and treatment-scale ultrasound changes to microscopic structural damage. Credit: Yashwanth Nanda Kumar et al One of the questions that stayed with me throughout my Ph.D. was surprisingly simple: Why are some tissues so much harder to break down with histotripsy than others? Histotripsy is a focused ultrasound technique that uses cavitation bubbles to mechanically break down tissue. Unlike thermal ablation, the goal is not to heat the tissue. Instead, the rapid activity of these bubbles creates mechanical stresses that progressively disrupt tissue structure. This process can be very effective in many soft tissues. Fibrous tissues, however, can behave quite differently. This was especially interesting to me because my doctoral research was motivated in part by benign prostatic hyperplasia, or BPH. BPH tissue can contain substantial amounts of smooth muscle and collagen, and these fibromuscular components can make the tissue mechanically tough and more resistant to histotripsy. To study that problem in a controlled way, we developed fibrous tissue-mimicking materials that reproduced important mechanical characteristics of prostate tissue and were intentionally difficult to break down. Once we had a material that resisted treatment, another question became increasingly important to me: If we deliver the same overall amount of ultrasound exposure, does it matter how that exposure is distributed in time? Same duty cycle, different treatment A common way to summarize pulsed ultrasound exposure is duty cycle, the fraction of time that the ultrasound is actually on. Two treatments can have the same duty cycle while being constructed very differently. One might use many extremely short pulses delivered rapidly, while another might use much longer pulses with more time between them. In our recent study published in Scientific Reports, we systematically varied pulse duration and pulse repetition frequency while maintaining the same 1% duty cycle across treatment conditions. If duty cycle alone were the dominant factor, we might expect the resulting treatments to look similar. They did not. Equal duty cycle produced very different treatment outcomes. Longer-pulse, lower-repetition-frequency exposures produced much more contiguous liquefaction of the tough fibrous material. Among the conditions we tested, 1,000-cycle pulses delivered at 10 Hz produced the highest volumetric liquefaction rates. By contrast, short-pulse, high-repetition-frequency exposures could generate substantial damage without producing a large, fully liquefied volume. One way I think about the longer-pulse result is repeated mechanical loading. Fibrous materials can stretch, rearrange and dissipate energy before they fail. During a longer histotripsy pulse, cavitation may repeatedly load the material, and that repeated loading may progressively damage structures that initially resist disruption. The time between pulses also appeared to matter. At lower pulse repetition frequencies, residual bubbles have more time to dissolve before the next exposure. This may allow cavitation to re-nucleate in new locations instead of repeatedly interacting with the same residual bubble population, helping the treatment progress more uniformly through the material. Open-top light-sheet microscopy revealed sparse, elongated and tubular regions of structural damage under short-pulse, high-repetition-frequency conditions, providing a three-dimensional view of incomplete histotripsy damage. Credit: Yashwanth Nanda Kumar et al What does incomplete damage actually look like? One of my favorite parts of this study came from asking a different question: What does incomplete histotripsy damage actually look like in three dimensions? We were already using B-mode ultrasound and phase-contrast microscopy to evaluate the treated regions. Through a collaboration with Prof. Jonathan Liu's team, including Qinghua Han, we also had an opportunity to explore open-top light-sheet microscopy. That part of the project was somewhat serendipitous. Prof. Liu's group uses open-top light-sheet microscopy to image prostate specimens, and I was simply curious whether the same approach could be used to visualize our transparent fibrous phantoms. It turned out to be extremely informative. Under some of the short-pulse, high-repetition-frequency conditions, the three-dimensional images revealed sparse, elongated and sometimes tubular regions of structural damage. These regions did not necessarily appear as a clearly measurable liquefied core on conventional B-mode ultrasound. That distinction mattered because our different imaging methods were not measuring the same physical endpoint. High-speed imaging showed us the cavitation behavior, B-mode ultrasound showed changes at the treatment scale, phase-contrast microscopy showed structural damage in thin sections, and light-sheet microscopy gave us a three-dimensional view of how that damage was distributed throughout the material. Looking at the lesion from several independent perspectives helped us understand something important: visible cavitation activity, structural damage and complete liquefaction are related, but they are not interchangeable measurements. Why this matters For me, the broader lesson from this work is that how we deliver an ultrasound exposure can matter just as much as how much exposure we deliver. Duty cycle is useful, but it does not fully describe the mechanical history experienced by a tough material. Two pulse sequences with the same duty cycle can create very different cavitation dynamics and very different patterns of damage and liquefaction. That becomes increasingly important as histotripsy is explored in mechanically tougher and more fibrous tissues. In those applications, treatment design may need to account not only for acoustic amplitude or total exposure, but also for how pulse timing interacts with tissue mechanics and with the bubble population left behind from one pulse to the next. There is also a longer-term clinical motivation behind this question. Our study was performed in a controlled tissue-mimicking material, so it does not tell us directly how a particular pulse sequence will perform in patients. But conditions such as BPH, where the target tissue can be fibromuscular and mechanically heterogeneous, were part of the motivation for studying this problem in the first place. If we can better understand why some tissues resist mechanical breakdown, and how pulse timing changes that response, it may eventually help us design histotripsy treatments that are more predictable and effective across a broader range of tissue types. The next step is to understand how these principles translate from controlled fibrous models to real heterogeneous tissues, where collagen, smooth muscle, blood vessels and other structures all influence the treatment response. Sometimes two treatments that look equivalent on paper are not equivalent at all. This story is part of Science X Dialog, where researchers can report findings from their published research articles. 在我攻读博士学位期间，一个令我困惑的问题出奇地简单：为什么有些组织用组织碎裂术分解起来比其他组织困难得多？组织碎裂术是一种聚焦超声技术，利用空化气泡机械性地分解组织。与热消融不同，其目标不是加热组织。相反，这些气泡的快速活动产生机械应力，逐渐破坏组织结构。 这个过程在许多软组织中非常有效。然而，纤维组织表现可能截然不同。这对我来说尤其有趣，因为我的博士研究部分是受良性前列腺增生（BPH）的启发。BPH 组织可能含有大量的平滑肌和胶原，这些纤维肌性成分会使组织在机械上变得坚韧，对组织碎裂术更具抵抗性。 为了以受控的方式研究这个问题，我们开发了模拟纤维组织的材料，这些材料再现了前列腺组织的重要机械特性，并且被有意地设计成难以分解。一旦我们有了能够抵抗治疗的材料，另一个问题对我来说变得越来越重要：如果我们施加的总超声暴露量相同，那么这种暴露在时间上的分布方式是否重要？ 相同占空比，不同治疗效果 总结脉冲超声暴露的一种常用方法是占空比，即超声实际开启的时间比例。两种治疗可以具有相同的占空比，但其构成方式可能截然不同。一种可能使用许多极短的脉冲快速递送，而另一种可能使用更长的脉冲，脉冲之间有更多的时间间隔。 在我们最近发表在《科学报告》上的研究中，我们系统地改变了脉冲持续时间和脉冲重复频率，同时在所有治疗条件下保持相同的 1% 占空比。如果占空比是唯一的决定性因素，我们可能会预期由此产生的治疗效果会相似。然而，结果并非如此。相同的占空比产生了截然不同的治疗结果。 长脉冲、低重复频率的暴露对坚韧的纤维材料产生了更连续的液化效果。在我们测试的条件下，以 10 赫兹频率递送 1,000 周期的脉冲产生了最高的体积液化率。相比之下，短脉冲、高重复频率的暴露可以产生显著损伤，但未能形成大范围的完全液化区域。 我对于长脉冲结果的一种思考方式是重复的机械载荷。纤维材料在失效之前可以伸展、重排和耗散能量。在较长的组织碎裂术脉冲期间，空化作用可能会反复对材料施加载荷，而这种重复载荷可能会逐渐损伤最初抵抗破坏的结构。 脉冲之间的时间似乎也至关重要。在较低的脉冲重复频率下，残余气泡有更多时间在下一次暴露前溶解。这可能使得空化作用在新的位置重新成核，而不是反复与相同的残余气泡群相互作用，从而帮助治疗更均匀地穿透材料。 开放式光片显微镜揭示了在短脉冲、高重复频率条件下，结构损伤呈现稀疏、细长和管状区域，提供了不完全组织碎裂术损伤的三维视图。图片来源：Yashwanth Nanda Kumar et al
