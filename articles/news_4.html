深度解析

这篇研究综述揭示了术前睡眠管理在围手术期照护中的关键作用。文章核心概念围绕两种干预药物：褪黑素和思诺思（佐匹坦）。褪黑素作为一种内源性神经激素，主要由松果体分泌，参与调节昼夜节律和睡眠，其药理作用包括镇静、抗焦虑、抗炎。在术前应用，它能直接缓解患者的紧张情绪，降低疼痛感知，甚至减少术后对阿片类镇痛药的依赖，尤其对老年患者具有认知保护优势。思诺思则是一种非苯二氮卓类GABA受体激动剂，通过选择性作用于GABA-A受体，增强神经递质GABA的抑制作用，从而促进睡眠，更侧重于纠正已有的失眠或严重睡眠障碍。 这些发现对医疗行业具有深远影响。传统上，围手术期管理重点在于手术室内的操作，但此研究强调了术前准备——特别是睡眠优化——作为一个可干预的关键窗口。通过改善患者术前睡眠质量，不仅能减轻术后疼痛、降低阿片类药物用量及相关副作用，还能促进更快更好的康复。这预示着未来围手术期管理将更加注重全流程、个性化和以患者为中心，通过早期干预改善患者预后，降低医疗成本，并提升整体医疗服务质量。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain Improving sleep before surgery through the use of melatonin or Ambien (zolpidem) may ease presurgical anxiety, reduce pain after surgery, decrease opioid requirements and help patients recover better, according to an analysis of 25 years of research presented at the ANESTHESIOLOGY 2026 annual meeting. Poor sleep and anxiety are common before surgery and can contribute to heightened pain sensitivity, increased opioid use and slower recovery. In the studies, patients typically took melatonin the night before surgery and/or 20 to 90 minutes before anesthesia. Ambien was taken at bedtime, either the night before surgery or on multiple nights beginning up to two days before surgery. The findings suggest that addressing sleep may be a modifiable part of preparing for surgery that helps support recovery. "While we usually focus on what happens in the operating room, our review suggests we may be overlooking a critical window for improving outcomes in the nights before surgery," said Sebastian Amaya, M.D., senior author of the study and an anesthesiology resident at Yale School of Medicine in New Haven, Connecticut. "Our paper is unique because it focused exclusively on pre-surgery-only use of melatonin or Ambien, showing that something as simple as improving sleep before a procedure can help patients have a more comfortable recovery while reducing the need for heavy painkillers and their potential risks." The researchers analyzed 16 studies of patients undergoing abdominal, gynecologic, orthopedic, urologic, dental and other surgical procedures published between 2000 and 2025, which included a total of more than 1,100 patients. Melatonin doses ranged from 1 to 20 mg, while Ambien was consistently dosed at 10 mg. Across the studies, melatonin, a naturally occurring hormone that helps regulate sleep, was associated with less anxiety, pain and inflammation, as well as more than a 30% reduction in opioid use in some abdominal surgery patients. Studies using wrist-worn activity monitors also suggested melatonin helped patients return to more normal patterns of sleep and daytime activity during recovery. The review also suggested that melatonin may offer advantages over traditional sedatives for older adults who may be more vulnerable to confusion after surgery. Ambien, a commonly prescribed sleep medication, helped patients with preexisting sleep problems sleep better before surgery and was linked to less pain after. The idea is that by helping patients get more normal, restorative sleep before the physical stress of surgery, it may indirectly improve how they experience pain and recover afterward. Patients undergoing colorectal surgery needed less opioid pain medication during and in the first 24 hours after the procedure and also had lower levels of certain inflammatory markers. Patients undergoing hip replacement reported less anxiety and depression and greater satisfaction with their recovery, researchers said. "Melatonin may be particularly valuable for patients who experience significant anxiety before surgery, those at higher risk for respiratory complications and older adults who may be vulnerable to cognitive side effects from traditional sedatives," said Amaya. "Ambien may be better suited for patients with preexisting insomnia or significant sleep disruption."
