深度解析

本文揭示了澳大利亚Z世代在初级医疗服务方面面临的严峻挑战，核心在于传统医疗模式在费用、便利性和全科医生可及性上的不足。作为解决方案，文章提出了由受过培训的药剂师开具处方药物的模式。这一专业概念在医学机制上，旨在通过扩大药剂师的执业范围，使其能够依据严谨的临床通用准则，针对非复杂性、日常健康状况（如轻度感染、常见疼痛等）进行初步诊断和药物处方。药剂师作为最易接触的医疗专业人员，其专业知识和遍布社区的药房网络，为患者提供了即时、便捷的就医途径，从而弥补了全科医生预约困难和候诊时间长的缺口。此举对行业影响深远：对于患者而言，这意味着能更及时地获得医疗服务，有效避免小病拖延成大病，并可能降低就医成本。对于药房和药剂师，这不仅提升了他们的专业地位和职能广度，也可能拓展其服务收入来源。从宏观医疗体系来看，药剂师处方权能有效分流部分初级医疗需求，减轻全科医生和急诊部门的负担，优化医疗资源的配置效率，推动更扁平化、响应更快的基层医疗服务模式。然而，这要求建立完善的药剂师培训认证体系和清晰的执业界限，以确保医疗质量和患者安全。

Credit: The Pharmacy Guild of Australia Gen Z Australians are increasingly delaying health care, missing work and study, or sometimes going without care because of cost, inconvenience and difficulty accessing a general practitioner (GP). Nearly one in three have taken time off work, study or caregiving responsibilities to see a GP, while one in five delayed care because of cost in the past 12 months. Faced with these barriers, Gen Z Australians overwhelmingly support trained pharmacists prescribing medications (82%) and trust pharmacists to provide treatment and management for everyday conditions (89%). The YouGov survey, commissioned by the Pharmacy Guild of Australia, found Gen Z Australians (1997–2009) are among those most affected by difficulties accessing primary health care. The vice president of the Pharmacy Guild of Australia, Matthew Gillespie, said the results show the traditional health care model does not fit modern life. "When young Australians are delaying treatment, missing work and study, or avoiding care altogether because they can't access it easily, that's a sign the system needs to evolve," Gillespie said. "Community pharmacies are supporting this evolution, with pharmacists returning to university so that they can safely and effectively treat and manage a range of everyday health conditions." The research showed younger Australians are struggling to access care through traditional models: 15% said they have skipped care altogether because they could not afford it, and 16% said their condition has worsened while waiting for an appointment in the past 12 months. For many young Australians, accessing health care is becoming more difficult, with more than one in four unable to get a GP appointment when they needed one. More than one in four also reported paying more than expected for an appointment. The research showed Gen Z Australians want health care that fits around their lives. Nearly two in five (39%) said more convenient access to care was a key benefit of pharmacist prescribing, while one in five (20%) valued spending less time away from work. Gillespie said pharmacist prescribing offered younger Australians another pathway into care. "Young Australians don't want endless waiting rooms and weeks-long delays for simple treatments for noncomplex conditions. "They've heard plenty of reasons why health care shouldn't change. But the status quo is leaving too many young Australians waiting longer, paying more and missing out on care altogether. "Australia's more than 6,000 community pharmacists are ready to be part of the solution." Provided by The Pharmacy Guild of Australia Who's behind this story?
