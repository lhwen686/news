Research reveals unseen changes in motor control after spinal cord injury

One of the study participants exerts force with their calf muscles while sensors measure electrical activity. Credit: Ruoli Wang/KTH Royal Institute of Technology

Even when people with incomplete spinal cord injuries can walk, everyday functions like standing, balancing or producing steady force may remain difficult. A new study shows why.

Using surface skin electrical sensors, a research team in Sweden identified previously unseen changes in motor coordination that result from incomplete spinal cord injuries (SCI). The study is the first to examine how individual motor units—that is, nerve-to-muscle connections that create movement—work together in people with SCI.

"Our study reveals, at the cellular level, how the central nervous system adapts to the injury to control movement," says Ruoli Wang, associate professor in biomechanics at Promobilia MoveAbility lab, KTH Royal Institute of Technology. She says the researchers' approach was completely noninvasive.

The results were published in the Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation.

The study's lead author, Ph.D. student Zhihao Duan, says the researchers found the nervous system struggles to spread signals smoothly across muscles at low levels of exertion after the injury. And it appears to overcompensate at higher levels of exertion, sending "louder," less refined signals.

A single muscle moves through hundreds of motor units, each turning on and off precisely to create a smooth force. Composed of a single motor neuron and its connecting muscle fibers, these motor units respond to shared signals from the nervous system, much like different sections of musicians led by an orchestra conductor. That shared input is what allows them to act in coordinated patterns.

To explore how well these units coordinate under the control of the central nervous system, the team examined 25 people (including 10 control participants). They used high-density electromyography (HD-EMG) to measure electrical activity in the functionally similar calf muscles—soleus and gastrocnemius—while volunteers pushed lightly or moderately against a force measurement device.

Duan says that at 20% effort, fewer of the motor units in the two calf muscles were working in a shared, coordinated way compared with people without injury. Their movements as a result were shaky and unstable. "They were much less being driven by the same coordinated signal from the nervous system," he says.

At a higher level of effort—50%—the SCI group showed stronger low-frequency synchronization between the two muscles. The body loses flexibility and precision in control of the movement. "This could be a sign of the nervous system compensating by sending louder, less refined signals," Duan says.

"One interesting finding is that after spinal cord injury the nervous system becomes more rigid and less able to change its approach as the muscles work harder. A healthy nervous system, on the other hand, is able to adapt its strategy as force demands, to adjust the shared neural drive level," Wang says.

Although the study was limited by a small sample size and challenges in identifying enough motor units per muscle from the skin surface, Wang says the results offer unique insight into how SCI reshapes motor control.

"This finding may open the door to a new rehabilitation biomarker, helping clinicians and researchers design new neurorehabilitation strategies to re-tune the spinal cord control and to restore coordinated neural input," she says.

一名研究参与者正在用他们的小腿肌肉发力，同时传感器测量电活动。图片来源：Ruoli Wang/KTH皇家理工学院

即使是不完全性脊髓损伤（SCI）患者能够行走，站立、平衡或产生稳定的力量等日常功能可能仍然困难。一项新研究揭示了其原因。

瑞典的一个研究团队利用皮肤表面电传感器，发现了不完全性脊髓损伤（SCI）导致的运动协调方面此前未曾观察到的变化。这项研究首次探讨了独立的运动单位——即产生运动的神经-肌肉连接——在SCI患者中如何协同工作。

“我们的研究在细胞层面揭示了中枢神经系统如何适应损伤来控制运动，”KTH皇家理工学院Promobilia MoveAbility实验室的生物力学副教授Ruoli Wang说。她表示，研究人员的方法是完全无创的。

研究结果发表在《神经工程与康复杂志》上。

该研究的第一作者、博士生Zhihao Duan表示，研究人员发现，损伤后，神经系统在低强度用力时难以在肌肉间平滑地传播信号。而在高强度用力时，它似乎会过度代偿，发送“更响亮”、不那么精细的信号。

单块肌肉通过数百个运动单位运动，每个运动单位精确地开启和关闭以产生平滑的力量。这些运动单位由单个运动神经元及其连接的肌纤维组成，它们响应来自神经系统的共享信号，就像由管弦乐队指挥引导的不同乐器组。正是这种共享输入使它们能够以协调的模式行动。

为了探究这些运动单位在中枢神经系统控制下的协调程度，研究团队检查了25人（包括10名对照参与者）。他们使用高密度肌电图（HD-EMG）来测量功能相似的小腿肌肉——比目鱼肌和腓肠肌的电活动，同时志愿者们轻轻地或适度地对抗测力设备。

Duan表示，在20%的用力程度下，与未受伤的人相比，两块小腿肌肉中以共享、协调方式工作的运动单位数量更少。因此，他们的运动表现出颤抖和不稳定。“他们较少受到来自神经系统的相同协调信号驱动，”他说。

在更高的努力程度（50%）下，SCI组在两块肌肉之间表现出更强的低频同步。身体失去了对运动控制的灵活性和精确性。“这可能是神经系统通过发送更响亮、不那么精细的信号进行代偿的迹象，”Duan说。

“一个有趣的发现是，脊髓损伤后，神经系统变得更加僵硬，随着肌肉用力程度的增加，改变其方法的能力减弱。而健康的神经系统则能够根据力量需求调整其策略，以调整共享神经驱动水平，”Wang说。

尽管这项研究受限于样本量小以及从皮肤表面识别每块肌肉足够多的运动单位存在挑战，Wang表示，研究结果为SCI如何重塑运动控制提供了独特的见解。

“这一发现可能为新的康复生物标志物打开大门，帮助临床医生和研究人员设计新的神经康复策略，以重新调整脊髓控制并恢复协调的神经输入，”她说。
