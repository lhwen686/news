How human movement predicts health measures across multiple body systems

深度解析

Newton Tech的研究揭示，人工智能步态基础模型通过分析三维骨骼运动，能够识别出编码在细微人类运动模式中的广泛健康信息。其核心技术原理在于，一个先进的AI基础模型能够超越传统步态测量的简单指标（如步速和步幅），深度解析复杂、高维的运动数据，从中发现与18个身体系统相关的多系统健康表型。这意味着运动本身不再仅仅是评估特定肢体功能的工具，而成为一个整合了心血管、代谢、肝脏、精神等多种生理状态的“多系统生物标志物”。 这一突破对医疗行业影响深远。在药物研发领域，它能提供比传统功能评估更灵敏、更客观的临床试验终点，助力制药公司更精准地评估治疗效果、疾病进展，甚至区分真实功能恢复与代偿策略，从而加速新药上市。在临床诊断和筛查方面，这种无创、无标记、基于视频的运动评估技术，可实现大规模、低成本的疾病早期筛查与健康监测，例如有效识别肝纤维化、抑郁症和糖尿病等风险，补充甚至部分替代昂贵的传统诊断工具。其易于部署的特性，有望将高级运动分析从专业实验室推向日常临床护理和社区筛查，极大提升医疗可及性与效率。

DOI: 10.1038/s41591-026-04724-9 Newton Tech, a developer of AI-powered movement intelligence technology for clinical research, has published a new study it co-authored in Nature Medicine showing that artificial intelligence can reveal extensive health information encoded in subtle patterns of human movement. In 3,328 adults, the model identified significant associations with 1,887 of 3,210 health measures spanning 18 body systems, from blood tests and liver imaging to sleep, mental health and clinical diagnoses. The paper is titled "A Gait Foundation Model Predicts Multi-System Health Phenotypes from 3D Skeletal Motion." For pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, the findings have immediate relevance to clinical development. Traditional functional assessments often reduce movement to a single number, such as the time required to walk a set distance. Newton Tech is applying AI-powered movement analysis in clinical trials to measure dozens of dimensions of how a patient moves, from joint-level coordination and balance strategy to mechanical effort and compensation, giving sponsors a more sensitive view of treatment response and disease progression than conventional assessments alone may provide. Using Newton Tech's camera-based Movement Lab technology, researchers collected three-dimensional movement data during five standardized motor assessments. The research team analyzed the data using an AI foundation model capable of identifying complex patterns in movement that conventional gait measurements, such as walking speed and stride length, often miss. Body Tracking skeleton model. Credit: Nature Medicine (2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41591-026-04724-9 The study, whose lead author is Adam Gabet, was conducted through the Human Phenotype Project, a large-scale research cohort. The study identified associations with a wide range of health measures, including cardiovascular and metabolic health, bone density, liver function, sleep, mental health and physical frailty. Importantly, movement data provided additional predictive information beyond what age, body mass index, visceral fat and height already explain. "Movement contains far more biological information than the handful of numbers conventional tests record," said Yuval Brodsky, CEO of Newton Tech and co-author of the study. "This paper shows the scale of that signal. Newton Tech has spent years building the datasets, domain-specific models and clinical infrastructure required to translate movement into practical, objective measures for drug development. Our goal is to give biopharma companies a scalable way to see treatment effects, disease progression and patient-to-patient differences that blunt endpoints can miss." The findings suggest that human movement could play a much broader role in understanding health and disease than previously recognized. "This study is an important step toward establishing movement as a multisystem biomarker," said Paolo Bonato, Ph.D., director of the Motion Analysis Laboratory at Spaulding Rehabilitation Hospital and associate professor of physical medicine and rehabilitation at Harvard Medical School. "Clinical gait analysis has long focused on a handful of measures tied to specific conditions. These findings show that the way a person moves carries information about health across nearly every body system, and that this information can be captured at scale, as this study did across more than 3,000 people." Revealing a new dimension of human health Newton Tech's Movement Lab captured and processed the movement data, and Newton Tech team members are co-authors of the study. Researchers analyzed 17,589 movement sequences from study participants. The models identified statistically significant associations with 1,887 of the 3,210 health measures evaluated. After accounting for age, body mass index, visceral fat and height, the models improved predictions across all 18 body systems evaluated in men and 17 of 18 in women. Among the most striking findings, researchers discovered that movement analysis could reveal information about aspects of health not traditionally associated with walking or conventional gait analysis, including: Liver health: Movement patterns predicted characteristics of liver function, including liver elasticity, the primary noninvasive measure of liver fibrosis. Adding movement data improved prediction of liver elasticity beyond age and body mass index by as much as, or more than, adding the full panel of standard liver blood tests, in both men and women. Liver health leaves a detectable imprint on how people move. Mental health: Movement data revealed associations with depression and other mental health measures, improving predictions of reported depression beyond established demographic and body composition factors. In a screening analysis, adding movement data increased the number of depression cases identified by 81% in men. The models also predicted use of antidepressants, a drug class known to affect movement. Metabolic health: Movement data predicted visceral adipose tissue, the fat surrounding internal organs that is associated with metabolic risk, with a correlation of 0.83, substantially outperforming conventional gait measurements. In the same screening analysis, adding movement data increased the number of diabetes cases identified by 83% in men. The models are also explainable, showing which parts of the body carry which health information. Lower-body movement was particularly informative for metabolic health and frailty, while torso movement contributed to predictions related to sleep and lifestyle. For drug developers, that explainability helps connect a change in how a patient moves to the physiological systems involved. Beyond drug development, these insights can help clinicians better understand patients' functional limitations, inform treatment decisions and track changes in physical function over time. Spaulding Rehabilitation Hospital in Boston, part of Mass General Brigham, is bringing Newton Tech's Movement Lab into its Motion Analysis Laboratory under a multiyear agreement. Spaulding's Motion Analysis Laboratory is the primary clinical gait laboratory for Boston Children's Hospital's cerebral palsy team, where instrumented gait analysis guides surgical planning. Newton Tech's system has already been installed and operated there, producing reports in Spaulding's clinical format. The program spans pediatric cerebral palsy, stroke, traumatic brain injury and other conditions. "Bringing this depth of analysis into routine clinical care requires systems that are fast and practical for busy clinics and trial sites," Spaulding's Bonato said. "Newton Tech's markerless approach captures detailed whole-body movement in minutes, without markers or a complex laboratory setup, which is why we are bringing it into our laboratory. It takes clinical-grade movement analysis beyond a handful of specialist gait labs and into everyday clinical care and clinical trials." Bringing advanced movement analysis into clinical trials today Clinical trials frequently rely on timed function tests, functional rating scales and other standardized assessments to evaluate whether patients are improving. These measures compress a rich, whole-body movement into a single number, typically a time or a distance, and discard most of the information it contains. They can fail to capture subtle changes in movement quality or distinguish true functional recovery from compensatory strategies. For drug developers, more detailed movement analysis can help answer questions that conventional functional endpoints may not fully address: whether patients are experiencing meaningful functional recovery or compensating for impairments, how individual patients differ in their movement patterns, whether observed changes are consistent with a therapy's intended mechanism of action, and whether a treatment causes movement-related side effects. In the Nature Medicine study, the models predicted which medications participants were taking, including antidepressants, proton pump inhibitors and antihistamines, beyond what age and body composition explain. The need is especially acute in heterogeneous diseases and areas of high unmet need, including Duchenne and other muscular dystrophies, ALS, Parkinson's and Alzheimer's diseases, stroke and traumatic brain injury, where diagnosis, progression and treatment response can be difficult to characterize and established functional endpoints may be variable or insufficiently sensitive. Disease-specific validation will be required, but a richer, objective movement phenotype could help reveal drug effects and progression that are otherwise difficult to separate from natural variability or compensatory behavior. Newton Tech's MovementOS platform transforms video recordings of standard movement assessments into hundreds of objective biomechanical markers per patient, including gait symmetry, stability, coordination, joint movement patterns, variability, smoothness and mechanical effort. These insights support patient stratification, exploratory and secondary endpoints, responder identification and treatment-response analyses at the level of the individual patient. By combining sophisticated AI analysis with accessible video-based technology, Newton Tech helps drug developers keep the standard functional assessments they have relied on for decades and extract far deeper insights from them, measuring hundreds of aspects of movement where these tests have traditionally recorded a single time. Unlike traditional gait analysis, which often requires an expensive, specialized laboratory, reflective markers and trained technical staff, Newton Tech's platform is designed for deployment at clinical trial sites using standardized, markerless video capture. It can be installed with minimal site training, and onsite processing converts recordings into non-visually identifiable skeletal-motion data for Newton's analysis. The platform can be used prospectively or applied retrospectively to existing clinical trial videos—including recordings made for rater quality control—helping sponsors extract substantially more information from functional assessments already embedded in their programs. Newton Tech's platform is already used in pharmaceutical clinical trials. In May 2026, NervGen Pharma reported an exploratory, post hoc gait analysis from its Phase Ib/IIa CONNECT SCI study of NVG-291 in chronic spinal cord injury, built on Newton Tech's blinded markerless video analysis of the study's walking-test recordings. In that analysis of 20 participants, a global statistical test combining measures of coordination, mechanical effort and postural stability favored NVG-291 over placebo (p=0.0197). Ten of 10 NVG-291 participants were classified as responders, compared with 1 of 10 on placebo (p=0.0001). The analysis identified treatment-associated movement patterns that were not captured by walking speed alone. This work was conducted separately from the Nature Medicine research and illustrates how detailed movement analysis can provide important additional information in clinical development. Opening new possibilities for disease screening and diagnostics Beyond clinical trials, the ability to extract information related to multiple physiological systems from a simple, noninvasive movement assessment that can be performed in almost any setting raises the possibility of using gait analysis to complement traditional diagnostic tools, help identify individuals who may benefit from further evaluation and monitor changes in health over time. In the study, adding movement data substantially increased the number of cases identified for conditions including depression, diabetes and peptic ulcer disease. Unlike blood draws, imaging, wearable sensors or other specialized diagnostic procedures, markerless movement assessment can be completed quickly with affordable equipment and minimal site training. Onsite processing converts video into non-visually identifiable skeletal-motion data before analysis, supporting repeatable deployment across trial sites, clinics and community settings. Screening and diagnostic applications remain investigational. Additional research, including prospective clinical studies and validation in more diverse populations, will be necessary to determine how effectively movement-derived biomarkers can support disease detection, diagnosis and monitoring. The same multisystem sensitivity may also be relevant to longevity research, where interventions are intended to affect several physiological systems at once and no single measure can establish that aging has been slowed. Longitudinal movement biomarkers could become one objective component of a broader evidence package for evaluating whether a drug, lifestyle program or other intervention is producing meaningful change across systems; that application also remains investigational and will require prospective validation. 除了临床试验，从一个可以在几乎任何环境下进行的简单、无创运动评估中提取与多个生理系统相关的信息的能力，提出了将步态分析作为传统诊断工具补充的可能性，有助于识别可能受益于进一步评估的个体，并监测健康随时间的变化。 在这项研究中，添加运动数据显著增加了抑郁症、糖尿病和消化性溃疡病等疾病的病例识别数量。 与抽血、影像学检查、可穿戴传感器或其他专业诊断程序不同，无标记运动评估可以在短时间内完成，只需经济实惠的设备和最少的现场培训。现场处理在分析前将视频转换为非视觉可识别的骨骼运动数据，支持在试验点、诊所和社区环境中可重复部署。 筛查和诊断应用仍处于研究阶段。未来的研究，包括前瞻性临床研究以及在更多样化人群中的验证，将是确定运动衍生生物标志物如何有效支持疾病检测、诊断和监测所必需的。 同样的多系统敏感性可能也与长寿研究相关，在长寿研究中，干预措施旨在同时影响多个生理系统，且没有单一指标可以确定衰老已被减缓。 纵向运动生物标志物可能成为更广泛证据包中的一个客观组成部分，用于评估药物、生活方式计划或其他干预措施是否在多个系统产生有意义的变化；该应用也仍处于研究阶段，需要前瞻性验证。 出版详情：Adam Gabet
