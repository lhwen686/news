英文原文

Original
A bacterial protein fragment called corisin drives the process of pulmonary fibrosis by infiltrating and disrupting the cell's quality-control system, researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and Mie University in Japan found in a new study. Meanwhile, blocking corisin with an antibody impeded the damage. "One of the most important principles in medicine is that understanding how a disease develops is the key to discovering better treatments. Our findings move corisin from being simply an intriguing bacterial peptide associated with fibrosis to a mechanistically defined, biologically active and causally supported driver of disease - and importantly, a promising therapeutic target," said Illinois animal sciences professor Isaac Cann, who led the study with Mie University immunology professor Dr. Esteban Gabazza. Cann and Gabazza are affiliates of the Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology at Illinois. Tissue fibrosis is a process by which healthy tissue is progressively damaged or killed and replaced by scar tissue. It can happen in the lungs, kidneys, liver, heart, skin and other organs, and fibrosis contributes to nearly half of all deaths in developed countries, Gabazza said. In 2020, Cann and Gabazza's team identified corisin, a peptide fragmented from a protein produced by bacteria that are a natural part of the human gut microbiome, as having the ability to kill lung cells and advance pulmonary fibrosis. Yet the questions remained of how it reaches the lungs and whether it directly drives fibrosis or merely exacerbates it. Though further study found that corisin can travel from the gut through the bloodstream, Cann and Gabazza decided to investigate whether corisin also could be produced in the lungs by bacteria residing in the respiratory tract. The team analyzed samples of fluid from the lungs of patients with pulmonary fibrosis, collected at a hospital in Japan as part of their routine clinical care, as well as some collected during acute episodes of worsening disease, and compared them to control samples from healthy volunteers. They found corisin-related bacterial DNA in the fluid, the first evidence that the peptide-producing bacteria were present in the lungs. Next, the researchers removed corisin from the patient fluid samples using an antibody they developed. The corisin-free fluid lost its lethal effects when applied to healthy cell cultures. This demonstrated that the corisin present in patients is biologically active and capable of directly damaging lung cells. Specifically neutralizing corisin with the antibody largely protected the cells from injury." Isaac Cann, animal sciences professor, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign The researchers then set out to understand how corisin damages lung cells. They found that once the peptide infiltrates the cells lining the lungs, it accumulates in the mitochondria, an organelle responsible for producing energy and for quality control of the proteins made by the cell's machinery. Corisin disrupts this quality-control system, triggering oxidative stress, premature cellular aging, cell death and scarring. To further cement that fibrotic effects can come from corisin produced locally in the lungs, rather than peptides that had traveled from the gut through the bloodstream, the researchers engineered mice to continuously produce corisin in their lungs. The mice spontaneously developed pulmonary fibrosis, with no outside bacterial source of the peptide. "Our experiments also revealed that corisin is remarkably potent. Corisin produced naturally exerts its harmful effects at extremely low levels. This tells us that even tiny amounts of native corisin are sufficient to trigger significant biological effects, making it directly relevant to human disease rather than simply an experimental observation," said Gabazza, who is also U. of I. adjunct professor of animal sciences. However, when treated with the researchers' corisin-binding antibody, the mice showed significantly reduced pulmonary fibrosis, decreased disease severity and improved survival. The researchers published their findings in the journal Nature Communications. "Together, these results provide compelling proof of concept that neutralizing corisin could represent an entirely new therapeutic strategy for pulmonary fibrosis and, potentially, fibrotic diseases affecting other organs," Cann said. The researchers plan to continue refining treatment options related to their antibody, for which they have a patent pending, to block corisin before it enters cells. They also plan to explore therapies designed to restore protein quality or protect mitochondria as alternate pathways to interrupt the disease process. "Although much more work is needed before these discoveries can be translated into clinical treatments, we believe they provide a strong scientific foundation for an entirely new therapeutic strategy," Cann said. This study was supported by the Japan Science and Technology Agency, the Japan Agency for Medical Research and Development, the Japan Society for the Promotion of Science, the Takeda Science Foundation, the Japan Association for Diabetes Education and Care, the Eli Lilly Japan Innovation Research Grant, the Daiwa Security Foundation and the Charles and Margaret Levin Family Foundation. Cann is also a professor of microbiology and nutritional sciences and a member of the Center for East Asian and Pacific Studies at Illinois.

AI Translation
一种名为corisin的细菌蛋白片段通过渗入并破坏细胞的质量控制系统，驱动了肺纤维化的进程，这是伊利诺伊大学厄巴纳-香槟分校和日本三重大学的研究人员在一项新研究中发现的。同时，用抗体阻断corisin可以阻止这种损伤。 伊利诺伊大学动物科学教授艾萨克·坎恩（Isaac Cann）与三重大学免疫学教授埃斯特班·加巴扎（Esteban Gabazza）博士共同领导了这项研究。坎恩表示：“医学最重要的原则之一是，了解疾病如何发展是发现更佳治疗方法的关键。我们的发现使corisin从一个仅仅与纤维化相关的有趣细菌肽，转变为一个机制明确、具有生物活性并有因果关系支持的疾病驱动因素——更重要的是，它是一个有前景的治疗靶点。”坎恩和加巴扎是伊利诺伊大学卡尔·R·沃伊斯基因组生物学研究所的成员。 加巴扎表示，组织纤维化是一个健康组织逐渐受损或死亡，并被瘢痕组织替代的过程。它可能发生在肺部、肾脏、肝脏、心脏、皮肤和其他器官，在发达国家，纤维化导致了近一半的死亡。 2020年，坎恩和加巴扎的团队发现corisin——一种从人类肠道微生物组中自然存在的细菌所产生的蛋白质中分离出的肽——具有杀死肺细胞并促进肺纤维化的能力。然而，它如何到达肺部以及是直接驱动纤维化还是仅仅加剧纤维化的问题仍然存在。尽管进一步的研究发现corisin可以通过血液从肠道传播，但坎恩和加巴扎决定调查corisin是否也能由呼吸道中的细菌在肺部产生。 研究团队分析了在日本一家医院作为常规临床护理一部分收集的肺纤维化患者的肺液样本，以及一些在疾病急性恶化期间收集的样本，并将它们与健康志愿者的对照样本进行了比较。他们发现肺液中存在与corisin相关的细菌DNA，这是肽产生细菌存在于肺部的首个证据。 随后，研究人员使用他们开发的抗体从患者的肺液样本中去除了corisin。当将不含corisin的肺液应用于健康细胞培养物时，其致死作用消失了。 “这表明患者体内存在的corisin具有生物活性，并能够直接损伤肺细胞。用抗体特异性中和corisin在很大程度上保护了细胞免受损伤。”伊利诺伊大学厄巴纳-香槟分校动物科学教授艾萨克·坎恩说。 研究人员随后着手了解corisin如何损伤肺细胞。他们发现，一旦这种肽渗透到肺内壁细胞中，它就会在线粒体中积累。线粒体是一种负责产生能量和细胞机器所制造蛋白质的质量控制的细胞器。Corisin会破坏这个质量控制系统，从而引发氧化应激、细胞过早老化、细胞死亡和瘢痕形成。 为了进一步证实纤维化效应可以来源于肺部局部产生的corisin，而非通过血液从肠道传播的肽，研究人员基因改造了小鼠，使其肺部持续产生corisin。这些小鼠自发地发展出肺纤维化，且没有外部细菌肽来源。 加巴扎表示：“我们的实验还揭示了corisin具有惊人的效力。自然产生的corisin在极低水平下就能发挥其有害作用。这告诉我们，即使微量的内源性corisin也足以触发显著的生物学效应，使其与人类疾病直接相关，而不仅仅是一个实验观察。”加巴扎也是伊利诺伊大学动物科学的兼职教授。 然而，当用研究人员开发的corisin结合抗体治疗时，小鼠的肺纤维化显著减轻，疾病严重程度降低，存活率提高。 研究人员在《自然通讯》杂志上发表了他们的研究结果。 坎恩说：“综上所述，这些结果提供了令人信服的概念验证，即中和corisin可能代表了一种全新的肺纤维化治疗策略，并可能适用于影响其他器官的纤维化疾病。” 研究人员计划继续完善与他们正在申请专利的抗体相关的治疗方案，以在corisin进入细胞之前将其阻断。他们还计划探索旨在恢复蛋白质质量或保护线粒体的疗法，作为中断疾病过程的替代途径。 坎恩表示：“尽管在这些发现转化为临床治疗之前还需要进行大量工作，但我们相信它们为一种全新的治疗策略提供了坚实的科学基础。” 这项研究得到了日本科学技术振兴机构、日本医疗研究开发机构、日本学术振兴会、武田科学基金会、日本糖尿病教育与护理协会、礼来日本创新研究基金、大和证券基金会以及查尔斯与玛格丽特·莱文家族基金会的资助。坎恩还是伊利诺伊大学微生物学和营养科学教授，以及东亚和太平洋研究中心的成员。
