再利用药物进入后期临床试验，成本最多可降低90%

Repurposed drugs move through late-stage trials at up to 90% lower cost

这种模式的行业影响深远。首先，它挑战了传统制药企业以专利和商业回报为驱动的研发范式，提供了一种更注重公共卫生效益的路径。其次，通过专注于仿制药的再利用，它大幅降低了药物成本，提升了全球患者对创新疗法的可及性。最后，它鼓励学术界和医疗机构发挥其临床专业知识和研究能力，填补了制药企业因商业利益考量而可能忽视的治疗领域（如罕见病或非盈利性适应症），对全球医疗健康公平性具有重要意义。

Universities and hospitals are repurposing existing drugs through late-stage trials with funded costs up to 90% lower than those in the pharmaceutical industry. This "hidden" research system, which operates outside the patent system, has huge potential to regularly provide society with affordable treatments.

Examples include using a cancer drug to treat a leading cause of blindness, changing a drug from treating breast cancer to preventing it, and using an old anti-inflammatory drug to treat COVID.

The new research led by King's College London and published in the Cambridge Law Journal explores the parallel drug innovation system operating outside pharmaceutical companies.

The research shows that hospitals and universities are conducting substantial numbers of late-stage clinical trials funded at less than 10% of pharmaceutical companies' reported costs.

This is partly because universities and hospitals are able to carry out these trials with fewer resources than industry.

In drug research and development, there are three main barriers to innovation: expertise, risk and capital. These barriers are high for organizations that develop new drugs. However, the barriers are lower when repurposing generic drugs.

The expertise required when repurposing drugs is lower because the drug is well studied and already manufactured, investigators select projects based on their expertise, and the trials are approved by grant and ethics bodies. Risk is lower because no organization's financial viability depends on the authorization and sales of the drug.

The research also explores how clinicians and scientists have different motivations for repurposing generic drugs than the incentives the patent system offers. These include helping patients recover more quickly and publishing studies to advance their careers.

Dr. Johnathon Liddicoat, reader in law at King's College London, said, "This alternative research system offers enormous potential to help patients at a fraction of the cost, and governments around the world are starting programs to formally recognize the research in the system. The drugs are already cheap because they are generic, and the programs will ensure patients have access."

The process of finding new uses for already authorized drugs is commonly known as "repurposing." Early in a drug's life cycle, companies are interested in repurposing, and recent data indicates that, on average, they obtain regulatory authorizations for 32 new uses annually.

These approvals permit the drugs to be marketed for those new uses in addition to the original use. However, once generic drugs enter the market, companies lose interest in repurposing because of competition. Yet it is also at this moment that the new system flourishes, with hospitals and universities using cheap, generic versions of the drugs in their research.

大学和医院正在通过后期临床试验，将现有药物进行重新用途开发，其资助成本比制药行业低90%之多。这种在专利系统之外运作的“隐性”研究体系，在定期为社会提供可负担治疗方案方面，拥有巨大潜力。

例如，将一种抗癌药物用于治疗一种主要的致盲原因；将一种治疗乳腺癌的药物转变为预防乳腺癌；以及使用一种旧的抗炎药物来治疗新冠肺炎。

这项由伦敦国王学院主导并在《剑桥法律期刊》上发表的新研究，探讨了制药公司之外并行运作的药物创新体系。

研究表明，医院和大学正在进行大量后期临床试验，其资助成本不到制药公司报告成本的10%。

这部分是因为大学和医院能够以比行业更少的资源进行这些试验。

在药物研发中，创新面临三个主要障碍：专业知识、风险和资金。对于开发新药的机构来说，这些障碍很高。然而，当重新利用仿制药时，这些障碍则较低。

重新利用药物所需的专业知识较低，因为药物已得到充分研究并已投入生产，研究人员根据自身专业知识选择项目，且试验由资助和伦理机构批准。风险较低，是因为没有机构的财务生存能力依赖于该药物的授权和销售。

该研究还探讨了临床医生和科学家在重新利用仿制药方面，与专利系统提供的激励机制有何不同动机。这些动机包括帮助患者更快康复以及发表研究以促进职业发展。

伦敦国王学院法律讲师约翰逊·利迪科特博士表示：“这种替代性研究体系以极小的成本为患者提供帮助，具有巨大潜力，世界各国政府正在启动项目，以正式认可该体系中的研究。这些药物由于是仿制药，本身就很便宜，而这些项目将确保患者能够获得这些药物。”

为已获批准药物寻找新用途的过程通常被称为“药物再利用”。在药物生命周期的早期，企业对药物再利用感兴趣，最新数据显示，它们平均每年可获得32个新用途的监管授权。

这些批准允许药物除了原始用途外，还能针对这些新用途进行销售。然而，一旦仿制药进入市场，由于竞争，企业便失去了进行药物再利用的兴趣。然而，也正是在此时，新的体系蓬勃发展起来，医院和大学在研究中使用廉价的仿制药版本。
