深度解析

这篇医学新闻揭示了微生物代谢产物10-HSA在HIV感染相关肠道损伤修复中的潜在价值。核心专业概念在于阐明10-HSA的作用机制：它由植物乳杆菌产生，能直接结合并激活细胞核受体PPAR-alpha。PPAR-alpha的激活进而促进组蛋白巴豆酰化这一表观遗传修饰，重编程基因表达，从而修复受损的线粒体功能、增强能量代谢，并支持肠道上皮屏障的重建。在HIV感染中，病毒会严重破坏肠道免疫和上皮细胞，导致慢性炎症和“肠漏”。传统ART虽能抑制病毒复制，但对肠道损伤的修复有限。10-HSA的作用机制通过恢复线粒体呼吸和减少活性氧，直接针对HIV导致的细胞功能障碍，提升肠道屏障完整性。这项研究的行业影响深远，它不仅为HIV治疗提供了超越病毒抑制的新策略，即通过修复宿主肠道生态系统来增强抗病毒效果并促进免疫恢复，也提示10-HSA可能作为一种生物治疗剂，应用于其他慢性胃肠道炎症性疾病，开辟了“宿主-微生物-病毒”相互作用视角下的疾病干预新途径。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Rapid repair of gut epithelial barrier structure and function (shown in yellow) driven by microbial metabolite 10-HSA. Credit: UC Davis Scientists at the UC Davis School of Medicine have identified a naturally occurring metabolite that repairs gut damage caused by HIV infection and significantly improves the effectiveness of antiretroviral therapy (ART) in a nonhuman primate model of HIV/AIDS. The biotherapeutic metabolite, 10-hydroxystearic acid (10-HSA), is produced by the beneficial gut bacterium Lactiplantibacillus plantarum. L. plantarum is a common lactic acid bacterium found in many over-the-counter probiotics and fermented foods. It also occurs naturally in the human gut. "Current HIV therapies are remarkably effective at controlling viral replication, but they do not fully repair the profound damage HIV causes in the gut," said Satya Dandekar, senior author of the study published in Nature Microbiology and a distinguished professor in the Department of Medical Microbiology and Immunology at UC Davis Health. "Our findings suggest that restoring the gut's structural and immune health can enhance antiretroviral treatment and open an entirely new avenue for achieving more durable control of HIV." HIV and the late stage of infection, AIDS, remain major global public health issues. In 2025, an estimated 40.9 million people were living with HIV, and approximately 1.2 million people acquired new HIV infections. In 2025, around 570,000 people died from AIDS-related illnesses worldwide. HIV damages the gut immune system Gut-associated lymphoid tissue, the body's largest immune tissue and a key regulator of the immune system, is an early target of HIV. The virus severely damages immune and epithelial cells in the gut lining. This damage leads to an inflamed, leaky gut, a weakened defense system and decreased nutrient absorption. The virus also disrupts mitochondrial function (mitochondria are organelles that generate energy within cells). Even with strict adherence to ART, many people living with HIV have persistent gut inflammation caused by the virus. Previous research from Dandekar's lab showed L. plantarum could quickly heal the chronically inflamed, leaky gut associated with HIV. For this study, the researchers identified metabolites produced by L. plantarum in the virally inflamed gut environment in the nonhuman primate model of HIV/AIDS. Among the hundreds of molecules created by L. plantarum, one metabolite, 10-HSA, emerged as the strongest candidate for repairing the gut barrier and reducing inflammation. Using X-ray crystallography to visualize 10-HSA's interactions at the atomic level, researchers demonstrated that 10-HSA directly binds to PPAR-alpha, a nuclear receptor that regulates key biological processes. This binding promoted histone crotonylation, an epigenetic modification that helps regulate gene activity. The resulting cascade of cellular events reprogrammed gene expression to restore mitochondrial function and energy metabolism while supporting repair of the gut lining. Researchers also found that 10-HSA increased mitochondrial respiration in gut epithelial cells under HIV-related inflammatory conditions, boosting energy-generating capacity and reducing harmful reactive oxygen species. Testing the approach in nonhuman primates To determine whether the benefits of 10-HSA translate to living organisms, the team conducted two independent studies at the UC Davis National Biomedical Research Institute in nonhuman primates infected with simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV), a close relative of HIV. The first study evaluated 10-HSA alone without ART. This led to intestinal repair, improved key markers of gut function, improved mitochondrial health, reduced inflammatory signaling and partially restored the gut microbiota. The second study evaluated 10-HSA in combination with ART. Significantly, the combined treatment showed faster clearance of viral burden than ART alone. The combination also promoted faster recovery of gut immune cells, reduced immune activation, restored epithelial barrier integrity and restored beneficial gut microbiota and microbial diversity. "The findings suggest repairing the tissue damage caused by HIV may be as important as suppressing the virus itself with antiretroviral therapy," said Dylan Kramer, first author of the study and recent Ph.D. graduate from the Dandekar Lab. "By rebuilding the gut barrier, restoring mitochondrial function and reducing inflammation, 10-HSA helps create conditions that support stronger immune recovery and more effective antiviral therapy for HIV." The authors caution that the results are from preclinical models. The studies showed no adverse effects. The research supports testing the safety and effectiveness of 10-HSA in humans. "Our research shows that host health, microbial health and viral control are deeply interconnected," Dandekar said. She noted that treating the damaged gut ecosystem facilitates recovery of the immune system, leading to regained functions that have been lost during HIV infection. Additionally, treating the damaged gut during HIV infection can improve outcomes beyond what antiviral drugs can achieve alone. "The restoration of gut barrier integrity and microbial balance through 10-HSA supplementation may represent a promising therapeutic strategy, with implications that extend beyond HIV to other chronic inflammatory diseases of the gastrointestinal tract," Dandekar said. Publication details Dylan Kramer et al, Microbiota-derived 10-hydroxystearic acid activates PPARα to restore gut epithelial barrier integrity and enhance anti-retroviral therapy, Nature Microbiology (2026). Dandekar说。“我们的发现表明，恢复肠道的结构和免疫健康可以增强抗逆转录病毒治疗的效果，并为实现更持久地控制HIV开辟一条全新的途径。” HIV及其感染的晚期——艾滋病，仍然是全球主要的公共卫生问题。2025年，估计有4090万人感染HIV，约120万人新感染HIV。2025年，全球约有57万人死于与艾滋病相关的疾病。 HIV损害肠道免疫系统 肠道相关淋巴组织是人体最大的免疫组织，也是免疫系统的关键调节器，是HIV的早期攻击目标。病毒严重损害了肠道内壁的免疫细胞和上皮细胞。这种损害导致肠道炎症、渗漏，防御系统减弱，营养吸收下降。病毒还会扰乱线粒体功能（线粒体是细胞内产生能量的细胞器）。 即使严格 adherence 抗逆转录病毒疗法，许多HIV感染者仍存在由病毒引起的持续性肠道炎症。Dandekar实验室之前的研究表明，植物乳杆菌可以快速治愈与HIV相关的慢性炎症和肠道渗漏。 在这项研究中，研究人员在HIV/艾滋病非人灵长类模型的病毒性炎症肠道环境中，识别出由植物乳杆菌产生的代谢产物。在植物乳杆菌产生的数百种分子中，代谢产物10-HSA作为修复肠道屏障和减轻炎症的最有力候选者脱颖而出。 研究人员使用X射线晶体学在原子水平上可视化10-HSA的相互作用，证明10-HSA直接与PPAR-alpha结合，这是一种调节关键生物过程的核受体。 这种结合促进了组蛋白巴豆酰化，这是一种有助于调节基因活性的表观遗传修饰。由此产生的细胞事件级联重编程了基因表达，以恢复线粒体功能和能量代谢，同时支持肠道内壁的修复。 研究人员还发现，在HIV相关的炎症条件下，10-HSA增加了肠道上皮细胞的线粒体呼吸，从而提高了能量生成能力并减少了有害的活性氧。 在非人灵长类动物中测试该方法 为了确定10-HSA的益处是否能转化为活体生物，研究团队在加州大学戴维斯分校国家生物医学研究所对感染了猴免疫缺陷病毒（SIV，一种与HIV密切相关的病毒）的非人灵长类动物进行了两项独立研究。 第一项研究单独评估了10-HSA，不与ART联用。这导致肠道修复、肠道关键功能指标改善、线粒体健康改善、炎症信号减少以及肠道微生物群部分恢复。 第二项研究评估了10-HSA与ART联合使用。值得注意的是，联合治疗显示出比单独ART更快的病毒载量清除。这种联合疗法还促进了肠道免疫细胞的更快恢复、减少了免疫激活、恢复了上皮屏障的完整性，并恢复了有益的肠道微生物群和微生物多样性。 “这些发现表明，修复HIV造成的组织损伤可能与使用抗逆转录病毒疗法抑制病毒本身同样重要，”该研究的第一作者、Dandekar实验室新近毕业的博士Dylan Kramer说。“通过重建肠道屏障、恢复线粒体功能和减少炎症，10-HSA有助于创造支持更强免疫恢复和更有效HIV抗病毒治疗的条件。” 作者们提醒，这些结果来自临床前模型。研究未显示任何不良反应。这项研究支持在人体中测试10-HSA的安全性和有效性。 “我们的研究表明，宿主健康、微生物健康和病毒控制是紧密相连的，”Dandekar说。她指出，治疗受损的肠道生态系统有助于免疫系统的恢复，从而重新获得在HIV感染期间丧失的功能。此外，在HIV感染期间治疗受损的肠道可以改善结果，超越单纯抗病毒药物所能达到的效果。 Dandekar说：“通过补充10-HSA恢复肠道屏障完整性和微生物平衡可能代表一种有前景的治疗策略，其影响不仅限于HIV，还可扩展到其他慢性胃肠道炎症性疾病。” 出版详情：Dylan Kramer 等人，《微生物群衍生的10-羟基硬脂酸激活PPARα以恢复肠道上皮屏障完整性并增强抗逆转录病毒疗法》，《自然·微生物学》（2026年）。DOI: 10.1038/s41564-026-02433-0
