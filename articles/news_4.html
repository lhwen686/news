你的梦境有气味吗？一项针对“盲性心智”的新研究揭示了想象力的巨大差异

Do your dreams have smells? New study on 'blind minds' reveals vast differences in imagination

该研究深入探讨了心盲症（aphantasia）这一神经现象，揭示了其在清醒想象与梦境体验之间的复杂关系和异质性。心盲症患者通常无法自主地在脑海中生成视觉图像，但本研究发现，部分患者仍能拥有生动的视觉梦境，而另一些患者则在清醒和梦境中都缺乏特定感官的想象体验。这引出了两种核心医学机制假设：一是可能存在一个在清醒时负责将想象感知提升至意识层面的神经过程发生故障，导致患者无意识地进行视觉想象；二是某些心盲症患者的大脑根本无法生成某些特定类型的想象感知，无论清醒与否。

这种异质性对医学和相关行业具有深远影响。在心理治疗领域，许多干预措施（如认知行为疗法、暴露疗法）依赖于患者的视觉化能力。了解心盲症患者能否从这些基于图像的治疗中获益，或需要定制化方法，至关重要。教育领域也面临挑战，教师常鼓励学生视觉化，但心盲症儿童可能难以执行。此外，该研究拓宽了我们对意识本质和大脑如何创造主观体验的理解，对神经科学领域探索大脑结构、功能与感知连接性具有重要启示。未来研究需进一步区分这些潜在机制，以期为心盲症患者提供更精准的诊断与干预策略，并深化对人类意识之谜的认识。

It's common to think we all have similar experiences of life. But the more we learn about other people's hidden thoughts, the more evidence there is that this is untrue. For instance, not everyone has the same ability to have imagined sensations. Most people can visualize—they can have imagined experiences of seeing people and scenes that aren't there. But not everyone can.

We are both visual aphantasics, which means we can't voluntarily imagine seeing things in our minds' eyes. When we read a book, neither of us can imagine seeing the characters.

Yet other types of our waking imagined experiences are quite different. Derek can imagine hearing snippets of music at will, but Loren has a silent mind. She can't even imagine hearing herself talk. Loren can have vivid imagined feelings of touch, taste and smell, but Derek doesn't have any of these.

So, when we're awake, our imaginations are quite different. But what about our dreams? In our new study published in Scientific Reports, we delved into the dreams of aphantasics to see how they overlap—or don't—with their imaginations when they're awake.

Dreams in blind minds

While neither of us can visualize during the day, like many aphantasics, Derek has vivid visual dreams. But Loren doesn't have visual or audio dreams. Her dreams are always lucid: experienced as an unfolding awareness of a storyline she can control, accompanied by a succession of different feelings of texture and movement.

Derek's dreams, by contrast, are filled with sights, sounds and sensations of movement—but they don't involve imagined feelings of touch, taste or smell.

So Loren has a silent mind whether she's awake or dreaming, whereas Derek can't have imagined feelings of touch, taste or smell while awake or dreaming.

This made us think that the imagined experiences aphantasics have while awake might correspond in some way to the content of their dreams. So, we conducted a study to find out.

We recruited 84 visual aphantasics and a comparison group of 121 non-aphantasics and asked them all to report on their dreams and their waking imagined experiences.

We used standard measures of people's imagined experiences and devised new measures to check for consistency, including a measure of everyday imagined experiences.

We found that aphantasics tended to report having similar imagined experiences while dreaming and when awake. But there were also differences. Some aphantasics reliably reported that there was no correspondence at all.

What is the brain doing?

Scientists are interested in the overlap of aphantasics' dreams and their waking imagined experiences because it could give us insight into the differences between aphantasics and people who can visualize.

Since many aphantasics report having visual dreams, some argue that their brains can generate imagined experiences, but some neural process necessary for awareness of imagined sensations must fail when they're awake. They might visualize without knowing it.

Our research suggests another possibility. Some aphantasics might have brains that can generate a full range of different types of dreamed sensations.

But other aphantasics might have brains that simply can't create some types of imagined sensation, regardless of whether they're awake or dreaming.

Further research is needed to tease apart these possibilities.

Dreams vary in non-aphantasics, too

While our study focused on aphantasics, we also asked about the dreams of other people. Here, too, there were differences. For instance, 37% of non-aphantasics reported that they never have sensations of smell in their dreams, and 33% reported that they never have dreamed sensations of touch.

People also often reported being unsure about the content of their dreams. So if you ask family and friends, they'll likely say they have different combinations of dreamed sensations.

In our study, we also found evidence for a relationship between the content of dreams and people's imagined experiences in their daily lives. For instance, if people often have dreamed sensations of smell, they'll more likely report imagining the smell of cooking when they think about dinner while awake.

Why do we care?

We've found it's relatively common for people to report having a different collection of imagined sensations, both when they dream and when they're awake.

Some readers will have strong imagined experiences of smell, and others will report that they never have these imagined experiences. Our ongoing research aims to understand these differences and relate them to the structure, functioning and connectivity of people's brains.

Understanding imagined experiences might give us insight into how human brains create a type of conscious experience—one of the great mysteries of science.

From a practical perspective, visualizations are among the world's most popular psychological treatments, and it's unclear if aphantasics can benefit from attempts at imagery.

There are also issues for education. Teachers often encourage children to visualize, and it's unclear what proportion of children can't do that.

One day soon, we hope to be able to explain why some people can, and others cannot, imagine smelling their dinner before they turn on the stove.

人们通常认为我们都有相似的人生体验。然而，我们对他人隐秘想法了解得越多，就越有证据表明这并非事实。例如，并非每个人都拥有相同的想象感知能力。大多数人能够进行视觉想象——他们能够体验到并不存在的人和场景。但并非所有人都能做到。

我们两人都是视觉心盲症患者，这意味着我们无法自主地在脑海中想象事物。当我们阅读一本书时，我们都无法想象书中的人物形象。

然而，我们清醒时期的其他想象体验却大相径庭。德里克可以随意想象听到音乐片段，但洛伦却有一个“沉默的心灵”。她甚至无法想象听到自己说话。洛伦可以拥有生动的触觉、味觉和嗅觉想象感受，但德里克却没有任何这些体验。

因此，当我们清醒时，我们的想象力截然不同。但我们的梦境又如何呢？在我们发表在《科学报告》上的新研究中，我们深入探讨了心盲症患者的梦境，以探究它们与清醒时的想象力之间是否存在重叠。

心盲症患者的梦境

虽然我们两人白天都无法进行视觉想象，但与许多心盲症患者一样，德里克拥有生动的视觉梦境。然而，洛伦却没有视觉或听觉梦境。她的梦境总是清晰的：表现为一种对她可以控制的故事情节的渐进式意识，并伴随着一系列不同的质感和运动感受。

相比之下，德里克的梦境充满了视觉、听觉和运动感受——但它们不涉及触觉、味觉或嗅觉的想象感受。

因此，洛伦无论是清醒还是做梦，都拥有一个“沉默的心灵”，而德里克无论清醒还是做梦，都无法拥有触觉、味觉或嗅觉的想象感受。

这使我们认为，心盲症患者清醒时的想象体验可能与他们的梦境内容在某种程度上存在对应关系。于是，我们进行了一项研究来探究。

我们招募了84名视觉心盲症患者和121名非心盲症患者作为对照组，并要求他们报告自己的梦境和清醒时的想象体验。

我们使用了评估人们想象体验的标准测量方法，并设计了新的测量方法来检查一致性，其中包括对日常想象体验的测量。

我们发现，心盲症患者倾向于报告他们在做梦和清醒时拥有相似的想象体验。但也存在差异。一些心盲症患者明确报告两者之间根本没有对应关系。

大脑在做什么？

科学家们对心盲症患者的梦境与其清醒时的想象体验之间的重叠感兴趣，因为这可以让我们深入了解心盲症患者和能够进行视觉想象的人之间的差异。

由于许多心盲症患者报告有视觉梦境，一些人认为他们的大脑能够产生想象体验，但某个对想象感知意识至关重要的神经过程在他们清醒时必定失效了。他们可能在无意识中进行视觉想象。

我们的研究提出了另一种可能性。一些心盲症患者的大脑可能能够产生各种不同类型的梦中感受。

但另一些心盲症患者的大脑可能根本无法创造某些类型的想象感受，无论他们是清醒还是做梦。

需要进一步的研究来区分这些可能性。

非心盲症患者的梦境也各不相同

尽管我们的研究侧重于心盲症患者，但我们也询问了其他人的梦境。在这方面也存在差异。例如，37%的非心盲症患者报告称他们从未在梦中闻到气味，33%的人报告称他们从未在梦中体验到触觉。

人们也常常表示不确定自己梦境的内容。因此，如果你问家人和朋友，他们很可能会说他们拥有不同组合的梦中感受。

在我们的研究中，我们还发现了梦境内容与人们日常生活中想象体验之间存在关联的证据。例如，如果人们经常在梦中闻到气味，他们更有可能在清醒时想到晚餐时想象出烹饪的气味。

我们为何关注？

我们发现，人们报告他们在做梦和清醒时拥有不同集合的想象感受，这种情况相对普遍。

一些读者会有强烈的嗅觉想象体验，而另一些人则会报告他们从未有过这些想象体验。我们正在进行的研究旨在理解这些差异，并将其与人们大脑的结构、功能和连接性联系起来。

理解想象体验可能会让我们深入了解人类大脑如何创造一种意识体验——这是科学的伟大奥秘之一。

从实践角度来看，视觉化是世界上最受欢迎的心理治疗方法之一，但心盲症患者能否从图像疗法中受益尚不清楚。

教育方面也存在问题。教师经常鼓励孩子们进行视觉想象，但目前尚不清楚有多少孩子无法做到这一点。

我们希望在不久的将来，能够解释为什么有些人可以在开炉前想象出晚餐的味道，而另一些人却不能。
