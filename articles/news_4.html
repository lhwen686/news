寻求仑卡奈单抗治疗的早期阿尔茨海默病患者对脑健康建议依从性低

深度解析

本文揭示了阿尔茨海默病患者在遵循大脑健康生活方式建议方面存在的普遍不足，即使是那些积极寻求先进抗淀粉样蛋白药物 lecanemab 治疗的患者也未能幸免。lecanemab 作为一种抗淀粉样蛋白抗体药物，旨在通过靶向并清除大脑中的淀粉样蛋白斑块来减缓疾病进展，代表了阿尔茨海默病治疗领域的重大突破，标志着从单纯症状管理向疾病修饰治疗的转变。然而，研究结果指出一个关键的临床悖论：尽管患者愿意承担新药的风险和治疗负担，他们却往往忽视了已被广泛证实能改善大脑健康的基础性生活方式干预。这包括充足的体育活动、健康饮食、良好睡眠、压力管理以及控制血压和血糖等。这一发现对阿尔茨海默病的综合管理策略产生了深远影响。它强调了在推广新型药物疗法的同时，医疗系统和社会必须加强对患者行为改变的支持，通过结构化、个性化、基于健康心理学的方法来提高患者对生活方式干预的依从性。这表明，要真正应对阿尔茨海默病的挑战，需要整合药物治疗与持续的行为支持，形成一个更为全面和可持续的医疗模式。

Original
This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies . Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Researchers at the Mass General Brigham Neuroscience Institute found that most patients with early Alzheimer's disease are not optimally adhering to recommendations for improving their brain health, including exercising, getting adequate sleep, preventing hypertension and eating a heart-healthy diet. The results, published in The Journal of Prevention of Alzheimer's Disease, show that fewer than 3% of the studied patients followed all 15 brain health recommendations the researchers examined. Both patients with early Alzheimer's disease who pursued treatment with lecanemab, an anti-amyloid antibody medication for Alzheimer's disease, and patients who did not pursue anti-amyloid therapy had poor adherence to brain health recommendations, with no significant difference between the groups. "Because patients pursuing anti-amyloid therapy were willing to receive lecanemab infusions every two weeks and tolerate the risks associated with this medication, we thought this highly motivated group might be pursuing every avenue available to protect their brain health," said Kirk Daffner, MD, lead author of the study and a neurologist at the Mass General Brigham Neuroscience Institute. "The results show there is much more work to be done." Growing evidence links adequate physical activity, a healthy diet, sufficient sleep, social and cognitive engagement, controlled blood pressure and blood sugar, good vision and hearing, and low levels of alcohol consumption with a lower risk of cognitive decline and dementia. Brain Health Vital Signs, developed by Daffner, Seth Gale, MD, and colleagues, assesses 15 of these potentially modifiable factors to provide an overall brain health "score" and recommendations for improvement. The researchers studied 150 patients with mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia due to Alzheimer's disease who were seeking anti-amyloid antibody therapy and compared them with 117 patients with mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia who were not pursuing the therapy. Among patients pursuing anti-amyloid therapy, more than 25% were not optimally adhering to 11 of the 15 studied brain health recommendations, and more than 50% were not optimally adhering to four of the recommendations. Similar low adherence rates were observed in patients with early Alzheimer's disease who did not pursue anti-amyloid therapy. Patients most commonly fell short of the recommendation to get at least 25 minutes of physical activity per day on most days of the week. Sixty-three percent of patients pursuing anti-amyloid therapy did not meet this recommendation. Other especially challenging guidelines included reducing stress and achieving low A1c levels, blood pressure and body mass index. "There is a fairly robust, accepted evidence base that a certain set of behaviors and medical factors support brain health, but effective implementation in clinical care is still mostly missing," said Gale, senior author and neurologist at the Mass General Brigham Neuroscience Institute. "While tools such as Brain Health Vital Signs can help patients and their providers better understand what they are doing well and where they can improve, it's clear that structured, sustainable interventions are also necessary." The ongoing Brain Health Champion Study, led by Gale, Daffner and colleagues, with results from its first cohort of patients published in 2019, found that those who received six months of individualized brain health coaching markedly improved their adherence to recommended brain health behaviors. Daffner and Gale say that offering more effective tools to providers and their teams, grounded in health psychology-based techniques, may best help support behavior change in patients.
