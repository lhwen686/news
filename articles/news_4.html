本文深入揭示了住院精神科注册护士（PMH-RNs）面临的严峻职业倦怠问题及其对医疗体系的深远影响。职业倦怠在临床上表现为情绪耗竭、去人格化和个人成就感降低，它不仅损害护士的心理健康，更直接威胁患者的护理质量和安全。PMH-RNs在处理急性精神健康危机患者中扮演着核心角色，其专业能力和稳定性对维持高质量的心理健康服务至关重要。研究强调，支持性工作环境，特别是充足的人员配备，是缓解职业倦怠、提高工作满意度和降低离职率的关键干预措施。人员配备不足不仅加剧了护士的工作负担，还可能导致护理错误、影响患者满意度及治疗依从性。随着未来十年近半数精神科护理人员将退休，当前的高流失率预示着严重的医疗人力资源危机，可能导致心理健康服务可及性下降、医疗成本上升。因此，医疗管理者和政策制定者必须优先投资于优化人员配备模型、加强跨学科协作及营造安全的生理心理环境，以确保精神科护理队伍的韧性与可持续发展，从而保障公众的心理健康福祉。

This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies . Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Zakir Rushanly from Pexels A new study published in the Journal of Psychiatric and Mental Health Nursing reveals that four in 10 inpatient psychiatric mental health registered nurses (PMH-RNs) experience high burnout. However, researchers found that supportive work environments significantly reduce burnout, job dissatisfaction and nurse turnover. The research—from Penn Nursing's Center for Health Outcomes and Policy Research (CHOPR)—evaluated survey data from 740 inpatient PMH-RNs across 297 general acute care and psychiatric hospitals in 10 U.S. states. Key findings of the study include: Widespread burnout and turnover: Approximately 38% of psychiatric nurses reported high burnout, 29.5% experienced job dissatisfaction and 22% planned to leave their employer within a year. Work environment impact: For every one-point increase in the quality of the work environment, nurses experienced a 70% decrease in the odds of burnout, a 59% decrease in job dissatisfaction and a 53% decrease in intent to leave. Staffing is critical: Staffing adequacy received the lowest ratings among organizational factors and was the primary driver associated with improvements across all job outcomes. "High levels of burnout and turnover among PMH-RNs are not inevitable," said lead author Justinna Dixon, Ph.D., RN, who holds dual appointments as a CHOPR and National Clinician Scholars Program postdoctoral fellow. "Improving work environments—specifically through ensuring adequate staffing levels and empowering PMH-RNs through strong leadership support—represents an actionable strategy for health care systems to retain this workforce central to caring for patients experiencing acute mental health crises." With nearly half of the psychiatric nursing workforce expected to retire over the next decade, study authors call on administrators and policymakers to prioritize optimal staffing models, supportive interdisciplinary teamwork and collaboration, and safe physical and psychological environments for PMH-RNs. More information Justinna Dixon et al, Associations Between the Nurse Work Environment and Job Outcomes Among Inpatient Psychiatric Mental Health Registered Nurses: A Cross‐Sectional Observational Study, Journal of Psychiatric and Mental Health Nursing (2026).
