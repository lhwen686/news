昼夜节律基因变异为儿童肥胖风险提供新指标

Circadian gene variants offer new indicator of obesity risk in children

深度解析

这项研究揭示了昼夜节律相关基因（即“时钟基因”）与儿童肥胖风险之间的新关联，强调了其对饮食行为和特定常量营养素摄入的影响。从医学机制上看，时钟基因在细胞层面精确调控着一系列生理过程，包括代谢、睡眠-觉醒周期以及能量稳态。这些基因的特定变异可能扰乱正常的昼夜节律，进而影响食欲调节、食物选择和能量消耗，最终增加肥胖的易感性。这是首次在儿科队列中进行的此类研究，意义重大，因为它将研究重点放在了儿童生命早期这一关键发育窗口期。 这项发现对行业产生了深远影响。首先，它为个性化精准医疗提供了新的视角。通过识别特定时钟基因变异，临床医生未来或能更早地识别出高风险儿童，并根据其独特的遗传 predispositions 提供量身定制的饮食干预策略，而非一概而论的健康建议。其次，它强化了早期预防的重要性。在儿童时期建立健康的睡眠和饮食习惯，结合对昼夜节律基因的理解，有望更有效地预防儿童肥胖及其带来的长期慢性疾病风险（如2型糖尿病）。此研究仍处于初期，未来需要更深入的纵向研究以验证这些关联，并将其转化为切实可行的临床指南和公共卫生策略，推动儿童肥胖管理进入基因与环境互动的新时代。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain New research from the Guelph Family Health Study (GFHS) has found that circadian rhythm-related genes could influence the risk of obesity in children, highlighting an important connection between sleep and eating behavior. Prior research has explored these associations, but University of Guelph researchers are the first to study this in a pediatric cohort. No studies have been conducted in children younger than 6 years old. "Our findings contribute to a growing body of evidence linking circadian genetics to dietary behavior," says Dr. David Ma, professor in the Department of Human Health Sciences. Natural human rhythms affect health early in life While humans all have the same genes, each person has variations of those genes that explain characteristics such as hair and eye color. In this new study published in the journal Nutrients, researchers examined variations in genes associated with sleep and found that certain gene variations may increase caloric intake from specific macronutrients. "Clock genes have garnered substantial interest as modulators of disease risk," Ma says. "These findings show that listening to the natural rhythms of our bodies is important because they can influence eating behavior and chronic disease risk later in life. This matters because healthy behaviors such as sleep, physical activity and eating behaviors are learned early in life." Clock genes, a subset of circadian rhythm-related genes, work together inside a cell to control cellular processes. They operate on a 24-hour schedule, like the circadian rhythm in humans that informs when they sleep, eat or have the highest energy levels. Researchers say clock genes are emerging as potential candidates for markers of early disease risk and could therefore inform personalized diet-based interventions. "This research is in its infancy," says Zach Ribau, a GFHS bioinformatician and graduate of the Master of Bioinformatics program. "It is a complex picture. In no way would this be the only thing to consider, but it is a factor in obesity in children." New research leads to new questions about biology Examining this makes researchers question what is known about biology and makes the circadian rhythm an exciting new concept to study, Ribau adds. Researchers used data from the dietary records of 168 children between the ages of 2 and 6 years, and eight clock genes linked to obesity, diabetes and dietary behaviors. These early stages of development are when early dietary habits can contribute to later disease risk, Ribau says. "In this paper, we looked at just one point in time," Ribau says. "Further observational studies are in development, and more granular hypotheses could reveal more. As the children and parents grow older, do the associations here hold up?" This kind of translational research, converting scientific discoveries into practical interventions, is the foundation of what the Guelph Family Health Study aims to do. The multidisciplinary, long-term research initiative investigates family behaviors, nutrition, sleep and stress, among other things. It looks at how these affect the long-term health of young children while proactively offering home-based strategies for healthy habits. "There is so much we can do," Ribau says. "Not just in terms of circadian genes, but many other interesting hypotheses to test and answers to find at GFHS." Publication details Zachary J. Ribau et al, Clock Gene Variants Are Associated with Energy and Macronutrient Intake in Early Childhood and Adulthood, Nutrients (2026).
