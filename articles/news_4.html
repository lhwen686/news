A landmark government policy designed to slash the cost of medicines for millions of Australians is falling well short of its potential because GPs and pharmacists have been slow to adopt it, new research has found. The study, published in the Medical Journal of Australia, examined uptake of Australia's 60-day dispensing policy for blood pressure medicines in the two years since it launched in September 2023.

The work found that despite clear financial benefits for patients, the policy has reached only 21.2% of antihypertensive prescriptions, less than half the government's projected 45% uptake. Patients taking antihypertensive medications saved approximately $65 million through 60-day prescribing in the first 20 months of the policy. But the researchers estimate that if uptake rose to 50%, annual savings from blood pressure medicines alone could reach $165 million for patients and $11.6 million for the government.

"The financial benefits for patients are substantial and well-established," said lead author Dr. Tian Wang from The George Institute for Global Health. "The challenge now is making sure those benefits actually reach people, and that requires action and support from GPs and pharmacists."

In interviews with 20 GPs across New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland, the researchers found significant variation in how the policy was being used. Eight GPs proactively offered 60-day prescriptions, six only did so if patients asked, and five rarely or never prescribed them.

The two main barriers GPs identified were difficulty keeping track of which medicines were eligible for 60-day supply, and reports that pharmacists were discouraging the longer prescriptions. Some GPs also struggled with prescribing software that defaulted to 30-day dispensing, making 60-day prescriptions a manual workaround rather than standard practice.

Four pharmacists interviewed for the study raised concerns about incorrect dispensing and potential medicine shortages if 60-day supply became more widespread. One pharmacist also cited lost income as a concern for pharmacy owners.

The government introduced the Additional Community Supply Support (ACSS) payment program in April 2024 to offset the financial impact on pharmacies, at a cost of $46.5 million between April 2024 and April 2025. While the policy generated $87 million in gross PBS savings over the study period, the ACSS payment reduced the government's net savings to $0.3 million. The primary financial beneficiaries of the policy to date have been patients.

The researchers say that the results from this study indicate that further action may be needed to change prescribing behavior. The government should consider:

Promoting 60-day prescriptions to patients as a way to save money and reduce prescription refills

Promoting 60-day prescribing to GPs for eligible medicines to ensure they are aware of the policy change

Working with GP software providers to make 60-day prescribing the default option for GPs for eligible medicines

Highlighting to pharmacists the benefits to consumers and the availability of the ACSS payments to support 60-day prescribing

"Most patients are still on 30-day prescriptions simply because their GP has not yet made the switch," Wang said. "Updating prescribing software is one of the most straightforward changes that could make a real difference."

一项旨在为数百万澳大利亚人大幅削减药品成本的里程碑式政府政策，因全科医生（GPs）和药剂师采纳缓慢，未能充分发挥其潜力，一项新研究发现。这项发表在《澳大利亚医学杂志》上的研究，调查了自2023年9月启动以来，澳大利亚针对降压药的60天配药政策在两年内的采纳情况。

研究发现，尽管该政策能为患者带来明显的经济利益，但仅覆盖了21.2%的降压药处方，不到政府预计45%采纳率的一半。在该政策实施的最初20个月里，服用降压药的患者通过60天配药节省了约6500万澳元。但研究人员估计，如果采纳率提高到50%，仅降压药一项，每年就能为患者节省1.65亿澳元，为政府节省1160万澳元。

“患者的经济利益是显著且已得到充分证实的，”乔治全球健康研究所的首席作者田旺博士说。“现在的挑战是确保这些利益真正惠及民众，这需要全科医生和药剂师的行动和支持。”

研究人员对新南威尔士州、维多利亚州和昆士兰州的20名全科医生进行了访谈，发现该政策的使用方式存在显著差异。八名全科医生主动提供60天处方，六名仅在患者要求时才提供，而五名则很少或从不开具。

全科医生指出的两个主要障碍是难以追踪哪些药品符合60天供应的条件，以及有报告称药剂师不鼓励开具更长期的处方。一些全科医生还苦于处方软件默认设置为30天配药，导致60天处方成为手动变通而非标准操作。

接受采访的四名药剂师对如果60天供应变得更普遍可能导致的配药错误和潜在药品短缺表示担忧。一名药剂师还指出，收入损失是药房老板关注的问题。

政府于2024年4月推出了“社区额外供应支持（ACSS）”支付计划，以抵消对药房的财政影响，该计划在2024年4月至2025年4月期间耗资4650万澳元。尽管该政策在研究期间产生了8700万澳元的PBS（药物福利计划）总节余，但ACSS支付将政府的净节余降至30万澳元。迄今为止，该政策的主要财政受益者是患者。

研究人员表示，这项研究的结果表明，可能需要采取进一步行动来改变处方行为。政府应考虑：

向患者推广60天处方，作为省钱和减少处方补充次数的方式

向全科医生推广符合条件的60天处方，以确保他们了解政策变化

与全科医生软件供应商合作，使60天处方成为全科医生针对符合条件药品的默认选项

向药剂师强调对消费者的好处以及ACSS支付的可用性，以支持60天处方

“大多数患者仍然使用30天处方，仅仅是因为他们的全科医生尚未做出改变，”王说。“更新处方软件是最直接、能产生实际影响的改变之一。”
