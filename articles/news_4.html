Christchurch mutation points to potential gene therapy avenue for Parkinson's

深度解析

这篇研究揭示了APOE基因的“基督城突变”在神经退行性疾病预防中的巨大潜力。核心机制在于，这种突变能有效抑制tau蛋白和α-突触核蛋白在大脑中的异常聚集与积累。tau蛋白的病理性聚集是阿尔茨海默病的关键特征之一，而α-突触核蛋白的错误折叠和积累则驱动着帕金森病和路易体痴呆的病理进展。传统的神经退行性疾病研究往往侧重于单一病理蛋白，但此发现揭示了一种可能对多种疾病病理具有广谱保护作用的共同机制。 从行业影响来看，这一发现为多种神经退行性疾病的治疗开辟了新途径。目前，针对APOE-2基因（另一种具保护作用的APOE变体）的基因疗法已在开发中，而基督城突变被发现具有更强的保护效力，这预示着其在基因疗法开发上拥有巨大前景。若能成功转化为人体临床试验，这将代表着帕金森病和路易体痴呆等疾病治疗策略的重大突破，可能从根本上改变疾病的进展轨迹，而非仅仅缓解症状。同时，它也深化了我们对这些复杂神经疾病共享病理通路的理解，为药物研发提供了新的靶点和方向。

DOI: 10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118301 A rare genetic mutation may protect against developing Parkinson's disease and Lewy body dementia, a new study from researchers at Oregon Health & Science University suggests. The study, published in the journal Cell Reports, for the first time suggests a link to Parkinson's involving the so-called Christchurch mutation of the APOE gene. The study raises the possibility of gene therapy approaches to forestall cognitive and motor impairments in Parkinson's and related conditions. Previous research—starting with the 2019 case of a woman from Colombia who carried the rare mutation first discovered in New Zealand—found that the mutation seems to forestall symptoms of dementia in people who are otherwise genetically destined to develop Alzheimer's disease. OHSU researchers working in a rodent model have now discovered a link to Parkinson's. "It opens up this idea that this Christchurch mutation could be helpful for a lot of things we didn't realize before," said senior author Vivek Unni, M.D., Ph.D., associate professor of neurology in the OHSU School of Medicine. Protection against two damaging proteins Other studies in animal models have shown the Christchurch mutation prevents the aggregation of the Alzheimer's-related tau protein in the brain. The OHSU study published confirms the same process in tau, while also going a step further and showing that the Christchurch mutation prevents the accumulation of a different type of protein, known as alpha-synuclein, that typifies the progression of Parkinson's and Lewy body dementia. OHSU scientists used mouse models that are predisposed to accumulate alpha-synuclein and tau proteins in the brain. Then, they added the Christchurch mutation to the APOE gene to see what would happen. In each case, mice with the Christchurch mutation did not accumulate tau or alpha-synuclein proteins in the brain, despite their predisposition. In effect, the mutation seemed to protect the brain from the accumulation of proteins believed to drive both Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases. Toward Christchurch gene therapy Already, gene therapies are being developed to leverage another, more common version of the APOE gene (APOE-2) that also seems to be protective against Alzheimer's. Unni said the Christchurch mutation seems to be even more protective than APOE-2 and "acts like a super-APOE-2," at least in the mouse model examined in the new study. His lab hopes to extend its preclinical research, with an eye toward eventually moving it far enough to convince drug companies to invest in APOE Christchurch gene therapy clinical trials with people. "It would take several years to test the idea," he said. Publication details Carlos M.
