为何微量维生素B12会随着年龄增长而愈发重要

Why tiny amounts of vitamin B12 matter more as we age

Two micrograms is an almost unimaginably small amount. It weighs less than a tiny fragment of a grain of table salt. Yet adults need only around this amount of vitamin B12 each day, depending on the guideline used, to support red blood cells, nerves and DNA production.

In 2026, it will be 100 years since George Minot and William Murphy reported that a liver-rich diet could treat pernicious anemia, then a frequently fatal disease. Their work transformed medicine and eventually led scientists to identify vitamin B12 as the substance in liver that treated the disease.

But the route to that breakthrough began with an unexpected clue from animal experiments. The American physician and pathologist George Whipple had shown that liver helped dogs recover from anemia caused by blood loss. Blood-loss anemia happens when the body loses red blood cells through bleeding. Pernicious anemia is different: The problem is not bleeding but poor absorption of vitamin B12. Even so, Whipple's experiments pointed researchers toward liver as a source of a powerful blood-forming factor.

Patients with pernicious anemia who had been close to death often improved dramatically within weeks of eating liver-rich diets. The success of liver treatment eventually led scientists to isolate the deep red compound now known as vitamin B12, or cobalamin.

Often mistaken

Despite decades of research, vitamin B12 deficiency remains common, particularly among older adults, vegans, vegetarians and people with conditions that affect absorption. Some people do not consume enough B12 because it is naturally found mainly in foods from animals, including meat, fish, eggs and dairy products. Others struggle to absorb it properly.

This becomes more common with age. Some older people produce less stomach acid, which is needed to release B12 from food. Others develop autoimmune gastritis, in which the immune system damages stomach cells involved in producing acid and intrinsic factor, the protein needed for vitamin B12 absorption. Weight-loss surgery and some medicines used for diabetes or acid reflux can also reduce absorption.

The symptoms of deficiency can develop slowly and are often mistaken for normal aging. People may feel exhausted, weak or short of breath. Some develop numbness or tingling in their hands and feet, poor balance, memory problems or what many describe as "brain fog." These symptoms are not specific to B12 deficiency, so persistent tiredness, tingling or balance problems should be checked rather than assumed to be a simple vitamin problem.

People at higher risk, including vegans, vegetarians, older adults and those taking medicines that affect stomach acid or diabetes treatment, may need testing or supplementation advice from a health professional.

Doctors have traditionally linked tiredness in B12 deficiency to anemia. Without enough vitamin B12, the bone marrow cannot produce healthy red blood cells. Instead, it releases unusually large, immature cells that carry oxygen less effectively around the body.

But anemia may not be the only reason people with low B12 feel exhausted.

Low energy

In humans, vitamin B12 is directly needed by only two enzymes, the proteins that help chemical reactions happen in the body. One helps the body make DNA, which cells need when they divide. The other helps mitochondria process certain fats and protein building blocks. Mitochondria are the tiny structures inside cells that help turn food into usable energy.

This mitochondrial role has attracted growing interest from researchers studying ageing, muscle function and vitamin B12 status. A 2026 study explored what happens when cells do not have enough B12. Researchers found that low B12 could interfere with the DNA inside mitochondria and reduce energy production in laboratory models of skeletal muscle (muscle cells studied outside the human body).

A related study in aged female mice found that B12 supplementation improved several signs of mitochondrial health in muscle, including the number and structure of mitochondria. Together, this work points to one possible reason some people with low B12 report fatigue before obvious anemia is detected.

These findings do not mean vitamin B12 supplements can reverse aging or act as an energy booster for people whose B12 levels are already normal.

Scientists have suspected a link between B12 and mitochondrial function for many years because one of the two B12-dependent enzymes works inside mitochondria. Earlier research has also suggested that low B12 status may be linked with poorer muscle function in older adults, although much of this work is observational and cannot prove cause and effect.

So if you're feeling persistently tired, is it worth paying for vitamin B12 injections at a wellness clinic or med spa? For most people, no. B12 injections are an established treatment for diagnosed deficiency, particularly when absorption is impaired, and the NHS uses hydroxocobalamin injections for vitamin B12 deficiency anemia.

But there is little evidence that B12 shots boost energy, weight loss or performance in people whose B12 levels are already normal. The more useful first step is to find out what is causing the tiredness.

The story of vitamin B12 is unusual because the body needs so little of it, yet the consequences of deficiency can be profound. Long before scientists understood its chemistry, doctors recognized that something in liver could restore strength, appetite and vitality to desperately ill patients.

A century later, researchers are still finding that this tiny cobalt-containing molecule does more than prevent anemia. It may also help explain how cells maintain energy and function as the body ages.

两微克是一个几乎难以想象的微小量。它比一小片食盐的重量还要轻。然而，根据所采用的指南，成年人每天仅需大约这么多的维生素B12，即可支持红细胞、神经和DNA的生成。

到2026年，乔治·米诺特和威廉·墨菲报告富含肝脏的饮食可以治疗恶性贫血（当时一种常致命的疾病）将届满100周年。他们的工作改变了医学，并最终使科学家们发现维生素B12是肝脏中治疗该疾病的物质。

但通往这一突破的途径始于动物实验的一个意外线索。美国医生兼病理学家乔治·惠普尔曾展示，肝脏有助于狗从失血性贫血中恢复。失血性贫血是身体因出血而失去红细胞时发生的。恶性贫血则不同：问题不在于出血，而在于维生素B12吸收不良。即便如此，惠普尔的实验仍将研究人员引向肝脏，认为它是强大造血因子的来源。

患有恶性贫血、曾濒临死亡的患者，在食用富含肝脏的饮食几周后，病情常会显著改善。肝脏治疗的成功最终促使科学家分离出这种深红色化合物，即现在所知的维生素B12，或称钴胺素。

经常被误诊

尽管经过数十年的研究，维生素B12缺乏症仍然很常见，尤其是在老年人、纯素食者、素食者以及患有影响吸收的疾病的人群中。有些人没有摄入足够的B12，因为它主要天然存在于动物性食物中，包括肉类、鱼类、鸡蛋和奶制品。另一些人则难以正确吸收它。

这种情况随着年龄的增长而变得更加普遍。一些老年人产生的胃酸较少，而胃酸是B12从食物中释放所必需的。另一些人则患上自身免疫性胃炎，其中免疫系统会损害参与产生胃酸和内因子的胃细胞，内因子是维生素B12吸收所需的蛋白质。减肥手术以及一些用于糖尿病或胃食管反流的药物也可能降低吸收。

缺乏症的症状可能发展缓慢，并常被误认为是正常的衰老。人们可能感到精疲力尽、虚弱或气短。有些人手脚出现麻木或刺痛感、平衡能力差、记忆力问题或许多人所描述的“脑雾”。这些症状并非B12缺乏症所特有，因此持续的疲劳、刺痛或平衡问题应进行检查，而非简单地认为是维生素问题。

高风险人群，包括纯素食者、素食者、老年人以及服用影响胃酸的药物或糖尿病治疗药物的人群，可能需要寻求专业医疗人员的检测或补充建议。

医生传统上将B12缺乏症中的疲劳与贫血联系起来。如果没有足够的维生素B12，骨髓就无法产生健康的红细胞。相反，它会释放异常巨大、不成熟的细胞，它们向全身输送氧气的效率较低。

但贫血可能并非B12水平低的人感到疲惫的唯一原因。

能量不足

在人体中，维生素B12只被两种酶（即帮助身体内化学反应发生的蛋白质）直接需要。其中一种酶帮助身体制造DNA，细胞在分裂时需要DNA。另一种酶帮助线粒体处理某些脂肪和蛋白质的构成单元。线粒体是细胞内微小的结构，有助于将食物转化为可利用的能量。

这种线粒体作用已引起研究衰老、肌肉功能和维生素B12状况的研究人员越来越大的兴趣。2026年的一项研究探讨了细胞缺乏B12时会发生什么。研究人员发现，在骨骼肌（在人体外研究的肌肉细胞）的实验室模型中，低B12水平会干扰线粒体内的DNA并减少能量产生。

一项对老年雌性小鼠的相关研究发现，补充B12改善了肌肉中线粒体健康的多个迹象，包括线粒体的数量和结构。综合来看，这项工作指出了在明显的贫血被检测到之前，一些B12水平低的人报告疲劳的一个可能原因。

这些发现并不意味着维生素B12补充剂可以逆转衰老，或者对B12水平已经正常的人起到增强能量的作用。

科学家们多年来一直怀疑B12与线粒体功能之间存在联系，因为两种B12依赖性酶中的一种在线粒体内发挥作用。早期的研究也表明，低B12状态可能与老年人较差的肌肉功能有关，尽管这些研究大多是观察性的，不能证明因果关系。

那么，如果您感到持续疲劳，是否值得去健康诊所或医疗水疗中心支付维生素B12注射费用呢？对大多数人来说，不值得。B12注射是已确诊缺乏症的既定治疗方法，特别是在吸收受损时，英国国家医疗服务体系（NHS）使用羟钴胺注射剂治疗维生素B12缺乏性贫血。

但几乎没有证据表明B12注射能增强B12水平正常人群的能量、促进减肥或提高表现。更有效的首要步骤是找出导致疲劳的原因。

维生素B12的故事很不寻常，因为人体对它的需求量极小，但缺乏症的后果却可能很严重。早在科学家们了解其化学性质之前，医生们就认识到肝脏中的某种物质可以恢复濒死病人的体能、食欲和活力。

一个世纪后，研究人员仍在发现这种微小的含钴分子不仅仅是预防贫血。它还可能有助于解释细胞在身体衰老时如何维持能量和功能。
