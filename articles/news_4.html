Epidurals not linked to increased harm for newborns or children

深度解析

这项发表于《英国医学杂志》的研究，通过大规模人口队列分析，为分娩期硬膜外镇痛（epidural analgesia）对新生儿及儿童健康的安全性提供了强有力的循证支持。核心专业概念在于，研究通过对近50万例分娩数据的13年长期随访，系统性评估了硬膜外镇痛与多种严重新生儿并发症（如脑损伤、败血症、严重呼吸问题、低Apgar评分、28天内死亡）以及儿童期脑瘫之间的潜在关联。其医学机制在于，此研究采用了严格的流行病学方法，在控制了多种混杂因素（如母亲年龄、既存疾病、胎龄等）后，未发现硬膜外镇痛与上述不良结局之间存在统计学意义上的关联。这对于临床实践和行业具有深远影响。首先，它为临床医生和准父母提供了重要的安全保障信息，有助于消除关于硬膜外镇痛潜在风险的担忧，促进知情选择。其次，这一结果将有助于强化并可能更新现有产科临床指南，倡导更广泛地提供和公平地获取硬膜外镇痛服务，将其作为安全有效的产时疼痛管理策略。这不仅能改善产妇的分娩体验，还能降低因剧烈疼痛带来的生理和心理压力，从而优化整体产科护理质量。

Original
This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies . Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain Having an epidural during labor is not associated with clinically significant increased risks of harm to newborn babies, including brain injury, severe breathing problems, sepsis and death, or cerebral palsy later in childhood, according to a study published in The BMJ. The researchers say these findings "support widening availability and equitable access to epidural analgesia as a safe component of intrapartum care." Epidural analgesia in labor provides effective pain relief and may help reduce complications in mothers after giving birth, but evidence of its effect on newborn and child health is limited. How the researchers assessed risk To address this, researchers analyzed data for 495,695 births in Scotland over a 13-year period (2007–2019) to examine whether epidural analgesia during labor was associated with serious neurological conditions occurring within 28 days of birth. Only women who delivered a single baby vaginally or via unplanned cesarean section between 24 and 42 weeks of pregnancy were included in the analysis. Further measures included other severe newborn illness, sepsis, low Apgar score (a routine test of a baby's health) five minutes after birth, death within 28 days of birth, and cerebral palsy diagnosed at any point during childhood. Factors such as maternal age, ethnicity, weight, existing preeclampsia or diabetes, smoking history, birth location and gestational age at birth were also taken into account. Serious complications remained rare Of nearly 500,000 women included in the study, around one in four had an epidural during labor. Overall, serious neurological conditions were rare, affecting fewer than 1 in 1,000 babies. These conditions occurred at the expected rate and were no more common among babies whose mothers had an epidural compared with those who did not. No association was found between epidural analgesia in labor and serious neurological conditions, other severe newborn illness, sepsis, low Apgar score at five minutes, death at 28 days, or cerebral palsy in childhood. Limits on how far it applies This is an observational study, so no firm conclusions can be drawn about cause and effect. The authors acknowledge that the study was limited to women delivering in Scotland, a predominantly white population, so the findings may not apply to more ethnically diverse populations or other health care settings. However, this was a large study with long-term follow-up of newborn and childhood outcomes, and results were consistent after additional analyses across various groups, including women considered to have high-risk pregnancies and preterm births, supporting the reliability of the findings. As such, the authors conclude, "These results should reassure parents and clinicians that epidural analgesia use in labor is safe for babies and support informed, evidence-based decision-making about analgesic options in labor." Publication details Epidural analgesia in labour and neonatal and childhood outcomes: national population based cohort study, The BMJ (2026). DOI: 10.1136/bmj-2026-343320 Journal information: British Medical Journal (BMJ)
