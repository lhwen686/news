深度解析

此项研究揭示了B细胞清除疗法在多发性硬化症（MS）治疗中的一个新颖且关键的作用机制。传统观点认为，这类疗法通过清除在中枢神经系统炎症中扮演致病角色的自身反应性B细胞，从而减轻疾病活动。然而，本研究出乎意料地发现，B细胞清除不仅直接靶向有害B细胞，还通过调节相关因子，促进了来源于肠道黏膜的调节性B细胞（如IgA B细胞）向循环系统和中枢神经系统的迁移。这些调节性B细胞在免疫稳态维持中发挥重要作用，它们能够抑制异常免疫反应，从而改善MS患者的临床预后。 这一发现深刻拓展了我们对B细胞清除疗法作用模式的理解，即其疗效并非单一的“减法”（清除有害细胞），而是一个复杂的“加法”（招募有益细胞）过程。在行业影响方面，这意味着未来的MS及其他自身免疫性炎症疾病的治疗策略，可不再局限于直接抑制或清除免疫细胞，而是可以探索如何通过调控肠道免疫轴，精确动员和利用内源性的保护性免疫细胞群。这为开发基于肠道免疫调节的新型免疫疗法提供了理论基础和潜在靶点，可能引导更精准、副作用更小的治疗方案，具有重要的临床转化价值。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain B-cell depletion therapies, in which B cells of the immune system that may drive disease activity in multiple sclerosis (MS) are temporarily removed, have contributed significantly to improved treatment for patients in recent years. Researchers from the University Hospital of Bonn (UKB), the Universities of Bonn, Basel, Toronto and Yale, along with their collaborators, have uncovered a previously unknown mechanism through which B-cell depletion therapies contribute to a better disease course in patients with MS. Surprisingly, the treatment appears to exert part of its beneficial effect by mobilizing regulatory immune cells that naturally reside in the gut. This study has now been published in the journal Science Translational Medicine. In multiple sclerosis (MS), the most common chronic inflammatory disease of the central nervous system, B cells, which are part of the immune system, mistakenly attack the protective sheath surrounding nerves, known as the myelin sheath. This role in the development and progression of MS has brought this type of white blood cell into focus as a therapeutic target. It is important to note that researchers now know that not all B cells are harmful in MS. In addition to disease-causing B cells, there are also beneficial B cells that, in accordance with their actual function, help regulate immune responses and may have a positive effect on the course of the disease. The international research team led by Professor Anne-Katrin Pröbstel from the UKB, as well as from the Universities of Basel and Bonn and the German Center for Neurodegenerative Diseases (DZNE), investigated how B-cell depletion therapies affect these protective B-cell populations. To this end, the first authors, Dr. Tradite Neziraj and Dr. Elisabeth Pössnecker, analyzed immune cells from various tissues. They collected cells from the blood, cerebrospinal fluid and intestinal mucosal tissue of control subjects participating in the study and B-cell-depleted MS patients and examined the cells for type and function. They observed that B-cell depletion therapy promoted the trafficking of beneficial B cells from the gut into the bloodstream and central nervous system of people with MS. Immunotherapy promotes cell migration from the gut "Our research shows that B-cell depletion changes the levels of factors that regulate B cells and is associated with increased migration of protective gut-derived B cells," explains Pröbstel, managing director of the Center for Neurology at the University Hospital Bonn (UKB), a member of the Cluster of Excellence ImmunoSensation3 at the University of Bonn, and research group leader at the DZNE. "Interestingly, higher levels of these B cell-regulating factors were linked to better outcomes in patients with MS." The findings provide new insights into why B-cell depletion therapies are so effective. Rather than simply removing harmful immune cells, the treatment may also help recruit beneficial immune cells from the gut. This discovery opens up new possibilities for developing future therapies that harness beneficial gut immune cells in MS and other inflammatory diseases.
