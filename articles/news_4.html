A research team led by City University of Hong Kong (CityUHK), in collaboration with the University of Zurich, has uncovered the first comprehensive map of how the anti-aging protein Klotho operates in distinct regions of the kidney, resolving long-standing scientific uncertainties about its physiological roles.

Klotho, widely recognized as a "longevity protein," is produced primarily by the kidneys and has long been regarded as a key factor in maintaining youthfulness and health. However, its segment-specific functions along the nephron remained unclear. In a study titled "Klotho in the kidney distal convolution regulates urinary Klotho excretion and kidney calcium reabsorption, but not phosphate homeostasis," recently published in Kidney International, the research team demonstrates with unprecedented precision that different kidney tubule segments carry out fundamentally different aspects of Klotho biology.

The study was led jointly by Professor Ganesh Pathare, from the Department of Infectious Diseases and Public Health of the Jockey Club College of Veterinary Medicine and Life Sciences at CityUHK, and Professor Johannes Loffing, from the University of Zurich, Switzerland. The team used single-cell RNA sequencing to show that Klotho is unevenly distributed across the nephron segment called the distal convolution (DC), with its expression markedly enriched in the late DC compared with the early DC.

This discovery guided the generation of a suite of targeted mouse models that selectively deleted Klotho in specific nephron segments, allowing the team to trace the renal origins of soluble Klotho (sKlotho), the secreted form of the protein.

Through three DC-specific knockout models, the team demonstrated that approximately 80% of urinary sKlotho originates from the late DC, while the remaining 20% derives from the early DC. Notably, when Klotho was removed only from the DC, the mice maintained normal serum sKlotho and phosphate homeostasis but developed pronounced hypercalciuria and significant reductions in bone mineral density, revealing a previously unappreciated role of DC-derived Klotho in regulating renal calcium handling and bone integrity.

Molecular analyses uncovered the mechanistic basis of these phenotypes. In the absence of Klotho in the DC, several key genes essential for calcium reabsorption, including Trpv5, Vdr, Pth1r and Klk1, were markedly downregulated, and the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway was significantly suppressed. These findings demonstrate how DC-derived Klotho regulates calcium transport mechanisms and the associated intracellular signaling landscape.

To clarify the role of Klotho in the proximal tubule (PT), the team generated an additional pan-tubular knockout model, in which Klotho was deleted across both the PT and DC. This model produced a profoundly different outcome: Mice rapidly developed severe phosphate retention, exhibited dramatic increases in FGF23, showed undetectable levels of both serum and urinary sKlotho, and experienced progressive weight loss.

These results sharply contrast with the DC-only deletion and establish the PT—not the DC—as the critical site where Klotho maintains systemic phosphate balance, prevents aging-like phenotypes and likely contributes the majority of circulating sKlotho. Importantly, the study also overturns a long-standing hypothesis suggesting that sKlotho secreted from the DC might act in a paracrine manner in the PT to regulate phosphate reabsorption.

Together, these discoveries provide the first definitive segment-specific functional map of Klotho in the kidney. The study clarifies that DC-derived Klotho governs urinary sKlotho production and renal calcium reabsorption, while PT-derived Klotho is essential for phosphate homeostasis and likely circulatory sKlotho levels. This new framework resolves long-standing uncertainties in Klotho biology and offers a scientifically robust foundation for future therapeutic strategies targeting Klotho in disorders involving chronic kidney disease and mineral-bone disorders.

由香港城市大学（CityUHK）领导的一个研究团队，与苏黎士大学合作，首次揭示了抗衰老蛋白Klotho在肾脏不同区域如何发挥作用的全面图谱，解决了其生理作用长期存在的科学不确定性。

Klotho被广泛认为是“长寿蛋白”，主要由肾脏产生，长期以来被认为是维持年轻和健康的关键因素。然而，其在肾单位沿线的节段特异性功能仍不清楚。在最近发表于《国际肾脏病》期刊上，题为“肾脏远曲小管中的Klotho调节尿Klotho排泄和肾脏钙重吸收，但不调节磷酸盐稳态”的研究中，研究团队以前所未有的精确度证明，不同的肾小管节段执行着Klotho生物学中根本不同的方面。

这项研究由香港城市大学赛马会兽医及生命科学学院传染病及公共卫生学系的Ganesh Pathare教授与瑞士苏黎士大学的Johannes Loffing教授共同领导。该团队利用单细胞RNA测序技术表明，Klotho在被称为远曲小管（DC）的肾单位节段中分布不均，其表达在晚期DC中比早期DC显著富集。

这一发现指导了靶向小鼠模型的生成，这些模型选择性地删除了特定肾单位节段中的Klotho，从而使团队能够追溯可溶性Klotho（sKlotho，该蛋白的分泌形式）的肾脏来源。

通过三种DC特异性敲除模型，团队证明约80%的尿sKlotho来源于晚期DC，而其余20%来源于早期DC。值得注意的是，当Klotho仅从DC中移除时，小鼠保持正常的血清sKlotho和磷酸盐稳态，但出现了明显的高钙尿症和骨矿物质密度显著降低，揭示了DC源性Klotho在调节肾脏钙处理和骨骼完整性方面先前未被认识的作用。

分子分析揭示了这些表型的机制基础。在DC中缺乏Klotho的情况下，几个对钙重吸收至关重要的关键基因，包括Trpv5、Vdr、Pth1r和Klk1，显著下调，并且丝裂原活化蛋白激酶（MAPK）信号通路显著受抑制。这些发现表明DC源性Klotho如何调节钙转运机制以及相关的细胞内信号传导网络。

为了阐明Klotho在近曲小管（PT）中的作用，团队生成了一个额外的泛小管敲除模型，其中Klotho在PT和DC中都被删除。该模型产生了截然不同的结果：小鼠迅速发展为严重的磷酸盐潴留，FGF23显著增加，血清和尿sKlotho水平均检测不到，并出现进行性体重减轻。

这些结果与仅删除DC的情况形成鲜明对比，并确立了PT（而非DC）是Klotho维持全身磷酸盐平衡、预防类衰老表型并可能贡献大部分循环sKlotho的关键部位。重要的是，这项研究还推翻了一个长期存在的假设，即从DC分泌的sKlotho可能以旁分泌方式作用于PT，从而调节磷酸盐的重吸收。

综合来看，这些发现首次提供了Klotho在肾脏中节段特异性功能的确切图谱。该研究阐明，DC源性Klotho控制尿sKlotho的产生和肾脏钙的重吸收，而PT源性Klotho对磷酸盐稳态和可能的循环sKlotho水平至关重要。这一新框架解决了Klotho生物学中长期存在的不确定性，并为未来针对慢性肾脏病和矿物质骨骼疾病中Klotho的治疗策略提供了科学坚实的基础。
