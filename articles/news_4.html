Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Plato Terentev from Pexels Ever heard of the winter blues? The phrase describes that blah, low-energy feeling that can settle in as days grow shorter, colder and grayer this time of year. For some people, though, it's more than a passing slump. As many as 10% to 20% of Americans may notice a mild dip in mood during the darker months, according to the Cleveland Clinic. But when that dip runs deep enough to disrupt daily life, it may be a form of depression called seasonal affective disorder. What is seasonal affective disorder? Seasonal affective disorder, or SAD, is depression that follows a seasonal pattern. It may be at play if you've noticed "significant changes in your mood and behavior when the seasons change," according to the U.S. National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH). Symptoms tend to return around the same time each year and can linger for four to five months. Despite its reputation, SAD isn't limited to winter. There are two types: Winter-pattern SAD is the more common form. It usually begins in late fall or early winter and lifts once spring arrives. Summer-pattern SAD flips that timing. It starts in late spring or early summer and eases by fall. SAD is also different from the "holiday blues." Family gatherings, travel and year-end deadlines can weigh on anyone, but SAD is driven by shifts in daylight rather than dates on the calendar, NIMH says. What causes SAD? Researchers haven't pinned down an exact cause, but several factors seem to work together. Genetics, brain chemistry and a body clock that's fallen out of sync could all play a part, Kelly Rohan, a psychologist at the University of Vermont, told NIH News in Health. Much of the research so far has focused on two brain chemicals: Serotonin helps regulate mood. People with SAD, especially the winter type, tend to have lower serotonin activity, and fewer daylight hours may push it down further. Melatonin helps control the sleep-wake cycle. In winter-pattern SAD, the body may make too much, leaving people groggy and prone to oversleeping. Summer-pattern SAD may involve too little, with long, hot days disrupting sleep, though that idea hasn't been well tested. "In people with SAD, changes in serotonin and melatonin disrupt normal daily rhythms," according to NIMH. That makes it hard for the body to keep up as daylight hours change, which can throw off sleep, mood and behavior. Signs and symptoms to watch for SAD shares many symptoms with other forms of depression. These can include: Feeling sad, empty or hopeless most of the day Losing interest in hobbies and activities you usually enjoy Low energy or feeling worn down Trouble focusing, remembering things or making decisions Feeling irritable, anxious, guilty or worthless Changes in sleep, appetite or weight Aches, pains or stomach problems with no clear physical cause Not everyone will have all of these. But if symptoms stick around for two weeks or more, it's worth talking with a health care provider, according to NIH News in Health. There's no blood test or scan for SAD. Instead, a provider will look for depressive episodes that follow the same season for at least two years in a row. They may also check things like thyroid function to rule out other causes, the Cleveland Clinic notes. How to treat seasonal depression SAD is treatable, and many people do best with a combination of approaches. Light therapy A bright light box helps make up for the sunlight you lose in darker months. It's been a mainstay for winter-pattern SAD since the 1980s, NIMH says, and it's thought to affect brain chemicals linked to mood and sleep. "Light therapy is one of our effective treatments that is actually really easily tolerated," Craig Sawchuk, a Mayo Clinic psychologist, told the Mayo Clinic News Network. He recommends using a 10,000-lux light box or lamp for about 20 minutes, within an hour of waking up. "That tends to be about the sweet spot of exposure to that light," he said. Sawchuk suggests sticking with it into the spring, or until your mood starts lifting on its own. Talk therapy Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) helps people recognize and reshape unhelpful thoughts and habits. A version tailored to SAD, called CBT-SAD, usually involves two group sessions a week for six weeks, NIMH says. It targets gloomy beliefs about the season (dreading winter's dark afternoons, for example) and helps people schedule enjoyable activities to counter the urge to hunker down. In head-to-head comparisons, CBT and light therapy eased symptoms about equally, though light therapy worked a bit faster on some of them. Antidepressant medications Antidepressants can be used alone or alongside therapy. Because SAD is linked to serotonin, providers often turn to selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) such as sertraline (Zoloft), fluoxetine (Prozac) and escitalopram (Lexapro). The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has also approved an extended-release form of bupropion. Taken daily from fall through early spring, it can help keep seasonal depression from returning, NIMH says. Everyday habits that can help Daily routines can make a difference, too. Experts at the Cleveland Clinic and NIH suggest you: Spend time outdoors each day, even when it's overcast, and open blinds to let in more light. Aim for 30 minutes of exercise at least three times a week. Stay connected with friends, family and hobbies, even when you'd rather stay home. Reach for nourishing meals instead of the sugary, starchy foods SAD can make you crave. Go easy on alcohol, which can worsen symptoms and interact with antidepressants. If SAD tends to hit at the same time each year, starting treatment early may help head off an episode. For example, someone whose symptoms usually start in October might begin light therapy in September. "Talk to your doctor," Rohan said. "The right treatment or combination of treatments could improve your quality of life in the affected season." © 2026 HealthDay. All rights reserved.

症状往往每年在同一时间出现，并可能持续四到五个月。 尽管SAD名声在外，但它并不局限于冬季。它有两种类型： 冬季型SAD是更常见的形式。它通常在晚秋或初冬开始，并在春季到来时缓解。 夏季型SAD则与此时间颠倒。它在晚春或初夏开始，并在秋季到来时减轻。 SAD也不同于“假日忧郁”。家庭聚会、旅行和年终截止日期可能会给任何人带来压力，但NIMH表示，SAD是由日照变化而非日历上的日期驱动的。 SAD的病因是什么？ 研究人员尚未确定确切的病因，但有几个因素似乎共同作用。佛蒙特大学心理学家凯利·罗汉（Kelly Rohan）在接受美国国立卫生研究院健康新闻采访时表示，遗传、大脑化学和不同步的生物钟都可能发挥作用。到目前为止，大部分研究都集中在两种大脑化学物质上： 血清素有助于调节情绪。患有SAD的人，尤其是冬季型SAD患者，血清素活性往往较低，而日照时间减少可能会使其进一步下降。 褪黑素有助于控制睡眠-觉醒周期。在冬季型SAD中，身体可能会产生过多的褪黑素，导致人们昏昏沉沉并容易过度睡眠。夏季型SAD可能涉及褪黑素过少，漫长炎热的白天会扰乱睡眠，尽管这个观点尚未得到充分验证。 “在SAD患者中，血清素和褪黑素的变化会扰乱正常的日常节律，”NIMH表示。这使得身体难以适应日照时间的变化，从而可能扰乱睡眠、情绪和行为。 需要注意的体征和症状 SAD与许多其他形式的抑郁症有共同的症状。这些可能包括： 一天中的大部分时间感到悲伤、空虚或绝望 对通常喜欢的爱好和活动失去兴趣 精力低下或感到疲惫不堪 注意力不集中、记忆力下降或难以做决定 感到易怒、焦虑、内疚或无价值 睡眠、食欲或体重发生变化 没有明确身体原因的疼痛、不适或胃部问题 并非所有人都会出现所有这些症状。但根据美国国立卫生研究院健康新闻的说法，如果症状持续两周或更长时间，就值得与医疗保健提供者交谈。没有针对SAD的血液检测或扫描。相反，提供者会寻找连续至少两年在同一季节出现的抑郁发作。克利夫兰诊所指出，他们还可能检查甲状腺功能等，以排除其他原因。 如何治疗季节性抑郁症 SAD是可治疗的，许多人通过综合方法效果最佳。 光疗 明亮的光疗箱有助于弥补在较暗月份失去的阳光。NIMH表示，自20世纪80年代以来，它一直是冬季型SAD的主要治疗方法，被认为会影响与情绪和睡眠相关的大脑化学物质。 “光疗是我们有效的治疗方法之一，实际上很容易耐受，”梅奥诊所心理学家克雷格·索丘克（Craig Sawchuk）告诉梅奥诊所新闻网。 他建议使用10,000勒克斯的光疗箱或灯，在醒来后一小时内使用约20分钟。 “这往往是光照的最佳时间，”他说。索丘克建议坚持到春天，或者直到你的情绪自行好转。 谈话疗法 认知行为疗法（CBT）帮助人们识别和重塑无益的想法和习惯。NIMH表示，一种为SAD量身定制的版本，称为CBT-SAD，通常包括每周两次、持续六周的团体治疗。 它针对关于季节的消极信念（例如，对冬季黑暗的下午感到恐惧），并帮助人们安排愉快的活动，以对抗想要躲起来的冲动。 在头对头比较中，CBT和光疗缓解症状的效果大致相同，尽管光疗在某些症状上起效稍快。 抗抑郁药物 抗抑郁药可以单独使用，也可以与治疗结合使用。由于SAD与血清素有关，提供者通常会使用选择性血清素再摄取抑制剂（SSRIs），如舍曲林（Zoloft）、氟西汀（Prozac）和艾司西酞普兰（Lexapro）。 美国食品药品监督管理局还批准了一种缓释形式的安非他酮（bupropion）。NIMH表示，从秋季到初春每天服用，它可以帮助防止季节性抑郁症复发。 有帮助的日常习惯 日常习惯也能有所帮助。克利夫兰诊所和美国国立卫生研究院的专家建议： 每天花时间在户外，即使是阴天，并拉开百叶窗让更多光线进入。 每周至少运动三次，每次30分钟。 保持与朋友、家人和爱好的联系，即使你宁愿待在家里。 选择有营养的食物，而不是SAD可能让你渴望的含糖、淀粉类食物。 适度饮酒，因为酒精会加重症状并与抗抑郁药相互作用。 如果SAD倾向于每年在同一时间发作，尽早开始治疗可能有助于避免发作。例如，症状通常在10月开始的人，可以在9月开始光疗。“与你的医生交谈，”罗汉说。“正确的治疗或治疗组合可以改善你在受影响季节的生活质量。” © 2026 HealthDay. 版权所有。
