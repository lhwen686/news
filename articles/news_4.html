深度解析

本研究深入揭示了非医疗政策（如移民执法行动）如何通过社会决定因素直接影响特定脆弱人群的医疗服务利用和整体人群健康。核心专业概念在于，恐惧和不信任感作为重要的心理社会因素，显著抑制了讲西班牙语的移民寻求急诊、住院和门诊等基础医疗服务。这种“规避行为”不仅导致个人健康状况恶化，还可能因延误诊断和治疗而增加社区内传染病传播风险或慢性病管理不善，从而对整个区域的公共卫生系统构成挑战。 尽管远程医疗在一定程度上弥补了面对面就诊的减少，但其有限的补偿作用凸显了其在危机时期提供全面医疗照护的局限性，尤其对于需要即时或物理检查的医疗需求。对医疗行业而言，这强调了卫生系统在应对外部社会冲击时，必须提升医疗服务的文化敏感性、可及性和公平性。政策制定者和临床医生需要认识到社会政策对健康结果的深远影响，并探索创新策略，例如建立社区信任、加强医疗服务外展、优化远程医疗平台功能，以确保即使在不利社会环境下，所有个体都能获得必要的、连续的医疗照护，从而维护整体人群健康。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain From December 2025 through February 2026, about 2,000 US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents descended on the Minneapolis-Saint Paul metro area, which increased raids, arrests, and violence as part of Operation Metro Surge (OMS). Many immigrants felt afraid to leave their homes, which led to fewer people seeking inpatient, outpatient, and emergency medical care, notes a paper published last week in JAMA Network Open. "Avoiding necessary medical care harms population health," wrote the authors, led by researchers at Hennepin Health care Research Institute in Minneapolis. "Our results can be used by clinicians, health systems, and policymakers as they respond to immigration enforcement actions." Telehealth enabled doctors to still help patients To understand how OMS affected how people interacted with the health care system, the scientists compared the number of patients' health care visits before and after the influx of ICE. Prior to the operation, there were more than 1.3 million health care interactions for adults who spoke Spanish, with the vast majority occurring in an outpatient setting (90.1%). There were more than 2 million visits for English-speaking Hispanic people and more than 62 million visits for non-Hispanic white adults. During increased ICE activity, people who spoke Spanish sought out health care less often, with drops in inpatient and outpatient appointments. Operation Metro Surge contributed to 3,071 fewer visits to the emergency department (ED), 459 fewer inpatient stays, and 29,705 fewer outpatient appointments. Telehealth meetings increased by 717 visits. "Lower-than-expected rates of ED, inpatient, and outpatient visits were more pronounced in areas or periods with higher OMS activity," the authors said. "Greater telehealth use minimally offset fewer in-person encounters." Publication details Maria Masotti et al., Health Care Utilization by Patients With Spanish-Language Preference in Minnesota During Operation Metro Surge, JAMA Network Open (2026).
