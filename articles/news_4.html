Using AI to accelerate neuroscience research

深度解析

该文章深刻阐释了人工智能（AI）与神经科学之间互促共生的关系。核心专业概念在于AI作为强大的分析与模拟工具，能够处理神经科学领域中庞大且复杂的生物数据，如大脑中约860亿神经细胞形成的数万亿连接，从而有效揭示大脑功能机制的深层模式，并模拟复杂的神经过程。这极大地提升了我们对大脑结构与功能的理解。 同时，神经科学对大脑学习、记忆等机制的深入洞察，反过来为AI领域的人工神经网络和计算模型改进提供了关键灵感，例如从果蝇研究中发现的记忆形成原理直接有助于优化AI算法。 其行业影响深远：AI工具的普及，尤其是开源工具，极大地降低了神经科学家分析复杂基因组数据的门槛，加速了研究发现。此外，AI正推动神经科学数据标准化和共享，打破数据孤岛，提高研究协作效率。这种融合将为神经系统疾病的诊断、治疗及预防带来革命性突破，通过创建更精准的预测模型，最终对人类健康产生深远影响。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Google DeepMind from Pexels Integrating artificial intelligence (AI) into neuroscience is changing how scientists engage with data, improving our understanding of the brain and leading to better ways of training computational models. The findings will be presented at Neuroscience 2026, the annual meeting of the Society for Neuroscience. The human brain contains approximately 86 billion nerve cells that form trillions of connections. To better understand this dynamic and complex system, neuroscientists are turning to AI. AI tools provide the analytical power to process and uncover patterns in vast amounts of data, as well as simulate brain processes. At the same time, insights into the workings of the brain provide inspiration for improving computational models. AI applications in neuroscience show transformative potential to advance research and impact people's lives. New findings show that: Studying artificial neural networks and fruit flies revealed a shared learning principle for forming flexible, robust memories (Guanglong Sun, Tsinghua University) A new free, open-source AI tool lets neuroscientists with no programming experience analyze complex genomic data by asking questions in plain English (Vivek Swarup, University of California, Irvine) By collaboratively developing data standards, the neuroscience community is breaking down data silos, streamlining workflows, and shaping the future of brain data sharing (Oliver Ruebel, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory) "The bidirectional connections between neuroscience and AI go back a long time," says event moderator Satrajit Ghosh, Ph.D., a computational neuroscientist at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology who uses neuroimaging and machine learning to create predictive models for human health. "These findings show how AI can help us understand the relationships between brain structure and function, as well as appreciate the complex connections between genes, environments, learning, and behavior across species."
