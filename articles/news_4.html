在显微镜载玻片大小的芯片上再造肠道复杂结构

A gut's complex architecture, recreated on a microscope slide–sized chip

深度解析

这项由劳伦斯利弗莫尔国家实验室（LLNL）开发的“肠道芯片”技术，是器官芯片领域的重大突破。其核心原理在于利用立体光刻3D打印软明胶甲基丙烯酸酯（GelMA）基生物树脂，并结合酶介导的蛋白质交联，成功复刻了小肠高度复杂的三维隐窝-绒毛结构。这种精密的生物制造技术，得以在微流控芯片上构建出具备生理相关功能的肠道组织，显著提高了细胞健康并促进了肠道样行为，克服了传统器官芯片简化系统和细胞培养耗时的问题。 该技术的行业影响深远。首先，它为药物发现和疾病建模提供了前所未有的平台，能够更精准地筛选药物候选物，并深入理解炎症性肠病等复杂胃肠道疾病的发病机制。其次，对于微生物组研究而言，这种芯片模型能够实时、动态地追踪宿主-微生物之间的复杂相互作用，是传统体内研究难以实现的，有望加速新型益生菌及微生物疗法的开发。此外，该技术有望大幅减少动物实验依赖，提升研究的伦理性和效率。其模块化设计预示着未来可构建多器官集成芯片，为系统性疾病研究开辟新途径。

Credit: Biofabrication (2026). DOI: 10.1088/1758-5090/ae8ccc Gut instinct, gut feeling or gutting it out—what exactly is the human gut? It's the workhorse of the digestive system and home to a vast, dynamic community of microorganisms that influence human health. In fact, the human body contains roughly equal numbers of nonhuman (microbial) and human cells. Now, we are poised to learn much more about the gut and its pivotal role, thanks to an advance by a team of researchers at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL). They replicated the gut in three dimensions, with all its spectacular architecture and activity, on a fluidic chip the size of a microscope slide. The results are described in a paper, "Development of a gut-on-a-chip microfluidic device with three-dimensionally printed human intestinal tissue for studying human–microbe interactions," published recently in Biofabrication. "The gut is an incredibly complex system, home to trillions of microorganisms, so it's very difficult to study directly," said Lindy Jang, the paper's lead author. "That's why we developed a simpler research device that mimics the human gut as closely as possible." LLNL researchers replicated the complex architecture of the small intestine, as seen on the computer monitor, for a novel gut-on-a-chip microfluidic device. Shown here are Lindy Jang, lead author on a publication about the work, and Michael Triplett, the chip designer. Credit: Blaise Douros/LLNL Building a truer gut model Organ-on-a-chip devices are novel in vitro microscale biomimetic platforms that reproduce the physiological environment of human organisms. They're not new. They've been around for more than 15 years, accelerating drug discovery and disease modeling and reducing the need for animal testing. But they are often highly simplified systems lacking the three-dimensional complexity of the human system. In this case, gut refers to the small intestine: the workhorse of the human digestive system in a long, narrow tube measuring 8 or 9 meters (26 or 30 feet) with a 2-centimeter (0.8-inch) diameter. It packs a whopping 250 square meters (2,700 square feet) of surface area, about the size of a doubles tennis court, made possible by its unusual architecture. "The gut has thousands of finger-like protrusions extending from its wall to maximize the absorptive surface area and cavities that regulate cell behavior," said research engineer Rick Hynes, who led the bioengineering thrust area of the project. "Replicating this architecture in three dimensions on a chip has been a challenge. Growing these structures from cells, which is how it's done for other organs, is time-consuming with a high failure rate." Printing the intestinal architecture The research team used stereolithographic 3D printing of soft gelatin methacrylate (GelMA)-based bioresins and employed enzyme (transglutaminase)-mediated crosslinking of proteins onto the scaffold surface, significantly improving cell health and promoting gut-like behavior. "We engineered a wildly complex biological system to behave like the gut in a reproducible laboratory environment," said Nicholas Be, the project principal investigator. "Recreating the environment of these cells was a substantial challenge. This is exactly the kind of problem where the Lab excels, bringing together different disciplines and expertise to accomplish something that would be difficult elsewhere." They printed layers of what became the human gut architecture and then seeded cells onto that structure. Unlike other organ-on-a-chip designs, this chip has a removable gasket that houses the human tissue and simultaneously seals the two sides while still allowing chemical communication. This three-part design gives more flexibility in building the tissues and conducting experiments while potentially enabling the chip to be adapted for other organ systems or even combined organ systems. "With this approach we can cultivate the cells separately off-chip until they are acting like gut cells," said Hynes. "And we can do this in a simple culture system in an incubator, rather than in extensive laboratory equipment." Tracking interactions as they unfold The LLNL chip also allows frequent and extensive temporal sampling during an experiment, something that is not possible with in vivo models, such as mice or human patients. "Usually, you get one set of results at the end of an experiment," said Hynes. "It's kind of like attempting to study the Amazon by only looking at the river delta once a day. You'll never get a complete picture. This pulls that veil back on those intricate events that happen upstream." The project also included generating and curating datasets that enable predictions about microbiome health and developing machine learning models to perform those predictions. "It's exciting to think about the questions we can now ask and test," Be said. "We can now poke at it in a lot of different ways and watch the results as they unfold." In addition to Jang, Be and Hynes, other authors are Claire Robertson, Michael Triplett, Aubree Hinckley, Monica Moya, Car Reen Kok, Mariam Mohagheghi, Tracy Weisenberger, Feliza Bourguet, Erica Bowers, Michael Morrison, Yaqing Wang, Aleksandr Noy and Ju An Park. 直觉、预感或竭尽全力——到底什么是人体肠道？它是消化系统的主力，也是一个庞大而活跃的微生物群落的家园，影响着人类健康。事实上，人体内非人类（微生物）细胞和人类细胞的数量大致相等。 如今，在劳伦斯利弗莫尔国家实验室（LLNL）研究团队的一项进展的帮助下，我们有望对肠道及其关键作用有更深入的了解。他们在一个载玻片大小的流体芯片上，以其所有壮观的结构和功能，三维复刻了肠道。 相关成果发表在近期《生物制造》杂志上的一篇论文中，题为《开发一种用于研究人-微生物相互作用的三维打印人体肠道组织肠道芯片微流控装置》。 “肠道是一个极其复杂的系统，栖息着数万亿微生物，因此很难直接研究，”该论文的第一作者林迪·张（Lindy Jang）说，“这就是我们开发一个能尽可能逼真模拟人体肠道的更简单研究装置的原因。” LLNL研究人员在电脑显示器上展示了小肠的复杂结构，将其复刻到一种新型肠道芯片微流控装置上。图示为该工作论文的第一作者林迪·张（Lindy Jang）和芯片设计师迈克尔·特里普利特（Michael Triplett）。图片来源：Blaise Douros/LLNL 构建更真实的肠道模型 器官芯片装置是新型体外微尺度仿生平台，能够重现人类机体的生理环境。它们并非新事物，已存在超过15年，在加速药物发现、疾病建模并减少动物实验需求方面发挥了作用。但它们通常是高度简化的系统，缺乏人体系统的三维复杂性。 在本例中，肠道指的是小肠：它是人体消化系统的主力，一个长8或9米（26或30英尺）、直径2厘米（0.8英寸）的细长管状结构。凭借其不寻常的结构，它拥有惊人的250平方米（2,700平方英尺）的表面积，大约相当于一个双打网球场的大小。 “肠道有成千上万的指状突起从其管壁延伸出来，以最大限度地增加吸收表面积，还有调节细胞行为的腔体，”领导该项目生物工程重点领域的研究工程师里克·海恩斯（Rick Hynes）说，“在芯片上三维复制这种结构一直是一个挑战。从细胞中培养这些结构——这是其他器官的常见做法——既耗时又失败率高。” 打印肠道结构 研究团队使用了软明胶甲基丙烯酸酯（GelMA）基生物树脂的立体光刻3D打印技术，并采用酶（转谷氨酰胺酶）介导的蛋白质交联到支架表面，显著改善了细胞健康并促进了肠道样行为。 “我们在可重复的实验室环境中，工程化了一个极其复杂的生物系统，使其表现出肠道的功能，”项目首席研究员尼古拉斯·比（Nicholas Be）说，“重建这些细胞的环境是一个巨大的挑战。这正是实验室擅长解决的问题，它汇集了不同学科和专业知识，以完成在其他地方难以实现的目标。” 他们打印了构成人体肠道结构的多层组织，然后将细胞接种到该结构上。与其他器官芯片设计不同，该芯片具有一个可拆卸的垫圈，用于容纳人体组织，同时密封两侧，仍允许化学物质交流。这种三部分设计在构建组织和进行实验方面提供了更大的灵活性，同时有可能使该芯片适用于其他器官系统甚至组合器官系统。 “通过这种方法，我们可以在芯片外单独培养细胞，直到它们表现出肠道细胞的特性，”海恩斯说，“而且我们可以在孵化器中的简单培养系统中完成这项工作，而不是需要大量实验室设备。” 实时追踪相互作用 LLNL的芯片还允许在实验过程中进行频繁而广泛的时间采样，这是小鼠或人类患者等体内模型无法实现的。 “通常，你只能在实验结束时得到一组结果，”海恩斯说，“这就像试图通过每天只看一次亚马逊河三角洲来研究它一样，你永远无法获得完整图景。这项技术揭开了上游发生的那些复杂事件的面纱。” 该项目还包括生成和整理能够预测微生物组健康的数据集，并开发机器学习模型来执行这些预测。 “能够思考我们现在可以提出和测试的问题，这令人兴奋，”比说，“我们现在可以通过多种不同方式进行探究，并实时观察结果的演变。”
