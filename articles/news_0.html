深度解析

BitPet研究展示了游戏化（Gamification）在健康促进领域的巨大潜力。其核心专业概念在于，通过将虚拟宠物养成、奖励系统（如步数积分、游戏进程）和社交互动等游戏机制融入日常活动，有效地激发了用户的内在动机（Intrinsic Motivation）和责任感，从而显著提升了现实世界中的身体活动水平。这种策略是行为改变理论（Behavioral Change Theory）在数字健康领域的实践应用，特别是利用了自我决定理论（Self-Determination Theory）中对自主性、能力感和归属感的需求，以及操作性条件反射（Operant Conditioning）中的正强化作用。 在医学和公共卫生领域，久坐不动的生活方式是导致心血管疾病、2型糖尿病和肥胖症等多种慢性非传染性疾病（Non-communicable Diseases, NCDs）的重要风险因素。BitPet这类运动游戏（Exergames）作为一种创新的数字干预措施，为大规模、低成本地促进人群健康提供了可行方案。它通过智能手机内置计步器等技术实现生理数据（如步数）与游戏进度的实时联动，有效将数字世界与物理健康行为相桥接。若能进一步在临床试验中验证其长期依从性和健康效益，此类技术有望成为未来疾病预防和健康管理的重要辅助工具，对全民健身和公共卫生政策产生深远影响。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: According to a new study, BitPet players were, on average, more than 20% more physically active on the days they played the game. Credit: Norwegian University of Science and Technology According to a master's thesis at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU), a mobile game can make physical activity more enjoyable. Two master's students investigated how the mobile game "BitPet" affects physical activity, motivation and social well-being. The three-week study involved 51 participants and combined questionnaires, interviews, step counts and activity data collected through the game itself. Encouraging people to move a little more "One of the clearest findings was that the virtual pet became an important source of motivation for many participants. The sense of responsibility for the pet, combined with step points, progression, in-game resources and photo competitions, encouraged many people to be a little more physically active," says Alf Inge Wang, one of the game's founders. Wang is also a professor at NTNU's Department of Computer Science and served as supervisor for the master's students. "Many participants said they chose to walk the long way home, walked to the shop instead of driving, or went for an extra walk simply to help their pet evolve," he explains. On average, players were more than 20% more physically active on the days they played the game. The study also found considerable variation in how motivating participants found the game, and that it had only a limited impact on levels of social activity. Participants ranged in age from 20 to 52, with an average age of 26. Most were between 24 and 26, and the group consisted of 65% women and 35% men. Although BitPet is designed to appeal to a broad audience, user testing indicates that the most engaged players are women ages 16 to 40. The same has been found true of similar virtual pet simulators. The game draws inspiration from classics such as Tamagotchi, Pokémon Snap, Animal Crossing, The Sims Pets and similar titles. Mathias Grønstad presenting the game at the Norwegian Gaming Convention. Credit: Spillkonventet 2024 The player's activity shapes the pet's fitness BitPet players care for digital pets that grow and develop in step with the user's real-world physical activity. The pet's fitness depends on how active the player is, which the game measures using the smartphone's built-in step counter. Players collect resources and encounter other pets on a map. Future versions of BitPet will also introduce location-based social gameplay designed to encourage face-to-face meetings and interaction between players. The game combines elements of digital pets, location-based gaming and augmented reality. The study's results are based on Android users. Due to a technical issue during the study, data from iOS users was not recorded correctly and was therefore excluded from the analysis. NTNU research helped shape the game "BitPet is built on several years of research into game design, motivation, physical activity and exergames," says Wang. Researchers at NTNU have contributed knowledge about how games can motivate people to become more physically active, and these insights have been applied directly to the game's mechanics. Theories of intrinsic motivation, reward systems, progression and player engagement underpin features such as step points, exploration, resource collection and the development of the virtual pet. Wang and Mathias Grønstad's new master's thesis forms part of this wider research collaboration and provides valuable insights into which game mechanics are most effective in practice. BitPet AS, led by Grønstad, has received NOK 5 million from the Research Council of Norway to validate its business model through further research and development. The company has now developed a playable beta version of the game, which has been launched in Norway on both the App Store and Google Play. Key medical concepts Activities, Physical
