绿色麻醉使手术室排放减半，麻醉成本削减近40%

Greener anesthesia cuts operating room emissions in half, slashes anesthetic costs nearly 40%

深度解析

本文深入阐述了通过优化麻醉实践实现医疗系统可持续发展的核心理念——“绿色麻醉”。其医学机制主要在于识别并替代具有高全球变暖潜能（GWP）的吸入性麻醉剂，如地氟醚和氧化亚氮。这些气体在临床应用后逸散到大气中，作为强效温室气体显著加剧了气候变化和手术室的碳足迹。研究推广使用七氟烷和静脉麻醉，因七氟烷的GWP远低于地氟醚，而静脉麻醉药物（如丙泊酚）几乎不向大气排放温室气体，从而从根本上降低了环境影响。技术原理则体现在通过降低新鲜气体流量以减少药物浪费，并利用数据反馈机制引导临床医生进行更为环保的药物选择。 这一变革对医疗行业具有深远影响。它不仅为医疗系统带来了显著的经济效益，通过减少昂贵的高排放麻醉药物使用节约了近40%的成本，更重要的是，它在不损害患者安全和护理质量的前提下实现了环境效益，有效改善了患者结局，例如未增加恢复室停留时间或术后并发症。这为其他医疗机构推广可持续麻醉实践提供了强有力的循证支持和商业逻辑。此外，通过培养医护人员的环保意识和数据驱动的决策能力，预示着未来医疗行业将更加注重环境责任，推动麻醉学领域向更可持续、更智能的方向发展。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: CC0 Public Domain Hospitals can sharply reduce the climate impact and cost of surgery without compromising patient safety by changing routine anesthesia practices, including reducing the use of high-emission anesthetic gases, suggests a new study presented at the ANESTHESIOLOGY 2026 annual meeting. Health care is a major contributor to greenhouse gas emissions, and operating rooms are among the most resource-intensive areas of hospitals. Some inhaled anesthetic gases, particularly desflurane and nitrous oxide, are potent greenhouse gases that trap heat in the atmosphere and may contribute to climate change and the carbon footprint of operating rooms. "Our findings show that hospitals can take meaningful steps to reduce the environmental impact of surgery without compromising safety or quality," said Etta Hanlon, M.S., lead study author and a researcher at Virginia Tech Carilion School of Medicine, Roanoke. "By making practical changes in anesthetic selection, reducing waste and giving anesthesiologists clear feedback, we reduced emissions and costs while maintaining strong patient outcomes." The findings suggest that greener anesthesia does not require costly technology. The six-site academic health system encouraged greater use of sevoflurane and intravenous anesthesia, reduced fresh gas flow rates to minimize waste and provided anesthesiologists with feedback on their emissions. Neil Macdonald, M.D., chair of the Department of Anesthesiology at Carilion Clinic and associate professor at the Virginia Tech Carilion School of Medicine, Roanoke, helped establish the clinician feedback effort, including individualized emissions reports to support informed clinical decision-making. Researchers compared anesthetic use, estimated emissions, costs and patient outcomes in 2021 with results from January 2022 through October 2025, after the health system began implementing the changes. The study included 145,917 anesthetic episodes. Average total emissions dropped from 57.6 kilograms of carbon dioxide equivalent (kgCO2e), a standard measure of climate impact, per anesthetic episode in 2021 to 31.6 kilograms after the intervention, a 45% reduction. Desflurane use also fell sharply, from 23.8% to 9.81% of cases, while sevoflurane use increased from 82.4% to 93.1%. By late 2025, the health system surpassed its goal of reducing emissions by 50%. Spending on inhaled anesthetics fell from $628,588 in 2021 to $379,010 in 2024, savings of nearly 40%. The researchers found patients continued to recover from surgery safely, with no increase in recovery room stays, hospital stays, breathing tube reinsertion, awareness or postoperative nausea and vomiting after the changes were implemented. "Patients shouldn't have to choose between environmentally responsible health care and safe, high-quality care," said Sara Wohlford, director of sustainability at Carilion Clinic. "These are changes health systems and clinicians can make behind the scenes, allowing patients to benefit from more sustainable care without having to do anything differently."
