鼻腔免疫佐剂可使小鼠免受呼吸道病毒侵害长达数月

Nasal immune adjuvant protects mice against respiratory viruses for months

深度解析

本研究深入探讨了利用固有免疫应对未知呼吸道病毒威胁的潜力，突破了传统疫苗特异性防护的局限。文章核心概念包括“训练免疫”和“疫苗佐剂K3-SPG”的医学机制。训练免疫是指固有免疫细胞（如巨噬细胞和固有淋巴细胞）在受到特定刺激后，通过表观遗传学改变（如染色质重塑）使其功能增强，从而对后续感染产生更强、更持久的非特异性保护。K3-SPG作为一种纳米佐剂，通过激活免疫传感器9型Toll样受体（TLR9），并依赖TNF-α炎症信号通路，诱导了这种训练免疫效应。其独特之处在于，它通过增强宿主的“感染耐受性”而非直接杀灭病毒来发挥保护作用，减轻了组织损伤。 这项研究对公共卫生和制药行业具有深远影响。它提出了一种创新的“佐剂介导的预防策略”，能在特定病原体出现时提供广谱的临时保护，有效填补特异性疫苗研发上市前的“免疫空白期”，显著增强全球大流行防范能力，例如对“100天任务”等倡议形成有力补充。这不仅拓宽了疫苗佐剂的应用范畴，从单纯增强疫苗免疫原性到直接诱导宿主保护，也为未来应对新型未知病原体提供了新的思路和潜在工具，尤其在快速响应和早期干预方面展现出巨大潜力。

英文原文

Original
The COVID-19 pandemic showed the challenges of protecting populations when a new respiratory virus emerges. Although vaccines were developed at unprecedented speed, there was still a period when vaccines were not yet available, and people remained vulnerable to infection. This raises an important question: Could the body's natural defenses be temporarily strengthened without knowing which virus will emerge? One possible answer lies in innate immunity, the body's first line of defense. Unlike adaptive immunity, which learns to recognize specific targets, innate immunity can respond to threats without prior exposure. Some studies have shown that certain stimuli can leave innate immune cells more responsive through a phenomenon called trained immunity. However, questions remain about how long this protection can last and which cells are responsible for maintaining it in the respiratory tract. To bridge these knowledge gaps, a research team led by Professor Ken Ishii from the Division of Vaccine Science, Department of Microbiology and Immunology at The Institute of Medical Science, The University of Tokyo, Japan, investigated how the vaccine adjuvant K3-SPG modulates innate immunity. The study was co-authored by PhD candidate Asuka Joy Tobuse of The University of Tokyo and Associate Project Scientist Kouji Kobiyama of the University of California San Diego, USA, formerly a member of Prof. Ishii's laboratory at The University of Tokyo. Their study will be published in Science Advances on October 9, 2026. K3-SPG is a nanosized adjuvant that combines a short piece of synthetic DNA called CpG oligodeoxynucleotide, which activates the immune sensor toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9), with a β-glucan called schizophyllan. The researchers first tested whether a single dose given through the nose could protect mice against influenza A virus. Treated mice lost less weight and had higher survival than control mice. Protection was still detectable 100 days after treatment, although it waned over time. The treatment also protected mice against SARS-CoV-2 in a susceptible mouse model. The route of administration was important. Nasal treatment produced stronger protection than treatment given under the skin or into the bloodstream. Despite improving survival and reducing lung damage, however, K3-SPG did not substantially reduce the amount of virus in the lungs. The findings suggest that the treatment mainly helped the host to tolerate the infection and limit tissue damage rather than directly preventing viral replication. The team then investigated how this protection developed. Using several analytical approaches, including single-cell RNA sequencing, they found evidence of a two-stage response. Macrophages were important during the early phase, while innate lymphoid cells became important later. Some of the innate lymphoid cells showed changes down to the chromatin level, which is the DNA–protein structure that helps regulate gene activity, suggesting longer-lasting changes in their function. The team also found that the response depended on TLR9, an immune receptor activated by K3-SPG, as well as the inflammatory signaling molecule TNF-α. "Our research has identified unique mechanisms by which vaccine adjuvants can induce protective innate immunity against respiratory viral infections, suggesting that adjuvants may have potential applications beyond their traditional role in enhancing vaccine responses," says Prof. Ishii. The researchers caution that these findings were demonstrated primarily in mice and do not establish that K3-SPG can protect people from respiratory viral infections. Further studies are needed to assess its safety, effectiveness, and suitability for human use, including differences in TLR9 biology and respiratory delivery. "Because an adjuvant-based preventive approach may not require prior knowledge of the specific pathogen, it could potentially provide an additional layer of protection while pathogen-specific vaccines are being developed and manufactured," explains Prof. Ishii. Such an approach could potentially complement pandemic preparedness efforts such as the 100 Days Mission by providing temporary protection while targeted vaccines are being developed.

COVID-19大流行揭示了当新型呼吸道病毒出现时保护人群所面临的挑战。尽管疫苗以前所未有的速度被开发出来，但仍然存在一个疫苗尚未问世的时期，在此期间人们仍然容易受到感染。这引出了一个重要问题：在不知道哪种病毒将出现的情况下，能否暂时增强身体的自然防御能力？ 一个可能的答案在于固有免疫，即机体的第一道防线。与学习识别特定目标的适应性免疫不同，固有免疫无需事先暴露即可对威胁作出反应。一些研究表明，某些刺激可以通过一种称为训练免疫的现象，使固有免疫细胞的反应性更强。然而，这种保护能持续多久以及哪些细胞负责在呼吸道中维持这种保护，仍存在疑问。 为了弥补这些知识空白，由日本东京大学医科学研究所微生物学与免疫学系疫苗科学部门的石井健教授领导的一个研究团队，调查了疫苗佐剂K3-SPG如何调节固有免疫。这项研究由东京大学的博士研究生Asuka Joy Tobuse和美国加州大学圣地亚哥分校的副项目科学家Kouji Kobiyama（他曾是石井教授在东京大学实验室的成员）共同撰写。他们的研究将于2026年10月9日发表在《科学进展》杂志上。 K3-SPG是一种纳米级佐剂，它结合了一段称为CpG寡脱氧核苷酸的合成DNA短链（可激活免疫传感器9型Toll样受体，即TLR9）和一种称为裂褶多糖的β-葡聚糖。研究人员首先测试了通过鼻腔给予单剂量K3-SPG是否能保护小鼠免受甲型流感病毒的侵害。接受治疗的小鼠体重减轻更少，存活率高于对照组小鼠。治疗后100天仍可检测到保护作用，尽管其强度随时间减弱。该治疗还在易感小鼠模型中保护小鼠免受SARS-CoV-2的侵害。 给药途径至关重要。鼻腔给药比皮下注射或静脉注射产生了更强的保护作用。然而，尽管K3-SPG改善了存活率并减少了肺部损伤，但它并未显著减少肺部病毒载量。这些发现表明，该治疗主要帮助宿主耐受感染并限制组织损伤，而非直接阻止病毒复制。 研究团队随后调查了这种保护是如何形成的。通过使用包括单细胞RNA测序在内的多种分析方法，他们发现了两阶段反应的证据。巨噬细胞在早期阶段发挥重要作用，而固有淋巴细胞在后期变得重要。一些固有淋巴细胞甚至在染色质层面显示出变化，染色质是调节基因活性的DNA-蛋白质结构，这表明其功能可能发生长期改变。 研究团队还发现，这种反应依赖于K3-SPG激活的免疫受体TLR9，以及炎症信号分子TNF-α。“我们的研究揭示了疫苗佐剂诱导针对呼吸道病毒感染的保护性固有免疫的独特机制，这表明佐剂除了在增强疫苗反应的传统作用之外，可能具有更广泛的潜在应用，”石井教授说。 研究人员提醒，这些发现主要在小鼠模型中证实，并未确立K3-SPG能够保护人类免受呼吸道病毒感染。需要进一步的研究来评估其安全性、有效性和人体适用性，包括TLR9生物学和呼吸道给药方式的差异。 “由于基于佐剂的预防方法可能不需要事先了解特定病原体，因此它有可能在病原体特异性疫苗开发和生产期间提供额外的保护层，”石井教授解释道。这种方法可以为大流行病防范工作（例如“100天任务”）提供潜在补充，通过在靶向疫苗开发期间提供临时保护。
