深度解析

该研究揭示了COVID-19加强疫苗减量接种策略的医学和经济双重优势。核心医学机制在于，即使使用标准剂量一半的疫苗，也能诱导与全剂量相当的强大且持久的体液（抗体）和细胞免疫反应，长达两年。这表明，在初次免疫建立后，诱导有效加强免疫所需的抗原阈值可能低于预期。此原理允许在不显著损害免疫保护的前提下，优化疫苗用量。 其行业影响深远：首先，显著降低疫苗接生产和采购成本，特别利好医疗资源有限的低收入国家，从而促进全球疫苗接种的公平可及性。其次，通过扩大现有疫苗供应，增强全球大流行病防范能力，为未来应对潜在的新发或再发病原体（如汉坦病毒、埃博拉、猴痘等）提供宝贵策略。此外，此研究还提供了实践指导，如疫苗接种时间的优化，以及长期免疫监测的重要性，为公共卫生政策制定者提供了循证依据，以实现更高效、可持续的疾病管理策略。

英文原文

Original
Smaller doses of COVID-19 booster vaccines can provide long-lasting immunity, helping to reduce costs and improve access to vaccination, according to new research. And the findings could also inform vaccine dosing and options during future pandemics. An international research program led by Murdoch Children's Research Institute (MCRI) and involving more than 2,000 participants across Australia, Indonesia and Mongolia has provided new evidence that will help shape future COVID-19 booster strategies. The $17.6 million program, funded by the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI), evaluated different COVID-19 booster approaches through three randomized clinical trials, tracking participants for up to two years. The trials found fractional doses, where people receive half the standard vaccine amount given as boosters, produced strong immune responses that remained comparable to full-dose boosters. MCRI Dr Nadia Mazarakis said fractional dosing could help extend vaccine supplies, widen coverage and improve equity in future vaccination programs, particularly in low-income countries. This research provides valuable evidence showing that smaller vaccine doses can still provide strong, long-lasting immunity, which is encouraging for countries looking for practical and affordable ways to deliver booster programs. Fractional dosing attracted global interest during the COVID-19 pandemic when vaccine supplies were limited. While circumstances have changed, the findings remain relevant because they show there may be opportunities to use vaccines more efficiently without compromising immune responses. Importantly, these vaccine strategies will reduce costs, extend supplies and support broader and more equitable access to booster programs, especially in resource constrained countries across Southeast Asia and Africa." Dr. Nadia Mazarakis, MCRI CEPI Clinical Development Science Lead Amol Chaudari said, "These pivotal insights - produced across different countries, with different vaccines and people who had received different first COVID vaccine doses - essentially show that public health officials can get 'more bang for their buck' with COVID vaccine boosters. If COVID cases were to ramp up again and there be strain on the vaccine supply, these findings could be key to expanding vaccination coverage quickly and preventing future infections and deaths. The findings could also prove helpful for future vaccines against other coronaviruses manufactured using similar technologies." Results from the Indonesian study, published in Nature Communications, followed more than 1,200 adults for up to two years after receiving either fractional or standard doses of Pfizer or AstraZeneca vaccines, or a standard CoronaVac vaccine. The researchers found that while fractional doses produced slightly lower immune responses shortly after vaccination, those differences narrowed over time and remained comparable to full doses at 24 months. A companion study of 601 Mongolian adults, published in Frontiers in Immunology, discovered a fractional Pfizer booster produced long-lasting antibody responses similar to a full dose for two years. A further analysis of the same cohort reported that key cellular immune responses, which help protect against serious infections, also remained strong. In Australia, researchers compared mRNA and protein-based booster vaccines as a fourth full dose. Published in the Journal of Infection, the study involving 496 healthy adults found mRNA vaccines generated higher antibody responses. Both vaccine types reduced infections and produced comparable immune responses over 12 months. The study recently received a further $2 million from the National Health and Medical Research Council (NHMRC) to follow the cohort for 30 months to examine the effects of repeated mRNA COVID-19 vaccination. Additional research from the team published in Vaccine, found vaccination in the morning, rather than afternoon, improved responses to the mRNA vaccine, but not the protein-based vaccine. The findings suggest simple changes to vaccination timing may offer meaningful benefits. MCRI Associate Professor Paul Licciardi said while the acute phase of the pandemic had passed, COVID-19 remained a significant cause of respiratory illness and death globally. "Understanding how long vaccine-induced immunity lasts remains important for future vaccination planning," he said. "As countries move towards long-term management of COVID-19, we need evidence about which booster strategies provide durable immunity while making the best use of healthcare resources. "This knowledge can also help future public health strategies to combat emerging pathogens like hantavirus, Ebola and mpox." Australia spends about 10 per cent of its gross domestic product (GDP) on healthcare, compared with 4.4 per cent in Mongolia and 2.7 per cent in Indonesia, highlighting the importance of identifying vaccination strategies that maximize the impact of available resources. MCRI Associate Professor Claire von Mollendorf said, "These trial results demonstrate that shaping effective public health policy requires monitoring long-term immune responses across diverse settings with variable infection rates and different primary vaccine schedules."

AI Translation
根据最新研究，较小剂量的COVID-19加强疫苗可以提供持久免疫力，有助于降低成本并改善疫苗接种的可及性。这些发现也可能为未来大流行病期间的疫苗剂量和选择提供参考。 由默多克儿童研究所（MCRI）牵头的一项国际研究项目，涉及澳大利亚、印度尼西亚和蒙古的2,000多名参与者，提供了新的证据，将有助于制定未来的COVID-19加强接种策略。 这项耗资1760万美元的项目由流行病防范创新联盟（CEPI）资助，通过三项随机临床试验评估了不同的COVID-19加强接种方法，对参与者进行了长达两年的追踪。 试验发现，分次剂量（即作为加强针接种的疫苗量为标准剂量的一半）产生了强大的免疫反应，且与全剂量加强针的免疫反应保持相当。 MCRI的纳迪亚·马扎拉基斯博士表示，分次剂量有助于延长疫苗供应、扩大覆盖范围并提高未来疫苗接种项目的公平性，特别是在低收入国家。 “这项研究提供了有价值的证据，表明较小的疫苗剂量仍能提供强大、持久的免疫力，这对那些正在寻找实用且经济实惠的加强接种方案的国家来说是令人鼓舞的。在COVID-19大流行期间，当疫苗供应有限时，分次剂量引起了全球关注。尽管情况已经改变，但这些发现仍然具有现实意义，因为它们表明在不损害免疫反应的情况下，可能存在更有效地利用疫苗的机会。重要的是，这些疫苗策略将降低成本、延长供应，并支持更广泛和更公平的加强接种项目可及性，尤其是在东南亚和非洲等资源受限国家。”MCRI的纳迪亚·马扎拉基斯博士说。 CEPI临床开发科学负责人阿莫尔·乔杜里表示：“这些关键见解——在不同国家、使用不同疫苗、针对已接种不同初次COVID疫苗剂量的人群产生——本质上表明，公共卫生官员在COVID疫苗加强针上可以‘花更少的钱办更多的事’。如果COVID病例再次激增，并且疫苗供应出现紧张，这些发现可能成为迅速扩大疫苗接种覆盖范围并预防未来感染和死亡的关键。这些发现也可能对使用类似技术制造的针对其他冠状病毒的未来疫苗有所帮助。” 发表在《自然通讯》上的印度尼西亚研究结果追踪了1,200多名成年人长达两年，他们接种了辉瑞或阿斯利康疫苗的部分剂量或标准剂量，或标准科兴疫苗。 研究人员发现，虽然分次剂量在接种后短期内产生的免疫反应略低，但这些差异随着时间推移而缩小，并在24个月时仍与全剂量相当。 一项对601名蒙古成年人进行的同期研究，发表在《免疫学前沿》上，发现部分剂量的辉瑞加强针产生了与全剂量相似的两年持久抗体反应。对同一队列的进一步分析报告称，有助于预防严重感染的关键细胞免疫反应也保持强劲。 在澳大利亚，研究人员比较了作为第四剂全剂量疫苗的mRNA和基于蛋白的加强疫苗。发表在《感染杂志》上的这项研究涉及496名健康成年人，发现mRNA疫苗产生了更高的抗体反应。两种疫苗类型均减少了感染，并在12个月内产生了相当的免疫反应。该研究最近获得了澳大利亚国家健康与医学研究理事会（NHMRC）的额外200万美元资助，以对该队列进行30个月的追踪，考察重复接种mRNA COVID-19疫苗的效果。 该团队发表在《疫苗》杂志上的额外研究发现，上午接种而非下午接种，改善了对mRNA疫苗的反应，但对基于蛋白的疫苗没有。这些发现表明，疫苗接种时间的简单改变可能带来显著益处。 MCRI副教授保罗·利西亚尔迪表示，尽管大流行的急性阶段已经过去，但COVID-19仍然是全球呼吸道疾病和死亡的一个重要原因。 他说：“了解疫苗诱导的免疫力持续多久对于未来的疫苗接种规划仍然很重要。随着各国转向COVID-19的长期管理，我们需要关于哪些加强接种策略能提供持久免疫力，同时最佳利用医疗资源的证据。” “这些知识还可以帮助未来的公共卫生策略对抗新出现的病原体，如汉坦病毒、埃博拉病毒和猴痘病毒。” 澳大利亚将其国内生产总值（GDP）的约10%用于医疗保健，相比之下，蒙古为4.4%，印度尼西亚为2.7%，这凸显了确定能最大化现有资源影响的疫苗接种策略的重要性。 MCRI副教授克莱尔·冯·莫伦多夫表示：“这些试验结果表明，制定有效的公共卫生政策需要监测在感染率各异、初次疫苗接种方案不同的多样化环境中产生的长期免疫反应。”
