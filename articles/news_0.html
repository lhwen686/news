阿斯顿大学与Labskin合作，培养更逼真的实验室培养人体皮肤

深度解析

阿斯顿大学与Labskin的合作标志着体外人体皮肤模型研究的重大进展。此次创新的核心在于将免疫细胞，特别是巨噬细胞，整合到现有的皮肤模型中。传统皮肤模型虽能模拟物理结构，但缺乏免疫系统的动态功能。巨噬细胞作为先天免疫系统的关键组成部分，在识别和清除病原体、调节炎症反应以及促进组织修复中扮演着不可或缺的角色。通过引入这些细胞，新模型能够更真实地模拟活体皮肤在面对刺激、感染或损伤时的复杂生理病理过程，尤其对理解慢性伤口和炎症性皮肤病的发生发展机制至关重要。此举不仅有助于加速新药和疗法的筛选与开发，提高早期研究的转化率，还可显著减少对动物试验的依赖，降低后期临床试验的风险，从而节省巨大的研发成本，对制药和化妆品行业的研发流程具有深远的影响。

英文原文

Original
Aston University has joined forces with Yorkshire-based biotechnology company Labskin to develop more realistic laboratory-grown human skin that includes immune cells. Labskin specializes in producing complex skin models that closely replicate the structure of real skin. These models - which come in thumbnail-sized sections around 1cm thick - are used by academic researchers and companies to study how products and treatments interact with the skin before they are trialled in people. However, while the models accurately reproduce the skin's physical layers, they do not currently include immune cells, which play a crucial role in inflammation and wound healing. Under the Knowledge Transfer Partnership (KTP), researchers from Aston's Department of Biosciences will work with Labskin to introduce immune cells called macrophages into the company's existing skin models. This will make the models more representative of real human skin and more useful for studying conditions such as chronic wounds and inflammatory skin disorders. Chronic wounds in particular have a serious impact on people's quality of life, and their treatment costs the NHS an estimated £5-6bn each year. The enhanced skin models will support a wide range of early-stage research, including studies into skin irritation, inflammation and healing. By providing more human-relevant information earlier in the development process (such as information on how the skin responds to treatment), the project also has the potential to reduce the use of animals in research and to help de-risk later stage clinical trials of new drugs and therapies. Professor Andrew Devitt, professor of inflammatory cell biology in Aston University's Department of Biosciences, and executive dean of the School of Medicine, Pharmacy and Biosciences, is leading the academic side of the project. He said: "Our research focuses on understanding how inflammation happens and is resolved - particularly in the context of chronic wounds, which are devastating for the individual and create a huge financial burden for the NHS. Working with Labskin, we can apply our research knowledge in a highly practical way and help develop models that are far closer to what happens in real human skin. This is perhaps the most exciting project I've been involved with in my professional career." We're really excited to get started with this project. Adding immune cells to our skin models will allow us to look at how the skin responds to products and treatments in much more detail. The KTP is a great example of how private companies can work together with universities to make a real impact." Dr. Nicola Kingswell, scientific director, Labskin Knowledge Transfer Partnerships, funded by Innovate UK, are collaborative projects that bring together a business, an academic partner, and a qualified associate to deliver strategic innovation and business improvement. Aston University is a national leader in the programme, ranked joint-first in the UK for volume of active projects and first for overall project quality.
