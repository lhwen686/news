BrainBots use light to deliver drugs across the blood–brain barrier

深度解析

这篇新闻报道详细介绍了一种名为BrainBots的创新纳米机器人系统，旨在克服血脑屏障（BBB）的巨大挑战，实现药物向大脑的精准递送。血脑屏障是临床医学中的一个核心专业概念，它由大脑毛细血管内皮细胞及其紧密连接构成，有效阻止了血液中大分子、亲水性物质和大多数药物进入大脑，是神经系统疾病治疗的瓶颈。BrainBots的技术原理在于其精巧的设计：通过封装产气燃料（碳酸氢铵），利用外部近红外光激活局部热量，熔化温敏性闸门，进而触发气体膨胀产生推进力，使纳米机器人能主动靠近并接触血脑屏障细胞。更关键的是，BrainBots表面修饰的适体（aptamers）能特异性识别并结合血脑屏障细胞，促进其通过转运作用（transcytosis）跨越屏障，实现药物的主动递送。 这项技术对行业影响深远。目前，许多神经系统疾病如胶质母细胞瘤、帕金森病等缺乏有效治疗手段，很大程度上归因于药物无法有效穿越血脑屏障。BrainBots的成功应用不仅在小鼠模型中显著提升了化疗药物阿霉素在大脑中的浓度，抑制了肿瘤生长，还显著降低了全身毒性，展现了靶向递送的巨大优势。这为未来开发新型、高效、低副作用的神经系统药物提供了革命性途径，有望惠及广大的脑部疾病患者。该研究也为其他靶向药物递送（如膀胱肿瘤）提供了借鉴，预示着纳米机器人技术在精准医疗领域的广阔前景。

英文原文

Original
As the primary command center of our bodies, human brains have several layers of protection to help keep them safe from dangerous invaders. Unfortunately, some of these safeguards-in particular, the blood–brain barrier (BBB)-make it hard to deliver medications to this essential organ. Researchers led by Caltech's Wei Gao, professor of medical engineering and a Heritage Medical Research Institute Investigator, have now developed a nanorobotic system called BrainBots that can successfully cross the BBB to deliver lifesaving therapeutics for neurological disorders and other diseases, including cancer. "We showed that our BrainBots can carry medication through the bloodstream and help deliver it across the blood–brain barrier, one of the biggest challenges in treating brain diseases," Gao says. In the study, light-activated BrainBots delivered approximately five times more medication to the brain than nonactivated counterparts. Gao is the corresponding author of a paper published online November 25 in Science Robotics about the team's advancements. The BBB is a protective filter that lines the brain's blood vessels and is notoriously hard to penetrate, making the treatment of brain disorders very difficult. Many medications are delivered through intravenous (IV) injection, but the BBB prevents most drugs from entering the brain in sufficient amounts. To address this issue, Gao and the team used established biocompatible and biodegradable materials to build nanoscale "robots" that can be activated by light to self-propel, pushing them toward the cells on the BBB, so they can then be ushered into the brain. BrainBots, which look like tiny, asymmetric balloons, work like this: A chamber is loaded with ammonium bicarbonate, a gas-generating fuel, then encapsulated in a shell with a temperature-sensitive gate and attached to the medication payload. The capsules, which are approximately 250 nanometers in size, or hundreds of times smaller than the width of a human hair, are injected into the bloodstream. Near-infrared light is then directed through the skull to activate the BrainBots near the BBB. The light generates localized heat that melts the gate and triggers the production of gas, propelling the BrainBots to make contact with BBB cells. The BrainBots are equipped with specialized DNA molecules called aptamers, which help them recognize and bind to cells lining the BBB, meaning they can be taken up by these cells and transported across the barrier into the brain. Once inside, the nanobot further degrades to release medication, and the entire structure eventually disintegrates. The team tested BrainBots in mouse models of glioblastoma, the most common type of brain cancer, and showed that the bots' ability to move closer to their BBB cell targets greatly enhanced their ability to deliver doxorubicin, a powerful chemotherapy drug, to the brain. The treatment slowed tumor growth and prolonged survival in mice without detectable disruption of the BBB. It also reduced systemic toxicity compared with conventional administration of the chemotherapy drug alone. "Because the BrainBots are so small and the blood circulation is so fast, we initially were not sure how much a method based on nanoscale propulsion would increase the passage of drugs through the BBB," Gao says. "We are thrilled to see it worked so well." In recent collaborations with Mikhail Shapiro, Caltech's Max Delbrück Professor of Chemical Engineering and Medical Engineering and a Howard Hughes Medical Institute (HHMI) Investigator, Gao and his lab developed nanobots for targeted drug delivery and tested a version that could autonomously carry anti-tumor therapeutics to bladder tumors. The new study extends this approach to the brain using light-activated nanorobots to help drugs cross the BBB. "Our new nanorobot is not limited to brain delivery, but we are trying to address one of the biggest problems in medicine-crossing the blood–brain barrier-as a starting point," Gao explains. "We targeted cancer, but there are a lot of neurological diseases, such as Parkinson's, for example, that we hope could benefit from this technology." While only a fraction of the BrainBots made it into the brain, the technology represents a major step in improving the delivery of therapeutics across the BBB. Those that remained in other parts of the body were expected to degrade as they are taken up by organs such as the liver and spleen, as is typical for most nanoparticle-based drug carriers, Gao says. Next, the team plans to further improve the nanorobotic system and evaluate its potential for future clinical applications. "We're working on optimizing the near-infrared light delivery and evaluating the long-term safety, stability, and degradation of the nanorobots in the body," Gao says. "Although more research is needed before we can move toward human studies, we're excited about the potential of this technology for treating brain diseases." The Science Robotics article is titled "Photothermally gated nanorobots for active blood–brain barrier transcytosis and brain delivery." Additional Caltech authors from Gao's lab are postdoctoral scholars Jounghyun Yoo and Sukyoung Won; visiting student researcher Suhyun Kim; graduate students Hong Han (MS '23), Dong Hung Truong, and Xiaotian Ma (MS '24); Gwangmook Kim, a visitor in medical engineering; and former postdoctoral scholar Songsong Tang. A full list of co-authors can be found in the paper. The research was supported by the Heritage Medical Research Institute, the Korea Health Technology R&D Project through the Korea Health Industry Development Institute, the National Research Foundation of Korea, and the Korea Basic Science Institute.

作为我们身体的主要指挥中心，人类大脑拥有多层保护，以帮助其免受危险入侵者的侵害。不幸的是，其中一些保护屏障——特别是血脑屏障（BBB）——使得向这个重要器官输送药物变得困难。 由加州理工学院医学工程学教授兼海瑞泰奇医学研究所研究员魏高（Wei Gao）领导的研究人员，现已开发出一种名为“BrainBots”的纳米机器人系统，该系统能成功穿过血脑屏障，为神经系统疾病和包括癌症在内的其他疾病输送挽救生命的疗法。 “我们证明了我们的BrainBots可以通过血流输送药物，并帮助药物穿过血脑屏障，而这正是治疗脑部疾病的最大挑战之一。”高教授说。在这项研究中，光激活的BrainBots向大脑输送的药物量是非激活对照组的约五倍。 高教授是11月25日在线发表在《科学·机器人学》（Science Robotics）杂志上、关于该团队最新进展的论文的通讯作者。 血脑屏障是覆盖大脑血管的保护性过滤器，以其难以穿透而闻名，这使得脑部疾病的治疗非常困难。许多药物通过静脉注射（IV）输送，但血脑屏障会阻止大多数药物以足够量进入大脑。为解决这个问题，高教授及其团队利用已有的生物相容性和可生物降解材料，制造出纳米级“机器人”，这些机器人可通过光激活实现自主推进，使其朝向血脑屏障上的细胞移动，从而被引导进入大脑。 BrainBots，外观像微小的、不对称的气球，其工作原理如下：一个腔室装载有碳酸氢铵（一种产气燃料），然后被包裹在一个带有温敏性闸门的壳中，并连接到药物载荷上。这些胶囊大小约为250纳米，比人类头发的宽度小数百倍，它们被注射到血流中。随后，近红外光通过颅骨引导，在血脑屏障附近激活BrainBots。光会产生局部热量，熔化闸门并触发气体的产生，从而推动BrainBots与血脑屏障细胞接触。BrainBots配备有特殊的DNA分子，称为适体，这些适体有助于它们识别并结合血脑屏障内皮细胞，这意味着它们可以被这些细胞摄取并穿过屏障进入大脑。一旦进入大脑，纳米机器人会进一步降解以释放药物，最终整个结构会完全分解。 该团队在胶质母细胞瘤（最常见的脑癌类型）的小鼠模型中测试了BrainBots，结果显示，这些机器人靠近血脑屏障细胞靶标的能力，极大地增强了它们向大脑输送强效化疗药物阿霉素的能力。这种治疗方法在小鼠体内减缓了肿瘤生长并延长了生存期，而没有检测到血脑屏障的破坏。与单独常规施用化疗药物相比，它还降低了全身毒性。 “由于BrainBots非常小，而且血液循环非常快，我们最初不确定基于纳米级推进的方法能将多少药物增加通过血脑屏障的量。”高教授说，“我们很高兴看到它取得了如此好的效果。” 在最近与加州理工学院麦克斯·德尔布鲁克化学工程和医学工程教授、霍华德·休斯医学研究所（HHMI）研究员米哈伊尔·沙皮罗（Mikhail Shapiro）的合作中，高教授及其实验室开发了用于靶向药物递送的纳米机器人，并测试了一种能够自主将抗肿瘤疗法输送到膀胱肿瘤的版本。这项新研究将这种方法扩展到大脑，使用光激活纳米机器人帮助药物穿过血脑屏障。 “我们的新纳米机器人不仅限于大脑递送，但我们正试图解决医学领域最大的问题之一——跨越血脑屏障——作为起点。”高教授解释说，“我们针对的是癌症，但也有许多神经系统疾病，例如帕金森病，我们希望它们能从这项技术中受益。” 虽然只有一小部分BrainBots进入了大脑，但这项技术代表着改善治疗药物跨血脑屏障递送的重大进展。高教授表示，那些留在身体其他部位的纳米机器人预计会像大多数基于纳米颗粒的药物载体一样，被肝脏和脾脏等器官吸收而降解。接下来，该团队计划进一步改进纳米机器人系统，并评估其未来临床应用的潜力。 “我们正在优化近红外光的递送，并评估纳米机器人在体内的长期安全性、稳定性和降解情况。”高教授说，“尽管在转向人体研究之前还需要更多的研究，但我们对这项治疗脑部疾病的技术潜力感到兴奋。” 这篇《科学·机器人学》文章的标题是“光热门控纳米机器人用于主动跨血脑屏障转运和大脑递送”（Photothermally gated nanorobots for active blood–brain barrier transcytosis and brain delivery）。来自高教授实验室的加州理工学院合著者还包括博士后研究员Jounghyun Yoo和Sukyoung Won；访问学生研究员Suhyun Kim；研究生Hong Han (MS '23)、Dong Hung Truong和Xiaotian Ma (MS '24)；医学工程访问学者Gwangmook Kim；以及前博士后研究员Songsong Tang。所有合著者名单可在论文中查阅。这项研究得到了海瑞泰奇医学研究所、韩国健康产业发展院（通过韩国健康技术研发项目）、韩国国家研究基金会和韩国基础科学研究所的支持。
