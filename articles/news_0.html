Medical cannabis compound alleviates nightmares associated with PTSD

本研究揭示了屈大麻酚（THC的一种合成形式）在治疗创伤后应激障碍（PTSD）相关噩梦方面的显著潜力。PTSD噩梦表现为创伤事件在睡眠中反复、强烈且生动地重现，导致严重的睡眠剥夺和巨大痛苦，而现有药物治疗往往效果不佳。其核心医学机制在于THC与人体内源性大麻素系统的相互作用，该系统在调节睡眠、应激反应和情感记忆处理中扮演关键角色。具体而言，THC通过减少快速眼动睡眠（REM sleep）期间的梦境活动，从而有效缓解夜间应激反应。 这项双盲、安慰剂对照研究的积极结果——超过半数参与者有反应，逾三分之一患者噩梦完全消失——凸显了屈大麻酚作为一种靶向疗法的潜力。其对噩梦和睡眠的特异性作用，且未明确改变整体PTSD严重程度或伴随抑郁症，暗示了其精确的作用机制。这一研究对行业和临床实践具有深远影响：它验证了大麻素在疼痛管理之外的治疗潜力，为精神疾病的药物开发开辟了新途径。同时，通过中断夜间创伤重现的折磨循环，提供安宁的睡眠，它有望显著改善PTSD患者的生活质量和整体健康状况。未来的研究将聚焦于长期疗效、安全性及其可能产生的耐受性。

英文原文

Original
Nightmares are among the most agonizing symptoms of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) – a condition that can develop after exposure to severe traumatic experiences, such as disasters, serious accidents, acts of violence, or wartime experiences. Individuals affected relive the events intensely at night, as if they were real, time and time again. This results in extreme sleep deprivation and fear of going to bed. Existing pharmacological treatment approaches often fail to adequately relieve these nightmares. Researchers at Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin investigated whether a prescription medication containing THC – one of the active compounds of the cannabis plant – could help. As they report in Nature Medicine, more than half of the participants responded to treatment, while more than a third experienced a complete disappearance of nightmares. Drastic or even life-threatening events can have lingering effects, like an echo. This is referred to as post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). The brain is unable to process these overwhelming experiences and stores the memories in such a way that they cannot be consciously controlled. They resurface again and again. Also at night, when people with PTSD relive the trauma over and over again until they wake up – usually in a state of panic. Antidepressants or blood pressure-lowering medications are commonly used to provide relief, among other treatments, but these are only partially effective and usually do not help relieving the nightmares. A medication specifically for treating trauma-related nightmares has not yet been approved in Germany. Prof. Stefan Röpke conducts research on trauma-related disorders at the Department of Psychiatry and Neurosciences on the Benjamin Franklin Campus of Charité. In their search for new treatment approaches and avenues, he and his team, along with other partners, focused on the principal psychoactive compound of the cannabis plant: tetrahydrocannabinol, or THC for short. This active ingredient, also known for its psychoactive effects, interacts with the body's endocannabinoid system, which is involved in regulating sleep, stress, and the processing of emotional memories. Stefan Röpke explains: "There is evidence suggesting that during REM sleep – the intense dream phases in which the brain processes experiences and regulates emotions – THC reduces dream activity and thereby alleviates nocturnal stress responses." A cannabinoid specifically targeting nightmares Cannabis-based medications are already finding use in pain management, typically when standard treatments prove insufficient or patients cannot tolerate them. In Germany, this has been possible since the so-called "Cannabis for medical purposes" law took effect in 2017. Since then, it has become easier to use plant-derived or synthetically produced cannabinoids for medical and medical-scientific purposes. For their current study the researchers selected a form of THC derived directly from the cannabis plant in the form of drops – the prescription medication Dronabinol. The aim was to determine whether this approach could alleviate nightmares associated with PTSD and whether its use is safe in this context. More than 170 patients with post-traumatic stress disorder and frequent, intense nightmares participated in the study. For a ten week period, they received either the medication or an identical-looking, cannabis-flavored placebo every evening before bedtime. Neither the healthcare providers nor the participants knew who was receiving which treatment – the gold standard of medical research, a so-called double-blind, placebo-controlled study. By the end of the study period, nightmares had decreased to a significantly greater extent in the Dronabinol group than in the placebo group. On a scale of zero to eight points, the nightmare burden decreased by an average of 3.7 points, compared to only 2.2 points in the placebo group – a difference that is clearly noticeable to those affected. More than a third of the patients treated with Dronabinol reported that they no longer experienced any nightmares after ten weeks. Another 21 percent reported that their nightmare burden had been at least halved. And about five out of six patients in the dronabinol group felt their health had markedly improved." Prof. Stefan Röpke, Department of Psychiatry and Neurosciences, Benjamin Franklin Campus of Charité No dependence or severe side effects According to the studies, the cannabinoid had a specific effect on nightmares and sleep, but not on all PTSD symptoms. Moreover, the severity of the post-traumatic stress disorder and any accompanying depression did not change conclusively. Nevertheless, the majority of participants experienced significant relief, as the tormenting nightly re-experiencing of the traumatic event was interrupted, allowing for restful sleep. Taking the medication did not cause any severe side effects; however, the researchers observed moderate and mild side effects such as dizziness, headache, or increased appetite more frequently. The study team did not observe any withdrawal symptoms after the end of the study period and the discontinuation of the evening doses. In the next steps going forward, the researchers now aim to determine whether taking the cannabinoid is also effective and safe over the long term, or whether a tolerance effect develops over time.

创伤后应激障碍（PTSD）最折磨人的症状之一是噩梦——这种疾病可能在经历过严重创伤性事件后发展，例如灾难、严重事故、暴力行为或战争经历。受影响的个体在夜间会反复强烈地重现这些事件，仿佛身临其境。这导致极度睡眠剥夺和对入睡的恐惧。现有药物治疗方法往往无法充分缓解这些噩梦。柏林夏里特医科大学医院（Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin）的研究人员调查了含有THC（大麻植物的活性化合物之一）的处方药是否能提供帮助。正如他们在《自然医学》中报告的那样，超过一半的参与者对治疗有反应，而超过三分之一的参与者经历了噩梦的完全消失。 剧烈甚至危及生命的事件可能会像回声一样，产生挥之不去的影响。这被称为创伤后应激障碍（PTSD）。大脑无法处理这些压倒性的经历，并以一种无法被意识控制的方式储存记忆。它们会反复浮现。在夜间也是如此，患有PTSD的人会反复重温创伤，直到惊醒——通常处于恐慌状态。抗抑郁药或降压药通常用于提供缓解，以及其他治疗方法，但这些药物仅部分有效，通常无助于缓解噩梦。德国尚未批准专门用于治疗创伤相关噩梦的药物。 斯特凡·勒普克教授在夏里特医科大学医院本杰明·富兰克林校区精神病学与神经科学系进行创伤相关障碍方面的研究。在寻找新的治疗方法和途径时，他及其团队与其他合作伙伴一起，将重点放在了大麻植物的主要精神活性化合物：四氢大麻酚，简称THC。这种活性成分也因其精神活性作用而闻名，它与人体内源性大麻素系统相互作用，该系统参与调节睡眠、压力和情感记忆的处理。斯特凡·勒普克解释说：“有证据表明，在快速眼动睡眠（REM睡眠）——大脑处理经历和调节情绪的强烈梦境阶段——THC能减少梦境活动，从而缓解夜间应激反应。” 一种专门针对噩梦的大麻素 大麻类药物已在疼痛管理中得到应用，通常在标准治疗效果不佳或患者无法耐受时使用。在德国，自2017年“医用大麻法”生效以来，这成为可能。自那时起，使用植物提取或合成生产的大麻素用于医疗和医学科学目的变得更加容易。在本研究中，研究人员选择了一种直接从大麻植物中提取的THC形式，以滴剂形式——处方药屈大麻酚（Dronabinol）。其目的是确定这种方法是否能缓解与PTSD相关的噩梦，以及在此背景下使用是否安全。 超过170名患有创伤后应激障碍并伴有频繁、强烈噩梦的患者参与了这项研究。在为期十周的时间里，他们每晚睡前服用药物或外观相同、带有大麻风味的安慰剂。医护人员和参与者都不知道谁接受了哪种治疗——这是医学研究的黄金标准，即所谓的双盲、安慰剂对照研究。 研究期结束时，屈大麻酚组的噩梦减轻程度显著大于安慰剂组。在0到8分的量表上，噩梦负担平均下降了3.7分，而安慰剂组仅下降了2.2分——这一差异对受影响者而言是显而易见的。 “超过三分之一的屈大麻酚治疗患者报告称，十周后他们不再经历任何噩梦。另有21%的患者报告称他们的噩梦负担至少减半。屈大麻酚组中约有六分之五的患者认为他们的健康状况显著改善。”——柏林夏里特医科大学医院本杰明·富兰克林校区精神病学与神经科学系斯特凡·勒普克教授 无依赖性或严重副作用 研究表明，这种大麻素对噩梦和睡眠有特定作用，但并非对所有PTSD症状都有效。此外，创伤后应激障碍的严重程度以及任何伴随的抑郁症均未发生确定性改变。尽管如此，大多数参与者都感受到了显著的缓解，因为痛苦的夜间创伤事件重现被中断，从而获得了安宁的睡眠。服用该药物没有引起任何严重的副作用；然而，研究人员更频繁地观察到中度和轻度副作用，如头晕、头痛或食欲增加。在研究期结束和停用晚间剂量后，研究团队未观察到任何戒断症状。在接下来的步骤中，研究人员现在旨在确定长期服用这种大麻素是否仍然有效且安全，或者是否会随着时间的推移产生耐受性。
