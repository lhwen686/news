深度解析

该研究提出了一种名为LM-QASAS的创新方法，其核心专业概念在于将“抗体语言模型”与“纵向B细胞受体（BCR）库分析”相结合，以自主识别和追踪对免疫刺激产生应答的BCR序列。传统免疫应答分析往往依赖于预先建立的“已知抗原特异性抗体数据库”，但LM-QASAS通过深度学习算法构建的抗体语言模型，能够学习和理解高度多样化的抗体序列中的生物学模式，将这些序列嵌入高维空间。在此空间中，序列间的距离反映了其功能或结构相似性，从而可以识别出在免疫应答过程中瞬时富集或变化的序列群体。 其医学机制在于，当机体受到抗原刺激后，特异性B细胞会增殖、分化并产生抗体。这些B细胞的BCR序列会发生选择性富集和克隆扩展。LM-QASAS能够通过分析不同时间点（如接种疫苗前后）的BCR库数据，捕捉到这种动态变化，识别出那些在应答峰值时密度增加、随后又下降的序列群，即使没有已知抗体数据库也能进行。 这项技术对行业影响深远。首先，它极大地加速了对“新发传染病”的免疫应答监测，因为无需等待大量已知抗体数据积累。这在应对全球流行病（如COVID-19）时具有关键意义。其次，它能更高效地评估“疫苗效力”，特别是在抗原信息有限的情况下。再者，该平台有望应用于“自身免疫性疾病”和“肿瘤免疫”等复杂疾病的体液免疫监测，为精准诊断和治疗提供更深层的数据支持。通过减少对外部数据库的依赖，LM-QASAS推动了免疫学研究从“数据库驱动”向“数据自主发现”的范式转变，预示着基于AI的免疫学分析将成为未来研究和临床实践的重要工具。

英文原文

Original
A research team comprising Associate Professor FUNAKOSHI Yohei, Associate Professor YAKUSHIJIN Kimikazu, Professor MINAMI Hironobu, and Associate Professor OHJI Goh of Kobe University, together with graduate student MASUDA Genki, then-graduate student IIZUMI Shunsuke, Associate Professor OHUE Masahito, and colleagues at the Institute of Science Tokyo, has developed a new analysis method, LM-QASAS. This method combines antibody language models with B-cell receptor (BCR) repertoires collected before and after vaccination to identify and track candidate BCR sequences that are likely to have responded to an immune stimulus, without using a database of known antigen-specific antibodies. LM-QASAS extends QASAS(Quantification of Antigen-Specific Antibody Sequence), a method for quantitatively evaluating immune responses to a specific antigen through antigen-receptor repertoire analysis. When the researchers analyzed longitudinal IgG BCR repertoire data from 10 participants in SARS-CoV-2-related cohorts, the top candidates selected using an antibody language model contained a higher proportion of sequences similar to known antibodies than candidates obtained by random sampling. In addition, a pseudo-reference database constructed from nine of the participants was able to track the rise, peak, and subsequent decline of the immune response in the participant left out of database construction. The findings suggest that LM-QASAS may make it possible to build pseudo-reference databases from the BCR repertoires of multiple individuals and use them to follow immune responses in new individuals, even for emerging infectious diseases for which little information on known antibodies has been accumulated. They also indicate that deep learning can capture biologically informative patterns in highly diverse antibody sequences. With validation across additional antigens and larger cohorts, the approach could develop into a platform for monitoring a broad range of humoral immune responses, including responses relevant to vaccines, infectious diseases, autoimmune diseases, and cancer immunity. The research was published online in Frontiers in Immunology on July 30, 2026. Main points The original QASAS method relies on databases of known antibody sequences, which limits its application when sufficient antibody information is unavailable. LM-QASAS uses AbLang2 to convert IgG BCR CDR-H3 sequences into 480-dimensional representations and identifies populations whose local density rises at the response peak and then declines. In SARS-CoV-2-related data from 10 participants, LM-QASAS enriched sequences similar to known SARS-CoV-2 antibodies and used pseudo-reference databases built from the other nine participants to track longitudinal response patterns. Using clinical samples from SARS-CoV-2 vaccine recipients and convalescent patients, the study showed that the antibody language model can capture biologically informative patterns in longitudinal BCR repertoire data despite the high diversity of CDR-H3 sequences. Validation across more antigens and larger cohorts may enable vaccine evaluation and monitoring of humoral responses in emerging infections, autoimmune diseases, and cancer immunity. Background of the research B cells carry membrane-bound forms of antibodies called B-cell receptors (BCRs) on their surface. The human body can theoretically generate as many as 100 trillion different BCRs. The complete collection of these sequences - the BCR repertoire - records an individual's immunological history, including infections, vaccinations, and autoimmune responses. Although next-generation sequencing can now read large numbers of BCR sequences at once, identifying the small subset that actually responded to a particular antigen remains difficult. The research group's previously developed QASAS method visualizes immune responses to a specific antigen over time by comparing BCR repertoires obtained before and after vaccination with databases of known antigen-specific antibodies. This approach is useful in fields such as COVID-19 research, for which many antibody sequences have been accumulated. For emerging infectious diseases and other conditions with limited antibody information, however, a suitable reference database may not be available. The team therefore sought a method that could discover candidate responsive sequences autonomously from the internal organization and temporal dynamics of the repertoire, without relying on a database of known antigen-specific antibody sequences. Content of the research 1. Searching for transiently dense sequence populations in antibody 'language space' LM-QASAS takes amino acid sequences from CDR-H3, a region of the antibody heavy chain that is particularly important for antigen recognition, and processes them with the antibody language model AbLang2. Each sequence is converted into a 480-dimensional embedding. Sequences with similar features learned by the model are positioned near one another in this high-dimensional language space. The researchers represented repertoires from before stimulation (Pre), the response peak (Peak), and the contraction phase (Post) as distributions in this space. They then searched for regions that were sparse at Pre, became locally dense at Peak, and decreased again at Post. Candidate sequences were identified using two complementary approaches: K-means clustering (K = 500) and k-nearest-neighbor analysis (k = 200). In this representative case, a new high-density region appeared at the response peak. Sequences similar to known SARS-CoV-2 antibodies from the public CoV-AbDab database were found to concentrate in this region. This density and the number of red points declined after the peak, although some sequences persisted. UMAP was used only to make the pattern visible in two dimensions; density estimation and candidate extraction were performed in the information-rich 480-dimensional space. These results suggest that, despite the high diversity of CDR-H3 sequences, an antibody language model can capture biologically informative patterns beyond those detectable by exact sequence matching alone. 2. Post-extraction validation in 10 SARS-CoV-2-related participants The validation dataset comprised longitudinal IgG BCR repertoires from four healthy recipients of an mRNA SARS-CoV-2 vaccine, three people who had recovered from COVID-19, and three people who had received an mRNA vaccine after undergoing hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. After LM-QASAS had extracted candidate sequences without using a known-antibody database, the researchers measured the proportion of those candidates with at least 85% sequence identity to known SARS-CoV-2 antibody sequences in CoV-AbDab. For the K-means version of LM-QASAS, 26.4% of the top 50 candidates matched this criterion, compared with 1.4% of randomly sampled sequences. The corresponding values were 24.8% versus 1.1% for the top 100 candidates and 15.6% versus 1.5% for the top 300. Among the four healthy mRNA vaccine recipients, the mean match rate for the top 300 candidates was 30.8%, compared with 2.3% for random sampling. These results support the idea that the enrichment of antigen-associated sequences among candidates can be identified without reference to an external antigen-specific database. 3. Tracking immune responses with pseudo-reference databases built from other participants The researchers next excluded one of the 10 participants and used the top 300 sequences extracted from the peak repertoires of the other nine to construct a method-specific pseudo-reference database. They then analyzed the participant who had been left out and repeated this leave-one-out procedure for all 10 participants. The LM-QASAS-derived pseudo-reference databases captured longitudinal patterns that rose, peaked, and declined without using an external database of known antigen-specific antibodies. In healthy mRNA vaccine recipients, peaks were generally observed around seven days after vaccination. Further developments LM-QASAS represents a means of extracting candidate antigen-responsive sequences directly from longitudinal BCR repertoires without waiting for known antibody sequences to accumulate, and then using those candidates as a pseudo-reference database to track responses in new individuals. Potential applications include rapid monitoring of humoral immunity soon after the emergence of a new infectious disease and evaluation of vaccines against antigens for which antibody information is limited. The study also has important limitations. Although candidate sequences showed greater overlap with known antibodies than randomly sampled sequences, random pseudo-reference databases also produced some peak-like longitudinal changes. The difference between LM-QASAS (K-means) and Seq-QASAS (k-NN) was not statistically significant at any extraction depth in this cohort. Moreover, in a separate analysis of 17 influenza vaccine recipients, clear longitudinal dynamics were not detected in 11 of them, indicating reduced sensitivity when the repertoire-level signal is weak or heterogeneous. Further methodological refinement and validation are therefore required. The team is currently working to standardize sampling schedules that capture the pre-stimulation, peak, and contraction phases. It also aims to establish a reproducible evaluation platform using longitudinal samples from recipients of varicella-zoster virus and respiratory syncytial virus vaccines. The long-term goal is to develop a platform that can assess humoral immune responses in infectious diseases, vaccine development, autoimmune diseases, and cancer immunity without being limited by the availability of databases of known antigen-specific antibody sequences.

日本神户大学的船越洋平副教授、药师神公子副教授、南博信教授和大路剛副教授，与研究生增田元气、时任研究生饭泉俊介以及东京科学大学的大上正人副教授及其同事共同组成的研究团队，开发了一种新的分析方法——LM-QASAS。该方法结合了抗体语言模型与疫苗接种前后收集的B细胞受体（BCR）库，能够在不使用已知抗原特异性抗体数据库的情况下，识别并追踪可能已对免疫刺激产生应答的候选BCR序列。LM-QASAS是QASAS（抗原特异性抗体序列量化分析）方法的扩展，QASAS是一种通过抗原受体库分析定量评估特定抗原免疫应答的方法。 研究人员在分析来自10名SARS-CoV-2相关队列参与者的纵向IgG BCR库数据时发现，使用抗体语言模型选出的顶层候选序列，其与已知抗体相似的序列比例高于随机抽样得到的候选序列。此外，研究人员从其中9名参与者的数据构建了一个伪参考数据库，能够追踪在数据库构建过程中被排除的另一名参与者的免疫应答的上升、达到峰值和随后的下降。 这些发现表明，LM-QASAS有望从多个个体的BCR库构建伪参考数据库，并利用这些数据库追踪新个体的免疫应答，即使对于已知抗体信息积累甚少的新发传染病也同样适用。研究结果还表明，深度学习能够捕获高度多样化抗体序列中具有生物学信息量的模式。通过在更多抗原和更大规模队列中的验证，该方法有望发展成为一个监测广泛体液免疫应答的平台，包括与疫苗、传染病、自身免疫性疾病和肿瘤免疫相关的应答。 该研究于2026年7月30日在线发表在《免疫学前沿》（Frontiers in Immunology）杂志上。 主要观点 原始的QASAS方法依赖于已知抗体序列数据库，这限制了其在抗体信息不足时的应用。 LM-QASAS使用AbLang2将IgG BCR CDR-H3序列转换为480维表示，并识别在应答峰值局部密度上升随后下降的群体。 在来自10名SARS-CoV-2相关参与者的数据中，LM-QASAS富集了与已知SARS-CoV-2抗体相似的序列，并利用从其他9名参与者构建的伪参考数据库追踪了纵向应答模式。 通过使用SARS-CoV-2疫苗接种者和康复患者的临床样本，该研究表明，尽管CDR-H3序列高度多样，抗体语言模型仍能捕获纵向BCR库数据中具有生物学信息量的模式。 在更多抗原和更大规模队列中进行验证，可能有助于评估疫苗以及监测新发感染、自身免疫性疾病和肿瘤免疫中的体液应答。 研究背景 B细胞表面带有膜结合形式的抗体，称为B细胞受体（BCRs）。理论上，人体可以产生多达100万亿种不同的BCR。这些序列的完整集合——即BCR库——记录了个体的免疫学历史，包括感染、疫苗接种和自身免疫应答。尽管下一代测序现在可以一次性读取大量BCR序列，但识别出其中实际对特定抗原产生应答的一小部分仍然困难重重。 该研究团队先前开发的QASAS方法，通过比较疫苗接种前后获得的BCR库与已知抗原特异性抗体数据库，可视化了对特定抗原的免疫应答随时间的变化。这种方法在COVID-19研究等领域非常有用，因为这些领域积累了大量抗体序列。然而，对于新发传染病及其他抗体信息有限的疾病，可能没有合适的参考数据库。因此，该团队寻求一种无需依赖已知抗原特异性抗体序列数据库，即可从库的内部组织和时间动态中自主发现候选应答序列的方法。 研究内容 1. 在抗体“语言空间”中搜索瞬时致密序列群体 LM-QASAS获取CDR-H3（抗体重链中对识别抗原尤为重要的区域）的氨基酸序列，并使用抗体语言模型AbLang2进行处理。每个序列被转换为480维嵌入。模型学习到的具有相似特征的序列在这个高维语言空间中彼此靠近。研究人员将刺激前（Pre）、应答峰（Peak）和收缩期（Post）的库表示为该空间中的分布。然后，他们搜索那些在Pre时稀疏、在Peak时变得局部致密、并在Post时再次减少的区域。候选序列通过两种互补方法识别：K均值聚类（K = 500）和k近邻分析（k = 200）。 在这个代表性案例中，应答峰出现了一个新的高密度区域。发现与公共CoV-AbDab数据库中已知SARS-CoV-2抗体相似的序列集中在该区域。这种密度和红色点的数量在峰值后下降，尽管一些序列持续存在。UMAP仅用于在二维空间中使模式可视化；密度估计和候选提取是在信息丰富的480维空间中进行的。这些结果表明，尽管CDR-H3序列高度多样，抗体语言模型仍能捕获超越仅通过精确序列匹配可检测的生物学信息模式。 2. 在10名SARS-CoV-2相关参与者中进行提取后验证 验证数据集包括来自4名健康mRNA SARS-CoV-2疫苗接种者、3名COVID-19康复者和3名在接受造血干细胞移植后接种mRNA疫苗者的纵向IgG BCR库。在LM-QASAS在不使用已知抗体数据库的情况下提取出候选序列后，研究人员测量了这些候选序列中与CoV-AbDab中已知SARS-CoV-2抗体序列具有至少85%序列同一性的比例。 对于LM-QASAS的K均值版本，前50个候选序列中有26.4%符合此标准，而随机抽样的序列中只有1.4%。对于前100个候选序列，相应的值为24.8%对1.1%；对于前300个候选序列，为15.6%对1.5%。在4名健康的mRNA疫苗接种者中，前300个候选序列的平均匹配率为30.8%，而随机抽样为2.3%。这些结果支持了可以在不参考外部抗原特异性数据库的情况下识别候选序列中抗原相关序列富集的观点。 3. 利用从其他参与者构建的伪参考数据库追踪免疫应答 研究人员接下来排除了10名参与者中的一名，并使用从其他9名参与者的峰值库中提取的前300个序列，构建了一个特定于该方法的伪参考数据库。然后，他们分析了被排除的那名参与者，并对所有10名参与者重复了这种“留一法”程序。LM-QASAS衍生的伪参考数据库捕获了上升、达到峰值和下降的纵向模式，而无需使用外部已知抗原特异性抗体数据库。在健康的mRNA疫苗接种者中，峰值通常在接种后约七天观察到。 进一步发展 LM-QASAS代表了一种无需等待已知抗体序列积累，即可直接从纵向BCR库中提取候选抗原应答序列的方法，然后利用这些候选序列作为伪参考数据库来追踪新个体的应答。潜在应用包括在新发传染病出现后迅速监测体液免疫，以及评估针对抗体信息有限的抗原的疫苗。 该研究也存在重要的局限性。尽管候选序列与已知抗体的重叠程度高于随机抽样序列，但随机伪参考数据库也产生了一些类似峰值的纵向变化。在该队列中，LM-QASAS（K均值）和Seq-QASAS（k-NN）在任何提取深度上均无统计学显著差异。此外，在对17名流感疫苗接种者的独立分析中，其中11人未检测到清晰的纵向动态，表明当库水平信号较弱或异质时，敏感性会降低。因此，需要进一步的方法学改进和验证。 该团队目前正在努力标准化捕获刺激前、峰值和收缩期的采样时间表。它还旨在利用水痘-带状疱疹病毒和呼吸道合胞病毒疫苗接种者的纵向样本，建立一个可复现的评估平台。长期目标是开发一个平台，能够在不局限于已知抗原特异性抗体序列数据库可用性的情况下，评估传染病、疫苗开发、自身免疫性疾病和肿瘤免疫中的体液免疫应答。
