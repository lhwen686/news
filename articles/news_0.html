阻断免疫“信号二”可扩增肠道耐受细胞，有望开启炎症性肠病新疗法

Blocking immune 'signal two' expands gut tolerance cells, may open new IBD treatments

Image visualizing immune cell aggregates in the intestine, demonstrating the presence of RORγt+ APCs (red and green) and T cells (blue and red), whose interactions are critical in establishing immune tolerance in the intestine. Credit: Dr. Gregory F. Sonnenberg.

Weill Cornell Medicine investigators made an unexpected finding about how the immune system normally suppresses inappropriate chronic inflammation in the intestine, potentially opening new avenues for therapies against inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), food allergy and other autoimmune conditions.

The study, published April 24 in the Journal of Experimental Medicine, focused on the cell-to-cell signals required for the immune system to tolerate commensal gut microbes as well as foreign proteins from food. The researchers examined a signal that had been considered necessary for expanding the population of tolerance-enforcing immune cells. They found unexpectedly that only blocking this signal was effective for driving tolerance in intestine through a specific cell subset—and powerfully suppressed gut inflammation in a preclinical model.

"We think this is a paradigm-shifting discovery that will lead to new treatment approaches for chronic inflammatory disorders of the intestine," said study senior author Dr. Gregory Sonnenberg, the Henry R. Erle, M.D.-Roberts Family Professor of Medicine and associate vice chair of research in medicine, and faculty member of the Division of Gastroenterology & Hepatology and the Jill Roberts Institute for Research in Inflammatory Bowel Disease at Weill Cornell Medicine.

The study's lead and co-corresponding author is Dr. Mengze Lyu, an instructor of microbiology and immunology in Medicine and member of the Sonnenberg Laboratory.

Specialized immune cells establish tolerance in the gut

How the immune system distinguishes friend from foe in different organs and tissues is a puzzle that has never been fully solved, and disorders of inappropriate immune activation remain common—particularly in the gastrointestinal tract, where foreign molecules from food and gut-resident microbes abound. Forms of IBD, particularly Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, and other gut inflammation conditions affect millions of people in the United States.

The gut relies heavily on local immune cells with distinct properties, complicating efforts to understand gut immunology. For example, prior research has shown that immune tolerance in the gut is enforced largely by a distinct subset of regulatory T cells called Treg cells that express the transcription factor RORγt. The Sonnenberg lab has been at the forefront of this area of research, where they were the first to discover novel antigen-presenting cells (APCs) expressing RORγt that are enriched in the gut and necessary for immune tolerance. Drs. Lyu and Sonnenberg and their colleagues also have found that gut RORγt+ Treg cells are dependent upon direct signals from these RORγt+ APCs.

A "two-factor authentication" system regulates T cell activity

Despite these gut-specific distinctions, the requirement for APCs in Treg cell or other T-cell activation is a general one—part of a "two-factor authentication" system that helps regulate immune cell activity. What immunologists call Signal One in this system is the APC's presentation, to a T cell, of the target molecule fragment, or "antigen."

In the new study, the researchers examined Signal Two, which for Treg cells and other T cells outside the gut involves the binding of a different set of receptors on T cells and APCs.

To the scientists' surprise, the usual "Signal Two" coupling of Treg cells and APC receptors, in the presence of Signal One, did not trigger an expansion of gut RORγt+ Treg cells. What did work was—in combination with Signal One—blocking that Signal Two coupling, which expanded the gut RORγt+ Treg cells and prevented gut inflammation in a preclinical model. Importantly, this is the exact opposite to what happens with conventional Treg cells outside of the gut, further highlighting the unique and complicated features of the intestinal immune system.

Therapeutic implications

There is already an approved drug called CTLA4-Ig or Abatacept that blocks the Signal Two interaction. Although it failed to benefit patients with IBD in a 2012 clinical report, the experiments by Drs. Lyu and Sonnenberg and their colleagues found that the RORγt+ APCs required for gut immune tolerance are depleted in inflammatory bowel disease—depriving gut RORγt+ Treg cells of the critical Signal One that is needed to trigger their expansion in combination with Signal Two blockade.

"Our results suggest that CTLA4-Ig could be effective against inflammatory bowel disease, if we could restore RORγt+ APCs that are missing, or give CTLA4-Ig to patients who are in remission and still have these cells present and functional in the intestine," Dr. Lyu said.

The researchers now are investigating these possibilities. And since gut RORγt+ Treg cells are protective in food allergy, adverse events from cancer immunotherapies, and other chronic inflammatory diseases, they suspect that the same Signal Two-blocking strategy could be broadly harnessed to promote gastrointestinal health.

威尔康奈尔医学院的研究人员意外地发现，免疫系统在肠道中如何正常抑制不适当的慢性炎症，这可能为炎症性肠病（IBD）、食物过敏和其他自身免疫性疾病的治疗开辟新途径。

这项于4月24日发表在《实验医学杂志》上的研究，重点关注了免疫系统耐受共生肠道微生物以及食物中外源蛋白所需的细胞间信号。研究人员检查了一个曾被认为是扩大耐受性免疫细胞群所必需的信号。他们意外地发现，只有阻断这一信号才能通过特定的细胞亚群有效驱动肠道耐受，并在临床前模型中强力抑制了肠道炎症。

“我们认为这是一项范式转变的发现，将为肠道慢性炎症性疾病带来新的治疗方法，”该研究的资深作者Gregory Sonnenberg博士说。他是威尔康奈尔医学院亨利·R·厄尔医学教授-罗伯茨家族医学教授、医学研究副主任，以及胃肠病学和肝病学部以及吉尔·罗伯茨炎症性肠病研究所的教员。

该研究的主要作者和共同通讯作者是吕梦泽博士，他是医学微生物学和免疫学讲师，也是Sonnenberg实验室的成员。

特殊免疫细胞在肠道中建立耐受

免疫系统如何在不同器官和组织中区分敌我，是一个尚未完全解开的谜团，而不适当的免疫激活障碍仍然很常见——尤其是在胃肠道，那里富含来自食物的外源分子和肠道常驻微生物。炎症性肠病（IBD）的各种形式，特别是克罗恩病和溃疡性结肠炎，以及其他肠道炎症状况，影响着美国数百万人。

肠道严重依赖具有独特特性的局部免疫细胞，这使得理解肠道免疫学的工作变得复杂。例如，先前的研究表明，肠道中的免疫耐受主要由表达转录因子RORγt的独特调节性T细胞亚群（称为Treg细胞）来强制执行。Sonnenberg实验室一直处于这一研究领域的前沿，他们首次发现了富集于肠道并对免疫耐受至关重要的表达RORγt的新型抗原呈递细胞（APCs）。吕博士、Sonnenberg博士及其同事还发现，肠道RORγt+ Treg细胞依赖于这些RORγt+ APCs的直接信号。

“双因素认证”系统调节T细胞活性

尽管存在这些肠道特异性差异，APCs在Treg细胞或其他T细胞激活中的作用是一个普遍要求——它是帮助调节免疫细胞活性的“双因素认证”系统的一部分。免疫学家称之为该系统中的“信号一”是APCs向T细胞呈递目标分子片段，即“抗原”。

在这项新研究中，研究人员检查了“信号二”，对于肠道外的Treg细胞和其他T细胞而言，它涉及T细胞和APCs上不同受体组的结合。

令科学家们惊讶的是，在信号一存在的情况下，Treg细胞和APCs受体通常的“信号二”结合，并未引发肠道RORγt+ Treg细胞的扩增。而真正有效的是——结合信号一——阻断这种信号二结合，这反而扩增了肠道RORγt+ Treg细胞，并在临床前模型中预防了肠道炎症。重要的是，这与肠道外传统Treg细胞的情况完全相反，进一步强调了肠道免疫系统独特而复杂的特征。

治疗意义

目前已有一种名为CTLA4-Ig或阿巴西普的获批药物，可以阻断信号二的相互作用。尽管2012年的一项临床报告显示它未能使IBD患者受益，但吕博士、Sonnenberg博士及其同事的实验发现，炎症性肠病中维持肠道免疫耐受所需的RORγt+ APCs会减少——从而剥夺了肠道RORγt+ Treg细胞在结合信号二阻断时触发其扩增所需的关键信号一。

“我们的结果表明，如果能够恢复缺失的RORγt+ APCs，或者将CTLA4-Ig给予处于缓解期且肠道中仍存在这些细胞并具有功能的患者，CTLA4-Ig可能对炎症性肠病有效，”吕博士说。

研究人员目前正在调查这些可能性。由于肠道RORγt+ Treg细胞在食物过敏、癌症免疫疗法的不良事件和其他慢性炎症性疾病中具有保护作用，他们怀疑相同的信号二阻断策略可以广泛应用于促进胃肠道健康。
