Plant-derived compound helps restore cancer cells' sensitivity to treatment

本研究揭示了一种利用天然植物化合物雷公藤内酯醇克服癌症对表观遗传药物耐药性的潜在策略。文章核心机制在于 DNA 甲基化这一表观遗传过程：癌细胞通过异常 DNA 甲基化沉默肿瘤抑制基因，导致其失控增殖。DNMT 抑制剂如地西他滨旨在通过去除异常甲基化来重新激活这些抗癌基因。然而，许多实体瘤存在 DCTPP1 酶，它像“细胞碎纸机”一样，在 DNMT 抑制剂整合到 DNA 并发挥作用前将其降解，从而产生耐药性。雷公藤内酯醇通过抑制 DCTPP1，有效阻断了这种降解途径，使 DNMT 抑制剂得以存活并成功作用于肿瘤 DNA，恢复肿瘤抑制基因表达，最终杀伤癌细胞。这一发现为克服 DNMT 抑制剂耐药性、扩大其临床应用范围（尤其对目前疗效有限的实体瘤）提供了新的方向，并为表观遗传治疗的精准化和联合用药提供了理论基础和潜在靶点，对新药研发具有重要指导意义。由于目前尚处于临床前阶段，未来仍需深入研究其效力、安全性和药物特性以支持人体临床试验。

英文原文

Original
A natural plant compound may help overcome a built-in defense that allows many cancers to resist a class of drugs designed to restore normal gene activity, according to a study by researchers at the Johns Hopkins Sidney Kimmel Comprehensive Cancer Center. The researchers found that triptolide, a natural compound from Tripterygium wilfordii, commonly known as thunder god vine, blocks an enzyme called DCTPP1 that cancer cells can use to break down DNA methyltransferase inhibitors, or DNMT inhibitors, before the drugs can fully work. When researchers combined triptolide or related compounds with the DNMT inhibitor decitabine, it dramatically boosted the drug's ability to reactivate tumor suppressor genes and kill cancer cells in laboratory models. The findings, published Aug. 26 in Nature Communications, suggest a potential strategy for overcoming resistance to DNMT inhibitors and expanding their use to more types of cancer. "This gives us a potential way to remove one of the barriers that prevents these drugs from working effectively in many cancers," says senior study author Vasan Yegnasubramanian, M.D., Ph.D., professor of oncology, pathology, and radiation oncology and molecular radiation sciences at the Johns Hopkins Kimmel Cancer Center and director of inHealth Precision Medicine at Johns Hopkins Medicine. An epigenetic process called DNA methylation is one of the ways cells control which genes are turned on or off. DNMTs are enzymes that add chemical tags called methyl groups to DNA, helping regulate whether genes are on or off. Cancer cells can hijack this biological process, using abnormal DNA methylation to shut down genes that normally help prevent uncontrolled cell growth. Drugs such as decitabine and azacitidine, known as DNMT inhibitors, can help remove abnormal methylation and reactivate cancer-fighting genes. The drugs have been effective against certain blood cancers, including myelodysplastic syndromes and leukemia, but have had limited success against solid tumors, which can have built-in defenses that neutralize the therapies before they can begin to work, Yegnasubramanian explains. Researchers screened chemical libraries for compounds that could disable these defenses and resensitize cancer cells to the drugs. Among the compounds they identified was triptolide, an active ingredient found in thunder god vine. In laboratory-grown cancer cells and animal models, triptolide significantly increased both the gene-regulating and cancer-killing effects of decitabine. DNMT inhibitors such as decitabine must first be converted inside cells into an active form that can become incorporated into DNA. Once there, they interfere with enzymes that maintain DNA methylation, allowing previously silenced genes to become active again. However, DCTPP1 acts like a cellular shredder, destroying the active form of DNMT inhibitors before the drugs can be incorporated into DNA. "Triptolide jams the shredder, inhibiting DCTPP1. By blocking this enzyme, the DNMT inhibitors survive, successfully integrating into the tumor DNA, allowing them to work as they are supposed to by inhibiting DNMTs, restoring the expression of crucial tumor suppressor genes and killing cancer cells," says Yegnasubramanian. The findings build on related research from the Johns Hopkins team published earlier this year. In that study, investigators examined the atomic structure of DCTPP1 and identified several classes of small molecules specifically designed to inhibit it. When the experimental inhibitors were combined with decitabine in prostate cancer cells, the cancer cells became more sensitive to the drug. The new study reaches the same target from a different direction. Rather than starting with DCTPP1 and designing molecules to block it, researchers screened chemical libraries for compounds that enhanced decitabine's effects and discovered that triptolide did so by inhibiting DCTPP1. Researchers also found that high levels of DCTPP1 were associated with resistance to DNMT inhibitors, providing further evidence that the enzyme may be an important source of cancer drug resistance and a potential target for making these drugs more effective. "Combining these standard therapies with triptolide or its derivatives may give clinicians a rational strategy to overcome drug resistance, potentially expanding the reach of epigenetic therapy to a much broader range of cancers," says Yegnasubramanian. The researchers caution that the findings are preclinical. Their study tested triptolide and related compounds experimentally and does not establish that the herb itself is a cancer treatment. Additional research will be needed to develop DCTPP1 inhibitors with the potency, safety and drug properties needed for testing in people. In addition to co-senior authors Yegnasubramanian and William Nelson, and lead author Jianyong Liu, other researchers were Qing-Li He, Jianya Zhou, Ajay M. Esopi, Kunhwa Kim, Jonathan Coulter, Rulin Wang, Michelle Rudek, Kathleen Gabrielson, Angelo De Marzo, James Berger and Jun Liu. The research was supported by the National Institutes of Health/National Cancer Institute grants P50CA058236, R01CA183965, R01CA070196, P30CA006973, S10RR026824, UL1TR003098, Prostate Cancer Foundation Challenge Awards, the Patrick C. Walsh Koch Research Award, the Ellen B. Masenheimer Fellowship, the Flight Attendant Medical Research Institute and the Commonwealth Foundation. 根据约翰霍普金斯西德尼·基梅尔综合癌症中心研究人员的一项研究，一种天然植物化合物可能有助于克服一种内置防御机制，该机制使许多癌症能够抵抗一类旨在恢复正常基因活性的药物。 研究人员发现，雷公藤（Tripterygium wilfordii），俗称雷公藤内酯醇，是雷公藤中的一种天然化合物，它能阻断一种名为 DCTPP1 的酶。癌细胞可以利用这种酶来分解 DNA 甲基转移酶抑制剂（简称 DNMT 抑制剂），使其在药物充分发挥作用之前失效。当研究人员将雷公藤内酯醇或相关化合物与 DNMT 抑制剂地西他滨联合使用时，它显著增强了该药物在实验室模型中重新激活肿瘤抑制基因和杀死癌细胞的能力。 这项于 8 月 26 日发表在《自然通讯》上的发现，提出了一种克服 DNMT 抑制剂耐药性并将其应用扩展到更多类型癌症的潜在策略。 资深研究作者 Vasan Yegnasubramanian 医学博士、哲学博士表示：“这为我们提供了一种潜在的方法，可以消除阻碍这些药物在许多癌症中有效发挥作用的障碍之一。”他同时是约翰霍普金斯基梅尔癌症中心的肿瘤学、病理学、放射肿瘤学和分子放射科学教授，以及约翰霍普金斯医学中心 inHealth 精准医疗主任。 一种名为 DNA 甲基化的表观遗传过程是细胞控制哪些基因开启或关闭的方式之一。DNMT 酶是向 DNA 添加名为甲基基团的化学标记的酶，有助于调节基因的开启或关闭。癌细胞可以劫持这一生物过程，利用异常 DNA 甲基化来关闭通常有助于阻止细胞失控生长的基因。 地西他滨和阿扎胞苷等药物，即 DNMT 抑制剂，可以帮助消除异常甲基化并重新激活抗癌基因。Yegnasubramanian 解释说，这些药物已对某些血液癌症有效，包括骨髓增生异常综合征和白血病，但对实体瘤的成功有限，因为实体瘤可能具有内在防御机制，可以在治疗开始发挥作用之前中和这些疗法。 研究人员筛选了化合物库，以寻找能够消除这些防御机制并使癌细胞对药物重新敏感的化合物。他们鉴定出的化合物之一是雷公藤内酯醇，这是雷公藤中的一种活性成分。在实验室培养的癌细胞和动物模型中，雷公藤内酯醇显著增加了地西他滨的基因调控和杀癌作用。 地西他滨等 DNMT 抑制剂必须首先在细胞内转化为活性形式，才能整合到 DNA 中。一旦整合，它们就会干扰维持 DNA 甲基化的酶，从而使之前被沉默的基因重新活跃起来。然而，DCTPP1 就像一个细胞碎纸机，在药物整合到 DNA 中之前就破坏了 DNMT 抑制剂的活性形式。 “雷公藤内酯醇卡住了这个碎纸机，抑制了 DCTPP1。通过阻断这种酶，DNMT 抑制剂得以存活，成功整合到肿瘤 DNA 中，从而按预期通过抑制 DNMTs 发挥作用，恢复关键肿瘤抑制基因的表达并杀死癌细胞。”Yegnasubramanian 说。 这些发现建立在约翰霍普金斯团队今年早些时候发表的相关研究基础之上。在该研究中，研究人员检查了 DCTPP1 的原子结构，并确定了几类专门设计用于抑制它的小分子。当实验性抑制剂与地西他滨在前列腺癌细胞中联合使用时，癌细胞对该药物变得更加敏感。 这项新研究从不同的方向达到了相同的目标。研究人员没有从 DCTPP1 入手设计分子来阻断它，而是筛选了化合物库，寻找能增强地西他滨作用的化合物，并发现雷公藤内酯醇通过抑制 DCTPP1 实现了这一点。 研究人员还发现，高水平的 DCTPP1 与 DNMT 抑制剂耐药性相关联，这进一步证明该酶可能是癌症药物耐药性的重要来源，也是提高这些药物有效性的潜在靶点。 Yegnasubramanian 表示：“将这些标准疗法与雷公藤内酯醇或其衍生物结合使用，可能为临床医生提供一种克服药物耐药性的合理策略，从而有可能将表观遗传疗法的应用范围扩展到更广泛的癌症类型。” 研究人员提醒，这些发现仍处于临床前阶段。他们的研究通过实验测试了雷公藤内酯醇及相关化合物，并未证实这种草药本身就是一种癌症治疗方法。还需要进行额外的研究，以开发具有所需效力、安全性和药物特性的 DCTPP1 抑制剂，用于人体试验。 除了共同资深作者 Yegnasubramanian 和 William Nelson，以及主要作者 Jianyong Liu 之外，其他研究人员还包括 Qing-Li He、Jianya Zhou、Ajay M.
