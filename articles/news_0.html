研究人员发现ALS神经元兴奋性的病因，提示一种新的潜在疗法

Researchers discover a cause of neuron excitability in ALS, suggesting a new potential treatment

Digging deep into the molecular mechanisms behind ALS, researchers at the Les Turner ALS Center at Northwestern Medicine have discovered why nerve cells overfire in the disease. Not only that—they have also designed a new drug to stop this overfiring, which could potentially slow or stop the disease from progressing.

"If we figure out the cause and we get the mechanism right, we can actually treat the disease," said Evangelos Kiskinis, associate professor of neurology and neuroscience at Northwestern University's Feinberg School of Medicine, who led the research.

Using patient nervous tissue and lab-grown human neurons—which he develops from stem cells—Kiskinis has focused his research on the protein TDP-43.

In ALS patients, this protein moves from its normal cellular location and disrupts cellular function. "We believe that understanding the functional process of TDP-43 pathology is the key to understanding what happens in sporadic ALS," he said. Sporadic ALS accounts for the vast majority of cases of the disease.

How TDP-43 disrupts firing

When Kiskinis and his team manipulated the expression of TDP-43, they found that it caused several defects associated with a neuron's ability to fire and communicate.

They discovered that TDP-43 affects an ion channel that acts as a brake for brain cells—when there is too much firing, the ion channel regulates it and slows it down. But many ALS patients show hyperexcitability in their brain cells. Kiskinis believes that TDP-43 causes this hyperexcitability by affecting the ion channel.

Not only has the research uncovered the explanation for this hyperexcitability, it has also led Kiskinis and his team to design a drug that could stop it.

The drug is a new type of therapeutic called an antisense oligonucleotide. A similar drug is used to treat spinal muscular atrophy and has been shown to substantially slow neurodegeneration. These types of drugs work by identifying and binding to a specific, defective RNA molecule in cells, then working to correct it.

"We discovered that the RNA, which makes this ion channel break, is defective because it is regulated by TDP-43," Kiskinis said. "So this type of therapy makes perfect sense to treat it."

From lab finding to therapy

The team is currently planning preclinical studies, including safety studies in animal models, and hopes to advance it to clinical trials in humans soon.

"We hope to establish a platform that would allow us to test this molecule on patients at Northwestern," he said.

Robert Kalb, director of the Les Turner ALS Center at Northwestern Medicine, said this far-reaching and comprehensive study links several enigmatic aspects of motor neuron disease: the electrical excitability of neurons, TDP-43 pathology and the rate of ALS progression in patients.

"Remarkably, preventing the generation of the abnormal ion channel with antisense oligonucleotides blunts this pathology," he said. "Together, this mechanistic work suggests a novel therapeutic avenue for future interrogations."

Broader hopes for sporadic ALS

Research like this is possible because Kiskinis and his team are building innovative cellular models by pooling together stem cell lines derived from multiple ALS patients. The eventual goal is to gather hundreds of stem cells from people living with sporadic ALS to find even more opportunities for treatments to stop the disease from progressing.

Current ALS drugs are showing positive results, but they are designed to treat only the genetic version of the disease. Kiskinis hopes that this new therapy could finally help push the field toward finding ways to treat sporadic ALS.

"Within this decade, we're going to see transformative changes in the way we treat ALS patients," he said. "I remain incredibly hopeful."

西北大学医学院Evangelos Kiskinis博士的实验室。图片来源：莱斯·特纳ALS基金会

西北大学医学院莱斯·特纳ALS中心的研究人员深入探究肌萎缩侧索硬化症（ALS）背后的分子机制，发现了神经细胞在该疾病中过度兴奋的原因。不仅如此，他们还设计了一种新药来阻止这种过度兴奋，这可能潜在地减缓或阻止疾病的进展。

“如果我们能找出病因，并正确理解其机制，我们就能真正治疗这种疾病。”领导这项研究的西北大学范伯格医学院神经病学和神经科学副教授Evangelos Kiskinis说。

Kiskinis利用患者的神经组织和实验室培养的人类神经元（他从干细胞中培养而来），将研究重点放在了TDP-43蛋白上。

在ALS患者中，这种蛋白会从其正常的细胞位置移动，并扰乱细胞功能。“我们认为，理解TDP-43病理学的生物学过程是理解散发性ALS发生机制的关键。”他说。散发性ALS占该病绝大多数病例。

TDP-43如何扰乱放电

当Kiskinis和他的团队操纵TDP-43的表达时，他们发现这会导致与神经元放电和沟通能力相关的几种缺陷。

他们发现TDP-43会影响一种离子通道，该通道充当脑细胞的“刹车”——当放电过多时，离子通道会调节并减慢它。但许多ALS患者的脑细胞表现出过度兴奋。Kiskinis认为，TDP-43通过影响离子通道导致了这种过度兴奋。

这项研究不仅揭示了这种过度兴奋的解释，还促使Kiskinis和他的团队设计出一种可以阻止它的药物。

这种药物是一种新型疗法，称为反义寡核苷酸（antisense oligonucleotide）。一种类似的药物已用于治疗脊髓性肌萎缩症，并已被证明能显著减缓神经退行性变。这类药物通过识别并结合细胞中特定的缺陷RNA分子，然后纠正它来发挥作用。

“我们发现，制造这种离子通道‘刹车’的RNA存在缺陷，因为它受到TDP-43的调节。”Kiskinis说，“因此，这种疗法来治疗它再合适不过了。”

从实验室发现到治疗

该团队目前正在规划临床前研究，包括动物模型中的安全性研究，并希望很快能将其推进到人体临床试验阶段。

“我们希望建立一个平台，使我们能够在西北大学的患者身上测试这种分子。”他说。

西北大学医学院莱斯·特纳ALS中心主任Robert Kalb表示，这项影响深远且全面的研究将运动神经元疾病的几个神秘方面联系起来：神经元的电兴奋性、TDP-43病理学以及ALS患者的疾病进展速度。

“值得注意的是，使用反义寡核苷酸阻止异常离子通道的生成可以减弱这种病理生理过程。”他说，“总之，这项机制性工作为未来的探索提供了一条新颖的治疗途径。”

对散发性ALS的更广泛希望

这项研究之所以可能，是因为Kiskinis和他的团队通过汇集来自多名ALS患者的干细胞系来构建创新的细胞模型。最终目标是收集数百名散发性ALS患者的干细胞，以寻找更多阻止疾病进展的治疗机会。

目前的ALS药物显示出积极效果，但它们仅被设计用于治疗该疾病的遗传版本。Kiskinis希望这种新疗法最终能推动该领域向寻找治疗散发性ALS的方法迈进。

“在这个十年内，我们将看到ALS患者治疗方式的变革性变化。”他说，“我仍然充满希望。”
