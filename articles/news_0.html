睡眠不足是否助长了全国的肥胖问题？

Is poor sleep contributing to the country's weight issues?

深度解析

本文深入探讨了青少年睡眠不足与肥胖危机之间的复杂关联，揭示了多个关键的生理和行为机制。核心概念包括青少年的昼夜节律延迟，这使得他们难以早睡，与早期上学时间形成冲突，导致普遍的睡眠剥夺。生理层面，睡眠不足会扰乱饥饿激素——瘦素（leptin，促进饱腹感）和生长素释放肽（ghrelin，刺激食欲）的平衡，导致个体感到饥饿并进食后满足感降低，从而摄入过量卡路里。更重要的是，长期睡眠不足会降低胰岛素敏感性，增加肥胖和2型糖尿病的风险，对心脏代谢健康造成深远影响。此外，大脑奖励系统在睡眠不足时会寻求补偿，促使个体渴望高糖高脂食物。从行为角度看，疲劳还会增加久坐行为，减少能量消耗。这些机制共同推动了体重增加，并可能延续至成年期。该研究对公共卫生和医疗行业具有重要启示。它强调了睡眠作为健康生活方式第三大支柱的关键作用，并呼吁社会各界（包括教育系统）重新审视早课铃等政策，以支持青少年的健康睡眠。通过行为干预，延长睡眠时间不仅能减少卡路里摄入，还能降低久坐时间，为对抗肥胖危机提供了新的策略。认识到睡眠障碍对健康危害的全面性，有助于医疗专业人员在临床实践中更全面地评估和管理患者的体重问题，从而减轻国家医疗保健系统的负担，提升全生命周期的健康水平。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain Some 70% of teens trying to adapt to a fall schedule are missing out on sleep. Early bells and tough classes combine with delayed biological clocks during adolescence, keeping their nightly sleep duration during the school year below the recommended eight to 10 hours for their age group. While most parents worry about (and lose sleep over) their teens' moods and grades, recent research suggests sleep deprivation can affect another U.S. health concern: the obesity crisis. "It (sleep) is still emerging as that third pillar of a healthy lifestyle, but I think people are increasingly recognizing that sleep is also an important aspect of weight management," said Stacey L. Simon, Ph.D., a sleep expert and professor of pediatrics at the University of Colorado Anschutz School of Medicine. What does research reveal about sleep and weight? Repeated studies in adults have shown that restricting volunteers' sleep leads them to consume between 200 and 300 extra calories, said Seth Creasy, Ph.D., an associate professor of endocrinology, metabolism and diabetes. Simon and Creasy, an obesity medicine researcher at the CU Anschutz Health and Wellness Center and the Colorado Nutrition Obesity Research Center on campus, were part of a study published this year that reviewed sleep as a behavioral treatment for obesity. "So diet and physical activity are still the two main drivers of weight gain from a behavioral standpoint, but sleep can help support both of those," Creasy said. The early bell's toll: Poor sleep raises risk across the lifespan Simon's research focuses largely on sleep in adolescents, whose circadian rhythms prevent them from falling asleep early, prompting calls for later school start times. Despite the American Academy of Pediatrics recommendation in 2014 that middle and high schools begin no earlier than 8:30 a.m., compliance has lagged, Simon said. "A majority of schools start much earlier than that. Certainly, what we see in our sleep clinic at Children's (Hospital Colorado) is that, during the school year, teens are forced to sleep outside of their biological clock." Given the toll the obesity crisis takes on people's health and the country's health care system, Creasy and Simon hope their research will help support behavioral interventions at home and in health care for all ages. "We're really seeing this across the lifespan: that individuals with short sleep duration are at increased risk for higher body weight," Simon said. Excess weight contributes to higher rates of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, some types of cancer, musculoskeletal diseases and premature mortality. The cardiometabolic health consequences of delayed sleep timing in teens are a chief concern for Simon. "We've seen that, in adolescents, short sleep duration and late timing of sleep are associated with poorer insulin sensitivity, which could over time increase risk for obesity and type 2 diabetes." What is keeping teens from sleeping well? An estimated 70% of high school students and 30% of adults don't get their recommended amount of sleep. The substantial lack of sleep in teens is particularly notable because of their unique developmental period, Simon said. Teens have a natural delay in their circadian rhythms working against them: "Their brains are just programmed to not feel sleepy until very late." In a recent sleep study by Simon and colleagues, parents and teens noted high anxiety levels, busy schedules and heavy use of electronic devices that added to their stress and lack of sleep. Teens have more autonomy when it comes to important behaviors, like what types of foods they eat and when and how often they eat, sleep and exercise. "So it's sort of this confluence of factors that all increase risk for greater weight gain. And, unfortunately, we know that those behaviors that are set even in adolescence can endure into adulthood," Simon said. Although the research still lags, some early evidence suggests females might draw an even shorter stick when it comes to the issue of sleep and weight gain "Our research found that adolescent females actually had earlier circadian timing compared to males, which may suggest an even greater sleep need," Simon said. And yet, females report higher levels of insomnia, she said. "So they may need more sleep, but they're actually getting less." Why would sleep deprivation affect calorie consumption? People actually expend more energy tossing and turning or staying up with insomnia, so the energy balance equation by itself cannot explain weight gain. "They may be overcompensating for those burned calories," Simon said. Studies have linked shorter sleep duration with an increase in the hunger hormones leptin and ghrelin. "So they are getting those cues to feel hungry, and they're feeling less satisfied after consuming food. So that again could lead to overconsumption," Simon said. "And there's been some really interesting research done looking at the reward system in the brain and how it's impacted by short sleep," she said. "It seems like when we aren't getting enough sleep at night, then it's almost like our brain is searching for some reward." Often, food—especially the high-sugar, high-fat variety—is the most available prize. On the intervention side, researchers looked at people who routinely slept less than about six hours a night and extended their sleep each night for two weeks, Creasy said. "These people started getting closer to the recommended amounts, about seven and a half hours of sleep," he said. "And just naturally, those people started eating less by 270 calories per day." How might inadequate sleep affect physical activity? It is harder to study how lack of sleep can affect calories burned the next day, so fewer studies exist on the energy expenditure side, Creasy said. However, it makes sense that simply being tired after a bad night's sleep could increase sedentary behavior, he said. Creasy was involved in one study where researchers restricted sleep for a group of New York City residents and then monitored their activity in their free-living environment, he said. "These people didn't use cars. So it was safe for them to be sleep-restricted for six weeks." "They were more sedentary. They ended up actually sitting more throughout the day, even adjusting for the amount of time that they were awake." Simon's team has done studies involving teens who are regularly sleep-deprived on school nights, in which researchers orchestrate a schedule that allows at least one extra hour in bed per night for the teens. "And what we found was, not only were they successful at getting extra sleep during that study period, but they also had a decrease in their sedentary behavior time. And so it was almost like that hour or so before bed that they were otherwise on their phones or engaged in some of those sedentary behaviors, when we intervened, they just replaced that time with sleep." “因此，饮食和体育活动仍然是导致体重增加的两个主要行为驱动因素，但睡眠可以帮助支持这两者，”克里西说。 早课铃的代价：睡眠不佳增加全生命周期的风险 西蒙的研究主要关注青少年的睡眠，他们的昼夜节律阻止他们早睡，从而引发了对推迟上学时间的呼吁。西蒙说，尽管美国儿科学会在 2014 年建议初高中不早于上午 8:30 开始上课，但遵守情况却滞后了。 “大多数学校的上课时间都比这早得多。当然，我们在科罗拉多儿童医院的睡眠诊所看到，在学年期间，青少年被迫在与其生物钟不符的时间睡觉。” 考虑到肥胖危机对人们健康和国家医疗保健系统造成的巨大负担，克里西和西蒙希望他们的研究能帮助支持针对所有年龄段人群在家中和医疗保健领域进行行为干预。 西蒙说：“我们确实在整个生命周期中都看到了这一点：睡眠时长不足的个体患体重过重的风险增加。” 超重会导致 2 型糖尿病、心血管疾病、某些类型癌症、肌肉骨骼疾病和过早死亡的发生率升高。 青少年睡眠时间延迟对心脏代谢健康的影响是西蒙主要关注的问题。“我们已经发现，在青少年中，睡眠时长不足和睡眠时间延迟与胰岛素敏感性降低有关，这可能随着时间的推移增加肥胖和 2 型糖尿病的风险。” 是什么让青少年难以安睡？ 估计有 70% 的高中生和 30% 的成年人没有达到建议的睡眠量。西蒙表示，青少年严重的睡眠不足尤其值得关注，因为他们正处于独特的发育期。 青少年在昼夜节律上存在一种自然的延迟，这与他们作息相悖：“他们的大脑被设定为直到很晚才感到困倦。” 在西蒙及其同事最近的一项睡眠研究中，父母和青少年都提到了高度焦虑、繁忙的日程以及大量使用电子设备，这些都增加了他们的压力和睡眠不足。 青少年在重要的行为方面拥有更多自主权，例如他们吃什么类型的食物，以及何时、多久吃、睡和锻炼。 “因此，这就像是多种因素的汇合，它们都增加了体重增加的风险。而且，不幸的是，我们知道即使在青春期形成的那些行为也可能持续到成年，”西蒙说。 尽管研究仍有不足，但一些早期证据表明，在睡眠和体重增加的问题上，女性可能处于更不利的境地。 西蒙说：“我们的研究发现，青春期女性的昼夜节律时间实际上比男性更早，这可能表明她们需要更多的睡眠。” 然而，她表示，女性报告的失眠水平更高。“所以她们可能需要更多的睡眠，但实际上却睡得更少。” 为什么睡眠不足会影响卡路里摄入？ 人们实际上会因辗转反侧或失眠而消耗更多能量，因此仅仅能量平衡方程本身并不能解释体重增加。西蒙说：“他们可能正在过度补偿那些消耗的卡路里。” 研究已将较短的睡眠时间与饥饿激素瘦素和生长素释放肽的增加联系起来。西蒙说：“因此，他们会收到感到饥饿的信号，并且在进食后感觉不那么满足。这可能再次导致过度摄入。” “还有一些非常有趣的研究，考察了大脑中的奖励系统如何受到短睡眠的影响，”她说，“似乎当我们夜间睡眠不足时，我们的大脑就像在寻找某种奖励。” 通常，食物——尤其是高糖、高脂肪的食物——是最容易获得的“奖品”。 克里西说，在干预方面，研究人员观察了那些习惯性每晚睡眠少于约六小时的人，并让他们每晚延长睡眠时间，持续两周。“这些人开始接近建议的睡眠量，大约是七个半小时，”他说。“然后很自然地，这些人每天开始少摄入 270 卡路里。” 睡眠不足可能如何影响体育活动？ 克里西说，研究睡眠不足如何影响次日卡路里消耗的难度更大，因此在能量消耗方面的研究较少。然而，他表示，一个糟糕的夜晚睡眠后仅仅感到疲倦就可能增加久坐行为，这是合乎逻辑的。 克里西参与了一项研究，研究人员限制了一群纽约市居民的睡眠，然后在他们的自由生活环境中监测其活动，他说。“这些人不开车。所以对他们来说，进行六周的睡眠限制是安全的。”
