New optical control platform blocks and restores neural signaling

深度解析

LATeNT平台在神经生物学领域取得了突破性进展，其核心在于结合了光氧电压（LOV）蛋白和破伤风神经毒素的活性片段。这一分子工具在特定蓝光刺激下，能精确裂解突触前膜囊泡上的VAMP2蛋白，VAMP2是神经递质释放的关键组分。通过选择性地破坏VAMP2，LATeNT能够高效、可逆地抑制特定突触的信号传导，且在光刺激移除后，信号功能可在约24小时内自然恢复。 相较于传统光遗传学主要控制神经元电活动，LATeNT实现了对突触化学信号传递更精细、更持久且可逆的时空控制。这种能力极大地提升了神经回路研究的精确性，如揭示海马体抑制性神经元在焦虑行为中的作用。此外，LATeNT成功应用于胰腺β细胞，精确调节胰岛素分泌，表明其潜在应用范围远超神经科学，可扩展至代谢和免疫疾病研究。未来结合基因递送技术，LATeNT有望为脑部疾病、癌症以及代谢/免疫失调等多种复杂疾病提供个性化精准治疗的新策略。

英文原文

Original
DGIST (President Kunwoo Lee) announced that a research team led by Professor Ji Won Um of the Center for Synapse Diversity and Specificity, Department of Brain Sciences, in collaboration with a research team led by Professor Alice Ting at Stanford University, has developed LATeNT, a novel optical control platform that uses light to block signal transmission between neurons and subsequently restore normal signaling. The technology is expected to be widely applicable to a broad range of life science and medical research, including elucidating the pathogenic mechanisms of brain disorders and regulating insulin secretion. Synapses are the primary sites of connection through which neurons exchange information. Dysfunction of synaptic signaling can cause a wide range of neuropsychiatric disorders, including anxiety disorders, depression, and autism spectrum disorder. Conventional optogenetic technologies have largely been limited to controlling the electrical activity of neurons, with technical limitations in suppressing signal transmission at specific synapses for extended periods while allowing it to be fully restored afterward. To overcome these limitations, the research team precisely designed LATeNT by fusing a light-oxygen-voltage (LOV) protein, which senses light, to tetanus neurotoxin that cleaves proteins involved in neurotransmitter release. LATeNT suppresses synaptic signaling by cleaving VAMP2, a protein essential for neurotransmission, only when exposed to blue light of a specific wavelength. Once the light stimulus is removed, it exhibits reversible properties with neurotransmission naturally returning to its original state within approximately 24 hours. In particular, LATeNT induces a strong inhibitory effect on neurotransmission with only weak blue-light stimulation. Applying this in a mouse model, the research team transiently blocked signaling from specific inhibitory neurons in the hippocampus, which regulates memory and emotion, and demonstrated that these neurons are instrumental in the neural circuits in the brain controlling anxiety-related behavior. Moreover, LATeNT exhibited stronger and more sustained inhibitory effects than conventional optogenetic tools. Furthermore, the research team applied LATeNT to the central nervous system, as well as pancreatic β cells, succeeding in precisely regulating insulin secretion using light. These findings suggest that LATeNT is a versatile platform with broad applicability across biotechnology transcending brain neural circuit research, including research on metabolic and immune disorders and the design of synthetic biology-based genetic circuits. LATeNT is the first molecular tool that can spatiotemporally control the function of specific synaptic proteins, significantly enhancing the precision of neural circuit research. When combined with AAV (adeno-associated virus) gene delivery technology or drug control systems in the future, it could be expanded into a platform for personalized precision therapy applicable not only to brain and neurological disorders but also to cancer, metabolic disorders, and immune disorders." Professor Ji Won Um, Center for Synapse Diversity and Specificity, Department of Brain Sciences This research was jointly conducted by the Center for Synapse Diversity and Specificity at DGIST Department of Brain Sciences with Stanford University, supported by the Ministry of Science and ICT and the National Research Foundation of Korea. Dr. Heegwang Roh and Dr. Dongwook Kim participated as co-first authors, and the findings were published online on July 31, 2026, in Nature Methods, a prestigious journal in the field (top 0.6% in its JCR category; impact factor: 28.3).

DGIST（校长李建宇）宣布，由脑科学系突触多样性与特异性研究中心严智元教授领导的研究团队，与斯坦福大学爱丽丝·廷教授领导的研究团队合作，研发出了一种新型光控平台LATeNT，该平台利用光来阻断神经元之间的信号传导，随后又能恢复正常的信号传导。该技术有望广泛应用于生命科学和医学研究的诸多领域，包括阐明脑部疾病的致病机制和调节胰岛素分泌。 突触是神经元之间信息交换的主要连接部位。突触信号传导功能障碍可导致多种神经精神疾病，包括焦虑症、抑郁症和自闭症谱系障碍。传统的基于光遗传学技术主要局限于控制神经元的电活动，在长时间抑制特定突触的信号传导方面存在技术局限性，同时难以确保其随后能够完全恢复。 为克服这些局限，研究团队精确设计了LATeNT，通过将感光的光氧电压（LOV）蛋白与可裂解参与神经递质释放的蛋白的破伤风神经毒素融合。LATeNT仅在暴露于特定波长的蓝光下，通过裂解神经传导必需的VAMP2蛋白来抑制突触信号传导。一旦移除光刺激，它便表现出可逆性，神经传导在大约24小时内自然恢复到原始状态。 尤其是，LATeNT仅需微弱的蓝光刺激即可对神经传导产生强烈的抑制作用。研究团队将其应用于小鼠模型，短暂阻断了海马体中特定抑制性神经元的信号传导（海马体负责调节记忆和情感），并证明这些神经元在控制焦虑相关行为的大脑神经网络中起着关键作用。此外，LATeNT比传统光遗传学工具表现出更强、更持久的抑制效果。 此外，研究团队还将LATeNT应用于中枢神经系统以及胰腺β细胞，成功利用光精确调节了胰岛素分泌。这些发现表明，LATeNT是一个多功能平台，在生物技术领域具有广泛的应用前景，超越了大脑神经网络研究，包括代谢和免疫疾病研究以及基于合成生物学的基因电路设计。 “LATeNT是首个能够时空控制特定突触蛋白功能的分子工具，显著提升了神经网络研究的精度。未来，当与AAV（腺相关病毒）基因递送技术或药物控制系统结合时，它有望扩展成为一个用于个性化精准治疗的平台，不仅适用于脑部和神经系统疾病，还可应用于癌症、代谢疾病和免疫疾病。”脑科学系突触多样性与特异性研究中心严智元教授说。 这项研究由DGIST脑科学系突触多样性与特异性研究中心与斯坦福大学联合开展，并得到了科学技术信息通信部和韩国国家研究基金会的支持。卢熙光博士和金东旭博士作为共同第一作者参与了研究，研究成果已于2026年7月31日在线发表于该领域著名期刊《自然—方法》（Nature Methods）（该期刊在其JCR类别中排名前0.6%；影响因子：28.3）。
