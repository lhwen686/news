深度解析

本研究揭示了一个重要的公共卫生盲点：物质使用在行人死亡事件中扮演的关键角色，而这一问题常被现有安全宣传所忽视。核心专业概念在于“多物质使用”（polysubstance use）对认知功能和神经认知产生的严重影响。研究强调，与单一物质使用相比，酒精和药物的协同作用可导致更严重的判断力、运动协调及反应时间障碍，这些是安全行人行为的关键要素。这种神经认知缺陷使得个体更易出现危险的横穿行为和对交通状况的错误评估。其行业影响深远。公共卫生领域需重新审视其预防策略，将“行人受损”纳入宣传重点，设计针对性的教育活动。政策制定者应考虑实施环境干预措施，如改善夜间照明和行人基础设施，尤其是在高风险时段和地点。临床医学方面，急诊科在处理行人创伤时，应提高对物质使用的警觉性，并将其纳入评估流程，以指导患者管理并为公共卫生监测提供数据。这促使我们将道路安全视角从单一的驾驶员责任扩展至对行人行为的全面考量，将行人物质受损识别为一个可干预的重要风险因素。

This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies . Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain In New York state, 44% of pedestrian deaths involved alcohol or drug use by the pedestrian, according to a retrospective analysis published online in the journal Injury Prevention. Pedestrian injuries are the seventh-leading cause of injury-related death in New York state. There are approximately 15,000 pedestrian injuries annually, of which 3,000 require hospitalization. Most public safety campaigns focus on drinking and driving, whereas pedestrian substance use and impairment are relatively neglected issues. The researchers analyzed data from the Fatality Analysis Reporting System, a national database covering all motorized vehicle traffic-involved fatalities on a public roadway. This includes deaths that occurred within 30 days of the crash, as well as alcohol and drug test results reported as part of postmortem examinations. During the three-year study period of 2018 to 2020, there were 771 pedestrian deaths recorded in New York state as a result of a collision with a vehicle. Substance use, defined as the presence of alcohol, drugs or both, was recorded in 338 of the pedestrians who died (44%). Nearly one in five fatalities (18%) involved drugs alone, while 12% involved both alcohol and drugs. Alcohol use (defined as a blood alcohol concentration of 0.01% or more) was recorded in 14% of the pedestrian deaths. The researchers, from the Bureau of Occupational Health and Injury Prevention at New York State Department of Health and Columbia University, write: "Alcohol and drug co-use is particularly concerning, as poly-substance use has been linked to more severe impairments in cognitive function and neurocognitive deficits compared with single drug use." They say further research is needed to better understand the role of polysubstance use in pedestrian injuries and fatalities. The number of deaths was highest among men (63%) and those 65 and older (34%). Hispanic males had the highest proportion of substance-involved deaths (58%). However, a large proportion of pedestrians (57%) did not have their race and ethnicity recorded, which limits interpretation of this finding. The study found that substance-involved deaths were most likely to occur between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m. Overall, the largest number of pedestrian deaths occurred on Saturday evenings. Pedestrians who had taken drugs were more likely to be on a road and not on a crosswalk when struck by a vehicle. Based on their findings, the researchers suggest that substance use may contribute to riskier crossing behaviors and that a reduction in visibility at night may give drivers insufficient time to react and avoid a collision. A limitation of the study is that the analysis examined only fatalities, and the findings cannot necessarily be extrapolated to nonfatal injuries. Additionally, the researchers coded an individual as negative for alcohol and drugs when testing was not conducted or results were unavailable, which may have underestimated the true prevalence of substance use. The researchers note that the presence of alcohol or drugs does not necessarily mean that impairment was solely responsible for the fatality or that it directly contributed to the crash. Nevertheless, they say impairment—including impaired decision-making and walking—may have played a role in the sequence of events leading to a fatal motor vehicle crash. They call for strategies that promote safe drinking practices and encourage safe travel home for anyone who may be impaired from alcohol or drugs. Publication details Substance use among fatally-injured pedestrians in New York State, USA: a retrospective analysis, Injury Prevention (2026).
