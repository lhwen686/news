英文原文

Original
New research from the Garvan Institute of Medical Research has revealed for the first time that two closely related bowel diseases, currently treated in the same way, are fundamentally different. The findings help explain why one carries a much higher risk of colon cancer – and point to the need for different approaches to treatment. Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) is a chronic liver disease affecting around 1,000 Australians that blocks the bile ducts and causes scarring of the liver. Around 70 per cent of patients also suffer from a form of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) which resembles ulcerative colitis. Despite similarities between these conditions, people with PSC have less severe flares but face a colorectal cancer risk three times higher than those with ulcerative colitis alone. That combination – milder-looking disease with much greater cancer risk – has long puzzled researchers. Now, findings published in Nature Communications bring us one step closer to an answer. Garvan researchers used cutting-edge methods to examine biopsies from several regions of the colon in patients with each condition, alongside healthy volunteers. The team showed that the gut of people with PSC-associated IBD had a distinct microbiome and showed a higher level of immune activity, even in the absence of inflammation, or 'flare-ups'. Dr Kylie James, Lab Head at the Garvan Institute and lead author of the paper says these findings open doors to more specialized treatment of PSC patients, which should ultimately lead to a reduction in colon cancer risk. "Chronic, low-level inflammation is a known contributor to cancer risk, and our findings may explain why these patients with a milder-looking disease have a much greater chance of developing colorectal cancer. Knowing more about this disease means we can now look to develop more targeted treatments against the inflammation, even when the person isn't having obvious symptoms of an IBD flare." Hidden inflammation, undetectable by current methods The Garvan team compared biopsies from eight patients with PSC-associated IBD, 11 patients with ulcerative colitis alone, and 10 people with no gastrointestinal disease. The researchers went beyond what colonoscopy and standard biopsy examination normally show, using single-cell sequencing, spatial mapping and microbiome analysis to provide a comprehensive comparison of these conditions. The researchers found that even in tissue that a pathologist had inspected under a microscope and classified as free from inflammation, there was a build-up of cytotoxic, or cell-killing, immune cells in the colon of patients with PSC-related bowel disease. These cells are a normal part of the immune system, but finding them so concentrated in tissue that would otherwise be considered 'normal' was unexpected. In IBD, we've long known that sustained remission should mean quiet immune cells, not just quiet symptoms. What we're finding here goes further – there's immune activity happening in tissue that a pathologist would call quiet, and it's in the very part of the colon where these patients face their highest cancer risk. We think this constant, background inflammation could be driving the development of colorectal cancer." Dr. Kylie James, Lab Head, Garvan Institute and lead author of the paper Dr Jacqueline Tearle, postdoctoral researcher at Garvan and first author of the study adds: "This is the first time this kind of immune activity has been mapped in these patients, and it tells us where to focus. If we can find ways to detect this activity in the clinic, there's real potential to reduce the cancer risk that comes with it." A shared immune signal during flare-ups The team's other main finding came from looking at patients during active flare-ups. In both diseases, a population of mast cells – immune cells involved in inflammation and allergic responses – built up in the gut when disease was active. Mast cells are fragile and are typically destroyed during the standard freezing of biopsy samples, which has meant they were often overlooked in earlier studies. By processing patient tissue immediately after collection, the researchers were able to preserve them and study them in detail. "We found quite a unique mast cell population, and its gene expression suggests it could contribute to cancer risk during periods of active inflammation or flare ups," says Dr Tearle. Towards treatment guided by biology These insights could improve PSC monitoring by focusing on underlying inflammation while providing a rationale for exploring more targeted treatments. "For patients living with PSC-related bowel disease, the risk of colorectal cancer is a real concern, and current tools don't always capture what's actually happening in the gut," says Associate Professor Simon Ghaly, gastroenterologist at St Vincent's Hospital Sydney, who led patient recruitment for the study. "Findings like these bring us closer to a more informed way of monitoring, and eventually treating, these patients." The team hopes that identifying these cellular features will eventually lead to treatments matched to the underlying biology of each condition, rather than to shared symptoms alone. They are now examining changes in the cells lining the colon using gut models grown from patients' own cells, to better understand how they translate into cancer risk.

加尔文医学研究所的新研究首次揭示，两种目前治疗方式相同但密切相关的肠道疾病，在本质上存在根本差异。这些发现有助于解释为何其中一种疾病携带更高的结肠癌风险，并指出需要采取不同的治疗方法。 原发性硬化性胆管炎（PSC）是一种慢性肝病，影响约1000名澳大利亚人，它会导致胆管阻塞和肝脏瘢痕形成。约70%的PSC患者同时患有一种类似于溃疡性结肠炎的炎症性肠病（IBD）。尽管这些疾病之间存在相似性，但PSC患者的病情发作通常不那么严重，却面临比单纯溃疡性结肠炎患者高三倍的结直肠癌风险。这种“病情看似较轻但癌症风险却高得多”的组合，长期以来一直困扰着研究人员。 如今，发表在《自然通讯》上的研究结果使我们离答案更近了一步。加尔文研究所的研究人员采用尖端方法，对患有这两种疾病的患者以及健康志愿者的结肠多个区域的活检样本进行了检查。该团队发现，PSC相关IBD患者的肠道具有独特的微生物组，并表现出更高水平的免疫活动，即便在没有炎症或“病情发作”的情况下也是如此。 加尔文研究所实验室主任、论文主要作者Kylie James博士表示，这些发现为PSC患者的更专业化治疗打开了大门，并最终应能降低结肠癌风险。 “慢性、低水平炎症是已知的癌症风险因素，我们的发现可能解释了为何这些看似病情较轻的患者患结直肠癌的几率要高得多。对这种疾病有了更深入的了解意味着我们现在可以着手开发针对炎症的更具靶向性的治疗方法，即使患者没有明显的IBD发作症状。” 隐藏的炎症，现有方法无法检测 加尔文团队比较了8名PSC相关IBD患者、11名单纯溃疡性结肠炎患者以及10名无胃肠道疾病者的活检样本。研究人员超越了结肠镜检查和标准活检通常能揭示的范围，利用单细胞测序、空间图谱和微生物组分析，对这些疾病进行了全面比较。 研究人员发现，即使是在病理学家显微镜检查后判定为无炎症的组织中，PSC相关肠病患者的结肠内也存在细胞毒性（或称杀伤细胞）免疫细胞的积聚。这些细胞是免疫系统的正常组成部分，但在本应被视为“正常”的组织中发现它们如此高度集中是出乎意料的。 “在IBD中，我们长期以来都知道持续缓解应意味着免疫细胞的平静，而不仅仅是症状的平静。我们在这里的发现更进一步——即使在病理学家会认为是平静的组织中，也存在免疫活动，而且正是在这些患者面临最高癌症风险的结肠部位。我们认为这种持续的、背景性炎症可能正在驱动结直肠癌的发展。” 加尔文研究所实验室主任、论文主要作者Kylie James博士说。 加尔文研究所博士后研究员、该研究的第一作者Jacqueline Tearle博士补充道：“这是首次在这类患者中绘制出这种免疫活动的图谱，它指明了我们应该关注的方向。如果我们能找到在临床中检测这种活动的方法，那么就有真实的潜力来降低随之而来的癌症风险。” 病情发作期间的共同免疫信号 该团队的另一个主要发现来自于对处于活跃发作期患者的观察。在这两种疾病中，当疾病活跃时，肥大细胞（参与炎症和过敏反应的免疫细胞）都在肠道中积聚。 肥大细胞很脆弱，通常在活检样本的标准冷冻过程中被破坏，这意味着它们在早期研究中常常被忽视。通过在采集后立即处理患者组织，研究人员得以保存并详细研究它们。 “我们发现了一个相当独特的肥大细胞群，其基因表达表明它可能在活跃炎症或病情发作期间增加癌症风险，”Tearle博士说。 迈向生物学指导下的治疗 这些见解可以通过关注潜在炎症来改善PSC的监测，同时也为探索更具靶向性的治疗方法提供了理论依据。 “对于患有PSC相关肠病的患者而言，结直肠癌的风险是一个真正的问题，而目前的工具并不总能捕捉到肠道内实际发生的情况，”悉尼圣文森特医院胃肠病学家、负责该研究患者招募的Simon Ghaly副教授说。“像这样的发现使我们更接近于一种更明智的方式来监测并最终治疗这些患者。” 该团队希望识别这些细胞特征最终能促使开发出与每种疾病潜在生物学相匹配而非仅仅针对共同症状的治疗方法。他们目前正在利用患者自身细胞培养的肠道模型，检查结肠内壁细胞的变化，以更好地理解这些变化如何转化为癌症风险。
