英文原文

Original
The Hairy Cell Leukemia Foundation and Blood Cancer United®, formerly The Leukemia & Lymphoma Society, today announced six new research grants as part of their ongoing strategic partnership to better understand what drives hairy cell leukemia and find better therapies on the road to cures for this rare form of blood cancer. For patients with classic hairy cell leukemia, we've achieved years – often decades – of remission. Patients with variant disease, and those who relapse, deserve treatments that work just as well. These grants are how we close that gap – and they exist because patients and families chose to fund this work." Anna Lambertson, executive director, Hairy Cell Leukemia Foundation The latest grants are part of the HCL2030 program, which continues and expands the organizations' six-year partnership. The grants will invest nearly $7 million over the next five years in research at worldclass institutions in the U.S. and Europe. Advances in understanding the underlying biology of hairy cell leukemia led to the current understanding that it has two distinct types: classic and variant. Classic hairy cell leukemia is much more common, accounting for up to 90% of cases. With current treatments, most patients will remain in remission for five to 15 years or more. Variant hairy cell leukemia, as well as classic hairy cell leukemia that has relapsed, carry a much worse prognosis. These areas are a focus of the latest round of grants. Research awards support all aspects of diagnosis, prognosis and care The current set of awards zero in on a range of aspects to help patients from initial diagnosis and throughout their treatment. This year's awardees: Christopher Oakes, Ph.D., at The Ohio State University, is finding ways to advance understanding and classification of hairy cell leukemia in individual patients, the first step in ensuring they get the right treatment. Gregory Abel, M.D., M.P.H., at Dana-Farber Cancer Institute in Boston, is examining quality of life, decision-making and outcomes for patients with hairy cell leukemia. Gerlinde Wernig, M.D., at Stanford University in California, is focused on finding new methods to improve disease prognosis, including understanding factors leading to relapse and development of variant disease. Reshmi Parameswaran, Ph.D., at Case Western Reserve University in Cleveland, is examining how hairy cell leukemia becomes resistant to treatment and developing new treatment strategies for its variant form. Enrico Tiacci, M.D., at the University of Perugia in Italy will be testing a combination all-oral treatment for patients whose hairy cell leukemia has relapsed. This is a continuation of his groundbreaking work, also supported by research funds from this partnership, that established a drug called vemurafenib as an alternative to chemotherapy-based standard of care. Francesco Forconi, M.D., Ph.D., at the University of Southhampton in the UK, is testing whether treatment with a combination of two drugs that inhibit different proteins involved in hairy cell leukemia can lead to deep, long-lasting remissions that would allow patients to stop treatment after a fixed period and remain in remission instead of having to take treatment indefinitely. "Medical research is not a solo sport. Even though researchers are working at different locations across two continents, we are all very much on the same team, and that is particularly important for rare diseases such as hairy cell leukemia. It takes cooperation, time and money to find the advances that will make a real difference for patients," said Lore Gruenbaum, Ph.D., chief scientific officer at Blood Cancer United.

“毛细胞白血病基金会”和“血癌联合会”（前身为“白血病与淋巴瘤协会”）今天宣布了六项新的研究拨款。这是其持续战略伙伴关系的一部分，旨在更好地了解毛细胞白血病的发病机制，并为这种罕见的血癌寻找更优的治疗方法，最终实现治愈。 “对于经典型毛细胞白血病患者，我们已经实现了数年乃至数十年的缓解。而对于变异型疾病患者和那些复发患者，他们同样值得获得疗效显著的治疗。这些拨款正是我们弥合这一差距的方式——它们之所以存在，是因为患者和家属选择了资助这项工作。”毛细胞白血病基金会执行董事安娜·兰伯森表示。 最新一批拨款是HCL2030项目的一部分，该项目延续并扩展了这两个组织长达六年的合作伙伴关系。这些拨款将在未来五年内，向美国和欧洲的世界级机构投入近700万美元用于研究。 对毛细胞白血病潜在生物学机制理解的进步，促成了当前将其分为两种截然不同类型——经典型和变异型——的认知。 经典型毛细胞白血病更为常见，约占病例总数的90%。通过目前的治疗方法，大多数患者能够保持五年到十五年甚至更长时间的缓解。 变异型毛细胞白血病，以及复发的经典型毛细胞白血病，预后要差得多。这些领域正是最新一轮拨款的重点关注方向。 研究奖项支持诊断、预后和护理的各个方面 当前的这批奖项聚焦于一系列方面，旨在帮助患者从初步诊断到整个治疗过程。今年的获奖者包括： 俄亥俄州立大学的克里斯托弗·奥克斯博士正在探索如何推进对个体患者毛细胞白血病的理解和分类，这是确保患者获得正确治疗的第一步。 波士顿丹娜法伯癌症研究所的格雷戈里·阿贝尔医学博士、公共卫生硕士正在研究毛细胞白血病患者的生活质量、决策制定和治疗结果。 加利福尼亚斯坦福大学的格林德·韦尼格医学博士致力于寻找改善疾病预后的新方法，包括理解导致复发和变异型疾病发展的因素。 克利夫兰凯斯西储大学的雷什米·帕拉梅斯瓦兰博士正在研究毛细胞白血病如何对治疗产生耐药性，并为其变异形式开发新的治疗策略。 意大利佩鲁贾大学的恩里科·蒂亚奇医学博士将对复发性毛细胞白血病患者测试一种全口服联合治疗方案。这是他开创性工作的延续，这项工作也得到了本次合作研究资金的支持，其成果是确立了药物维罗非尼（vemurafenib）作为化疗标准疗法的替代方案。 英国南安普顿大学的弗朗西斯科·福科尼医学博士、哲学博士正在测试两种抑制毛细胞白血病相关不同蛋白质的药物联合治疗，是否能带来深度、持久的缓解，从而使患者在固定疗程后能够停药并保持缓解，而无需无限期地接受治疗。 “医学研究并非单打独斗的运动。尽管研究人员分布在两大洲的不同地点，但我们都在同一个团队中，这对于毛细胞白血病这类罕见疾病尤为重要。要找到能真正改善患者状况的突破性进展，需要合作、时间和资金。”血癌联合会首席科学官洛尔·格鲁恩鲍姆博士说。
