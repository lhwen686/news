Scientists map protective cell networks hiding dormant breast tumor cells

Original
Researchers at the MRC Laboratory of Medical Sciences (LMS), Imperial College London and UCL Genetics Institute have created a detailed map of cells within breast tumors and identified distinct neighborhoods of proliferating and dormant cells. The study, published in Genome Medicine, reveals that protective immune and connective cells surround the hibernating cancer cells, potentially shielding them from treatments. It suggests future therapies may need to target not only different cancer cell states, but also the local environments that help them survive, to both halt tumor growth and prevent cancer coming back later. The landscape of a tumor is diverse. Nestled among the frantically dividing cells are immune cells, newly-formed blood vessels, and a class of suspiciously quiet cancer cells that can have devastating effects. These dormant, or 'quiescent', cells can evade treatment and subsequently lead to cancer spread or resurgence. Researchers at the LMS, Imperial and UCL are hunting these cells, asking where they sit within untreated tumors, what characterizes them, and what surrounds them. The team have used publicly-available data to create a map of breast cancer tumors, and found distinct pockets of quiescent cells, surrounded by cells that may act as a protective guard. Silent but deadly "Quiescent cancer cells are very dangerous," explains Dr Alexis Barr, co-lead author and head of the Cell Cycle Control group at the LMS. "These cells can hide from chemotherapy and then remain in this dormant quiescent state in the tumor, and then later reactivate to drive proliferation." Some cancer cells take up this state, essentially pausing their progress, in response to the stress of tumor life, where nutrients and blood flow can take time to keep up with rapid tumor expansion. Like a bear hibernating through harsh conditions, they wait until more favorable conditions return, potentially after any treatment is finished. Alexis adds, "If we want to achieve long term control of peoples' tumors and prevent tumor relapse, we have to focus on these dormant quiescent cancer cells, and have to understand more about them." To do this, Alexis worked with Dr Maria Secrier's computational biology team at UCL to establish a detailed map of the tumor and its surrounding immune and support cells. They combined single-cell RNA sequencing, which showed which genes individual cells were using, with spatial transcriptomics, which then mapped the cells and their neighbors within the tumor. "We found cells that resemble therapy-resistant cells already residing in the tumor before we give any treatment," says Maria, which suggests that features linked to treatment resistance can be built into the tumor rather than only developing in response to treatment. This was the case both in aggressive tumor types and slower-developing classes – a surprising finding because quiescence had previously been associated with slower-growing disease. Gated communities The team's tumor analysis didn't just map cancer cells, but also the wide variety of support cells that are drawn into tumor growth. And they found a repeating pattern: dormant cells were commonly found near CXCL10-positive macrophages (a type of immune cell) and myofibroblastic cancer-associated fibroblasts (a type of tumor-supporting cell). These cells appear to have been co-opted to protect the cells, possibly forming a barrier to keep cancer-killing immune cells or cancer treatments from disturbing the sleepy neighborhood. "The cancer cells are really encapsulated within these areas of macrophages and fibroblasts that we think act as shields for these dormant cancer cells," said Maria. "But we don't yet know the direction of cause and effect: whether the surrounding cells push cancer cells into dormancy or if the cancer cells attract or alter their surroundings. It's very likely coming from both sides." Treating every part of a tumor Many chemotherapy drugs are most effective against rapidly dividing cells. But as any teacher knows, you can't ignore the quiet ones. This study suggests proliferating and dormant regions may have different drug sensitivities. The analysis found increased activity of the complement pathway (a part of the immune system) within dormant niches, which may make these regions vulnerable to treatments targeting this pathway. The surrounding support cells may provide another potential target, although their role in maintaining dormancy still needs to be tested. "Different parts of the tumor will likely respond to different drugs," said Maria. "If we understand what drug combinations we can use to target both the proliferative and the dormant areas, potentially that could be more successful than current therapies. This is giving us a first insight into how we can then intervene with different therapeutics that specifically target different areas of the tumor where the cells have adapted and have evolved differently." Alexis said, "It is clearly important to focus on proliferative cancer cells, but we also need to understand this population of quiescent dormant cancer cells. And that's been less studied." The predictions of this research will now need to be tested experimentally, but identifying in-built treatment-resistant regions in tumors and deciphering their support systems may ultimately offer a way to design more effective treatment combinations. Putting quiescent cells on the map will lead towards better cancer treatments that last. This work was primarily funded by a UKRI Future Leaders Fellowship, the Medical Research Council and the Biotechnology and Biological Sciences Research Council.

英国医学研究委员会医学科学实验室（LMS）、伦敦帝国理工学院和伦敦大学学院遗传学研究所的研究人员绘制了一份乳腺肿瘤内细胞的详细图谱，并识别出增殖细胞和休眠细胞的独特区域。这项发表在《基因组医学》（Genome Medicine）上的研究揭示，保护性免疫细胞和结缔组织细胞围绕着这些休眠的癌细胞，可能使其免受治疗影响。这表明未来的疗法可能不仅需要靶向不同状态的癌细胞，还需要靶向帮助它们生存的局部微环境，以期既能阻止肿瘤生长，又能预防癌症后期复发。 肿瘤的内部环境是多种多样的。在那些疯狂分裂的细胞中，夹杂着免疫细胞、新形成的血管以及一类异常静默但可能带来毁灭性后果的癌细胞。这些休眠或“静止”细胞可以逃避治疗，随后导致癌症扩散或复发。LMS、帝国理工学院和伦敦大学学院的研究人员正在追踪这些细胞，探究它们在未经治疗的肿瘤中的位置、特征以及周围环境。该团队利用公开数据绘制了乳腺癌肿瘤的图谱，并发现了明显的静止细胞区域，这些区域被可能充当保护屏障的细胞所包围。 沉寂却致命 “静止癌细胞非常危险，”该研究的共同主要作者、LMS细胞周期控制小组负责人亚历克西斯·巴尔博士解释道。“这些细胞可以躲避化疗，然后以这种休眠静止状态留在肿瘤中，之后再重新激活以驱动增殖。” 一些癌细胞进入这种状态，实质上是暂停了它们的进展，以应对肿瘤生存的压力，即营养物质和血流可能难以跟上肿瘤的快速扩张。就像熊在恶劣条件下冬眠一样，它们等待更有利的条件回来，可能是在任何治疗结束后。亚历克西斯补充说：“如果我们想实现对患者肿瘤的长期控制并预防肿瘤复发，我们就必须关注这些休眠静止癌细胞，并对其进行更深入的了解。” 为此，亚历克西斯与伦敦大学学院玛丽亚·塞克里尔博士的计算生物学团队合作，建立了肿瘤及其周围免疫细胞和支持细胞的详细图谱。他们将单细胞RNA测序（显示单个细胞使用哪些基因）与空间转录组学（随后绘制了肿瘤内细胞及其邻近细胞的图谱）相结合。 “我们发现，在进行任何治疗之前，肿瘤中就已经存在类似耐药细胞的细胞，”玛丽亚说。这表明与治疗抵抗相关的特征可能是肿瘤固有的，而不仅仅是作为对治疗的反应而产生。侵袭性肿瘤类型和进展缓慢的类型都存在这种情况——这是一个令人惊讶的发现，因为此前静止状态一直与生长缓慢的疾病相关联。 封闭社区 该团队的肿瘤分析不仅绘制了癌细胞的图谱，还绘制了被肿瘤生长吸引而来的各种支持细胞的图谱。他们发现了一个重复出现的模式：休眠细胞通常发现于CXCL10阳性巨噬细胞（一种免疫细胞）和肌成纤维细胞样肿瘤相关成纤维细胞（一种肿瘤支持细胞）附近。这些细胞似乎已被癌细胞利用来保护自己，可能形成一道屏障，阻止杀伤癌细胞的免疫细胞或癌症治疗干扰这些静默区域。 玛丽亚说：“癌细胞确实被包裹在这些巨噬细胞和成纤维细胞区域中，我们认为它们是这些休眠癌细胞的保护屏障。但我们尚不清楚因果关系的方向：是周围细胞促使癌细胞进入休眠，还是癌细胞吸引或改变了周围环境。很可能两者兼而有之。” 治疗肿瘤的每一个部分 许多化疗药物对快速分裂的细胞最有效。但正如任何老师都知道的，你不能忽视那些静默的学生。这项研究表明，增殖区和休眠区可能具有不同的药物敏感性。分析发现，在休眠生态位中补体途径（免疫系统的一部分）的活性增加，这可能使这些区域对靶向该通路的治疗敏感。周围的支持细胞可能提供另一个潜在靶点，尽管它们在维持休眠中的作用仍需实验验证。 “肿瘤的不同部分可能对不同的药物有反应，”玛丽亚说。“如果我们能理解哪些药物组合可以同时靶向增殖区和休眠区，那么这可能比目前的疗法更成功。这为我们提供了初步的见解，了解如何通过特异性靶向肿瘤中细胞已经适应并以不同方式演变的区域，来采用不同的治疗方法进行干预。” 亚历克西斯说：“显然，关注增殖性癌细胞很重要，但我们也需要了解静止休眠癌细胞这个群体。而这方面研究较少。” 这项研究的预测现在需要进行实验验证，但识别肿瘤中固有的耐药区域并破解其支持系统，最终可能提供一种设计更有效治疗组合的方法。将静止细胞纳入研究范围，将有助于开发更持久有效的癌症治疗。 这项工作主要由英国研究与创新局未来领袖奖学金、医学研究委员会以及生物技术与生物科学研究委员会资助。
