A new depression treatment may rival electroconvulsive therapy while avoiding one of its biggest drawbacks

An international clinical trial led by researchers at the Center for Addiction and Mental Health (CAMH) and University of California San Diego School of Medicine, published in The Lancet Psychiatry, has found that magnetic seizure therapy (MST) is as effective as electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)—the current gold-standard treatment for severe, treatment-resistant depression—with significantly fewer cognitive side effects.

The study is the first large-scale, randomized clinical trial to directly compare MST with ECT, marking an important step toward a new treatment option for patients.

The trial, co-led by CAMH's Dr. Daniel Blumberger and Dr. Zafiris Daskalakis at UC San Diego, was a randomized, double-blind, non-inferiority study conducted between 2018 and 2024. It enrolled nearly 300 participants across three academic centers: CAMH in Toronto, University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center in Dallas, and UC San Diego.

Researchers found that nearly half (48%) of patients in both treatment groups experienced a meaningful clinical response, demonstrating that MST is comparable to ECT in improving depressive symptoms. At the same time, MST showed a substantially better cognitive safety profile, particularly in preserving memory.

ECT, while highly effective, is known to cause cognitive side effects including post-treatment confusion and both short- and long-term memory loss, which remain a major barrier for patients considering the treatment.

"This is a major milestone for the field," said Dr. Daniel Blumberger, Senior Scientist at CAMH and co-lead of the study. "For decades, we've known that ECT is one of the most effective treatments for severe depression, but its cognitive side effects have limited its use.

"Our findings show that magnetic seizure therapy can deliver similar benefits with much less impact on memory, which could make this kind of treatment a more viable option for many people who need it."

Up to a third of people with major depressive disorder do not respond to standard treatments such as medications and psychotherapy. While ECT can be life-saving, only a small proportion of eligible patients choose to undergo it, largely due to concerns about cognitive side effects.

MST works by using magnetic stimulation to induce a therapeutic seizure, similar to ECT, but in a more targeted way that avoids areas of the brain associated with memory.

Researchers emphasize that further work is needed to support regulatory approval, training, and implementation. However, the findings lay the groundwork for MST to become a new standard option for people with severe depression, particularly those who might otherwise decline ECT.

"This study represents an important first step toward bringing MST into wider clinical use," added Dr. Zafiris Daskalakis, Professor and Chair of the Department of Psychiatry and Dr. Igor and JoAnn Grant Endowed Chair at UC San Diego School of Medicine.

"If approved and implemented more broadly, it could transform how we deliver brain stimulation therapies and significantly improve the patient experience."

由成瘾与精神健康中心（CAMH）和加州大学圣迭戈分校医学院的研究人员主导、发表在《柳叶刀·精神病学》上的一项国际临床试验发现，磁惊厥治疗（MST）与目前重度难治性抑郁症的金标准治疗——电休克治疗（ECT）——效果相当，但认知副作用显著减少。

这项研究是首次大规模、随机临床试验，直接比较了MST与ECT的效果，标志着为患者提供新治疗方案迈出了重要一步。

该试验由CAMH的Daniel Blumberger博士和加州大学圣迭戈分校的Zafiris Daskalakis博士共同牵头，是一项于2018年至2024年间进行的随机、双盲、非劣效性研究。该研究招募了近300名参与者，分布在三个学术中心：多伦多的CAMH、达拉斯的德克萨斯大学西南医学中心以及加州大学圣迭戈分校。

研究人员发现，两个治疗组中近一半（48%）的患者都出现了有意义的临床反应，这表明MST在改善抑郁症状方面与ECT相当。同时，MST显示出显著更优的认知安全性，尤其是在记忆力保护方面。

ECT虽然高效，但已知会引起认知副作用，包括治疗后意识模糊以及短期和长期记忆丧失，这些仍然是患者考虑该治疗方案的主要障碍。

“这是该领域的一个重要里程碑，”CAMH高级科学家兼该研究共同负责人Daniel Blumberger博士说。“几十年来，我们一直知道ECT是治疗重度抑郁症最有效的疗法之一，但其认知副作用限制了其应用。”

“我们的研究结果表明，磁惊厥治疗能够提供类似疗效，同时对记忆力的影响小得多，这可能使这种治疗成为许多有需要的人更可行的选择。”

高达三分之一的重度抑郁症患者对药物和心理治疗等标准疗法无反应。尽管ECT可以挽救生命，但只有一小部分符合条件的患者选择接受它，这主要源于对认知副作用的担忧。

MST通过磁刺激诱导治疗性惊厥，其原理与ECT类似，但方式更具靶向性，可避开与记忆相关的脑区。

研究人员强调，仍需开展进一步工作以支持监管审批、培训和实施。然而，这些发现为MST成为重度抑郁症患者的新标准选择奠定了基础，特别是对于那些可能因此拒绝ECT的患者。

“如果获得批准并更广泛地实施，它将改变我们提供脑刺激疗法的方式，并显著改善患者体验。”
