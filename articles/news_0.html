New US Medicare pricing policies could reshape global pharmaceutical markets

深度解析

该文章深度剖析了美国联邦医疗保险（Medicare）新推出的“最惠国”定价政策及其对全球制药行业和药品市场的影响。其核心专业概念在于“最惠国”定价机制，这是一种参照定价模式，将美国联邦医疗保险支付的药品价格与其他高收入国家的较低价格挂钩，旨在大幅削减美国的处方药支出，解决美国药品价格长期偏高的问题。 从商业逻辑角度看，这项政策给制药商带来了巨大的战略调整压力。由于政策规定美国药品价格将参照其他国家的低价，如果制药商在这些参考国保持低价，其在美国联邦医疗保险中可能损失的收入将远超在这些国家的销售额。因此，制药商有强烈动机调整其全球定价和上市策略，例如提高在参考国的药品价格、延迟新药在海外市场的上市、或通过开发新剂型、将公开折扣转为保密回扣等方式，以规避政策影响。 这项政策的行业影响深远。一方面，它有可能为美国政府和纳税人节省数十亿美元，并推动全球药品定价模式的变革。另一方面，它可能引发全球药品可及性问题，特别是在参考国家，新药上市可能会被延迟。此外，文中指出的制药商与政府达成的“私下协议”是政策有效性的一大挑战，这些保密协议显著削弱了预期的节省额，揭示了在药品定价改革中，政策制定者与行业利益博弈的复杂性。这使得政策的透明度和实际效果受到质疑，并凸显了医疗成本控制与制药创新及市场策略之间的微妙平衡。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: SHVETS production from Pexels A new modeling study published in The Lancet suggests that U.S. Medicare's new "Most-Favored-Nation" pricing policy, which ties what Medicare pays for medicines to prices charged in other high-income countries, could push pharmaceutical manufacturers to raise prices or delay launches. For about three in four medicines studied, the resulting Medicare savings would be worth almost four times that medicine's entire annual sales in the country used to set its price, potentially giving manufacturers a strong incentive to change how they price and launch products outside the U.S. The policy could cut Medicare's prescription drug spending by $5.2 billion (16%) under GLOBE, which covers medicines given in hospitals and clinics, and $6.4 billion (18%) under GUARD, which covers medicines bought at pharmacies, during its initial phase, which would cover about 25% of the Medicare program. These savings could rise to an estimated $21 billion under GLOBE and $25.5 billion under GUARD if expanded to all Medicare beneficiaries. But the study estimates that reported confidential deals between manufacturers and the Trump administration could cut the policy's overall Medicare savings: For the initial group of 17 manufacturers with announced agreements, the study's projected savings would be cut by 71%. Medicare is the U.S. government health insurance program covering around 68 million older and disabled people living in the U.S. It was historically barred from negotiating medicine prices directly with manufacturers until the 2022 Inflation Reduction Act authorized it to negotiate prices for a small number of costly medicines. The Trump administration's new "Most-Favored-Nation" policy goes further, aligning what Medicare pays for brand-name medicines with prices in comparable high-income countries, adjusted for purchasing power. Under the GLOBE and GUARD pricing models, a randomly selected 25% of Medicare beneficiaries would be covered over a five-year period. Because both pricing models tie what Medicare pays to prices charged in other countries, they could also reshape drug pricing and market strategy outside the U.S. as manufacturers respond to the new U.S. benchmarks. How the pricing models work "The Trump administration's Most-Favored-Nation pricing models have the potential to deliver real savings to the U.S. federal government and taxpayers. But if manufacturers can evade participation in these models by striking side deals, most of those savings might not be realized," said Thomas Hwang of Brigham and Women's Hospital and lead author of the study. "People living in the United States have long paid more for medicines than virtually anywhere else in the world. The Most-Favored-Nation pricing models were meant to address this gap, but their scope is limited by various exemptions and will likely face legal challenges," added study author Aaron Kesselheim of Brigham and Women's Hospital (U.S.). Researchers analyzed 195 patented medicines that together account for $87.9 billion of Medicare's annual spending ($32.7 billion through GLOBE and $55.2 billion through GUARD). Because manufacturers' actual rebates and discounts are confidential, the researchers estimated Medicare's actual ("net") prices for these medicines. They then compared these net prices with prices in the 19 reference countries under the proposed rules to estimate how much Medicare could save. The researchers also modeled two changes to the policy: expanding the rules to cover all Medicare beneficiaries instead of the current 25% and excluding manufacturers with confidential deals. Pressure on overseas pricing Without any exemptions, the researchers estimate Medicare could save $5.2 billion (16%) under GLOBE and $6.4 billion (18%) under GUARD during the policy's initial phase. Expanding the rules to cover all Medicare beneficiaries could raise these savings to $21 billion and $25.5 billion. Under these payment models, the lowest price used as an international benchmark was found to be 71% lower than what Medicare pays, with South Korea, Norway and Australia as the most common countries referenced by Medicare to set pricing. The study also suggests that among the 138 medicines with available sales data, the estimated cut to Medicare spending was about 3.8 times bigger than that medicine's total annual sales in the country used to set its U.S. reference price. In addition, for about 73% of these medicines (101 of 138), the estimated cut would exceed the medicine's entire annual sales there. For most of these medicines, keeping prices low in other countries would cost manufacturers more in lost Medicare revenue than they earn from selling there, creating a strong incentive to raise prices or block them from being visible to Medicare for referencing. Manufacturers could try to do that by developing different formulations, converting existing discounts into confidential rebates or delaying launches. "Policies in the U.S. may affect access to medicines globally. Policymakers should ensure that availability of important medicines is not delayed as a result," said study author Kerstin Vokinger of ETH Zurich and University of Zurich (Switzerland). "Referenced countries, from Germany to Japan to Australia, are facing substantial pressure from the U.S. administration and industry to raise prices and spending on medicines," added Hwang. "But this is colliding with the reality that other countries have limited budget room to give." Side deals erode projected savings An initial group of 17 pharmaceutical companies struck separate, confidential deals with the Trump administration that reportedly include exemptions from Medicare's GLOBE and GUARD rules. These companies account for 131 of the 195 medicines studied (67%). If those medicines were excluded from the pricing rules, the researchers estimate the potential savings would shrink by 71%, leaving just $3.3 billion (28.7%) in savings ($0.9 billion under GLOBE and $2.4 billion under GUARD). "The latest round of deals announced on Aug. 31 brings the reported tally to 26 companies. By our updated estimate, exemptions would push savings lost from an estimated 71% in our study to nearly 80%. Because these deals aren't public, it is challenging for policymakers and the public to judge whether Most-Favored-Nation pricing is delivering what was promised," said Hwang. The authors note some study limitations. The estimates rely on modeled Medicare net prices and on international prices drawn from public databases, which may not reflect other countries' confidential discounts. The medicine list, based on historical Medicare data, may not capture newly approved medicines or future policy changes. Finally, the data presented in this study are estimates and cannot predict how manufacturers or insurers will respond to the new rules in practice, including whether manufacturers will change their launch or pricing plans in reference countries. 然而，该研究估计，制药商与特朗普政府之间据报达成的秘密协议可能会削减该政策的整体联邦医疗保险节省额：对于首批17家已宣布达成协议的制药商，研究预测的节省额将减少71%。 联邦医疗保险是美国政府的医疗保险计划，覆盖约6800万居住在美国的老年人和残疾人。历史上，联邦医疗保险被禁止直接与制药商谈判药品价格，直到2022年《通胀削减法案》授权其可就少数昂贵药品进行价格谈判。特朗普政府新的“最惠国”政策更进一步，它将联邦医疗保险支付的品牌药品价格与可比高收入国家的经过购买力调整后的价格挂钩。根据GLOBE和GUARD定价模型，将在五年内随机选择25%的联邦医疗保险受益人进行覆盖。 由于这两种定价模型都将联邦医疗保险的支付价格与其他国家收取的费用挂钩，因此随着制药商对新的美国基准做出反应，它们也可能重塑美国境外的药品定价和市场策略。 定价模型的工作原理 布莱根妇女医院的托马斯·黄（Thomas Hwang）是该研究的主要作者，他表示：“特朗普政府的最惠国定价模型有可能为美国联邦政府和纳税人带来真实的节省。但如果制药商能够通过达成私下协议来规避参与这些模型，那么大部分节省可能无法实现。” 布莱根妇女医院（美国）的研究作者亚伦·凯塞尔海姆（Aaron 研究人员分析了195种专利药品，这些药品合计占联邦医疗保险年度支出的879亿美元（其中GLOBE模式占327亿美元，GUARD模式占552亿美元）。由于制药商的实际回扣和折扣是保密的，研究人员估计了联邦医疗保险为这些药品支付的实际（“净”）价格。 随后，他们将这些净价格与拟议规则下19个参考国家的价格进行比较，以估算联邦医疗保险可能节省的金额。研究人员还模拟了该政策的两项变动：将规则范围扩大到覆盖所有联邦医疗保险受益人，而非目前的25%；以及排除已达成保密协议的制药商。 对海外定价的压力 在没有任何豁免的情况下，研究人员估计，在政策初始阶段，联邦医疗保险在GLOBE模式下可节省52亿美元（16%），在GUARD模式下可节省64亿美元（18%）。将规则范围扩大到覆盖所有联邦医疗保险受益人，可使这些节省额分别增至210亿美元和255亿美元。在这些支付模型下，作为国际基准的最低价格被发现比联邦医疗保险支付的价格低71%，其中韩国、挪威和澳大利亚是联邦医疗保险最常引用的定价参考国家。 该研究还表明，在138种有销售数据的药品中，联邦医疗保险支出削减的估计额大约是该药品在美国参考定价国家全年总销售额的3.8倍。此外，对于其中约73%的药品（138种中的101种），估计的削减额将超过该药品在该国全年销售额的总和。 首批17家制药公司与特朗普政府达成了单独的保密协议，据报道其中包括豁免联邦医疗保险的GLOBE和GUARD规则。这些公司占据了所研究的195种药品中的131种（67%）。如果这些药品被排除在定价规则之外，研究人员估计潜在的节省额将缩减71%，仅剩下33亿美元（28.7%）的节省（GLOBE模式下9亿美元，GUARD模式下24亿美元）。
