新冠免疫力对相关蝙蝠冠状病毒具有交叉反应性

COVID-19 immunity shows cross-reactivity against related bat coronaviruses

深度解析

这篇研究深入阐释了SARS样冠状病毒（沙贝病毒）的宿主细胞进入机制、免疫识别及其对公共卫生的深远影响。核心机制在于病毒通过刺突蛋白与宿主细胞表面的血管紧张素转换酶2（ACE2）受体结合，从而实现入侵。研究将病毒分为“广谱型”（能利用多种物种ACE2）和“专一型”（受体利用受限），发现广谱型主要与SARS-CoV-2亲缘关系密切。该研究的关键发现是COVID-19感染或疫苗接种所诱导的免疫力，对密切相关的蝙蝠沙贝病毒具有交叉识别和中和能力，甚至对部分远缘病毒也显示出一定程度的广谱保护。这表明，广泛的SARS-CoV-2暴露可能已为未来相关病毒的溢出感染设置了更高的免疫屏障。奥密克戎变异株在受体结合域的突变，进一步凸显了病毒进化对宿主易感性的动态影响。从行业角度看，这项研究为下一代疫苗和治疗药物的开发提供了重要线索。通过识别冠状病毒刺突蛋白的保守区域，研究人员有望开发出广谱保护性的“泛沙贝病毒”疫苗和单克隆抗体，从而应对未来潜在的冠状病毒大流行威胁。这不仅将改变疫苗研发的策略，从针对特定病原体转向针对病毒家族，也将显著提升全球大流行防范能力，指导更精准的病毒监测和风险评估。

英文原文

Original
People who recovered from COVID-19 infection or were vaccinated against SARS-CoV-2 are more likely to have immunity to other closely related bat coronaviruses that could have the potential to threaten future human health. In a joint study by The Pirbright Institute and King's College London (KCL), scientists found bat coronaviruses most likely to infect a wide range of animal species are those most closely related to SARS-CoV-2 and most likely to be recognized by immunity generated through COVID-19 infection. The research, funded by the Biotechnology and Biological Sciences Research Council (UK Research and Innovation - UKRI), Wellcome, and the Medical Research Council (MRC - part of UKRI), is published today in two companion articles in PLOS Biology. Researchers examined how a diverse group of bat sarbecoviruses - the coronavirus sub-group that includes SARS-CoV-1 and SARS-CoV-2 - interact with ACE2, the cellular receptor used by many coronaviruses to enter host cells. They investigated the ability of these viruses to use ACE2 proteins from a broad range of bat and mammalian species, including humans, livestock, rodents and animals previously proposed as intermediate hosts. The team discovered so-called "generalist" sarbecoviruses, which can use ACE2 receptors from many different species, were largely confined to the same evolutionary group as SARS-CoV-2. Pirbright postdoctoral scientist and lead author, Dr Nazia Thakur, said: "Our findings suggest that the bat coronaviruses currently considered most likely to spill over into humans are also most likely to be recognized by existing COVID-19 immunity. While spillover risk can never be eliminated, widespread exposure to SARS-CoV-2 may have raised the barrier for related viruses to establish themselves in human populations." The team analyzed 15 representative bat coronavirus spike proteins using libraries of ACE2 receptors from 34 species. Broad ACE2 usage was a defining feature of viruses closely related to SARS-CoV-2, including the BANAL viruses discovered in Laos. Viruses from other evolutionary groups, including a novel UK bat coronavirus RhGB07, showed more restricted receptor usage. To aid in their analyses the Spike structure of RhGB07 was also resolved by collaborators in Switzerland. The study also investigated how SARS-CoV-2 host range has evolved during the pandemic. Researchers found variants, particularly those within the Omicron lineage acquired mutations in the receptor binding domain which resulted in shifting patterns of ACE2 usage across animal species, highlighting the dynamic relationship between viral evolution and host susceptibility. To understand the implications for human health, the scientists examined blood samples from people who had recovered from COVID-19. Antibodies in these samples successfully neutralized a range of closely related bat coronaviruses. Importantly, researchers also detected lower levels of neutralization against more distant bat sarbecoviruses, suggesting some degree of broader protection. These viruses showed the strongest antigenic similarity to SARS-CoV-2, meaning they were more readily recognized and neutralized by antibodies generated following COVID-19 infection. In contrast, more distantly related bat coronaviruses tended to be "specialists", capable of using ACE2 receptors from only a limited range of hosts." Katie Doores, Professor of Viral Immunology at KCL Further experiments at KCL and Pirbright identified several monoclonal antibodies capable of recognising a remarkably diverse range of sarbecoviruses. The second companion article identified where these monoclonal antibodies bind, highlighting conserved regions of the coronavirus spike protein, and offering important clues for the development of next-generation vaccines and therapeutics. Dr Dalan Bailey, head of Pirbright's Viral Glycoproteins group, added: "One of the encouraging findings was evidence of cross-neutralization of even some of the more distantly related bat coronaviruses. This suggests that broadly protective, pan-sarbecovirus vaccines or therapeutics may be achievable." The work highlights the importance of understanding virus host range, receptor usage and immune recognition together rather than in isolation. By identifying characteristics that distinguish higher-risk viruses from those less likely to spill over into humans, researchers hope to strengthen global pandemic preparedness efforts.

感染过COVID-19或接种过SARS-CoV-2疫苗的人，更有可能对其他密切相关的蝙蝠冠状病毒产生免疫力，而这些病毒可能威胁未来人类健康。 英国皮尔布赖特研究所和伦敦国王学院（KCL）的一项联合研究发现，最有可能感染多种动物物种的蝙蝠冠状病毒，与SARS-CoV-2的亲缘关系最密切，并且最有可能被COVID-19感染所产生的免疫力识别。 这项研究由生物技术与生物科学研究理事会（英国研究与创新局 - UKRI）、维康信托基金和医学研究理事会（MRC - 英国研究与创新局的一部分）资助，今天以两篇配套文章的形式发表在《公共科学图书馆·生物学》上。 研究人员检查了一组多样化的蝙蝠SARS样冠状病毒（沙贝病毒）——包括SARS-CoV-1和SARS-CoV-2在内的冠状病毒亚群——如何与ACE2相互作用，ACE2是许多冠状病毒进入宿主细胞所使用的细胞受体。 他们调查了这些病毒利用来自多种蝙蝠和哺乳动物（包括人类、牲畜、啮齿动物以及先前被认为是中间宿主的动物）ACE2蛋白的能力。 该团队发现，所谓的“广谱型”沙贝病毒（能够利用多种不同物种的ACE2受体）主要局限于与SARS-CoV-2相同的进化群。 皮尔布赖特研究所博士后科学家兼主要作者纳齐亚·塔库尔博士表示：“我们的发现表明，目前被认为最有可能溢出感染人类的蝙蝠冠状病毒，也最有可能被现有的COVID-19免疫力识别。尽管溢出风险永远无法消除，但SARS-CoV-2的广泛暴露可能提高了相关病毒在人类群体中定植的门槛。” 该团队利用来自34个物种的ACE2受体库，分析了15种代表性蝙蝠冠状病毒的刺突蛋白。广泛的ACE2受体利用是与SARS-CoV-2密切相关病毒的一个显著特征，包括在老挝发现的BANAL病毒。来自其他进化群的病毒，包括一种新型英国蝙蝠冠状病毒RhGB07，则显示出更受限的受体利用。为了辅助分析，RhGB07的刺突结构也由瑞士的合作者解析。 该研究还调查了SARS-CoV-2宿主范围在疫情期间是如何演变的。研究人员发现，变异株，特别是奥密克戎谱系内的变异株，在受体结合域获得了突变，导致了跨动物物种的ACE2利用模式发生变化，这突显了病毒进化与宿主易感性之间的动态关系。 为了理解其对人类健康的影响，科学家们检查了从COVID-19康复者血液样本。这些样本中的抗体成功中和了一系列密切相关的蝙蝠冠状病毒。重要的是，研究人员还检测到对更远缘的蝙蝠沙贝病毒的中和水平较低，这表明存在一定程度的广谱保护。 这些病毒与SARS-CoV-2显示出最强的抗原相似性，这意味着它们更容易被COVID-19感染后产生的抗体识别和中和。相比之下，亲缘关系更远的蝙蝠冠状病毒往往是“专一型”的，只能利用有限宿主范围内的ACE2受体。——KCL病毒免疫学教授凯蒂·杜尔斯 KCL和皮尔布赖特研究所的进一步实验发现了几种能够识别极其多样化沙贝病毒的单克隆抗体。第二篇配套文章确定了这些单克隆抗体的结合位点，突出了冠状病毒刺突蛋白的保守区域，为下一代疫苗和治疗药物的开发提供了重要线索。 皮尔布赖特研究所病毒糖蛋白小组负责人达兰·贝利博士补充道：“其中一个令人鼓舞的发现是，甚至一些亲缘关系更远的蝙蝠冠状病毒也显示出交叉中和的证据。这表明开发广谱保护性、泛沙贝病毒疫苗或治疗药物是可能实现的。” 这项工作强调了将病毒宿主范围、受体利用和免疫识别结合起来理解的重要性，而非孤立地看待。通过识别区分高风险病毒与不太可能溢出感染人类病毒的特征，研究人员希望加强全球大流行防范工作。
