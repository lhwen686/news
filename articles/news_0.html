新研究阐明TMEM63B蛋白调节细胞膜的机制

New study explains how TMEM63B protein regulates cell membranes

深度解析

该研究深入揭示了机械敏感性脂质扰乱酶TMEM63B的精细调控机制，核心在于其C端尾部的“自动抑制域”作为分子刹车。在细胞静息状态下，此区域通过关键残基亮氨酸776与蛋白内部结构域的疏水相互作用，稳定TMEM63B的非活性构象，确保质膜磷脂不对称性得以维持。磷脂不对称性对细胞信号转导、膜融合、细胞凋亡等多种生理过程至关重要。一旦亮氨酸776等关键位点发生修饰，抑制作用解除，TMEM63B便会组成型激活，导致磷脂酰丝氨酸（PS）等通常位于胞浆侧的磷脂异常暴露于细胞表面。这种持续的脂质扰乱会破坏膜完整性和功能，对细胞稳态造成严重影响。从行业影响来看，这项发现为神经退行性疾病的病理机制提供了新的靶点和理解框架。鉴于TMEM63B突变已与神经发育和神经退行性疾病相关联，揭示其失活调控机制有助于阐明疾病发生时异常膜功能是如何产生的。未来，针对TMEM63B自动抑制域的药物开发，或可作为潜在的治疗策略，通过恢复正常的磷脂不对称性，干预神经系统疾病的进展。这不仅深化了基础生物学认知，也为精准医疗和药物研发开辟了新路径。

英文原文

Original
An autoinhibitory domain in the C-terminal tail of the TMEM63B, a mechanosensitive lipid scramblase, helps maintain its inactive state under resting conditions, report researchers from Institute of Science Tokyo. Any modifications in this regulatory region, particularly the Leu776 residue, reverses the inhibitory action and causes constitutive lipid scrambling, disrupting normal phospholipid asymmetry. These findings provide new insights into TMEM63B regulation and may improve our understanding of how its dysregulation contributes to neurodegenerative disease pathogenesis. The plasma membrane is a thin, flexible barrier that surrounds and protects cells. It maintains a carefully controlled distribution of lipids between its inner and outer layers. This asymmetrical distribution of lipids is important for normal cellular function, but under certain conditions, it gets rapidly disrupted by lipid scramblases. Lipid scramblases are proteins that move phospholipids between two sides of the membrane. Cells, therefore, need to regulate when the activity of these proteins. One such protein is TMEM63B, a mechanosensitive lipid scramblase that responds to changes in the physical properties of the cell membrane. Previous studies have stated that TMEM63B becomes activated when properties such as membrane thickness or curvature are altered. But an important question remains: how does TMEM63B stay inactive when the membrane is at rest? To fill this gap, a research team led by graduate student(at the time of the study) Megumi Nishimura, Lecturer Yugo Miyata, and Professor Katsumori Segawa, all from the Department of Medical Chemistry, Medical Research Laboratory, Institute of Integrated Research, Institute of Science Tokyo (Science Tokyo), Japan, in collaboration with Associate Professor Norimichi Nomura from Kyoto University, Japan, and Professor Tomohiro Nishizawa from Yokohama City University, Japan, has now identified a novel molecular mechanism in TMEM63B. The study, made available online on June 4, 2026, and published in Volume 302, Issue 7 of the Journal of Biological Chemistry on July 1, 2026, shows that the C-terminal tail of TMEM63B plays a key role. It has as an autoinhibitory region that keeps the scramblase 'switched off' under resting conditions. The researchers started with the investigation of YN9303-24, an antibody previously shown to promote the open conformation of TMEM63B. To pinpoint where the antibody binds (epitope), the researchers used several approaches: chimeric proteins (that combine regions from different proteins), progressively shortened versions of the C-terminal tail (truncations), and targeted deletions of specific amino acids. These experiments showed that the antibody-binding site lies within the intracellular C-terminal tail of TMEM63B. They identified a short sequence of three amino acids, called the AQV motif, at positions 773–775 within this region, which was recognized by the antibody. Additionally, they found that the neighboring sequence, LQD at residues 776–778, was specifically important for controlling TMEM63B itself. Eliminating these three amino acids (LQD) resulted in the protein becoming constitutively active, producing lipid scrambling even without membrane stimulation. Further experiments identified Leucine at 776 (Leu776) as the key residue modulating the protein confirmation and activity. Replacing Leu776 with alanine strongly increased exposure of the phospholipid phosphatidylserine (PS) on the cell surface, whereas PS is normally predominant on the inner cytoplasmic side of the plasma membrane under resting conditions. Changes to the neighboring Gln777 or Asp778 didn't result in significant changes. The increased uptake of fluorescent phosphatidylcholine provided additional evidence that lipid scrambling was enhanced in the mutant protein. The C-terminal tail functions like a molecular brake, keeping TMEM63B inactive under resting conditions until changes in the membrane allow it to become activated." Professor Katsumori Segawa, Department of Medical Chemistry, Institute of Science Tokyo Additional structural analysis helped explain how this brake may work. In the open-state structure, the AQVLQD motif lies next to two intracellular helices, IL2H2 and IL2H3, which form part of a beam-like intracellular domain of TMEM63B. Leu776 is positioned close to several hydrophobic residues in these helices, suggesting that interactions between the C-terminal tail and this intracellular domain could help stabilize the inactive state. When such interactions are disrupted, the restraint on TMEM63B may get released, allowing the lipid translocation pathway to become active. Moreover, this novel mechanism has implications for neurological diseases. Earlier studies have linked disease associated TMEM63B mutations with neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative disorders. "Understanding how the C-terminal tail restrains TMEM63B gives us a clearer picture of how inappropriate activation of this scramblase could disrupt membrane function and potentially contribute to neurological disorders," notes Segawa. Overall, the study reveals a simple yet important principle of TMEM63B regulation: the C-terminal tail acts as a molecular brake. Under resting conditions, this region helps keep the scramblase inactive. Disrupting a critical part of the tail, particularly Leu776, releases this brake and triggers constitutive lipid scrambling. These findings provide a new framework for understanding how TMEM63B is regulated and may guide future studies investigating its role in disease pathogenesis and potential therapeutic strategies.

东京科学大学的研究人员报告称，机械敏感性脂质扰乱酶TMEM63B的C端尾部存在一个自动抑制域，有助于其在静息条件下保持非活性状态。对该调控区域，特别是亮氨酸776残基的任何修饰，都会逆转其抑制作用，导致组成型脂质扰乱，从而破坏正常的磷脂不对称性。这些发现为TMEM63B的调控提供了新见解，并可能增进我们对该蛋白失调如何促进神经退行性疾病发病机制的理解。 质膜是包围并保护细胞的薄而柔软的屏障。它在内外层之间维持着精心控制的脂质分布。这种脂质的不对称分布对于正常的细胞功能至关重要，但在某些条件下，它会被脂质扰乱酶迅速破坏。脂质扰乱酶是负责在膜两侧之间移动磷脂的蛋白质。因此，细胞需要调控这些蛋白质的活性时机。 TMEM63B就是这样一种蛋白质，它是一种机械敏感性脂质扰乱酶，对细胞膜物理性质的变化做出响应。此前的研究表明，当膜厚度或曲率等性质发生改变时，TMEM63B会被激活。然而，一个重要问题依然存在：当膜处于静息状态时，TMEM63B是如何保持非活性状态的？ 为了填补这一空白，由当时的研究生Megumi Nishimura、讲师Yugo Miyata和教授Katsumori Segawa（均来自日本东京科学大学医学化学系、综合研究所医学研究实验室）领导的研究团队，与日本京都大学副教授Norimichi Nomura和日本横滨市立大学教授Tomohiro Nishizawa合作，现已在TMEM63B中发现了一种新的分子机制。该研究于2026年6月4日在线发布，并于2026年7月1日发表在《生物化学杂志》第302卷第7期，研究表明TMEM63B的C端尾部扮演着关键角色。它具有一个自动抑制区域，可在静息条件下使扰乱酶保持“关闭”状态。 研究人员首先调查了YN9303-24，这种抗体此前已显示能促进TMEM63B的开放构象。为了精确定位抗体结合位点（表位），研究人员采用了多种方法：嵌合蛋白（结合不同蛋白质区域的蛋白）、C端尾部逐渐缩短的版本（截短体），以及特定氨基酸的靶向缺失。这些实验表明，抗体结合位点位于TMEM63B的细胞内C端尾部。他们在此区域内识别出一个由三个氨基酸组成的短序列，称为AQV基序，位于773-775位，该基序被抗体识别。 此外，他们发现邻近的LQD序列（位于776-778位残基）对于TMEM63B自身的控制尤为重要。消除这三个氨基酸（LQD）导致该蛋白变为组成型活性，即使没有膜刺激也能产生脂质扰乱。进一步的实验确定亮氨酸776（Leu776）是调节蛋白质构象和活性的关键残基。将亮氨酸776替换为丙氨酸会强烈增加磷脂酰丝氨酸（PS）在细胞表面的暴露，而磷脂酰丝氨酸在静息条件下通常主要分布于质膜的细胞内侧（胞浆侧）。对邻近的谷氨酰胺777或天冬氨酸778的改变并未导致显著变化。荧光磷脂酰胆碱摄取的增加提供了额外证据，表明突变蛋白中的脂质扰乱作用增强。 “C端尾部就像一个分子刹车，在静息条件下保持TMEM63B的非活性状态，直到膜发生变化使其能够被激活。”——东京科学大学医学化学系Katsumori Segawa教授。 进一步的结构分析有助于解释这个“刹车”可能如何工作。在开放状态结构中，AQVLQD基序位于两个细胞内螺旋IL2H2和IL2H3旁边，这两个螺旋构成了TMEM63B梁状细胞内结构域的一部分。亮氨酸776靠近这些螺旋中的几个疏水残基，这表明C端尾部与这个细胞内结构域之间的相互作用可能有助于稳定非活性状态。当这种相互作用被破坏时，对TMEM63B的限制可能会被解除，从而使脂质转运通路变得活跃。 此外，这一新机制对神经系统疾病具有重要意义。早期的研究已将与疾病相关的TMEM63B突变与神经发育和神经退行性疾病联系起来。Segawa指出：“理解C端尾部如何限制TMEM63B，能让我们更清楚地认识到这种扰乱酶的不适当激活如何破坏膜功能，并可能导致神经系统疾病。” 总而言之，这项研究揭示了TMEM63B调控的一个简单而重要的原理：C端尾部充当分子刹车。在静息条件下，该区域有助于使扰乱酶保持非活性。破坏尾部的关键部分，特别是亮氨酸776，会解除这个刹车并触发组成型脂质扰乱。这些发现为理解TMEM63B如何被调控提供了一个新框架，并可能指导未来研究其在疾病发病机制中的作用以及潜在的治疗策略。
