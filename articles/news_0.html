Newly discovered protein controls the space between mitochondrial membranes

深度解析

这项研究揭示了线粒体功能调控的一个全新维度，即膜间隙（IMS）体积的遗传控制和可调节性。核心专业概念在于SPRIZ蛋白作为“分子支柱”的独特作用，它连接线粒体的内外膜，并维持精确的纳米级间距。以往对线粒体的理解多集中于其分子机器和生化反应，而此发现强调了物理空间本身对线粒体代谢和信号转导的关键影响。 在医学机制上，IMS尺寸的微小变化足以影响钙离子等重要信号分子的转运速度，进而调控线粒体能量生产和细胞内稳态。这项发现的行业影响深远：它为帕金森病等线粒体功能障碍相关疾病的致病机制提供了新视角，即IMS的异常间距可能导致线粒体功能受损或清除障碍。这预示着SPRIZ或其调控通路可能成为开发新型诊断生物标志物和靶向治疗策略的潜在靶点。此外，通过工程化手段调节IMS尺寸的能力，也为线粒体疾病的基础研究和药物筛选提供了强大的新工具。

英文原文

Original
Mitochondria are the tiny powerpacks that supply most of the energy our cells need. Their ability to generate this energy relies on a distinctive architecture: two membranes, one tucked inside the other, separated by a narrow gap called the intermembrane space (IMS). This tiny space helps sustain the chemistry of energy production, making its organization critical to how these cellular powerpacks function. Now, a new study led by a University of Ottawa Faculty of Medicine investigator reports a potentially transformative discovery: the volume of the IMS is genetically controlled and adjustable. By controlling where this space expands or narrows within each mitochondrion, cells can actively shape the internal architecture of their powerpacks and tune how they function. Published in Science Advances, the study identifies SPRIZ-short for 'SPatial Regulator of the mitochondrial Intermembrane Zone'-as a key protein that cells use to shape the internal architecture of their powerpacks. Its discovery opens a new path to understanding how disruptions in this delicate spacing may contribute to diseases in which mitochondria fail to meet cells' energy needs, or in which damaged powerpacks are not properly maintained or cleared away, as occurs in Parkinson's disease. SPRIZ: A molecular pillar Working with collaborators at uOttawa and in the United States, the team combined computer simulations of molecular movement, genetics, large-scale protein analysis and advanced imaging, including three-dimensional electron tomography. Together, these approaches showed how SPRIZ bridges the two mitochondrial membranes, forming a molecular pillar that holds open the space between them. This ability to connect the membranes while keeping them apart is what makes SPRIZ distinctive, explains Dr. Luca Pellegrini, a full professor in the Faculty's Department of Biochemistry, Microbiology and Immunology who led the study. While many membrane tethers bring membranes close enough to exchange materials, SPRIZ appears to use that connection to maintain a defined spacing. What makes SPRIZ so unexpected is that it uses a familiar mechanism-a tether-to do something different: it connects the two mitochondrial membranes while acting as a rigid pillar that holds them a set distance apart, so the room between them keeps a defined height." Dr. Luca Pellegrini, a full professor in the Faculty's Department of Biochemistry, Microbiology and Immunology, uOttawa The team put this molecular spacing system to the test: altering it in laboratory experiments substantially changed the size of the IMS. Like adjusting the height of a ceiling, changing these molecular supports reshaped the room within. The findings show that cells have genetic instructions for setting the dimensions of this essential space, giving them control over the internal layout of their powerpacks. Why a few nanometers matter Even a tiny change in the room between mitochondrial membranes can change what happens inside it. When the team expanded the IMS, molecules gained greater access to this space, while mitochondrial calcium uptake changed-showing that its dimensions help control how signals reach the machinery within these cellular powerpacks. "A change of only a few nanometres in the height of that room is enough to slow the calcium that must cross it to reach the reactor," Dr. Pellegrini says. Experiments in mice revealed just how precisely SPRIZ acts. Removing the gene altered the spacing between mitochondrial membranes while leaving the mitochondria's overall structure intact. The mice remained viable, allowing the team to distinguish a targeted change in the IMS from widespread damage to the powerpacks. The team also demonstrated that SPRIZ can be used to tailor this space. By engineering a longer version of the molecular pillar, the researchers pushed the membranes farther apart and measured how the expanded IMS affected molecular access and calcium uptake. This ability to tailor the dimensions of the IMS gives researchers a way to test whether different functions within these cellular powerpacks require different amounts of space. A new dimension in mitochondrial evolution The team found evidence of the same molecular pillar architecture in humans and every other vertebrate species examined. This shared feature traces back roughly 500 million years: their evolutionary analysis places the emergence of SPRIZ around the origin of vertebrates and found it absent from plants, fungi-including yeast-and invertebrates such as sponges, worms and insects. For Dr. Pellegrini, this opens a broader perspective on how cellular powerpacks evolved to meet the changing demands of animal life. As animals diversified in body size, tissues and organs, their mitochondria had to support increasingly varied tasks while adapting to fluctuations in nutrient supply and energy needs. The arrival of SPRIZ may have given vertebrate cells an additional way to meet those demands: tailoring the dimensions of the IMS to the work each cell performs. This places the discovery in a larger evolutionary story-one in which mitochondria adapted not only through changes to their molecular machinery, but also through control over the space in which that machinery works. Collaborative science The discovery brought together expertise in mitochondrial metabolism, physiology and evolution. At the uOttawa Faculty of Medicine, collaborators included Drs. Mary-Ellen Harper, Eric Lee, Morgan Fullerton and Yan Burelle. The team also benefited from the contributions of two prominent U.S. scientists: mitochondrial biologist Dr. György Hajnóczky and evolutionary biologist Dr. Eugene Koonin. "That scientists of this standing chose to join the effort speaks to the importance of the questions this discovery opens up," Dr. Pellegrini says. At the heart of that effort were his students: Raman Shulinski, Roman Loban and Victoria Caminiti. Their contributions helped turn a question about the smallest dimensions of mitochondria into a discovery with implications across cell biology. "Raman Shulinski, the first author of this study, is a graduate student, and two of my students who helped carry this work forward are undergraduates," Dr. Pellegrini says. "For young scientists at the start of their careers to play such a central role in a discovery like this shows what our students can accomplish at the University of Ottawa." Dr. Pellegrini's lab is slated to move into the University of Ottawa's Advanced Medical Research Centre (AMRC) when it opens in 2027. The AMRC, the biggest investment in uOttawa history, will feature 350,000 sq. ft. of advanced research space, with a dozen state-of-the-art core facilities not found anywhere else in the region.

AI Translation
线粒体是微小的能量工厂，为我们的细胞提供大部分所需的能量。它们产生能量的能力依赖于独特的结构：两层膜，一层包裹在另一层内，由一个被称为膜间隙（IMS）的狭窄空间隔开。这个微小空间有助于维持能量产生的化学过程，因此其组织结构对这些细胞能量工厂的功能至关重要。 现在，一项由渥太华大学医学院研究员领导的新研究报告了一项可能具有变革意义的发现：膜间隙的体积受基因控制且可调节。通过控制这个空间在每个线粒体内部的扩张或收缩位置，细胞可以主动塑造其能量工厂的内部结构并调节其功能。 这项发表在《科学进展》上的研究，确定了SPRIZ——“线粒体膜间区空间调节器”的简称——作为细胞用来塑造其能量工厂内部结构的关键蛋白。 它的发现开辟了一条新途径，帮助我们理解这种精细间距的紊乱如何导致线粒体无法满足细胞能量需求，或者受损的能量工厂未能得到妥善维护或清除的疾病，例如帕金森病。 SPRIZ：一个分子支柱 研究团队与渥太华大学和美国的研究人员合作，结合了分子运动的计算机模拟、遗传学、大规模蛋白质分析和先进成像技术，包括三维电子断层扫描。这些方法共同展示了SPRIZ如何连接两层线粒体膜，形成一个分子支柱，撑开它们之间的空间。 领导这项研究的渥太华大学医学院生物化学、微生物学和免疫学系正教授Luca Pellegrini博士解释说，这种连接膜同时又使其保持分离的能力，正是SPRIZ的独特之处。尽管许多膜锚蛋白使膜足够接近以交换物质，但SPRIZ似乎利用这种连接来维持一个确定的间距。 Pellegrini博士说：“SPRIZ之所以如此出人意料，是因为它利用了一种熟悉的机制——锚蛋白——来做一些不同的事情：它连接两层线粒体膜，同时充当一个坚固的支柱，将它们固定在一定距离之外，从而使它们之间的空间保持一个确定的高度。” 研究团队对这个分子间距系统进行了测试：在实验室实验中改变它，显著改变了膜间隙的大小。就像调整天花板的高度一样，改变这些分子支架重塑了内部空间。研究结果表明，细胞拥有设定这一重要空间尺寸的遗传指令，从而能够控制其能量工厂的内部布局。 几纳米为何重要 线粒体膜之间空间即使是微小的变化，也可能改变其内部的活动。当研究团队扩大膜间隙时，分子更容易进入该空间，同时线粒体钙摄取发生变化——这表明其尺寸有助于控制信号如何到达这些细胞能量工厂内部的机器。 Pellegrini博士说：“该空间高度仅几纳米的变化，足以减缓必须穿过它才能到达反应器的钙的传输速度。” 对小鼠进行的实验揭示了SPRIZ作用的精确性。去除该基因改变了线粒体膜之间的间距，同时线粒体的整体结构保持完整。小鼠保持存活，这使得研究团队能够区分膜间隙的靶向改变与线粒体的广泛损伤。 研究团队还证明了SPRIZ可以用于定制这个空间。通过设计一个更长的分子支柱版本，研究人员将膜推得更远，并测量了扩大的膜间隙如何影响分子进入和钙摄取。这种定制膜间隙尺寸的能力为研究人员提供了一种方法，可以测试这些细胞能量工厂内的不同功能是否需要不同的空间量。 线粒体进化的新维度 研究团队在人类和所有其他受检的脊椎动物物种中发现了相同的分子支柱结构。这一共同特征可追溯到大约5亿年前：其进化分析表明SPRIZ的出现与脊椎动物的起源大致同期，并且在植物、真菌（包括酵母）以及海绵、蠕虫和昆虫等无脊椎动物中均未发现。 对Pellegrini博士而言，这为线粒体如何进化以适应动物生命不断变化的需求开辟了更广阔的视角。随着动物体型、组织和器官的多样化，它们的线粒体必须支持日益多样的任务，同时适应营养供应和能量需求的波动。SPRIZ的出现可能为脊椎动物细胞提供了一种额外的方式来满足这些需求：根据每个细胞所执行的工作来调整膜间隙的尺寸。 这使得这项发现融入了一个更大的进化故事——一个线粒体不仅通过其分子机器的变化，还通过对其机器运作空间的控制来适应的故事。 合作科学 这项发现汇集了线粒体代谢、生理学和进化的专业知识。在渥太华大学医学院，合作者包括Mary-Ellen Harper博士、Eric Lee博士、Morgan Fullerton博士和Yan Burelle博士。研究团队还受益于两位杰出美国科学家的贡献：线粒体生物学家György Hajnóczky博士和进化生物学家Eugene Koonin博士。 Pellegrini博士说：“这些知名科学家选择加入这项工作，足以说明这项发现所开启的问题的重要性。” 这项工作的核心是他的学生们：Raman Shulinski、Roman Loban和Victoria Caminiti。他们的贡献帮助将一个关于线粒体最小尺寸的问题，转化为一个对细胞生物学具有深远影响的发现。 Pellegrini博士说：“这项研究的第一作者Raman Shulinski是一名研究生，另外两名帮助推进这项工作的学生是本科生。对于刚开始职业生涯的年轻科学家来说，能在这项发现中扮演如此核心的角色，表明了我们的学生在渥太华大学能够取得的成就。” Pellegrini博士的实验室计划在渥太华大学高级医学研究中心（AMRC）于2027年开放时迁入。AMRC是渥太华大学历史上最大的一笔投资，将拥有35万平方英尺的先进研究空间，配备该地区独有的十几个最先进的核心设施。
