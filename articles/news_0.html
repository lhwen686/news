Study uncovers a protective role for microglia in Parkinson's disease

深度解析

这项研究为帕金森病的病理机制和潜在治疗策略提供了颠覆性认知。核心发现是小胶质细胞，作为大脑的常驻免疫细胞，并非仅仅是炎症的驱动者，其中一个特定亚群能够通过“细胞啃噬”（trogocytosis）机制，精确清除神经元内有害的α-突触核蛋白聚集体。Trogocytosis是一种细胞间物质转移过程，小胶质细胞选择性地“啃食”神经元含有病变蛋白的部分，而非整体吞噬，从而保护神经元完整性。这种精密的清除作用还受到P2RY12、CD22信号以及细胞因子IL-10的微调，以避免损伤健康组织。此外，糖蛋白GPNMB被鉴定为该保护性清除过程中的关键介质。 这一发现对医疗行业影响深远。它改变了我们对帕金森病免疫反应的理解，为开发“疾病修饰疗法”开辟了新途径，即通过增强小胶质细胞的特定清除功能，而非笼统地抑制免疫反应。未来，针对GPNMB或相关调控通路的药物研发，有望精确靶向并减缓甚至阻止帕金森病的进展。同时，小胶质细胞亚群及其相关蛋白也可能成为帕金森病早期诊断、疾病进展监测及治疗效果评估的新型生物标志物，推动精准医疗的发展。

英文原文

Original
Researchers at the University of Oxford have uncovered a previously unrecognized protective role for microglia, the brain's resident immune cells, in Parkinson's disease. The study, funded by the Medical Research Council (MRC) and the National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR) Biomedical Research Centre: Oxford, shows that a distinct population of human microglia can selectively remove harmful clumps (aggregates) of the Parkinson's-causing protein alpha-synuclein from dopamine-producing neurons. Parkinson's disease affects more than 10 million people worldwide. The disease is characterized by the progressive loss of dopamine-producing neurons and the build-up of abnormal clumps of the protein alpha-synuclein within nerve cells. Identifying new potential ways of preventing or removing these clumps could enable new treatments to slow or stop the progression of Parkinson's disease. Microglia are the brain's resident immune cells, acting as its first line of defence and helping to keep the brain healthy. They respond to damage, clear away cellular debris and help shape connections between nerve cells, but when persistently activated they can contribute to inflammation and damage to neurons. The researchers developed human stem cell models (derived from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)) allowing human dopamine-producing neurons and microglia to be studied together. The team examined how microglia responded when alpha-synuclein aggregates formed inside neurons, either as a consequence of increased alpha-synuclein gene dosage or after exposure to alpha-synuclein fibrils which act as a 'template', encouraging the endogenous neuronal protein to misfold the same way. The researchers found that microglia were able to reduce the harmful alpha-synuclein aggregates associated with Parkinson's disease by selectively removing small portions of the neuron through a process known as trogocytosis, where cells "nibble" material from another cell. This reveals a new mechanism through which microglia can remove harmful material while preserving the overall structure and function of the neuron. What is striking is the precision of this response. The microglia were not simply engulfing damaged neurons but instead removing parts of the neuron containing aggregated alpha-synuclein, suggesting that, at certain stages of disease, microglia help neurons dispose of potentially harmful material." Dr. Hung-Ju Chueh, first author of the study This response was fine-tuned by both sensing (P2RY12) and inhibitory (CD22) signals between the two cell types, as well as a "self-regulated brake" on microglia mediated by the cytokine IL-10, which stops them from damaging healthy tissue. Using single-cell RNA sequencing, a technique that allowed them to examine the gene profiles of individual cells, the researchers identified a subpopulation of activated microglia associated with this beneficial clearance response. Professor George Tofaris, senior author of the study, said: "Our findings highlight that the immune response in Parkinson's disease is more nuanced than simply being beneficial or harmful. "We have identified a population of human microglia that can actively remove pathological alpha-synuclein from neurons. Understanding how to enhance and monitor such beneficial microglial functions, without triggering damaging inflammation, could open up new avenues for developing disease-modifying treatments." Professor Tofaris adds that "We also found that the protein glycoprotein non-metastatic melanoma protein B (GPNMB) appears to be an important part of this protective. Genetic variation at the GPNMB locus has previously been associated with Parkinson's disease through genome-wide association studies, but the functional role of GPNMB in disease has remained unclear." The researchers found that GPNMB was upregulated and interacted with pathological alpha-synuclein within microglia when they were exposed to neurons containing aggregates. GPNMB was also increased in microglia within the substantia nigra, the brain region most affected in Parkinson's disease, in tissue from individuals with incidental alpha-synuclein pathology or Parkinson's disease. When the researchers selectively reduced GPNMB expression in microglia using genetic approaches (CRISPRi), the cells became less effective at clearing alpha-synuclein aggregates from neurons, providing evidence that GPNMB plays an active role in this protective process.

AI Translation
牛津大学的研究人员揭示了小胶质细胞（大脑的常驻免疫细胞）在帕金森病中一个先前未被认识到的保护作用。 这项研究由医学研究理事会（MRC）和国家健康与护理研究所（NIHR）牛津生物医学研究中心资助，结果显示，一种独特的人类小胶质细胞群能够选择性地从产生多巴胺的神经元中清除导致帕金森病的有害蛋白质α-突触核蛋白团块（聚集体）。 帕金森病影响全球超过1000万人。该病的特征是产生多巴胺的神经元逐渐丧失，以及神经细胞内异常的α-突触核蛋白团块积累。识别预防或清除这些团块的新潜在方法，有望开发出减缓或阻止帕金森病进展的新疗法。 小胶质细胞是大脑的常驻免疫细胞，作为其第一道防线，帮助维持大脑健康。它们响应损伤，清除细胞碎片，并帮助塑造神经细胞之间的连接，但当它们持续激活时，也会导致炎症和神经元损伤。 研究人员开发了人类干细胞模型（源自诱导多能干细胞（iPSCs）），从而能够共同研究人类产生多巴胺的神经元和小胶质细胞。研究团队检查了当α-突触核蛋白聚集体在神经元内部形成时，小胶质细胞如何响应，无论这是α-突触核蛋白基因剂量增加的结果，还是暴露于作为“模板”的α-突触核蛋白原纤维之后，后者促使内源性神经元蛋白质以同样方式错误折叠。 研究人员发现，小胶质细胞能够通过一个名为“细胞啃噬”（trogocytosis）的过程，选择性地移除神经元的小部分，从而减少与帕金森病相关的有害α-突触核蛋白聚集体，在这个过程中，细胞会从另一个细胞“啃食”物质。 这揭示了一种新的机制，小胶质细胞可以通过该机制清除有害物质，同时保留神经元的整体结构和功能。 “令人震惊的是这种响应的精确性。小胶质细胞并非简单地吞噬受损的神经元，而是移除含有聚集α-突触核蛋白的神经元部分，这表明在疾病的某些阶段，小胶质细胞帮助神经元处理潜在的有害物质。”该研究的第一作者，洪如珏博士（Dr. Hung-Ju Chueh）表示。 这种响应通过两种细胞类型之间的感应（P2RY12）和抑制性（CD22）信号进行微调，此外，由细胞因子IL-10介导的小胶质细胞上的“自我调节刹车”也阻止它们损伤健康组织。 利用单细胞RNA测序（一种使他们能够检查单个细胞基因谱的技术），研究人员识别出一种与这种有益清除响应相关的激活小胶质细胞亚群。 该研究的通讯作者乔治·托法里斯教授（Professor George Tofaris）表示：“我们的发现强调，帕金森病中的免疫反应比简单地有益或有害要微妙得多。” “我们已经识别出一种能够主动从神经元中清除病理性α-突触核蛋白的人类小胶质细胞群。了解如何在不引发破坏性炎症的情况下增强和监测这种有益的小胶质细胞功能，可能为开发疾病修饰疗法开辟新途径。” 托法里斯教授补充说：“我们还发现，糖蛋白非转移性黑色素瘤蛋白B（GPNMB）似乎是这种保护作用的重要组成部分。通过全基因组关联研究，GPNMB基因座的遗传变异先前已与帕金森病相关联，但GPNMB在疾病中的功能作用仍不清楚。” 研究人员发现，当小胶质细胞暴露于含有聚集体的神经元时，GPNMB被上调，并与小胶质细胞内的病理性α-突触核蛋白相互作用。在黑质（帕金森病中受影响最严重的大脑区域）的小胶质细胞中，以及在来自具有偶然性α-突触核蛋白病理或帕金森病个体组织中，GPNMB的表达也增加了。 当研究人员使用遗传学方法（CRISPRi）选择性地降低小胶质细胞中的GPNMB表达时，这些细胞清除神经元中α-突触核蛋白聚集体的效率降低，这提供了GPNMB在该保护过程中发挥积极作用的证据。
