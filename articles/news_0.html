New AI tool predicts bowel cancer relapse risk from routine slides

深度解析

本研究介绍的SÉMIL算法是人工智能在医学诊断领域取得突破的例证，其核心专业概念在于利用“语义增强多实例学习”技术评估肿瘤的“侵袭前沿”特征。侵袭前沿是指肿瘤细胞向周围健康组织浸润的前沿区域，其生长模式对于预测肿瘤恶性程度和患者预后具有关键临床意义。然而，人工病理学家对这一复杂特征的分类存在主观性和一致性难题。SÉMIL通过深度学习整合病理切片图像与文字描述，实现了对这些特征的客观量化分析，特别是“语义增强”使其能够理解病理学特征的深层生物学含义，而“多实例学习”则有效地从全切片多区域数据中提取关键信息，从而提供更准确的复发风险评估。 该技术对行业的影响深远。它提升了二期肠癌患者的“风险分层”精准度，使临床医生能够更准确地识别需要辅助化疗的高风险患者，避免低风险患者不必要的毒副作用，进而推动“精准医疗”实践。SÉMIL可无缝整合到现有“数字病理工作流程”中，降低了实施成本，并作为辅助工具增强了临床决策的客观性和一致性。未来，结合其他生物标志物，AI在癌症个性化治疗策略制定中的作用将持续扩大，有望显著改善患者预后。

英文原文

Original
A new artificial intelligence (AI) tool developed by La Trobe University researchers could help predict which stage-two bowel cancer patients are at risk of relapse, in an advance that could ultimately lead to thousands getting life-saving treatment earlier. Published in the journal Gastroenterology, the research documents the creation of SÉMIL (Semantically-Enhanced Multiple Instance Learning), an AI algorithm that uses images and written descriptions to analyze routine pathology slides to identify patients at greater risk of recurrent bowel cancer. Researchers analysed more than 1600 pathology slides and validated the findings across 1220 stage-two bowel cancer patients in three independent cohorts and multiple Australian institutions. The study also found that when AI-based assessment and pathologist evaluation aligned, this produced the most accurate risk rating for stage-two cancer patients. Lead author and PhD candidate Francis Magisson, from La Trobe University's School of Computing, Engineering and Mathematical Sciences (SCEMS) said the technology assessed the tumor's growth pattern at its invasive front, a feature that is prognostically important but difficult for pathologists to classify consistently. It then assigned each tumor to a higher or lower risk group. "This information could be used to assist pathologists and clinicians to identify which stage-two cancer patients are at higher risk of relapse and may need closer monitoring or additional treatment such as chemotherapy," Francis said. Current Australian clinical guidelines recommend only using chemotherapy after surgery for high-risk stage-two bowel cancer patients. Therefore, more accurately determining which patients are at high risk is very valuable. Associate Professor Zhen He, who leads the Digital Biology program at La Trobe's Institute for Molecular Science (LIMS), SCEMS, and the Australian Centre for Artificial Intelligence in Medical Innovation (ACAMI) said the research highlighted the growing role of AI in supporting precision medicine. "SÉMIL could be integrated into existing digital pathology workflows without requiring expensive new tests or tissue samples to provide more consistent and objective assessments of cancer pathology," Professor He said. "In future, AI-based pathology assessments could be combined with other emerging biomarkers to improve risk stratification and treatment planning." Associate Professor David Williams, an anatomical pathologist from Austin Health and the Olivia Newton-John Cancer Research Institute which is affiliated with the La Trobe School of Cancer Medicine said the tool was designed to support, not replace, clinical decision-making. "One of the biggest challenges in stage-two bowel cancer is identifying which higher-risk patients require treatment and weighing up the potential benefits of administering chemotherapy against side effects," Associate Professor Williams said. "Our study shows that AI-based assessment has potential to provide pathologists with an additional layer of information that could help clinicians make more informed decisions about next-stage treatment options." Bowel cancer is Australia's fourth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the second most common cause of cancer death. Worldwide, it is the third most common cancer. The study also involved research collaborators from the La Trobe Centre for Molecular Science, La Trobe School of Cancer Medicine, WEHI, Monash University, University of Melbourne, UNSW Sydney, Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre and other leading cancer centres around Australia.

中文翻译

AI Translation
乐卓博大学研究人员开发的一款新人工智能 (AI) 工具，有望帮助预测哪些二期肠癌患者存在复发风险，这一进展最终可能使数千名患者更早获得挽救生命的治疗。 这项发表在《胃肠病学》杂志上的研究，记录了SÉMIL（语义增强多实例学习）的创建。SÉMIL是一种AI算法，它利用图像和书面描述分析常规病理切片，以识别出肠癌复发风险更高的患者。 研究人员分析了超过1600张病理切片，并在三个独立队列和多家澳大利亚机构的1220名二期肠癌患者中验证了研究结果。 研究还发现，当基于AI的评估与病理学家的评估结果一致时，能够为二期癌症患者提供最准确的风险评估。 该研究的主要作者、乐卓博大学计算机、工程与数学科学学院 (SCEMS) 的博士研究生弗朗西斯·马吉森表示，这项技术评估了肿瘤在侵袭前沿的生长模式，这是一个预后上很重要但病理学家难以保持一致分类的特征。随后，该技术将每个肿瘤分配到高风险或低风险组。 弗朗西斯说：“这些信息可以用来协助病理学家和临床医生识别哪些二期癌症患者复发风险较高，可能需要更密切的监测或额外治疗，例如化疗。” 当前的澳大利亚临床指南建议仅对高风险二期肠癌患者在术后进行化疗。因此，更准确地确定哪些患者处于高风险状态，具有非常重要的价值。 乐卓博大学分子科学研究所 (LIMS)、SCEMS 和澳大利亚医学创新人工智能中心 (ACAMI) 数字生物学项目负责人何珍副教授表示，这项研究凸显了AI在支持精准医疗方面日益增长的作用。 何教授说：“SÉMIL可以整合到现有的数字病理工作流程中，无需昂贵的新检测或组织样本，即可对癌症病理学提供更一致和客观的评估。” “未来，基于AI的病理评估可以与其他新兴生物标志物结合，以改善风险分层和治疗计划。” 奥斯汀健康中心和隶属于乐卓博癌症医学院的奥利维亚·牛顿-约翰癌症研究所的解剖病理学家大卫·威廉姆斯副教授表示，该工具旨在支持而非取代临床决策。 威廉姆斯副教授说：“二期肠癌面临的最大挑战之一是识别哪些高风险患者需要治疗，并权衡化疗的潜在益处与副作用。” “我们的研究表明，基于AI的评估有潜力为病理学家提供额外的信息层，从而帮助临床医生对下一阶段的治疗方案做出更明智的决定。” 肠癌是澳大利亚第四大常见诊断癌症，也是第二大常见癌症死亡原因。在全球范围内，它是第三大常见癌症。 该研究还得到了来自乐卓博分子科学中心、乐卓博癌症医学院、WEHI、莫纳什大学、墨尔本大学、新南威尔士大学悉尼分校、彼得·麦卡勒姆癌症中心以及澳大利亚其他领先癌症中心的研究合作者的协助。
