People with autism show remarkable variability in brain activity

深度解析

本研究揭示了自闭症谱系障碍（ASD）神经生物学基础的一个关键且被忽视的维度：个体间的高度异质性。通过复杂的计算模型分析电影刺激下的大脑影像数据，研究人员发现，尽管ASD患者与非ASD对照组在群体平均大脑活动上表现出相似性，但ASD患者内部的个体间变异性显著高于对照组，尤其是在社会脑系统中。这一发现挑战了传统上过度依赖群体平均值来理解和治疗ASD的范式。从医学机制和临床角度看，ASD的诊断和干预目前主要基于行为表型。然而，这种高个体变异性表明，ASD可能不是一个单一的病理实体，而是由多种神经回路功能障碍或连接异常驱动的一系列亚型。识别这些神经生物学亚组对于开发精准医学方法至关重要。未来的临床研究和实践需要从关注群体平均反应转向深入探究个体层面的神经特征，从而实现更具靶向性的诊断生物标志物和个体化治疗方案。这项工作为ASD的精准分型和未来个性化干预策略的制定提供了重要的理论基础和研究方向。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain In a new study in JNeurosci, Dorit Kliemann and James Traer, from the University of Iowa, led a research collaboration to compare brain activity between people with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and individuals who do not have autism. Modeling brain responses to movies The researchers analyzed data previously collected at Indiana University and Caltech. These data were measured by recording the brain activity of adults with and without ASD as the participants watched different movies. Movie selections spanned a variety of genres, including animation, action, romantic comedy and Hitchcockian thrillers. The researchers used the imaging data from these viewing sessions to train a computational model that searched for relationships between brain activity and sounds, visuals or abstract social features of the scenes. Once trained, the model accurately predicted activity across different brain systems. Kliemann emphasizes the relevance of this approach to studying social difficulties in ASD, adding, "The computational model allowed us to look for robust brain responses to many different aspects of the complex, dynamic contexts in the movies." Similar averages mask individual differences When comparing group averages, the model predicted activity in different brain systems similarly for adults with and without ASD. However, comparing individuals to each other within each group revealed that individuals with ASD strayed more from their group average. This higher level of variability in activity was observed in nearly all the brain systems the researchers assessed, but it was most striking in the social brain system. The researchers stress that because current approaches to understanding and treating ASD rely on group average comparisons, this work emphasizes the need for a new perspective. Says Traer, "We were surprised by the similarity of group averages. The results suggest that we need to focus on understanding the details of individual brain responses. How and why do they differ?" Adds Kliemann, "Identifying ASD subgroups may help us build a more precise understanding of the condition and help develop ways to provide individually tailored support." The research team plans to continue exploring how, where and when this variability in brain activity relates to individual differences in behaviors related to ASD. Publication details Heterogeneous Encoding Patterns of Dynamic Audiovisual Social Stimuli in Autism Spectrum Disorder, JNeurosci (2026).
