Ozone device developed to tackle hidden source of hospital infections

深度解析

AI 深度解析： 本新闻报道的核心在于介绍一项利用臭氧（O3）对医院洗手盆排水管进行消毒的创新技术，旨在解决医疗相关感染（HAIs）的顽固根源。在医疗环境中，排水管常滋生有害细菌，并形成生物膜。生物膜是微生物群落附着于表面并分泌胞外多糖基质的结构，其特点是具有极高的抗药性和对常规液体消毒剂的物理屏障作用，使得传统清洁方式难以根除深层细菌。 该设备利用等离子体物理原理，将环境空气中的氧气转化为臭氧。臭氧作为一种强氧化剂，其消毒机制是通过破坏细菌的细胞壁、细胞膜，并氧化其内部酶系统，从而导致细菌死亡。由于臭氧是气体，它能有效穿透并氧化生物膜内部，到达液体消毒剂无法触及的微小缝隙和深层区域，实现彻底消毒。此外，臭氧在作用后会迅速分解为普通氧气，不产生化学残留，相较于传统化学清洁剂更为环保安全。 这项技术对医疗行业的影响深远。首先，它有望显著降低HAIs的发生率，提升患者安全。其次，减少对刺激性化学清洁剂的依赖，既降低了职业暴露风险，也符合绿色环保趋势。其数字审计追踪功能，提升了清洁工作的标准化和可追溯性，有助于优化医院感染控制流程。这项创新预示着未来医院感染控制将更趋向于智能化、高效化和无残留化，为全球医疗机构提供了一种可持续的解决方案。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Left to right: Dr Adam Dowdell, Research Assistant; Prof Declan Diver, Professor of Plasma Physics and Glanadair co-founder; Dr Craig Stark, Plasma Physicist A University of Glasgow spinoff has developed a handheld device that uses ozone generated from fresh air to tackle one of the most persistent and overlooked sources of hospital infection—contaminated sink drains. Developed through more than 15 years of university research and now being commercialized by Glanadair, the latest version of the technology is a device that creates ozone on demand using only air and electricity and can be placed easily over a drain opening in a hospital sink. Ozone is generated by the device inside the drain system, allowing it to reach bacteria hidden in areas that are difficult or impossible to clean using conventional methods such as bleach. Once treatment is complete, any remaining ozone is safely converted back into ordinary oxygen, leaving no chemical residue behind. The Glasgow team believes using ozone, widely recognized as one of the most powerful disinfectants available, to target the root of drain contamination could help reduce the risk of health care-associated infections while reducing reliance on harsh chemical cleaning agents. Declan Diver, a professor who co-founded Glanadair with Dr. Hugh Potts and is one of the inventors of the technology, said, "About 10% of patients who go into the hospital pick up another infection when they are there—and the source of that infection is the drains. "Hospital drains can harbor harmful bacteria within biofilms that are very difficult to remove. Because ozone is a gas, it can penetrate those biofilms and reach places that liquid disinfectants often cannot. "We essentially make disinfectant from fresh air. The challenge has always been how to generate and control ozone safely and effectively. That's what this device allows us to do." The science underpinning the device is already well established. In a 2025 peer-reviewed study published in the Journal of Hospital Infection, researchers demonstrated that plasma-generated ozone significantly reduced bacterial contamination within drains while preventing ozone from escaping into the surrounding environment. The research showed that ozone generated from ambient air could safely disinfect otherwise inaccessible drain systems and piping. The technology builds on earlier University of Glasgow innovations that used ozone to extend the shelf life of fresh fruit and vegetables. Drawing on that experience, the team refined the underlying science and engineering to create a more powerful and practical system for infection-control applications. Alongside its cleaning capabilities, the device can also record when a sink has been disinfected and by whom, creating a digital audit trail for hospital cleaning teams. With a patent recently filed and now pending, the technology is being prepared for wider deployment and clinical workflow trials, with hospitals already expressing interest in evaluating the system. Meanwhile, Glanadair is seeking investment to support manufacturing and has reached the final stages of the Scottish EDGE competition. For Diver, the appeal of the technology lies in its simplicity. He said, "We're taking something that is freely available all around us, the air we breathe, and turning it into a powerful tool for infection prevention. If we can make hospitals safer using air, electricity and smart engineering, then that's a very exciting prospect." Key medical concepts OzoneCross InfectionBiofilms
