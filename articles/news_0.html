Inflammatory messenger substance interferon-alpha can trigger Sjögren's syndrome

深度解析

这项研究揭示了自身免疫性疾病干燥综合征的关键病理机制，标志着向精准医疗迈出了重要一步。核心概念在于“免疫多样性”：研究发现干燥综合征并非单一疾病，而是至少存在两种亚型，它们在分子水平上表现出显著差异。其中一种亚型，约占患者的60%，其特征是干扰素α（一种重要的炎症介质）水平异常升高，并伴有特定的蛋白质特征改变及TRIM21分子的缺失；而另一亚型则无此现象。通过小鼠实验和对英国生物样本库的长期数据分析，研究人员证实了干扰素α水平升高与免疫系统特征性变化之间存在因果关系，并且在患者发病前十多年就可检测到这种分子信号。这一发现的行业影响深远。首先，它为干燥综合征的精准诊断和分层治疗提供了生物标志物，有望使针对性地抑制干扰素α作用的药物，能在正确的目标患者群体中进行更有效的临床试验，从而提高成功率，避免无效治疗。其次，了解疾病的异质性有助于药物研发企业开发出更具针对性的新疗法，优化患者的长期预后。最后，这种对“免疫多样性”的深入理解，将推动整个自身免疫性疾病领域向个性化医疗转型，减少“一刀切”的治疗模式，最终造福更多患者。

英文原文

Original
Findings from Bonn, Edinburgh and Newcastle could give a boost to pharmaceutical research. Interferon alpha is an inflammatory mediator that can cause Sjögren's syndrome, an autoimmune disease. This is revealed in a recent study carried out by the University Hospital Bonn together with the University of Bonn, the University of Edinburgh and Newcastle University. Their findings, which have been published in the journal "Lancet Rheumatology," could make it easier in the medium term to develop drugs to fight this chronic condition. Sjögren's syndrome (also known as Sjögren's disease) has a wide range of symptoms, from dry eyes and itchy skin to muscle and joint pain and chronic fatigue. The condition, an autoimmune disease, is in the group known as collagen disorders, which also includes lupus. With these diseases, the immune system attacks the body's own structures inside organs and connective tissue, damaging them. Sjögren's syndrome mainly affects the exocrine glands, i.e. groups of cells that secrete water or mucus at the surface of the skin or inside the digestive tract, causing chronic inflammation and preventing them from doing their job properly. "This is why people living with the condition complain of low saliva production as well as dryness in their eyes and mucous membranes, among other things" explains Professor Rayk Behrendt, who was responsible for leading the Bonn-based part of the study. He is a researcher in the Institute of Clinical Chemistry and Clinical Pharmacology at the University Hospital Bonn and a member of the Life and Health Transdisciplinary Research Area and ImmunoSensation3 Cluster of Excellence at the University of Bonn. Is dysregulated interferon alpha the culprit? Just like with other collagen disorders, other parts of the body will also be damaged as time goes on, which is why doctors call it a "systemic disease." The actual cause of the condition is unknown. For a long time, however, there's been the suspicion that an inflammatory mediator, specifically interferon alpha, is dysregulated in people affected by the disease. Their bodies are producing too much of it, and this is what might be triggering the various symptoms." Professor Rayk Behrendt, researcher in the Institute of Clinical Chemistry and Clinical Pharmacology, University Hospital Bonn However, this hypothesis had not yet been truly proven - all that was known was that many people with Sjögren's syndrome have an elevated level of interferon alpha. Yet many patients do not respond to treatments that weaken the effect of interferon alpha. The latest study has now provided an indication of why this is the case. "We studied the immune system of over 170 women and men with Sjögren's disease very closely," Behrendt explains. A highly sensitive detection method Except when it is fighting viral infections, the body normally only produces interferon alpha in tiny quantities, so the researchers used a new, ultra-sensitive method to detect individual molecules of it. They discovered that around 60 percent of patients had increased levels of interferon alpha, and it was precisely this 60 percent that also exhibited other characteristic differences in their immune system compared to their fellow patients. For example, the activity of certain immune genes - and thus the release of the corresponding proteins - was different. "We also found a molecule called TRIM21 in the other 40 percent, which the patients with elevated interferon alpha levels didn't have," says Dr. Ivana Jorgacevic, who carried out some of the experiments. In experiments using mice, the researchers found that these heightened levels of interferon alpha were responsible for both the characteristically altered protein signature and the absence of TRIM21. The working groups also analyzed data on nearly 50,000 people from a UK database known as the UK Biobank. These individuals had all provided regular blood samples over many years, and around 250 of them were diagnosed with Sjögren's syndrome and half of these received the diagnosis until partway through this period. "Analysis of the protein signature in the blood of these 250 patients showed that elevated interferon alpha had already been detected more than ten years before they developed the disease," Jorgacevic explains. "Plus, they had no TRIM21." Behrendt adds: "The combination of data from our own cohort, our analysis of the UK Biobank data and our studies on mice enabled us to demonstrate that there is a causal link between interferon alpha and characteristic changes in the immune system." Two different types of Sjögren's syndrome There therefore appears to be at least two different types of Sjögren's syndrome. Although they differ in terms of both the amount of interferon produced in the body and a characteristic protein signature, they are clinically indistinguishable, at least initially. This "immune diversity" is not just of academic interest: "Now, for the first time, we can take treatments geared toward suppressing the interferon effect and trial them really specifically on patients with elevated interferon levels," Behrendt says. "We might also find new approaches to treatment that will help at least a large percentage of patients over the long term." The ImmunoSensation3 Cluster of Excellence works to understand precisely these kinds of immune diversity and is therefore now also keen to investigate why interferon alpha plays a role in some patients but not others. Institutions involved and funding secured: The University Hospital Bonn, the University Hospital Dresden, Heidelberg University Hospital, Cambridge University Hospitals, the University of Bonn, the University of Edinburgh, Newcastle University, University College Cork and the Warsaw-based company JJB Biologics were involved in the study. The work was funded by the German Research Foundation, the Precision Medicine Alliance Scotland, the Wellcome Trust and the Medical Research Council, which is part of UK Research and Innovation.

波恩、爱丁堡和纽卡斯尔的研究发现有望推动药物研发。 干扰素α是一种炎症介质，可导致自身免疫性疾病——干燥综合征。这是波恩大学医院联合波恩大学、爱丁堡大学和纽卡斯尔大学进行的一项最新研究揭示的。他们的研究结果已发表在《柳叶刀风湿病学》杂志上，从中期来看，这可能有助于开发治疗这种慢性疾病的药物。 干燥综合征（也称干燥症）症状广泛，从干眼症、皮肤瘙痒到肌肉关节疼痛和慢性疲劳。这是一种自身免疫性疾病，属于结缔组织病范畴，其中也包括狼疮。患有这些疾病时，免疫系统会攻击器官和结缔组织内部的自身结构，从而造成损害。 干燥综合征主要影响外分泌腺，即在皮肤表面或消化道内部分泌水或黏液的细胞群，导致慢性炎症并妨碍其正常功能。“这就是为什么患有这种疾病的人会出现唾液分泌减少、眼睛和黏膜干燥等症状，”负责波恩部分研究的雷克·贝伦特教授解释道。他现任波恩大学医院临床化学与临床药理学研究所的研究员，同时也是波恩大学生命与健康跨学科研究领域和ImmunoSensation3卓越集群的成员。 干扰素α失调是罪魁祸首吗？ 就像其他结缔组织病一样，随着时间推移，身体其他部位也会受损，因此医生称之为“全身性疾病”。该病的实际病因尚不清楚。 “然而，长期以来一直怀疑，在受该病影响的人群中，炎症介质，特别是干扰素α，出现失调。他们的身体产生了过多的干扰素α，这可能正是引发各种症状的原因。”波恩大学医院临床化学与临床药理学研究所研究员雷克·贝伦特教授说道。 然而，这一假说尚未得到真正证实——此前所知的只是许多干燥综合征患者的干扰素α水平升高。但许多患者对削弱干扰素α作用的治疗无效。最新的研究现在提供了原因的线索。“我们非常密切地研究了170多名干燥症患者的免疫系统，”贝伦特解释道。 一种高灵敏检测方法 除了对抗病毒感染时，人体通常只产生微量的干扰素α，因此研究人员采用了一种新的超灵敏方法来检测其单个分子。他们发现大约60%的患者干扰素α水平升高，而正是这60%的患者，与同期患者相比，其免疫系统也表现出其他特征性差异。例如，某些免疫基因的活性——以及相应蛋白质的释放——是不同的。“我们还在另外40%的患者体内发现了一种名为TRIM21的分子，而干扰素α水平升高的患者则没有这种分子，”进行部分实验的伊万娜·约尔加采维奇博士说。 在小鼠实验中，研究人员发现，升高的干扰素α水平既导致了特征性改变的蛋白质特征，也导致了TRIM21的缺失。研究团队还分析了来自英国生物样本库近5万人的数据。这些个体多年来都定期提供血液样本，其中约250人被诊断患有干燥综合征，其中一半是在此期间的某个时候才被诊断出来。“对这250名患者血液中蛋白质特征的分析表明，在他们患病十多年前就已经检测到干扰素α升高，”约尔加采维奇解释说。“此外，他们体内没有TRIM21。”贝伦特补充道：“我们自有队列的数据、对英国生物样本库数据的分析以及对小鼠的研究相结合，使我们能够证明干扰素α与免疫系统特征性变化之间存在因果关系。” 两种不同类型的干燥综合征 因此，干燥综合征似乎至少存在两种不同的类型。尽管它们在体内产生的干扰素量和特征性蛋白质特征方面有所不同，但至少在初期临床上无法区分。这种“免疫多样性”不仅仅具有学术意义：“现在，我们首次能够针对性地将抑制干扰素作用的治疗方法，在干扰素水平升高的患者中进行试验，”贝伦特说。“我们也可能会找到新的治疗方法，长期帮助至少大部分患者。”ImmunoSensation3卓越集群致力于精确理解这种免疫多样性，因此现在也热衷于研究为何干扰素α在某些患者中发挥作用而在另一些患者中不发挥作用。 参与机构和获得资金：波恩大学医院、德累斯顿大学医院、海德堡大学医院、剑桥大学医院、波恩大学、爱丁堡大学、纽卡斯尔大学、科克大学学院以及位于华沙的JJB Biologics公司参与了这项研究。这项工作由德国研究基金会、苏格兰精准医疗联盟、威康信托基金会和英国研究与创新组织下属的医学研究理事会资助。
