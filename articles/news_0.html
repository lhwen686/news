New statistical framework untangles genetic and environmental autism risk

深度解析

PGS-TRI统计框架代表着理解自闭症谱系障碍等复杂儿童疾病的方法学上的重大突破。其核心在于“病例-父母三人组”分析模式，通过同时考量孩子及其双亲的遗传数据，首次实现了对基因“直接效应”与“间接效应”的精确区分。直接效应指孩子从父母遗传的基因对其自身健康的影响；而间接效应则强调父母自身的基因型如何塑造他们为孩子提供的成长环境，进而影响孩子的健康表型。例如，文章提及母亲对肥胖或特定神经认知特征的遗传易感性可能通过影响育儿环境来增加孩子的自闭症风险，这正是间接效应的体现。这项创新对临床和科研影响深远。它不仅能提高基于多基因评分的自闭症风险预测准确性，还能揭示新的潜在生物标志物和治疗靶点，如CADM2基因。更重要的是，PGS-TRI强调了在基因研究中纳入多样化祖先人群的紧迫性，以确保风险评估工具的普适性和公平性，避免加剧现有健康不平等。其最终目标是实现更个性化的风险评估和干预策略，全面考量遗传与环境的复杂交互，为自闭症患儿提供更精准的支持。

英文原文

Original
A powerful new research tool from a University of Virginia School of Medicine scientist and colleagues is set to advance our understanding of how genetics and environment contribute to autism risk. UVA's Ziqiao Wang, PhD, and collaborators at Johns Hopkins have assembled a "statistical framework" called PGS-TRI that can untangle the complex web of genetic and environmental factors that contribute to childhood conditions. By analyzing "case-parent trios" – data from a child and both parents – this tool provides a more precise look at how "nature" and "nurture" interact within families. "Traditionally, genetic studies focus on the 'direct effects' of genes passed from parent to child. However, a child's health is also shaped by 'indirect effects'-how a parent's own genetic makeup influences the environment they provide for their child," said Wang, an assistant professor who recently joined UVA's Department of Genome Sciences. "PGS-TRI analyzes families, both parents and children, to figure out which health effects come directly from your genes versus which come from the environment your parents created." Understanding autism spectrum disorders Large-scale genetic studies have led to the development of genetic risk scores that estimate a person's predisposition to diseases and health conditions based on their DNA profiles. The new framework allows researchers and clinicians to analyze these scores using family data and to characterize the risk of conditions such as autism and other developmental conditions in children based on family DNA and environmental influences such as maternal diet and lifestyle factors. For their study, the researchers analyzed more than 18,000 case-parent trios, autistic children and their parents, across diverse ancestral populations in the Simons Foundation Powering Autism Research for Knowledge (SPARK) consortium and the Genes and Environment Autism Research Study (GEARS). PGS-TRI will help scientists better understand the role of genes and distinguish their effects from the results of those other, outside factors. Initial tests of the tool have already offered reassuring evidence that "polygenic scores" used to calculate autism risk are generally accurate. But these scores were more accurate for families from the Americas, Europe and South Asia than for families of African or East Asian ancestry. This difference likely reflects the groups used to build the scores, the PGS-TRI developers say. The results highlight the need to include people of diverse backgrounds in genetic research, to ensure the findings benefit all patients, they note. Another finding is that maternal genetic susceptibility for certain traits, including obesity and certain neurocognitive characteristics, may increase the risk of autism in children. In addition to vouching for the validity of prior research, PGS-TRI is already producing promising leads for future work. The researchers used the tool to identify the CADM2 gene as a promising target for developing ways to prevent autism. We hope that PGS-TRI will empower researchers to look beyond just the DNA a child inherits and start understanding the broader family environment that shapes their health, leading to more personalized and effective ways to support children with complex conditions like autism. This tool gives us opportunities to study the potential causal relationships of parents' phenotype and their child's disease risks and better understand how nature and nurture work together to influence a child's health." Ziqiao Wang, PhD, assistant professor, UVA Findings published Wang helped assemble the tool while completing her postdoctoral training at Johns Hopkins; the project was overseen by Nilanjan Chatterjee, PhD, of Hopkins' Department of Biostatistics, Bloomberg School of Public Health and School of Medicine. The scientists have described their new tool in the scientific journal Nature Genetics.

AI Translation
弗吉尼亚大学医学院的一位科学家及其同事开发出一种强大的新型研究工具，有望加深我们对遗传和环境如何共同影响自闭症风险的理解。 弗吉尼亚大学的王梓侨博士及其在约翰霍普金斯大学的合作者构建了一个名为PGS-TRI的“统计框架”，它能够梳理导致儿童期疾病的遗传和环境因素的复杂网络。通过分析“病例-父母三人组”（即来自一个孩子及其双亲的数据），该工具能更精确地审视“先天”与“后天”如何在家庭内部相互作用。 “传统上，基因研究侧重于基因从父母传递给孩子的‘直接效应’。然而，孩子的健康也受到‘间接效应’的影响——即父母自身的基因构成如何影响他们为孩子提供的环境，”王梓侨说，她是一名助理教授，近期加入了弗吉尼亚大学基因组科学系。“PGS-TRI通过分析家庭中的父母和孩子，以确定哪些健康效应直接来源于你的基因，哪些则来源于父母创造的环境。” 了解自闭症谱系障碍 大规模基因研究促成了基因风险评分的开发，这些评分根据个人的DNA图谱估算其对疾病和健康状况的易感性。这个新框架使研究人员和临床医生能够利用家庭数据分析这些评分，并根据家庭DNA以及母体饮食和生活方式等环境影响，来评估儿童自闭症和其他发育状况的风险。 在他们的研究中，研究人员分析了来自西蒙斯基金会自闭症研究知识赋能（SPARK）联盟和基因与环境自闭症研究（GEARS）的18,000多个病例-父母三人组（包括自闭症儿童及其父母），这些家庭涵盖了不同的祖先人群。 PGS-TRI将帮助科学家更好地理解基因的作用，并将其效应与外部其他因素的影响区分开来。该工具的初步测试已经提供了令人欣慰的证据，表明用于计算自闭症风险的“多基因评分”通常是准确的。然而，这些评分对于来自美洲、欧洲和南亚的家庭更为准确，而对于非洲或东亚血统的家庭则准确性稍低。PGS-TRI的开发者表示，这种差异可能反映了用于构建评分的群体。他们指出，研究结果强调了在基因研究中纳入不同背景人群的必要性，以确保研究成果惠及所有患者。 另一个发现是，母亲对某些特质（包括肥胖和某些神经认知特征）的遗传易感性可能增加孩子患自闭症的风险。 除了证实先前的研究有效性外，PGS-TRI还为未来的研究提供了有前景的线索。研究人员利用该工具将CADM2基因确定为开发自闭症预防方法的潜在靶点。 “我们希望PGS-TRI能赋能研究人员，让他们超越对孩子所继承DNA的关注，开始理解塑造其健康的更广泛的家庭环境，从而为支持患有自闭症等复杂疾病的儿童提供更个性化和有效的方法。该工具为我们提供了研究父母表型与孩子疾病风险之间潜在因果关系的机会，并更好地理解先天与后天如何共同影响孩子的健康。”——弗吉尼亚大学助理教授王梓侨博士。 研究成果已发表 王梓侨在约翰霍普金斯大学完成博士后培训期间协助开发了该工具；该项目由霍普金斯大学生物统计学系、布隆伯格公共卫生学院和医学院的Nilanjan Chatterjee博士监督。科学家们已在科学期刊《自然·遗传学》上详细介绍了他们的新工具。
