细胞共享通用开启机制，但演化出独特的基因沉默策略

Cells share universal on switches but evolve unique strategy to silence genes

深度解析

这篇文章揭示了基因调控机制在进化中的显著差异与保守性。核心专业概念围绕着“染色质调控”展开，这是一个复杂的表观遗传学过程。染色质是DNA在细胞核内缠绕组蛋白形成的复合结构，其上的化学修饰（如组蛋白修饰）如同“标签”，精细地控制着基因的开启与关闭，从而决定了细胞身份（如肝细胞与神经元的分化）和生物体的发育。研究发现，基因“开启”的信号在进化上高度保守，这表明激活基因的基本机制是生命得以维持的普遍基础；然而，基因“关闭”的机制却在不同生命分支间呈现出惊人的多样性。这种多样性被解释为基因组与内部寄生DNA（如转座元件和内源性病毒）之间长期“军备竞赛”的结果。转座元件（俗称“跳跃基因”）能在基因组内自我复制和移动，若不被有效沉默，可能对宿主造成破坏性影响。因此，不同物种进化出独特的沉默策略，以适应并对抗这些寄生元件的威胁。这项研究不仅深化了我们对基因组进化的理解，还开发了iChIP2等新型方法，能够在更广泛的物种中高效分析染色质状态，为解析疾病（如癌症）中基因调控的异常提供了新的工具和视角，对比较基因组学和医学研究具有深远的行业影响。

英文原文

Original
The signals that cells use to switch genes on have remained almost unchanged across two billion years of evolution, but the ones used to switch genes off vary dramatically from one branch of life to another, according to a new study from the Centre for Genomic Regulation (CRG) in Barcelona. The findings, published today in Nature Genetics, are thanks to the broadest comparative exercise to date of how different life forms regulate their genomes. The study carried out the first detailed analysis of chromatin, the protein scaffold that controls how DNA is read, in several major branches of life that have been largely absent from studies to date, including lineages such as discobans, rhizarians, ichtyosporeans and cryptomonads. The cell's instructions for activating genes are essentially the same in a human, a sea anemone and a soil amoeba." Dr. Arnau Sebé-Pedrós, ICREA Research Professor and senior author of the study "But the instructions for silencing genes and other genomic elements like transposons have been continuously evolving since our last common eukaryotic ancestor. Different branches of life have developed different molecular toolkits to do the same thing," he adds. The work helps understand how genomes evolved on Earth and could have implications for medical research into diseases involving faulty gene regulation. It also delivers a new method, developed at the CRG, which can help support international efforts to characterise life on Earth at the molecular level. DNA is wrapped, inside every cell, around proteins called histones. Small chemical tags attached to these proteins tell the cell which stretches of DNA to read and which to ignore. The system, known as chromatin regulation, is what allows the same genome to produce a liver cell or a neuron, and a leaf or a root. Faults in the regulation of chromatin underpin many human diseases, including cancers. The chemical tags are ancient, dating back roughly two billion years to a single-celled organism known as the Last Eukaryotic Common Ancestor, or LECA, the founder of all complex cellular life, from which every plant, animal, fungus and protist on Earth descends. The enzymes that add and remove the tags are also broadly shared across plants, animals, fungi and microbial eukaryotes. Until now, however, almost all detailed knowledge of how these tags work has come from a handful of laboratory species such as humans, mice, fruit flies, yeast and the model plant Arabidopsis. The vast majority of life's diversity has remained unexplored at this level. The project began in 2017, when Dr Sebé-Pedrós and Dr David Lara-Astiaso were using a technique called iChIP to study chromatin in comb jellies and placozoans, animals not traditionally studied in the lab. The two researchers wondered whether the approach could be scaled up for use in other species in the eukaryotic tree of life. "We wanted to map epigenetic states in scarce cell types in mice and humans," recalls Lara-Astiaso, now at the Arc Institute in California. "Eventually, we managed to transform that precursor into a general method for mapping genome regulation across the tree of life - more streamlined, more sensitive, and finally able to handle the particularities of very different species." The new method, iChIP2 could label chromatin from many species with unique molecular barcodes and reads them all in a single experiment. It helped profile twelve chemical tags, or histone modifications, across twelve phylogenetically diverse species, spanning amoebae, fungi, plants, algae, single-celled predators and animals. Some had never had their chromatin mapped before. "We initially hoped to build a completely universal protocol, but species differ too much for that. Plants and algae have cell walls that require specialised preparation, for example," says Cristina Navarrete, co-first author of the study. "Once a lab has extracted chromatin from their favourite species, iChIP2 takes over robustly, and from very small amounts of material," she adds. The species included two amoebozoans, Acanthamoeba castellanii and Dictyostelium discoideum; the freshwater amoeba Naegleria gruberi; the marine photosynthetic predator Bigelowiella natans; the alga Guillardia theta; the ciliate Tetrahymena thermophila; two fungi, the chytrid Spizellomyces punctatus and baker's yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae; the colonial ichthyosporean Creolimax fragrantissima; two plants, Arabidopsis thaliana and the moss Physcomitrium patens; and the sea anemone Nematostella vectensis. The researchers found that the signature of an active gene, marked by the pattern of histone modifications clustered around its start and along its body, was nearly identical in every species the team examined. The signature of a silenced gene was not. Different lineages used different combinations of modifications, in different patterns, to keep stretches of DNA silent. In some species, one modification silenced transposable elements while a different tag silenced unused genes. In others, the same modifications piled up together on the same regions. In the soil amoeba Acanthamoeba, a chemical mark that signals gene activation in animals had been repurposed to switch genes off. "We've established so many new rules from looking at such few species," says Dr Sean Montgomery, one of the authors of the study. "It's the power of looking at non-model organisms to see how evolution has brought about many differing solutions to the same problems," he adds. The researchers suggest the diversity reflects an ancient and ongoing conflict between genomes and the parasitic DNA within them, like transposable elements, also known as 'jumping genes', and endogenized viruses. Every genome carries within it stretches of jumping genes, sequences that copy and paste themselves into new locations, sometimes harmlessly, sometimes destructively. In a human genome, they account for roughly half of all DNA. Keeping jumping genes silenced is a matter of survival, but they evolve. Their parasitic nature means they acquire new sequences and sometimes even fragments of the chromatin machinery itself to evade detection. "If a species loses its repressive mechanisms completely, it can't tolerate parasitic elements like transposable elements or endogenized viruses. The result is that it's no longer there. It's dead," says Sebé-Pedrós. Over hundreds of millions of years, the result is host and parasite adapting and a tree of life on which each branch has developed its own bespoke strategy to silence genes. Some of those strategies, the team suggests, were later borrowed for other purposes. The work lands at an important moment for comparative genomics. International efforts such as the Earth BioGenome Project and the Wellcome Sanger Institute's Tree of Life programme, with which Dr Sebé-Pedrós is affiliated, are sequencing the genomes of life on Earth at unprecedented speed. The data generated by the initiatives offer potential new insights into how life has evolved on Earth, but a genome sequence alone says little about how the genome is used. Methods like iChIP2 make it possible to ask how life forms regulate their genomes.

AI Translation
细胞用于开启基因的信号在长达二十亿年的进化过程中几乎未变，但根据巴塞罗那基因组调控中心（CRG）的一项新研究，用于关闭基因的信号在不同生命分支之间却存在显著差异。 这项今天发表在《自然遗传学》上的发现，得益于迄今为止对不同生命形式如何调控其基因组所进行的最广泛的比较研究。该研究首次详细分析了染色质——一种控制DNA读取方式的蛋白质支架——在几个迄今为止在研究中鲜有涉及的主要生命分支中的情况，这些谱系包括盘变虫纲、有孔虫、鱼孢菌和隐藻等。 “细胞激活基因的指令在人类、海葵和土壤变形虫中基本相同。”研究的资深作者、ICREA研究教授阿瑙·塞贝-佩德罗斯博士说。 “但是，用于沉默基因和转座子等其他基因组元件的指令，自我们最后一个共同的真核祖先以来一直在不断演化。不同生命分支为实现同一目标开发了不同的分子工具包，”他补充道。 这项工作有助于理解地球上基因组的演化，并可能对涉及基因调控故障的疾病医学研究产生影响。它还提供了一种由CRG开发的新方法，可以支持国际社会在分子层面表征地球生命的努力。 在每个细胞内，DNA都缠绕在称为组蛋白的蛋白质周围。附着在这些蛋白质上的小化学标记告诉细胞要读取哪些DNA片段以及要忽略哪些。这个系统，称为染色质调控，正是使同一基因组能够产生肝细胞或神经元，以及叶子或根的原因。染色质调控的故障是许多人类疾病（包括癌症）的基础。 这些化学标记历史悠久，可以追溯到大约二十亿年前的单一细胞生物——最后一个真核共同祖先（LECA），它是所有复杂细胞生命的创始者，地球上所有的植物、动物、真菌和原生生物都源于它。 添加和去除这些标记的酶在植物、动物、真菌和微生物真核生物中也广泛共享。然而，直到现在，几乎所有关于这些标记如何工作的详细知识都来自少数实验室物种，如人类、小鼠、果蝇、酵母和模式植物拟南芥。绝大多数生命的多样性在这个层面仍未被探索。 该项目始于2017年，当时塞贝-佩德罗斯博士和大卫·拉拉-阿斯蒂亚索博士正在使用一种名为iChIP的技术研究梳水母和扁盘动物的染色质，这些动物传统上不在实验室中进行研究。两位研究人员想知道这种方法是否可以推广到生命树中的其他真核物种。 “我们想绘制小鼠和人类稀有细胞类型中的表观遗传状态，”现在加州弧度研究所工作的拉拉-阿斯蒂亚索回忆道。“最终，我们成功地将这种前体转化成为一种普遍方法，用于绘制生命树中的基因组调控——它更精简、更灵敏，并且最终能够处理非常不同物种的特殊性。” 新方法iChIP2能够用独特的分子条形码标记许多物种的染色质，并在一次实验中读取所有这些信息。它帮助分析了十二种化学标记或组蛋白修饰，涉及十二种系统发育多样性的物种，包括变形虫、真菌、植物、藻类、单细胞捕食者和动物。 有些物种以前从未进行过染色质图谱绘制。“我们最初希望建立一个完全通用的方案，但物种差异太大，无法实现。例如，植物和藻类有细胞壁，需要专门的制备，”研究的共同第一作者克里斯蒂娜·纳瓦雷特说。“一旦实验室从他们偏爱的物种中提取了染色质，iChIP2就能稳定接管，并且只需非常少量的材料。”她补充道。 这些物种包括两种变形虫：棘阿米巴（Acanthamoeba castellanii）和盘基网柄菌（Dictyostelium discoideum）；淡水变形虫：格兰氏那格里阿米巴（Naegleria gruberi）；海洋光合捕食者：大眼藻（Bigelowiella natans）；藻类：吉拉迪亚藻（Guillardia theta）；纤毛虫：嗜热四膜虫（Tetrahymena thermophila）；两种真菌：壶菌（Spizellomyces punctatus）和面包酵母（Saccharomyces cerevisiae）；群体性鱼孢菌：香肠棒霉（Creolimax fragrantissima）；两种植物：拟南芥（Arabidopsis thaliana）和普通湿生藓（Physcomitrium patens）；以及海葵：星状海葵（Nematostella vectensis）。 研究人员发现，活跃基因的特征，即其起始位点及其主体周围聚集的组蛋白修饰模式，在团队检查的每个物种中几乎相同。而沉默基因的特征则不然。不同的谱系使用不同修饰的组合，以不同的模式，来保持DNA片段的沉默。 在某些物种中，一种修饰沉默了转座元件，而另一种标记则沉默了未使用的基因。在其他物种中，相同的修饰在相同的区域堆积。在土壤变形虫棘阿米巴中，在动物中表示基因激活的化学标记被重新用于关闭基因。 “通过研究如此少的物种，我们已经建立了许多新规则，”研究作者之一肖恩·蒙哥马利博士说。“这就是研究非模式生物的力量，可以观察进化如何为相同的问题带来了许多不同的解决方案。”他补充道。 研究人员认为，这种多样性反映了基因组与其内部寄生DNA（如转座元件，也称为“跳跃基因”，以及内源性病毒）之间古老而持续的冲突。每个基因组都携带着跳跃基因的片段，这些序列会复制并粘贴到新的位置，有时无害，有时具有破坏性。在人类基因组中，它们约占所有DNA的一半。 保持跳跃基因沉默是生存的关键，但它们会进化。它们的寄生性质意味着它们会获取新的序列，有时甚至获取染色质机器本身的片段以逃避检测。 “如果一个物种完全失去了其抑制机制，它就无法容忍转座元件或内源性病毒等寄生元件。结果就是它不复存在了。它死了，”塞贝-佩德罗斯说。 经过数亿年，其结果是宿主和寄生虫的适应，以及生命之树上每个分支都发展出自己定制的基因沉默策略。团队认为，其中一些策略后来被借用于其他目的。 这项工作正值比较基因组学的重要时刻。地球生物基因组项目（Earth BioGenome Project）和维康桑格研究所（Wellcome Sanger Institute）的生命之树项目（Tree of Life programme）等国际合作，塞贝-佩德罗斯博士也参与其中，正在以前所未有的速度对地球上的生命基因组进行测序。 这些倡议产生的数据为生命如何在地球上进化提供了潜在的新见解，但单独的基因组序列对基因组如何使用知之甚少。iChIP2等方法使得探究生命形式如何调控其基因组成为可能。
