特异性EFEMP1变异导致不同形式的视力丧失

Distinct EFEMP1 variants cause different forms of vision loss

深度解析

这篇新闻报道详细阐述了EFEMP1基因变异所引发的一种新型迟发性视网膜变性（L-ORD）。文章的核心发现在于，同一基因的不同变异可导致截然不同的临床表型：p.Arg345Trp变异主要影响中心视力，而新发现的p.Arg140Trp变异则特异性地导致周边和夜间视力丧失。其医学机制是p.Arg140Trp变异引起异常增厚的物质在眼部细胞间堆积，进而损害视网膜功能。值得注意的是，L-ORD早期阶段的视网膜结构可能保持完整，但视杆感光细胞在黑暗中的恢复能力出现显著异常，这为疾病提供了一个宝贵的功能性诊断标志物，有助于在感光细胞尚未大量死亡前进行早期识别。 这项研究对眼科领域具有重要影响。它不仅通过精准的基因分型加深了对视网膜变性异质性的理解，促进了精准医疗的发展，而且在诊断方面，功能性标志物的发现为L-ORD的早期识别提供了新策略。此外，通过研究这种罕见的遗传性疾病，科学家们获得了关于细胞外基质异常堆积和感光细胞功能障碍的宝贵见解，这些机制可能与更常见的年龄相关性视网膜疾病（如年龄相关性黄斑变性）存在共性，从而为开发针对更广泛视网膜疾病的新型治疗方法提供了潜在靶点和思路。

英文原文

Original
Scientists identified a new vision condition linked to a gene already connected to a different vision-loss disease. While the previously identified disease causes loss of central vision, the new condition affects peripheral and night vision. The two different conditions are caused by two different variants in the same gene, according to an international team of scientists that included researchers from the Scheie Eye Institute of the Perelman School of Medicine at the University of Pennsylvania. No name has been formally given for the newly discovered condition, but the researchers are referring to it as EFEMP1-associated late-onset retinal degeneration (L-ORD). It occurs due to the p.Arg140Trp variant in the EFEMP1 gene, which causes abnormally thick material to build up between the eye's cells. The work began with a clinical description of a United States family in 1998, which then expanded to multiple European families based on the newly discovered genetic information. The work was published in JAMA Ophthalmology. The different disease expression in this family was thought to be similar to a condition caused by C1QTNF5 gene variants. However, none of the variants associated with that disease were found. Our collaborators then discovered a new variant in the EFEMP1 gene that normally causes a different disease where the macula, which is tied to central vision, is affected first." Artur V. Cideciyan, PhD, a research of Ophthalmology and co-director of the Center for Hereditary Retinal Degenerations Families reveal a distinct pattern of vision loss Three unrelated families in different countries that happened to share the p.Arg140Trp variant were studied and compared to a different family with the p.Arg345Trp variant in the EFEMP1 gene, which caused the already-known condition affecting central vision. In addition to Penn Medicine's team, researchers from the University of Edinburgh, the University of Basel, the Charles University, and the University of Bonn contributed to the research. They discovered that the newly discovered disease starts slowly in the periphery of the retina, which sits at the back of the eye and converts light into signals for the brain to interpret as sight. People can have good vision for most of their life, but eventually begin to lose peripheral vision and have trouble seeing in low-light conditions, such as dusk or nighttime. This slow decline and a lack of initial damage to the eye's rod cells (a variety of "photoreceptors" that specialize in low-light vision) makes L-ORD difficult to catch. But the slow recovery of vision when transitioning from lighted to low-light conditions is a useful clue for pinpointing the condition. "We can detect a major abnormality in the way rod photoreceptors recover in darkness with retinas that still look structurally intact," Cideciyan said. "This gives us a functional marker of disease at a stage when photoreceptor cells have not yet been lost." Researchers wish to investigate how widely the variant occurs Currently, the researchers are unsure how widespread the variant is and, thus, how many people might have this form of L-ORD. Though they think it might be relatively rare, larger studies will have to be done to determine that. But the discovery of the new gene variant for L-ORD will help broaden understanding of sight conditions that come with age. "Aging is a risk factor for many medical conditions, including retinal diseases such as age-related macular degeneration, the most common retinal condition of aging," said Tomas S. Aleman, MD, a professor of Ophthalmology and the other co-director of the Center for Hereditary Retinal Degenerations. "Lessons learned from these infrequent, late-onset genetic eye diseases offer glimpses at possible mechanisms of disease for much more frequent retinal problems." This study was supported by grants from the University of Edinburgh Chancellor's Fellowship award, RS Macdonald Charitable Trust Seedcorn Award, the BrightFocus Foundation (M2024009N), the German Research Foundation (532367710), the Medical Research Council (MC_UU_00035/9, MC_UU_00007/10), the Swiss National Science Foundation (204285), the Ministry of Health of the Czech Republic (NW24-06-00083), Charles University in Prague (UNCE/24/MED/022 and SVV 2600631), and the Samuel G. Jacobson, MD, PhD, Memorial Fund.

AI Translation
科学家们发现了一种新的眼部疾病，它与一个已经与另一种视力丧失疾病相关的基因有关。此前已发现的疾病会导致中心视力丧失，而新发现的眼部疾病则影响周边视力与夜间视力。根据包括宾夕法尼亚大学佩雷尔曼医学院谢伊眼科研究所研究人员在内的国际科学家团队表示，这两种不同的疾病是由同一基因中的两种不同变异引起的。 新发现的疾病尚未获得正式命名，但研究人员将其称为EFEMP1相关性迟发性视网膜变性（L-ORD）。它由EFEMP1基因中的p.Arg140Trp变异引起，该变异导致异常增厚的物质在眼部细胞间堆积。 这项工作始于1998年对一个美国家庭的临床描述，随后根据新发现的遗传信息扩展到多个欧洲家庭。该研究发表在《美国医学会眼科学杂志》上。 “这个家庭的不同疾病表现曾被认为与C1QTNF5基因变异引起的疾病相似。然而，并未发现任何与该疾病相关的变异。我们的合作者随后在EFEMP1基因中发现了一个新的变异，该基因通常会导致另一种疾病，其中首先受到影响的是与中心视力相关的黄斑。”眼科学研究员兼遗传性视网膜变性中心联席主任Artur V. Cideciyan博士说。 家庭揭示独特的视力丧失模式 研究人员对来自不同国家的三个无亲缘关系家庭进行了研究，这些家庭碰巧都具有p.Arg140Trp变异，并将其与另一个具有EFEMP1基因p.Arg345Trp变异的家庭进行了比较，后者会导致已知的、影响中心视力的疾病。除了宾夕法尼亚大学医学院的团队外，来自爱丁堡大学、巴塞尔大学、查理大学和波恩大学的研究人员也为这项研究做出了贡献。 他们发现，新发现的疾病缓慢地始于视网膜周边，视网膜位于眼球后部，将光线转化为信号供大脑解读为视觉。患者在生命中的大部分时间可能拥有良好的视力，但最终会开始丧失周边视力，并在低光照条件下（如黄昏或夜间）视物困难。 这种缓慢的衰退以及初期对眼部视杆细胞（一种专门负责低光照视觉的“感光细胞”）缺乏损伤，使得L-ORD难以早期发现。但从明亮环境过渡到低光照环境时视力恢复缓慢，是诊断该疾病的有用线索。 Cideciyan说：“我们可以在视网膜结构看起来仍完整的情况下，检测到视杆感光细胞在黑暗中恢复方式的显著异常。这为我们提供了一个在感光细胞尚未丧失阶段的疾病功能性标志物。” 研究人员希望调查该变异的普遍性 目前，研究人员尚不确定该变异的普遍程度，也因此不确定有多少人可能患有这种形式的L-ORD。尽管他们认为它可能相对罕见，但仍需进行更大规模的研究来确定这一点。 然而，L-ORD新基因变异的发现将有助于拓宽对与年龄相关的视力状况的理解。 “衰老是许多疾病的风险因素，包括视网膜疾病，例如年龄相关性黄斑变性，这是最常见的与衰老相关的视网膜疾病，”眼科学教授兼遗传性视网膜变性中心另一位联席主任Tomas S. Aleman博士说。“从这些罕见的、迟发性遗传性眼部疾病中汲取的经验，为更常见的视网膜问题的潜在疾病机制提供了启示。” 本研究得到了爱丁堡大学校长奖学金、RS Macdonald慈善信托基金种子奖、BrightFocus基金会（M2024009N）、德国研究基金会（532367710）、医学研究委员会（MC_UU_00035/9，MC_UU_00007/10）、瑞士国家科学基金会（204285）、捷克共和国卫生部（NW24-06-00083）、布拉格查理大学（UNCE/24/MED/022和SVV 2600631）以及Samuel G. Jacobson医学博士纪念基金的资助。
