人工智能驱动的血液检测能跨越不同人群检测肝癌

AI-powered blood test detects liver cancer across distinct populations

深度解析

该研究详细阐述了一种基于AI和液体活检的肝癌早期检测新方法，核心在于DELFI平台及其片段组学分析技术。DELFI利用血液中循环的游离DNA（cfDNA）片段，通过分析其长度、丰度和模式来识别癌症信号。癌细胞死亡后释放的cfDNA片段长度和修饰模式与健康细胞不同，形成独特的“片段组”（fragmentome）特征。该研究进一步引入了MethID方法，不仅识别肿瘤细胞来源的cfDNA，还能溯源至肝细胞、血管和免疫细胞，揭示了肝癌发展过程中更全面的生物学变化，远超单一肿瘤信号的捕获能力。这种多源信号整合是提高检测敏感性和特异性的关键。 从行业影响来看，这项技术展现出颠覆现有肝癌筛查模式的潜力。目前主要依赖超声和甲胎蛋白（AFP），但它们对早期癌症的敏感性有限。DELFI结合AFP和临床风险因素，能显著提升早期和晚期肝癌的检出率。其在不同地理和病因（如病毒性肝炎、代谢性肝病、黄曲霉毒素暴露）人群中的一致有效性，预示着其在全球范围内的普适性。此外，全基因组片段组学分析有望发展成为一个通用平台，用于多种疾病的非侵入性检测，正如其在肺癌筛查领域的初步成功（FirstLook Lung）。未来，通过前瞻性临床验证和多模态液体活检策略的融合，将进一步优化肝癌早期检测的精准度，挽救更多患者生命。

英文原文

Original
Researchers at the Johns Hopkins Kimmel Cancer Center validated an artificial intelligence (AI)-powered blood test that accurately detected liver cancer in people from two geographically and biologically distinct populations and also uncovered the underlying biological signals that make the test effective. The findings, published July 31 in Cell Press Blue, build on the team's earlier development of the DELFI (DNA Evaluation of Fragments for Early Interception) liquid biopsy platform, which analyzes millions of fragments of cell-free DNA circulating in the bloodstream. The new research validates a previously developed liver cancer classifier in independent high-risk populations and provides new insights into the biology behind its performance. This study also advances research from March 2026, in which the researchers demonstrated that a similar genome-wide fragmentome technology could detect liver fibrosis and cirrhosis, conditions that often precede liver cancer. "Our earlier studies showed that fragmentome analyses could detect liver cancer and, more recently, chronic liver diseases that increase cancer risk," says Victor Velculescu, M.D., Ph.D., the Cancer Genetics and Epigenetics Professor, co-director of the cancer genetics and epigenetics program, and co-senior author of the study. "This study demonstrates that the approach works with high performance across different patient populations while revealing the biological signals in the bloodstream that make this type of detection possible." Liver cancer is one of the leading causes of cancer deaths worldwide, and its incidence continues to rise because of increasing rates of metabolic liver disease and other risk factors. Detecting liver cancer early dramatically improves treatment options, but current screening methods, which rely primarily on ultrasound imaging and the blood protein alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), can miss many early cancers, the researchers explain. To determine whether the DELFI approach could reliably detect liver cancer regardless of its underlying cause, investigators analyzed blood samples from 377 people from Guatemala and Romania, with and without hepatocellular carcinoma, the most common form of liver cancer. The two populations represent markedly different causes of liver cancer. Most participants in Romania developed liver disease related to viral hepatitis or alcohol use, while participants in Guatemala primarily had metabolic liver disease, obesity and diabetes, with many also exposed to aflatoxin, a naturally occurring toxin linked to liver cancer. Study co-author John Groopman, Ph.D., Anna M. Baetjer Professor at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health and associate director of the population sciences at the Johns Hopkins Kimmel Cancer Center, is a leading expert on the role of aflatoxins in initiating liver cancer. Despite these differences, the blood test consistently detected liver cancer across both populations. When combined with AFP and simple clinical risk factors, such as age and sex, the approach identified early- and late-stage cancers with greater sensitivity than existing blood testing alone. Using a newly developed method for tracing where DNA fragments originate, called MethID, the team showed that the DELFI test works because it captures more than signals from tumor cells. It also captures signals from liver cells, blood vessels and immune cells responding to the cancer. "As a result, these DNA fragments contain much more information than whether cancer is present," says Zachariah Foda, M.D., Ph.D., assistant professor of medicine at the Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine and co-senior author of the study. "It tells us where these fragments originate and how they change during cancer development, allowing us to better understand the biology of the disease and improve our ability to detect it." The researchers also identified molecular signatures that differed between populations. For example, they detected a distinctive mutation pattern across the genome associated with aflatoxin exposure in participants from Guatemala, but they also showed that the overall fragmentome classifier remained effective regardless of the underlying cause of the liver cancer. The study suggests that genome-wide fragmentome analysis captures both universal biological features of liver cancer and region-specific molecular changes, making it adaptable to diverse patient populations around the world. The findings also extend the researchers' broader vision for fragmentome technology, suggesting that genome-wide analysis of cell-free DNA could ultimately provide a foundation for noninvasive blood tests capable of detecting multiple diseases using a common technology platform. As an example, Velculescu and colleagues working with DELFI Diagnostics recently reported the clinical validation of a blood test for lung cancer screening, called FirstLook Lung, which is already available in certain health systems nationwide. The investigators say future studies will focus on prospective clinical validation and further refining multimodal liquid biopsy approaches that combine fragmentome analysis with protein biomarkers and clinical risk factors to improve early detection of liver cancer. In addition to Foda and Velculescu, other researchers were Hope Orjuela, Carter Norton, Shashikant Koul, Daniel C. Bruhm, Akshaya Annapragada, Jason Zavras, Sarah Short, Keerti Boyapati, Adrianna Bartolomucci, Vilmos Adleff, Nicholas Vulpescu, Kaui Patricio Lebarbenchon, Jacob Carey, Carter Portwood, Andrei Sorop, Razvan Iacob, Speranta Iacob, Liana Gheorghe, Simona Dima, Manuel Ramírez-Zea, Katherine McGlynn, Jillian Phallen, Robert Scharpf, and John Groopman. This research was supported by the Dr. Miriam and Sheldon G. Adelson Medical Research Foundation, the Commonwealth Foundation, the Cole Foundation, a research grant from Delfi Diagnostics, National Institutes of Health grants CA121113, CA006973, CA233259, CA062924, CA271896, T32GM136577, and T32GM007814-40, and Department of Defense grant HT94252510480.

AI Translation
约翰·霍普金斯·基梅尔癌症中心的研究人员验证了一种人工智能（AI）驱动的血液检测方法，该方法能准确检测来自两个地理和生物学上截然不同人群的肝癌患者，并揭示了使该检测有效的潜在生物信号。 这些发现于7月31日发表在《细胞出版社蓝刊》上，是该团队早期开发DELFI（DNA片段早期干预评估）液体活检平台的基础上取得的进展。DELFI平台能够分析血液中数百万个游离DNA片段。这项新研究在一个独立的高风险人群中验证了先前开发的肝癌分类器，并为其性能背后的生物学机制提供了新的见解。该研究也推进了2026年3月的研究成果，当时研究人员展示了类似的基因组范围片段组学技术可以检测肝纤维化和肝硬化，这些疾病往往是肝癌的前兆。 该研究的共同资深作者、癌症遗传学和表观遗传学教授、癌症遗传学和表观遗传学项目联席主任维克多·维尔库列斯库医学博士表示：“我们早期的研究表明，片段组学分析可以检测肝癌，最近的研究还发现它可以检测增加患癌风险的慢性肝病。这项研究表明，该方法在不同患者群体中均表现出高效能，同时揭示了血液中使这种检测成为可能的生物信号。” 肝癌是全球癌症死亡的主要原因之一，由于代谢性肝病和其他风险因素发病率不断上升。研究人员解释说，早期发现肝癌能显著改善治疗选择，但目前的筛查方法主要依赖超声成像和血液蛋白甲胎蛋白（AFP），可能会遗漏许多早期癌症。 为了确定DELFI方法是否能可靠地检测肝癌，无论其根本原因如何，研究人员分析了来自危地马拉和罗马尼亚的377人的血样，其中包括患有和未患肝细胞癌（最常见的肝癌形式）的患者。这两个人群代表了肝癌截然不同的病因。罗马尼亚的大多数参与者患肝病与病毒性肝炎或饮酒有关，而危地马拉的参与者主要患有代谢性肝病、肥胖和糖尿病，其中许多人还接触过黄曲霉毒素，这是一种与肝癌相关的天然毒素。研究的共同作者、约翰·霍普金斯彭博公共卫生学院的安娜·M·贝彻教授以及约翰·霍普金斯·基梅尔癌症中心人口科学副主任约翰·格鲁普曼博士，是黄曲霉毒素在引发肝癌中作用的权威专家。 尽管存在这些差异，该血液检测在两个人群中均能持续检测出肝癌。当与甲胎蛋白（AFP）和简单的临床风险因素（如年龄和性别）结合使用时，该方法在早期和晚期癌症的识别上比现有单一血液检测具有更高的敏感性。 研究团队利用一种新开发的、用于追踪DNA片段来源的方法——MethID，表明DELFI检测之所以有效，不仅因为它捕获了肿瘤细胞的信号，还捕获了肝细胞、血管和响应癌症的免疫细胞的信号。 约翰·霍普金斯大学医学院助理教授、该研究的共同资深作者扎卡里亚·福达医学博士表示：“因此，这些DNA片段包含的信息远不止于癌症是否存在。它告诉我们这些片段的来源以及它们在癌症发展过程中如何变化，使我们能够更好地理解疾病的生物学特性，并提高我们检测疾病的能力。” 研究人员还识别出了在不同人群之间存在差异的分子特征。例如，他们在危地马拉参与者中检测到与黄曲霉毒素暴露相关的独特全基因组突变模式，但他们也表明，无论肝癌的根本原因是什么，整体片段组学分类器仍然有效。 这项研究表明，全基因组片段组学分析能够捕获肝癌的普遍生物学特征以及区域特异性分子变化，使其能够适应全球不同患者群体。 这些发现也扩展了研究人员对片段组学技术的更广泛愿景，表明对游离DNA进行全基因组分析最终可能为非侵入性血液检测奠定基础，从而能够使用通用技术平台检测多种疾病。例如，维尔库列斯库及其与DELFI诊断公司合作的同事最近报告了肺癌筛查血液检测FirstLook Lung的临床验证，该检测已在美国部分医疗系统中投入使用。 研究人员表示，未来的研究将侧重于前瞻性临床验证，并进一步完善多模态液体活检方法，将片段组学分析与蛋白质生物标志物和临床风险因素相结合，以改善肝癌的早期检测。 除了福达和维尔库列斯库之外，其他研究人员还包括霍普·奥尔胡埃拉、卡特·诺顿、沙希坎特·库尔、丹尼尔·C·布鲁姆、阿克沙亚·安纳普拉加达、杰森·扎夫拉斯、莎拉·肖特、基尔蒂·博亚帕蒂、阿德里安娜·巴托洛穆奇、维尔莫斯·阿德莱夫、尼古拉斯·瓦尔佩斯库、考伊·帕特里西奥·勒巴尔本琼、雅各布·凯里、卡特·波特伍德、安德烈·索罗普、拉兹万·伊亚科布、斯佩兰塔·伊亚科布、莉安娜·格奥尔基、西蒙娜·迪马、曼努埃尔·拉米雷斯-塞亚、凯瑟琳·麦克格林、吉莉安·法伦、罗伯特·夏普夫和约翰·格鲁普曼。 这项研究得到了米里亚姆和谢尔顿·G·阿德尔森医学研究基金会、英联邦基金会、科尔基金会、德尔菲诊断公司的研究拨款、美国国立卫生研究院拨款CA121113、CA006973、CA233259、CA062924、CA271896、T32GM136577和T32GM007814-40，以及国防部拨款HT94252510480的支持。
