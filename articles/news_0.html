Summer heatwaves raise death risk for heart disease patients

深度解析

本研究深入揭示了心血管疾病（CVD）患者对极端高温的脆弱性，无论短期热浪还是长期平均高温，均显著增加其死亡风险。从医学机制上看，极端高温对CVD患者构成严重的生理挑战。为维持体温平衡，机体需通过外周血管扩张和增加心输出量及心率来散热，这导致心肌耗氧量显著增加。对于既有心力衰竭、心房颤动或缺血性心脏病等病症的患者，这种额外的负荷可加剧心肌缺血、诱发心律失常或导致心力衰竭失代偿，从而引发不良心血管事件和死亡率上升。 研究还强调了社会经济因素在热相关死亡风险中的作用，提示教育水平、社区剥夺程度及绿地覆盖率等社会决定因素对弱势群体的气候韧性产生深远影响。这要求公共卫生领域采取多维度干预策略，包括在城市规划中融入适应气候变化的建筑与基础设施、推行基于自然的城市绿化方案以降低局部温度，以及针对个体化的健康宣教与降温措施。临床实践中，医务人员应将高温风险评估纳入CVD患者的常规管理，指导患者合理补水、调整药物并识别热应激早期症状，以期在气候变化日益加剧的背景下，有效降低热相关发病率和死亡率。

英文原文

Original
A U.K. analysis of health records from more than 60,000 people with cardiovascular disease shows that, over a 16-year period, those exposed to higher long-term mean summer temperatures had a higher risk of death, as did those exposed to more heatwaves. Ka Yan Lai of the University of Hong Kong and colleagues present these findings in the open-access journal PLOS Climate. According to global climate data, the past decade has been Earth's hottest on record, and researchers expect future summer heat extremes to intensify. Prior research has linked extreme temperatures with higher overall death rates, and evidence suggests some subgroups, such as people with heart-related health conditions, may be particularly susceptible. However, few studies have investigated potential links between extreme temperatures and mortality among people with cardiovascular disease. To address this gap, Lai and colleagues analyzed the health records of 63,860 volunteers in a large-scale study called U.K. Biobank, each of whom was newly diagnosed with cardiovascular disease between 2006 and 2022. They used temperature records to determine heat exposure at one-kilometer square grids and linked them to each patient's residential address. Over the 16-year study period, 12,237 of the patients passed away. The researchers found that people exposed to higher long-term mean summer temperatures had a higher risk of death, with every one-degree increase linked to a 15 percent higher risk. Meanwhile, people exposed to four or more short-term heatwave events had a 24-percent higher mortality risk than those exposed to two or fewer. These risks remained evident for subgroups of patients with specific types of cardiovascular disease, such as atrial fibrillation and heart failure. The analysis also showed that mortality risks linked to hotter temperatures were higher for people without college degrees and for people living in neighborhoods with higher deprivation levels and lower amounts of greenspace, such as parks. Further analysis examining the short-term impacts of extreme heat events shows consistent results. People showed a higher risk of death when exposed to higher daily summer temperature measured on the day of death and five days prior, as compared to their exposure during the control time window. The risk remained evident for subgroups of patients with atrial fibrillation and ischemic heart disease. The researchers conclude that efforts to reduce heat-related mortality among people with cardiovascular disease will likely require a combination of community-based strategies such as provisioning for heat-resistant housing, infrastructure and urban greening as well as personalized adaptations. Future research could address some of this study's limitations, such as by accounting for additional factors like air-conditioning usage or travel to places with different temperatures. The health impact of extreme heat events has rarely been studied among people with pre-existing conditions. In this study, we found evidence of both long-term and short-term heat impacts on the survival of people with heart conditions." Dr Lai adds: "Our findings suggest the need for multilevel interventions, from urban design and planning policies to cooling strategies and personal choice of where to live, to strengthen climate resilience and save lives, particularly in the vulnerable subgroups." Ka Yan Lai, University of Hong Kong Co-author Prof. Chinmoy Sarkar summarizes: "Projected greater intensity and frequency of extreme heat events together with demographic shift towards aging societies will imply protecting vulnerable and frail residents with chronic diseases such as CVD will be of paramount public health importance. A mix of community strategies such as urban design, nature-based interventions (greening) for urban ventilation can be carefully paired with individualized behavioral adaptations (home cooling) to protect against extreme heat exposure."

一项对英国超过6万名心血管疾病患者健康记录的分析显示，在16年期间，那些长期暴露于较高平均夏季气温的人，以及那些经历更多热浪的人，死亡风险更高。香港大学的赖嘉欣及其同事在开放获取期刊《公共科学图书馆·气候》上发表了这些研究结果。 根据全球气候数据，过去十年是有记录以来地球上最热的十年，研究人员预计未来夏季极端高温将加剧。此前研究已将极端气温与更高的总体死亡率联系起来，并且有证据表明某些亚组，例如患有心脏相关健康问题的人，可能特别易感。 然而，很少有研究调查极端气温与心血管疾病患者死亡率之间的潜在关联。为了弥补这一空白，赖嘉欣及其同事分析了英国生物样本库（U.K. Biobank）一项大型研究中63,860名志愿者的健康记录，他们均在2006年至2022年间被新诊断为心血管疾病。研究人员使用气温记录来确定一平方公里网格内的热暴露情况，并将其与每位患者的居住地址关联起来。 在为期16年的研究期间，有12,237名患者去世。研究人员发现，长期暴露于较高平均夏季气温的人死亡风险更高，每升高一度，死亡风险增加15%。同时，暴露于四次或更多短期热浪事件的人，其死亡风险比暴露于两次或更少热浪事件的人高出24%。 这些风险在患有特定类型心血管疾病（如心房颤动和心力衰竭）的患者亚组中仍然显著。 分析还显示，对于未获得大学学位的人，以及居住在剥夺程度较高且公园等绿地面积较少社区的人来说，与高温相关的死亡风险更高。 进一步分析极端高温事件的短期影响显示出一致的结果。与对照时间窗口的暴露情况相比，人们在死亡当天及之前五天暴露于较高日平均夏季气温时，死亡风险更高。对于心房颤动和缺血性心脏病患者亚组，该风险仍然显著。 研究人员总结道，旨在降低心血管疾病患者热相关死亡率的努力，可能需要结合基于社区的策略（如提供耐热住房、基础设施和城市绿化）以及个性化适应措施。未来的研究可以解决本研究的一些局限性，例如考虑空调使用情况或前往不同温度地区旅行等额外因素。 “极端高温事件对患有既往病症人群的健康影响鲜有研究。在这项研究中，我们发现了长期和短期热影响对心脏病患者生存的证据。”赖嘉欣博士补充道：“我们的研究结果表明，需要多层次的干预措施，从城市设计和规划政策到降温策略和个人居住地选择，以增强气候韧性并挽救生命，尤其是在脆弱亚组中。”——香港大学赖嘉欣 合著者钦莫伊·萨卡尔教授总结道：“预计极端高温事件的强度和频率将增加，加上人口结构向老龄化社会转变，这意味着保护患有心血管疾病等慢性病的脆弱和体弱居民将具有至关重要的公共卫生意义。可以仔细地将城市设计、基于自然的干预措施（绿化）以改善城市通风等社区策略，与个性化行为适应措施（家庭降温）相结合，以抵御极端高温暴露。”
