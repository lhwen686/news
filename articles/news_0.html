深度解析

这篇文章深刻揭示了2型糖尿病发病模式的重大转变，即青少年群体中新发病例的急剧增加和加速。其核心医学机制在于，2型糖尿病主要特征是胰岛素抵抗和胰岛素分泌功能受损。传统的观点认为这是与衰老和长期不健康生活方式相关的成人疾病。然而，研究表明，在青少年中，肥胖率的上升导致了胰岛素抵抗的提前发生和加剧，进而加速了胰腺β细胞功能的衰竭。与成人患者相比，青少年发病的2型糖尿病更具侵袭性，β细胞功能下降更快，血糖更难控制，并可能在更早的年龄出现肾脏、眼睛、神经和心脏等严重并发症，显著缩短预期寿命。 这种趋势对医疗行业和社会公共卫生带来了深远影响。首先，诊断复杂性增加。由于青少年肥胖的普遍，高BMI伴高血糖的青少年可能是1型或2型糖尿病，需要通过检测糖尿病自身抗体和胰岛素抵抗来精确鉴别，这要求提升儿科临床医生的诊断能力和完善诊断路径。其次，医疗负担加重。年轻患者对糖尿病管理和并发症治疗的需求将大幅增长，对儿科内分泌科及相关专科服务的容量构成挑战。最后，公共卫生策略亟待调整。文章强调，在青少年中，单纯的生活方式干预效果不佳，提示需要更广泛、更具支持性的家庭和社区层面预防策略，如推广健康饮食、鼓励体育活动和确保健康食品的可及性，尤其应关注资源匮乏的社区，将健康选择变为容易选择，从而应对这一严峻的健康危机。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pavel Danilyuk from Pexels Type 2 diabetes has traditionally been a disease that has affected mostly middle-aged and older adults. But new research from the U.S. to be presented at the Annual Meeting of the European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD) in Milan, Italy (Sept. 28–Oct. 2) shows a sharp increase in the rate of new cases of type 2 diabetes since the turn of the century, which has continued to accelerate in recent years. The study is by Dr. Tessa Crume of the Colorado School of Public Health in Aurora, Colorado, and colleagues. Youth-onset type 1 and type 2 diabetes incidence has increased steadily in the U.S. for several decades, resulting in a significant burden of diabetes-related morbidity and mortality. In this new study, the authors examined long-term trends in youth-onset type 1 and type 2 diabetes in Colorado between 2002 and 2023. The Colorado Diabetes Registry has conducted continuous, population-level annual analyses of diabetes incidence among people younger than 20 since 2002 across health systems in the state using diagnosis codes, laboratory results and medication data. Eligible participants were nonmilitary, noninstitutionalized individuals residing in Colorado at the time of diagnosis. Population denominators were obtained from census data. Statistical modeling was used to estimate trends over time. Incidence rates were calculated per 100,000 people younger than 20 for type 1 diabetes and per 100,000 youth ages 10–19 for type 2 diabetes (since cases of type 2 diabetes in children under 10 are extremely rare). Type 2 cases surged in teens Between 2002 and 2023, 8,094 children and teens under 20 were newly diagnosed with type 1 diabetes, and 1,560 youth ages 10–19 were diagnosed with new-onset type 2 diabetes. The incidence of type 1 diabetes increased slightly from 25.07 per 100,000 in 2002 to 27.39 per 100,000 in 2023. There was a stable trend of incident type 1 diabetes between 2002 and 2014 (annual percentage change [APC] = 0.66%), followed by an increasing trend between 2015 and 2023 (APC = 2.62%). However, the incidence of type 2 diabetes among youth ages 10–19 increased almost 10-fold from 2.37 per 100,000 in 2002 to 22.30 per 100,000 in 2023. The incidence of type 2 diabetes increased by 3.8% across the whole period from 2002 to 2023; by 2.92% annually between 2002 and 2013; and even more sharply, by 4.72% annually, between 2014 and 2023. The authors say, "Across this period of over two decades since the turn of the century, new-onset type 2 diabetes incidence in youth increased by almost 10-fold, compared with type 1 diabetes incidence that only slightly increased. These findings document an expanding population of youth at risk for diabetes-related complications across the life course and underscore the importance of sustained long-term surveillance." Dr. Crume explains, "A generation ago, type 2 diabetes in a teenager was so rare that many pediatric clinicians could go through much of their career without seeing a case; it was a disease of middle age. In Colorado, we have now watched it rise nearly tenfold in young people in just two decades, and the increase is accelerating rather than slowing. "This is not a local anomaly: in the U.S.-based SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth study, youth type 2 diabetes is climbing more than twice as fast as type 1, and among older teenagers new type 2 cases have already overtaken type 1. A disease we once considered exclusively adult-only, type 2 diabetes, is now outpacing the classic form of childhood diabetes—type 1." Diagnosis is getting more complicated The authors explain that rising obesity rates among children mean it is no longer possible to reliably tell type 1 from type 2 diabetes in a newly diagnosed teenager; the conditions increasingly overlap in how they present clinically. A teenager with a high BMI presenting with high blood sugar could have either type 1 or type 2 diabetes. Distinguishing them requires testing for diabetes autoantibodies, which mark the autoimmune process of type 1, and for insulin resistance, which characterizes type 2. That classification determines treatment, so the diagnostic pathway has to be established to ensure the correct diagnosis. Crume says, "The services needed, then, are not only about capacity to treat a growing number of young people; they are about ensuring that every child with new-onset diabetes receives proper autoantibody and metabolic testing rather than being classified on impression." She explains that doctors are increasingly less surprised to see type 2 diabetes in young people and teenagers. "Twenty years ago, a pediatric endocrinologist might have paused at a teenager with type 2 diabetes; today it is a routine and growing part of their caseload. "Where surprise does persist is in primary care and in the community, where type 2 diabetes in a child can still be underestimated or missed because of the old assumption that this is an adult disease. So the risk is not that clinicians are shocked; it is that a young person's diabetes is overlooked or treated improperly." Early complications raise the stakes And she concludes with a stark warning about the tidal wave of health problems coming for these and other young people. "The danger is considerable, and it is why these trends matter so much. Type 2 diabetes in a young person is a more aggressive disease than the same diagnosis in a middle-aged adult. "The insulin-producing cells of the pancreas decline faster, blood sugar is harder to control, and complications affecting the kidneys, eyes, nerves and heart can appear within just a few years rather than decades. A child diagnosed at 14 faces the prospect of these complications in early adulthood, and youth-onset type 2 diabetes is associated with a shortened life expectancy. "While newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes can sometimes be pushed into remission, the evidence in young people is sobering. Lifestyle change alone has proven markedly less effective in adolescents than in adults, and many young patients require medication and still struggle to maintain control. That is exactly why early-life prevention at the family and community level is essential. "The most effective changes are shared across the whole household rather than placed on the child: everyday habits like family meals, water over sugary drinks and being active together. But families need support to make those choices possible: nutritious school meals, daily physical activity and safe, affordable access to healthy food in every neighborhood. Since this disease falls hardest on the communities with the fewest resources, making the healthy option the easy option has to be something we value collectively." 2型糖尿病传统上是一种主要影响中老年人的疾病。但美国一项新的研究表明，自本世纪初以来，2型糖尿病新发病例率急剧上升，近年来持续加速。这项研究将提交给在意大利米兰举行的欧洲糖尿病研究协会（EASD）年（9月28日至10月2日）年会。该研究由科罗拉多州奥罗拉市科罗拉多公共卫生学院的Tessa Crume博士及其同事进行。 在美国，青少年发病的1型和2型糖尿病发病率几十年来稳步增长，导致糖尿病相关的发病率和死亡率负担显著增加。在这项新研究中，作者调查了2002年至2023年间科罗拉多州青少年发病1型和2型糖尿病的长期趋势。 科罗拉多糖尿病登记中心自2002年以来，利用诊断编码、实验室结果和药物数据，对全州卫生系统内20岁以下人群的糖尿病发病率进行了持续的、人口水平的年度分析。符合条件的参与者为在诊断时居住在科罗拉多州的非军人、非机构化个体。 人口分母数据来源于人口普查。研究使用统计模型来估计随时间变化的趋势。1型糖尿病的发病率按每10万20岁以下人群计算，2型糖尿病的发病率按每10万10-19岁青少年计算（因为10岁以下儿童的2型糖尿病病例极为罕见）。 青少年2型糖尿病病例激增 在2002年至2023年间，共有8,094名20岁以下的儿童和青少年新诊断为1型糖尿病，1,560名10-19岁青少年新诊断为2型糖尿病。1型糖尿病的发病率略有上升，从2002年的每10万25.07例增至2023年的每10万27.39例。2002年至2014年间，1型糖尿病发病率保持稳定趋势（年度百分比变化[APC] = 0.66%），随后在2015年至2023年间呈现上升趋势（APC = 2.62%）。 然而，10-19岁青少年2型糖尿病的发病率几乎增加了10倍，从2002年的每10万2.37例增至2023年的每10万22.30例。2002年至2023年整个期间，2型糖尿病发病率上升了3.8%；2002年至2013年间每年上升2.92%；2014年至2023年间上升幅度更大，每年上升4.72%。 作者表示：“在本世纪初以来的二十多年里，青少年新发2型糖尿病的发病率几乎增加了10倍，而1型糖尿病发病率仅略有上升。这些发现记录了一个不断扩大的青少年群体面临着贯穿生命周期的糖尿病相关并发症风险，并强调了持续长期监测的重要性。” Crume博士解释说：“一代人之前，青少年2型糖尿病非常罕见，许多儿科临床医生在职业生涯的大部分时间里可能都不会遇到一个病例；它是一种中年人的疾病。在科罗拉多州，我们现在看到它在年轻人中仅在二十年内就增加了近十倍，而且这种增长正在加速而不是减缓。” “这并非局部异常：在美国的‘SEARCH for Diabetes in 作者解释说，儿童肥胖率的上升意味着在刚诊断的青少年中，已经无法可靠地区分1型和2型糖尿病；这两种疾病在临床表现上日益重叠。一个身体质量指数（BMI）高且出现高血糖的青少年，可能患有1型或2型糖尿病。 区分它们需要检测糖尿病自身抗体（其标志着1型的自身免疫过程）和胰岛素抵抗（其特征是2型）。
