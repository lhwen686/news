CDC warns of E. coli outbreak linked to frozen blueberries.

这篇新闻警示的核心专业概念是产志贺毒素大肠杆菌（STEC）感染及其严重的临床并发症——溶血性尿毒综合征（HUS）。STEC，特别是大肠杆菌O157:H7，通过摄入受污染的食物（在此例中为冷冻蓝莓）进入人体。其致病机制在于细菌在肠道内定植并产生志贺毒素，这些毒素随血液循环到达靶器官，尤其对肾脏内皮细胞具有高度亲和力。志贺毒素会损害血管内皮，导致微血管病性溶血性贫血、血小板减少和急性肾损伤，即HUS。HUS是一种危及生命的综合征，需要紧急医疗干预。

此次事件对行业产生了深远影响。首先，它凸显了食品供应链中冷冻农产品（特别是即食产品）严格质量控制和可追溯性的极端重要性。食品生产商需加强从农场到餐桌的全面风险管理，包括源头控制、生产环境卫生、加工工艺优化及成品检测。其次，对于公共卫生系统而言，快速的疫情监测、风险评估和召回机制至关重要，以有效阻断疾病传播并保护公众健康。同时，临床医生需提高对STEC感染及HUS的早期识别能力，以便及时进行支持性治疗，改善患者预后。此类事件不仅造成经济损失和品牌信誉损害，更促使整个食品和公共卫生行业不断完善其安全标准和应对策略。
Original Text
A CDC food safety alert has been posted at https://www.cdc.gov/ecoli/outbreaks/blueberries-07-26/index.html.

Key Points: Twelve people in two states have gotten sick from the same strain of coli that has been linked to frozen blueberries.

On July 3, 2026, Frutas y Hortalizas del Sur S.A. recalled frozen blueberries.

frozen blueberries. Affected product: Frozen GreenWise Organic IQF Blueberries, 10 oz Lot Code: 60401 Best By Date: February 9, 2028 Shipped to Publix retail stores throughout 8 states including Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, and Virginia.



What the Public Should Do:

Do not eat any recalled frozen blueberries. Throw them away or return them to where you bought them.

Wash items and surfaces that may have touched the recalled blueberries using hot soapy water or a dishwasher.

Call your healthcare provider if you have any severe E. coli symptoms.

What Businesses Should Do:

Do not sell or serve recalled frozen blueberries.

Wash and sanitize items and surfaces that may have come in contact with recalled blueberries.

E. coli Symptoms:

Most people infected with Shiga toxin-producing E. coli experience severe stomach cramps, diarrhea (often bloody), and vomiting.

Symptoms usually start 3 to 4 days after swallowing the bacteria and most people recover without treatment after 5 to 7 days.

Some people may develop serious kidney problems (hemolytic uremic syndrome, also called HUS) and would need to be hospitalized.

Clues that someone is developing HUS include decreased frequency of urination, feeling very tired, and losing pink color in cheeks and inside the lower eyelids.

For more information about E. coli, see the About Escherichia coli Infection.

If you have questions about cases in a particular state, please call that state's health department.
