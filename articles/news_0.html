深度解析

该报告生动地展示了尖端技术如何重塑肿瘤学领域。精准医疗方法是这一转变的核心，包括分子靶向疗法、免疫检查点抑制剂和嵌合抗原受体 (CAR) T细胞疗法。这些疗法通过特异性靶向癌细胞中的基因改变，或通过重新教育患者自身的免疫系统识别并摧毁肿瘤，超越了传统的广谱化疗。例如，蛋白水解靶向嵌合体 (PROTACs) 利用细胞固有的蛋白质降解机制，提供了一种消除先前被认为“不可成药”的致癌蛋白质的新颖机制。这种向生物学驱动治疗的转变有望提高疗效并降低毒性，从而显著改善许多患者的生存率。 人工智能 (AI) 是另一个关键驱动力，它加速了整个癌症护理链条的发展。在基础研究中，AI与空间成像技术结合，能够生成高分辨率肿瘤图谱，解析肿瘤微环境内复杂的相互作用，这对识别新的治疗靶点至关重要。在药物开发方面，AI模型可以快速筛选庞大的化合物库，极大地缩短了药物发现的时间。在临床上，AI通过改进影像分析来增强早期检测，预测患者预后，并个性化治疗方案，从而简化护理流程，并可能减轻医护人员的职业倦怠。 最后，预防性和治疗性癌症疫苗正获得显著关注。虽然HPV疫苗等预防性疫苗早已存在，但报告中提及的黑色素瘤和乳腺癌的个体化mRNA新抗原疫苗的开发，代表着一项重大飞跃。这些疫苗利用患者独特的肿瘤突变来刺激高度特异且持久的T细胞免疫反应，有望实现长期缓解且副作用最小。总而言之，这些创新预示着一个高度精确、数据驱动和免疫智能的癌症干预新时代，从根本上改变了患者预后和癌症治疗的未来格局。

Today, the American Association for Cancer Research (AACR) released the 16th edition of its annual Cancer Progress Report. This comprehensive report provides current data on cancer incidence, mortality, and survivorship. It also outlines how basic, translational, and clinical cancer research and cancer-related population science-largely supported by federal investments in the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the National Cancer Institute (NCI), the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)-are improving health and saving lives. The AACR Cancer Progress Report 2026 highlights the latest advances in understanding, preventing, detecting, and treating cancer as well as important information about supporting cancer patients and survivors. This year's report also looks ahead to the future of cancer research, describing the promising impact of technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI) and liquid biopsy along with cutting-edge therapeutics like cancer vaccines and RNA medicines. Several patients who have benefited from recent scientific breakthroughs generously shared their personal stories in the report, thereby illustrating the real-world impact of cancer research. A cornerstone of AACR's educational and advocacy work, the report closes with specific policy recommendations to achieve a healthier future for all Americans and a strong Call to Action that urges Congress and other policymakers to protect the investments in research and merit-based systems that make progress against cancer possible. Over the course of my 25-year career as a physician-scientist, I have witnessed a remarkable transformation in our understanding of cancer and how we diagnose and treat patients. Advances in genomics, immunology, and artificial intelligence have given us more insight than ever before into the complexities of cancer. This knowledge is helping us to develop highly effective treatments-and entirely new therapeutic approaches-that more precisely match each patient's disease. As this year's report illustrates, progress against cancer is proceeding rapidly, with more breakthroughs on the horizon. We can't stop here. Now is the time to invest in medical research, encourage collaboration across laboratories and disciplines, and support the next generation of cancer scientists. By doing so, we will accelerate discoveries and translate them into increasingly precise, biology-driven approaches that will transform the lives of patients with cancer." Keith Flaherty, MD, FAACR, AACR President, chair of the AACR Cancer Progress Report 2026 Steering Committee Research is driving progress against cancer Years of coordinated efforts and multidisciplinary collaborations among stakeholders in the medical research community have led to remarkable progress against cancer. According to the AACR Cancer Progress Report 2026: From July 1, 2025, to June 30, 2026, FDA approved 11 new anticancer therapeutics, including: the first molecularly targeted therapy for a rare, aggressive brain tumor with a specific genetic alteration; the first approval of a proteolysis-targeting chimera, a molecularly targeted therapeutic that harnesses the cell's protein degradation machinery to kill cancer cells, for patients with breast cancer; the first immune checkpoint inhibitor for ovarian cancer; and the first chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy for patients with marginal zone lymphoma. During this same time frame, FDA also approved: new uses for five previously approved anticancer therapeutics; a wearable, noninvasive device for treating pancreatic cancer; an at-home human papillomavirus (HPV) self-collection kit; and several AI-based tools to improve early detection and diagnosis of cancers. Reductions in smoking rates and advances in prevention, early detection, and treatment have contributed to the decline of the overall cancer death rate in the United States by 35% between 1991 and 2024-resulting in 4.8 million fewer cancer deaths. Increasingly, research progress is contributing to the survival of individuals with advanced and metastatic cancers. For all cancers combined, the five-year relative survival rate for distant-stage disease has more than doubled over the past three decades, increasing from 17% in the mid-1990s to 36% during 2016-2022. New frontiers in cancer research In addition to documenting recent progress against cancer, the AACR Cancer Progress Report 2026 explores several emerging and ongoing areas of research. GLP-1 use and cancer risk Weight loss interventions, including glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists, may reduce the risk of developing obesity-related cancers: A recent study found that semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy) use was associated with a 12% reduction in obesity-related cancer risk. In a separate study, GLP-1 use among individuals with type 2 diabetes was associated with a 40% and 46% lower risk of esophageal and colorectal cancer, respectively, compared with insulin therapy. GLP-1 use may also be associated with reductions in the risk of skin, breast, prostate, hematologic, and some gynecologic cancers. As the AACR Cancer Progress Report 2026 explains, however, evidence regarding GLP-1 use and cancer risk reduction remains inconsistent. A large review of 48 clinical trials involving more than 94,000 participants treated with GLP-1s found no significant reduction in the overall risk of obesity-related cancers. Other studies have found that GLP-1 use may increase the risk of some cancers. For example, liraglutide (Victoza/Saxenda) use increased the risk of thyroid and respiratory cancer by 70% and 62%, respectively. In short, the role of these drugs in cancer prevention, while promising, requires further investigation. Artificial intelligence AI is enabling faster and more efficient approaches to cancer research and clinical care. According to the report: AI can accelerate basic research discoveries. Researchers recently combined AI with advanced spatial imaging technologies to generate high-resolution maps of tumors, revealing the spatial organization and interactions of cancer and immune cells within the tumor microenvironment. AI is reshaping cancer drug development. Using one recently developed AI model, researchers screened approximately 10,000 human proteins against more than 500 million compounds, identifying more than 2 million candidate molecules. AI demonstrates benefits in cancer screening and early detection, improving the interpretation of routine medical images and allowing for more accurate analysis of complex digital images of tissue samples, as reflected by recent FDA approvals of several AI-assisted devices and software tools. AI-driven technologies are improving clinicians' ability to predict patient outcomes, personalize treatment plans, and enhance clinical trial design. They also may help prevent burnout among health care providers. Cancer vaccines After decades of research and steady progress, preventive and therapeutic cancer vaccines are gaining momentum in the clinic and represent a promising treatment strategy for many different types of cancer. According to the AACR Cancer Progress Report 2026, new technologies are allowing researchers to improve vaccine design, identify new tumor-specific targets, and optimize combinations with other therapies. Recent advances include: A small individualized pancreatic cancer vaccine trial has led to durable remissions in patients such as Donna Gustafson, who shared her story in this year's report. Patients with high-risk melanoma who received an individualized mRNA neoantigen vaccine alongside immunotherapy after surgery experienced sustained improvements in recurrence-free and distant metastasis-free survival compared with immunotherapy alone. In patients with early-stage triple-negative breast cancer, an individualized mRNA vaccine generated durable neoantigen-specific T-cell responses, with most patients remaining relapse-free for up to six years after vaccination. A small clinical trial testing Nous-209, an mRNA-based vaccine designed to invoke the immune response against cells carrying Lynch syndrome-associated gene mutations, found that the vaccine was safe, caused no serious side effects, and triggered a strong, durable immune response in people with Lynch syndrome. Despite progress, cancer remains a threat While new technologies and treatments are bringing substantial hope and improving outcomes for many patients, cancer still afflicts millions of Americans. In 2026, it is estimated that more than 2.1 million new cancer cases will be diagnosed, and more than 626,000 people will die from cancer in the United States alone. By 2050, the number of new cancer cases in the United States is projected to surpass 2.5 million, a 19% increase from the current number. Moreover, survival rates remain stubbornly low for some cancers. For example, between 2016 and 2022, the overall five-year relative survival rate was 13.7% for pancreatic cancer and 6.6% for glioblastoma, an aggressive form of brain cancer, compared with 91.9% for female breast cancer and 98.2% for prostate cancer. As discussed in the AACR Cancer Progress Report 2026, the growing incidence of early-onset cancers, defined as cancers diagnosed in people aged 18 to 49 years, has emerged as a pressing public health concern in recent years. Globally, cancer incidence among individuals under the age of 50 increased by nearly 80% between 1990 and 2019. While incidence rates are increasing across many different cancers, certain gastrointestinal cancers-such as colorectal, stomach, and esophageal cancers-have contributed disproportionally to the rise in early-onset cancers. Researchers have identified a number of overlapping risk factors that may be driving these increases, including rising obesity rates, more sedentary lifestyles, greater exposure to environmental pollutants, and shifts in dietary patterns. Cancer also exerts a significant financial toll on individuals and society. In the United States, the direct costs associated with cancer care, many of which are borne by patients, were estimated to be more than $236 billion in 2020. The overall economic impact of cancer, including indirect costs and lost productivity, is far higher and expected to grow. One analysis cited in the report estimates that cancer will cost the U.S. economy $5.3 trillion over the next three decades, thus calling attention to the important role of cancer research in averting such adverse effects on the U.S. economy. AACR calls on policymakers to protect cancer research for the benefit of patients As demonstrated throughout this year's report, decades of federal investment in cancer research have led to remarkable advances for patients. Progress in cancer prevention, early detection, treatment, and survivorship has helped individuals in every community live longer, healthier lives. This progress will only continue, however, if Congress, the Administration, federal agencies, and state policymakers protect the systems that make meritorious scientific and medical research possible. Most urgently, Congress must stop the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) from finalizing or implementing its proposed rule, "Regulation for Federal Financial Assistance." As discussed in previous AACR statements, this proposal would inject political considerations into funding decisions and broaden federal agencies' authority to terminate grants for reasons unrelated to scientific merit or recipient performance. Thankfully, bipartisan action in Congress has blocked the rule through December 11, 2026. A temporary delay, however, is not a lasting solution. At this pivotal moment, the AACR Cancer Progress Report 2026 ends with a strong Call to Action urging policymakers to stand firmly in support of cancer research and cancer patients and to ensure that federal grantmaking remains guided by evidence, scientific merit, transparency, and accountability: Sustain the foundations of discovery science and medicine Provide robust and sustained federal investment in medical and cancer research with at least $51.303 billion for NIH and at least $7.999 billion for NCI in fiscal year 2027. Ensure that federal research funds are implemented as Congress intended. When funding is delayed, narrowed, redirected, or structured in ways that reduce the number of meritorious grants supported, the consequences negatively impact the entire cancer research enterprise and, ultimately, the lives of patients who are waiting for better options. Prioritize and sustain the cancer research workforce. Protect scientific integrity and independence Preserve scientific merit, not political or ideological considerations, as the foundation of federal research funding. Safeguard the stability of active research awards and clinical trials. Active research grants should not be terminated for reasons unrelated to recipient performance, scientific integrity, or responsible stewardship of federal funds. Protect researchers' ability to publish and share scientific findings to maximize innovation. Accessing the scientific literature, participating in scientific and clinical conferences, and exchanging knowledge with colleagues are essential to scientific progress and to ensuring that the public benefits from taxpayer-funded research. Apply rigorous, risk-based oversight to international scientific collaboration. Protecting research integrity and national security is essential, but oversight should be targeted and proportionate to risk. Ensure that every patient benefits from progress against cancer Strengthen FDA's ability to translate discoveries into benefits for cancer patients. Sustain evidence-based public health programs that prevent cancer and promote earlier detection. Modernize the infrastructure that connects research and care by investing in interoperable data systems, responsible digital health technologies, the cancer workforce, and community-based care infrastructure. Remove barriers to cancer prevention and care through expanding access to evidence-based prevention and screening services, broadening participation in clinical trials, and supporting comprehensive treatment and survivorship care. "As this year's AACR Cancer Progress Report illustrates, decades of robust, sustained, and predictable federal investment in cancer research have transformed patient care and saved millions of lives," said AACR Chief Executive Officer Margaret Foti, PhD, MD (hc). "Advances in our understanding of cancer and in the technologies available to cancer researchers and oncologists mean that the promise and momentum of scientific discovery have never been greater. If we are to continue making progress for the millions of Americans affected by cancer, it is essential that Congress protect funding for cancer research as well as the rigorous federal grantmaking system that has helped establish the United States as the global scientific leader. Only by sustaining this commitment to meritorious research, free of political interference, will we be able to discover the next generation of scientific breakthroughs that lead to lifesaving cancer cures."

此外，某些癌症的生存率仍然顽固地低下。例如，在2016年至2022年间，胰腺癌的五年总体相对生存率为13.7%，胶质母细胞瘤（一种侵袭性脑癌）为6.6%，而女性乳腺癌为91.9%，前列腺癌为98.2%。 正如AACR 2026年癌症进展报告所讨论的，早发性癌症（定义为在18至49岁人群中诊断出的癌症）的发病率不断上升，近年来已成为一个紧迫的公共卫生问题。在全球范围内，1990年至2019年间，50岁以下人群的癌症发病率增加了近80%。虽然许多不同癌症的发病率都在上升，但某些胃肠道癌症——如结直肠癌、胃癌和食管癌——在早发性癌症的增长中占了不成比例的份额。研究人员已识别出一些可能导致这些增长的重叠风险因素，包括肥胖率上升、久坐不动的生活方式、更多地接触环境污染物以及饮食模式的变化。 癌症还给个人和社会带来了巨大的经济负担。在美国，2020年与癌症护理相关的直接成本（其中大部分由患者承担）估计超过2360亿美元。癌症的总体经济影响，包括间接成本和生产力损失，远高于此，并预计将持续增长。报告引用的一项分析估计，未来三十年癌症将给美国经济造成5.3万亿美元的损失，这强调了癌症研究在避免此类对美国经济不利影响方面的重要作用。 AACR呼吁政策制定者保护癌症研究以造福患者 正如今年报告中所示，数十年来联邦对癌症研究的投资为患者带来了显著的进展。癌症预防、早期检测、治疗和生存期方面的进展帮助各个社区的个人活得更长、更健康。然而，只有国会、行政部门、联邦机构和州政策制定者保护使有价值的科学和医学研究成为可能体系，这种进展才能继续。 最紧迫的是，国会必须阻止联邦预算与管理办公室 (OMB) 最终确定或实施其拟议的“联邦财政援助条例”。正如AACR之前声明中讨论的，该提案将把政治考量引入资助决策，并扩大联邦机构因与科学价值或受资助方表现无关的原因终止拨款的权力。值得庆幸的是，国会的两党行动已将该条例的实施阻止至2026年12月11日。然而，暂时的延迟并非长久之计。 在这个关键时刻，AACR 2026年癌症进展报告以一份强有力的“行动呼吁”作为结尾，敦促政策制定者坚定支持癌症研究和癌症患者，并确保联邦拨款始终以证据、科学价值、透明度和问责制为指导： 维护发现科学和医学的基础 为医学和癌症研究提供强劲和持续的联邦投资，在2027财政年度至少为NIH提供513.03亿美元，至少为NCI提供79.99亿美元。 确保联邦研究资金按国会意图实施。当资金被延迟、缩减、转移或以减少有价值拨款数量的方式进行结构化时，其后果将对整个癌症研究事业，并最终对等待更好选择的患者生活产生负面影响。 优先关注并支持癌症研究队伍。 保护科学诚信和独立性 将科学价值而非政治或意识形态考量作为联邦研究资助的基础。 保障活跃研究奖项和临床试验的稳定性。活跃的研究拨款不应因与受资助方表现、科学诚信或联邦资金负责任管理无关的原因而被终止。 保护研究人员发表和分享科学发现的能力，以最大限度地促进创新。查阅科学文献、参与科学和临床会议以及与同事交流知识对于科学进步和确保公众从纳税人资助的研究中受益至关重要。 对国际科学合作实施严格的、基于风险的监督。保护研究诚信和国家安全至关重要，但监督应具有针对性并与风险相称。 确保每位患者都从抗癌进展中受益 加强FDA将发现转化为癌症患者福祉的能力。 支持基于证据的公共卫生项目，以预防癌症并促进早期检测。 通过投资可互操作的数据系统、负责任的数字健康技术、癌症医护人员和社区为基础的护理基础设施，实现研究与护理连接基础设施的现代化。 通过扩大基于证据的预防和筛查服务的可及性、拓宽临床试验的参与度以及支持全面的治疗和生存期护理，消除癌症预防和护理的障碍。 “正如今年的AACR癌症进展报告所阐释的，数十年来联邦对癌症研究的强劲、持续和可预测的投资，已经改变了患者护理并挽救了数百万生命。”AACR首席执行官玛格丽特·福蒂 (Margaret Foti) 博士 (荣誉医学博士) 表示，“我们对癌症的理解以及癌症研究人员和肿瘤学家可用的技术进步，意味着科学发现的承诺和动力从未如此之大。如果我们想继续为数百万受癌症影响的美国人取得进展，国会就必须保护癌症研究的资金以及帮助美国成为全球科学领导者的严谨联邦拨款制度。只有通过维持对基于价值研究的承诺，并使其免受政治干预，我们才能发现下一代科学突破，从而带来拯救生命的癌症疗法。”
