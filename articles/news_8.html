深度解析

这篇文章深入揭示了英伟达（Nvidia）从一家领先的芯片制造商向AI生态系统关键战略投资者的角色转变。其核心商业逻辑在于，通过大规模投资“游戏规则改变者和市场创造者”类型的初创公司，如Anthropic和OpenAI，英伟达并非单纯追求财务回报，而是旨在战略性地扩展其核心产品——AI芯片的市场需求。这种“投资即生态”的模式，确保了有更多创新企业采纳并推动其GPU技术，从而形成一个相互促进的良性循环。英伟达甚至成为数据中心建设的重要金主，提供巨额担保，进一步巩固了其在AI基础设施领域的影响力。然而，文章也指出，在创始人兼首席执行官黄仁勋（Jensen Huang）可能卸任后，这种激进的投资策略面临不确定性。新任临时CEO克雷斯（Kress）倾向于更保守的资本管理，强调将自由现金流返还给股东，并对未来投资进行更严格的评估。这一转变对整个风险投资（VC）生态系统具有深远影响。英伟达作为AI领域的重要资金来源，其投资策略的调整可能导致AI初创公司面临资本回撤，尤其是在其曾大力支持的那些资本密集型项目上。VC们正为此做准备，预示着未来AI创业公司的融资环境可能会变得更加严峻，从而影响整个AI行业的创新速度和发展格局。

英文原文

Original
Nvidia’s equity in private companies continues to grow: it revealed its stakes are now worth $65.5 billion on Tuesday, up from the $22 billion reported in January. The increase includes both new investments and significant markups of existing portfolio companies. The company invested $16 billion into equities in the past quarter alone, it said, and $58 billion in the past nine months of its fiscal year. This amount includes buying shares in public and private companies, yet it shows how the chipmaker made massive bets over the course of the year, especially compared to last year; in November 2025, the company reported spending $5 billion buying equity in the first nine months of that fiscal year. But it’s unclear if this growth will continue under the interim CEO and Jensen Huang’s successor. Nvidia stated in late 2023 that the goal of its corporate investing is to expand the AI ecosystem by backing startups it considers to be “game changers and market makers.” Over the nearly three years that followed, the chipmaker has invested in nearly 350 startups, according to PitchBook. The companies Nvidia backed include Anthropic, OpenAI; neolabs like Thinking Machines and Reflection; neoclouds, like Lambda, Nscale, and Crusoe; as well as other companies developing AI infrastructure, robotics startups, and applications. It’s also become a big financier of the data center build out, reporting $118 billion in guarantees with $105 billion of that the backstop it supplied for OpenAI’s $500 billion data center in Ohio. The company made those commitments by allocating a meaningful portion of its excess cash flows. In Tuesday’s earning call, Kress said Nvidia would continue providing financing assistance to strong customers and that “as the market matures and additional sources of capital become available,” the company would be “rigorously evaluating future investments, commitments, and guarantees.” But the question for the VC ecosystem is whether the company will continue to back startups at anywhere near the same level without Huang at the helm. Huang’s rampant dealmaking has reportedly been a source of concern for Kress. Investors have told TechCrunch that they anticipate that she will take a more conservative approach to startup investing, becoming more selective with new investments and writing smaller-sized checks into its existing portfolio companies. “We intend to return all excess free cash flow net of strategic uses to shareholders during this last quarter and going forward,” Kress said on the earnings call on Tuesday. Just how strategic startups will be under her leadership, and under the future permanent CEO, remains to be seen. But for now, VCs are bracing for a meaningful pullback in capital.
