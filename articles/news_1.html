AI 深度解析： 本文报道的ERAS-0015是一种创新型泛RAS分子胶，其核心专业概念在于精准肿瘤治疗中对RAS基因突变的靶向。RAS基因突变是多种人类癌症（包括胰腺癌和肺癌）中最常见的致癌驱动因素之一，但由于其蛋白质结构特征，长期以来被认为是“不可成药”的靶点。ERAS-0015通过其独特的“分子胶”机制，能特异性地结合处于“开启”状态的RAS突变蛋白，诱导蛋白质间异常相互作用，从而阻断癌细胞持续接收到的生长信号，最终抑制肿瘤生长。 这项I期临床试验的初步结果显示，ERAS-0015在难治性RAS突变型肺癌和胰腺癌患者中展现出令人鼓舞的临床活性（缓解率达44%-60%）和良好的耐受性，这对于这类预后极差、治疗选择有限的患者群体而言具有革命性意义。此前，针对RAS突变的药物开发主要集中于KRAS G12C等特定亚型，而ERAS-0015作为“泛RAS”抑制剂，有望覆盖更广泛的RAS突变类型。其成功将极大拓宽RAS突变型癌症的治疗版图，推动精准医疗向前发展，为患者带来新的生存希望，并可能引领新一代靶向药物的研发浪潮。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain Results from the AURORAS-1 Phase I clinical trial of an anticancer drug called ERAS-0015 have shown that it is safe and has caused tumors to shrink in hard-to-treat diseases such as lung and pancreatic cancer. Presented at the 38th EORTC-NCI-AACR Symposium on Molecular Targets and Cancer Therapeutics in Barcelona, Spain, the findings from 148 patients are significant because ERAS-0015 targets tumors whose growth is driven by mutations in the RAS gene. Until recently, it was not thought possible to design drugs that could target these mutations successfully. Dr. Judy Wang, a medical oncologist at Florida Cancer Specialists and Research Institute, Sarasota, U.S., told the symposium, "AURORAS-1 has enrolled patients who have tried a number of other treatments for their cancer. In this context, ERAS-0015 treatment caused a 44% response rate in patients with RAS-mutant pancreatic cancer and a 60% response rate in patients with RAS-mutant lung cancer. "A 'response' means the patients experienced considerable tumor shrinkage, and these response rates are important as they show clinically meaningful activity from ERAS-0015 treatment in patient groups that have few if any therapeutic options to address their cancer. "In addition to this, ERAS-0015 was well tolerated with few severe side effects." ERAS-0015 is a "pan-RAS molecular glue," which means that it brings proteins together at the molecular level in a way that blocks tumor growth signals from several different cancer-driving RAS mutations. Certain RAS mutations work like a switch that is locked in the "on" position, continuously conveying signals that lead to and sustain cancer. ERAS-0015 is designed to bind to the RAS "switch" while it is in the 'on' state and thereby blocks those signals. "The overall goal of precision oncology is to match a patient's mutation, or the 'Achilles heel' of a specific individual's cancer, to a drug that directly targets that mutation. This is what we are attempting to do by studying the effectiveness of ERAS-0015 in patients with RAS alterations," said Wang. The AURORAS-1 trial started in June 2025 and is evaluating ERAS-0015 on its own and in combination with other anticancer therapies in people living with several types of RAS-mutant solid tumors. Enrolled patients have non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) that has started to spread beyond the site of the primary tumor (locally advanced or metastatic), pancreatic cancer, colorectal (bowel) cancer and other solid tumors. The results presented were for 83 patients with pancreatic cancer and 37 with NSCLC; the trial is continuing to enroll patients. Doses of ERAS-0015 started at 2 mg and increased up to 40 mg. During an average (median) follow-up period of just over four months, there was no sign that the maximum tolerated dose had been reached. Tumors shrank in 11 of 25 patients (44%) with pancreatic cancer who received 32 mg of ERAS-0015 and who had been previously treated with chemotherapy. Tumors shrank in three of five patients (60%) with lung cancer who received 32 mg and who had been treated previously with an immune checkpoint inhibitor or platinum-based chemotherapy. The most common adverse side effects at doses of 16–32 mg in 10% or more of patients were rash, diarrhea, nausea and inflammation of the lining of the mouth. Twelve patients (9.8%) reported severe (grade 3 or higher) treatment-related side effects. No side effects limited the drug doses. "We are encouraged by the preliminary clinical activity and tolerability of ERAS-0015 in patients who have tried a number of other treatments for RAS-driven lung and pancreatic cancer," said Wang. "Our goal is to give clinicians new, better options for treating their patients with RAS-driven cancers. Early results suggest ERAS-0015 has the potential to be one of these options." Limitations of the study are those associated with Phase I trials that always include only a limited number of patients. Further study in greater numbers of patients and in Phase II and III clinical trials is needed to confirm the safety and efficacy of ERAS-0015. "If these early results are confirmed, ERAS-0015 could offer patients with RAS-driven cancers a new option. Clinical development of ERAS-0015 would be impossible without the patients, caregivers, investigators and participating trial centers that put their trust in us to take part in our study. We are deeply grateful for their contribution to AURORAS-1," she concluded. Elena Garralda, chair of the EORTC-NCI-AACR Symposium and co-director of the Vall d'Hebron Institute of Oncology's clinical research program, was not involved with the study. She commented, "This is the first presentation of these preliminary results from the AURORAS-1 clinical trial and they have been eagerly awaited. They show that the RAS inhibitor, ERAS-0015, has encouraging antitumor activity in cancers that are hard to treat successfully and for which there are few treatment options available. We look forward to further results from larger clinical trials, which are investigating whether ERAS-0015 can help a broader group of patients. "This is one of several studies being presented at the EORTC-NCI-AACR Symposium that are beginning to show it is possible to design drugs that target cancers driven by RAS mutations, which was not thought possible until recently."
