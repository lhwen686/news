深度解析

这篇新闻报道揭示了微生物组工程在临床应用中的重大突破。其核心专业概念在于“靶向活体生物治疗药物”的研发，即通过精心筛选和组合特定功能的有益细菌（如SER-155中的16种菌株），而非笼统的抗生素或粪便移植，来精确干预人体微生物环境。其医学机制在于这些精选菌株能够协同作用：它们可竞争有害病原体所需的营养，分泌抑制病原体生长的化合物，并调节宿主的免疫反应，从而恢复微生物组的健康平衡。这项技术原理标志着从被动观察到主动“设计”微生物组的转变。 这项研究的行业影响深远。它为高危患者（特别是接受异基因干细胞移植的血液癌症患者）预防危及生命的血液感染和脓毒症提供了一种创新策略，解决了当前抗生素无法根治微生物组失调的局限。这种精准的微生物疗法有望开辟全新的药物研发方向，推动生物技术和制药行业向更个性化、功能化的“微生物药物”发展。未来，这种微生物组工程技术或能应用于调节免疫疗法效果、治疗自身免疫疾病等更广泛的领域，彻底改变对人类健康的干预方式。

The first-of-its-kind therapy delivers 16 carefully selected beneficial bacteria designed to restore the microbiome and fight harmful pathogens. Credit: City of Hope Researchers at City of Hope and collaborators have pioneered a new approach to preventing dangerous bloodstream infections in patients receiving donor stem cell transplants for blood cancers. The first-of-its-kind therapy delivers 16 carefully selected beneficial bacteria designed to restore the microbiome and fight harmful pathogens. In the multicenter phase 1 clinical trial, scientists from City of Hope, Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center and Seres Therapeutics found that the new therapy, called SER-155, was well tolerated with no treatment-related side effects. Patients who received the therapy also had fewer bloodstream infections and lower levels of harmful bacteria linked to infection. The Seres Therapeutics–sponsored study, published in Nature Medicine, provides early evidence that live biotherapeutics—medicines made from beneficial bacteria—can be safely delivered to highly vulnerable patients and may help prevent serious infections, said senior author Marcel van den Brink, M.D., Ph.D., City of Hope chief physician executive and the Morgan and Helen Chu Presidential Chair of City of Hope National Medical Center. "This is a landmark study for the field," van den Brink said. "The immediate result is fewer bloodstream infections, but the larger significance is what it tells us about the future of medicine. For the first time, we've shown that it's possible to design a consortium of beneficial bacteria with a specific clinical purpose and to safely deliver it to highly vulnerable patients. That opens the door to an entirely new class of therapies." Treating the microbiome The microbiome—the trillions of bacteria and other microbes that live in and on the body—is increasingly recognized as a key factor in cancer care. Researchers are finding that it can influence how patients respond to treatment, recover from therapy or develop side effects. City of Hope is helping to turn those discoveries into new ways to prevent complications, improve treatment outcomes and support recovery. For this nationwide clinical trial, researchers focused on patients undergoing allogeneic stem cell transplantation, an effective but high-risk treatment for leukemia and other blood cancers. While the treatment can be lifesaving, it leaves patients highly vulnerable to infection as their immune systems recover. Bloodstream infections are among the most serious complications and can lead to sepsis, a life-threatening condition. Currently, patients undergoing transplants are given antibiotics to prevent infection. However, there are no therapies available that treat the underlying disruption of the microbiome that makes patients vulnerable to infection in the first place. Fecal transplants have been used to restore healthy gut bacteria in other conditions that disrupt the microbiome, like C. difficile infection. But these treatments are like a grab bag of bacteria—it can be hard to know what strains they actually contain. That makes them potentially dangerous for immunocompromised patients. A targeted therapy using bacteria For the new live biotherapeutic, researchers carefully identified and selected strains of bacteria that would offer targeted, infection-preventing benefits. These included bacteria that are known to eat up the nutrients used by bad bacteria, release pathogen-killing compounds or regulate the body's immune response. "Instead of giving patients a broad mix of bacteria, we selected specific strains for specific jobs and put them into a single pill," van den Brink said. "That level of precision is what makes this approach fundamentally different from simply trying to replenish the microbiome. We're moving from observing the microbiome to engineering it for specific therapeutic outcomes." In the phase 1 clinical trial, researchers first treated patients with an antibiotic to clear their existing microbiomes and make it easier for the introduced bacteria to establish new ecosystems. They then gave patients pills containing the selected bacteria. Treatment was given before transplant and repeated at least once after transplant, since many patients are given antibiotics to prevent infection. Because the treatment involves giving live bacteria to severely immunocompromised patients, a key question was whether the approach was safe. Researchers showed that it was. "The safety profile was excellent—we didn't see any complications with infections," van den Brink said. The study also showed that the therapy worked as intended. Researchers found that the good bacteria successfully established themselves in the microbiome, especially after the second course of treatment. Although the study wasn't focused on clinical outcomes, early results were promising. Patients who received the therapy had lower levels of the most harmful pathogens and a significantly lower risk of bloodstream infections. Beyond the encouraging results, van den Brink noted that the study is important because it demonstrates the potential for scientifically designed live biotherapeutic drugs. "We are only beginning to understand the therapeutic potential of the microbiome," he said. How cancer patients can support their microbiome Larger clinical trials are needed before the new therapy can be approved for use and made available to patients. However, research shows there are other strategies that can help support the microbiome during cancer treatment, van den Brink said. These include: Practice antibiotic stewardship: Patients should discuss the responsible use of antibiotics with their doctors, including avoiding the use of broad-spectrum antibiotics, which can disrupt the microbiome and promote antibiotic resistance. Eat a high-fiber diet: "Prebiotics," like whole grains, fruits, vegetables and other high-fiber foods, provide nutrients for good bacteria, supporting a healthy microbiome. "Diet is a drug," he said. "Fiber and fiber-rich diets are shown to be a benefit in every setting where it has been studied." Avoid probiotics: Over-the-counter probiotic capsules contain a mix of bacteria that may be dangerous for immunocompromised patients. In some cases, these capsules may contain strains of bacteria that studies have linked to worse outcomes in cancer, van den Brink noted. City of Hope's microbiome program is working on follow-up research, including studies and clinical trials to develop more therapies targeting the microbiome as a strategy for improving immunotherapy. 微生物组——生活在人体内外的数万亿细菌和其他微生物——正日益被认为是癌症治疗的关键因素。研究人员发现，它能影响患者对治疗的反应、从疗法中恢复的情况或副作用的发生。希望之城正致力于将这些发现转化为预防并发症、改善治疗结果和支持康复的新方法。 在这项全国性临床试验中，研究人员专注于接受异基因干细胞移植的患者，这是一种治疗白血病和其他血液癌症的有效但高风险的疗法。尽管该治疗可以挽救生命，但它使患者在免疫系统恢复期间极易受到感染。血液感染是最严重的并发症之一，可能导致危及生命的脓毒症。 目前，接受移植的患者会服用抗生素来预防感染。然而，目前尚无任何疗法能够治疗导致患者易受感染的微生物组的潜在破坏。 粪便移植已被用于在其他干扰微生物组的疾病（如艰难梭菌感染）中恢复健康的肠道细菌。但这些治疗就像一个“细菌盲盒”——很难知道它们实际包含哪些菌株。这使得它们对免疫功能低下患者具有潜在危险。 一种使用细菌的靶向疗法 对于这种新型活体生物治疗药物，研究人员仔细识别并筛选了能够提供靶向、预防感染益处的细菌菌株。这些细菌包括已知能消耗有害细菌所需营养、释放杀灭病原体的化合物或调节身体免疫反应的细菌。 “我们不是给患者广泛混合的细菌，而是针对特定任务选择了特定菌株，并将它们放入一个药丸中，”van den Brink说。“这种精确性使这种方法与仅仅尝试补充微生物组根本不同。我们正在从观察微生物组转向对其进行工程化，以实现特定的治疗结果。” 在1期临床试验中，研究人员首先用抗生素治疗患者，以清除他们现有的微生物组，使引入的细菌更容易建立新的生态系统。然后，他们给患者服用含有选定细菌的药丸。治疗在移植前进行，并在移植后至少重复一次，因为许多患者会服用抗生素来预防感染。 因为该治疗涉及给严重免疫功能低下患者施用活细菌，一个关键问题是该方法是否安全。研究人员证明它是安全的。 “安全性非常好——我们没有看到任何感染并发症，”van den Brink说。 研究还表明该疗法按预期发挥作用。研究人员发现，有益细菌成功地在微生物组中定植，尤其是在第二疗程治疗后。 尽管这项研究不侧重于临床结局，但早期结果令人鼓舞。接受该疗法的患者有害病原体水平较低，血液感染的风险也显著降低。 除了这些令人鼓舞的结果，van den Brink指出，这项研究很重要，因为它展示了科学设计的活体生物治疗药物的潜力。 他说：“我们才刚刚开始理解微生物组的治疗潜力。” 癌症患者如何支持他们的微生物组 在新疗法获批使用并向患者提供之前，还需要进行更大规模的临床试验。然而，van den Brink表示，研究表明还有其他策略可以帮助在癌症治疗期间支持微生物组。这些策略包括： 实践抗生素管理：患者应与医生讨论抗生素的负责任使用，包括避免使用广谱抗生素，因为这可能会破坏微生物组并促进抗生素耐药性。 食用高纤维饮食：“益生元”，如全谷物、水果、蔬菜和其他高纤维食物，为有益细菌提供营养，支持健康的微生物组。他说：“饮食就是一种药物。纤维和富含纤维的饮食在所有研究过的环境中都被证明有益。” 避免益生菌：非处方益生菌胶囊含有混合细菌，可能对免疫功能低下患者有危险。van den Brink指出，在某些情况下，这些胶囊可能含有与癌症患者预后不良相关的细菌菌株。
