深度解析

本研究揭示了胰高血糖素样肽-1（GLP-1）受体激动剂在治疗2型糖尿病和肥胖症时，可能增加患者脱发（斑秃）的风险。GLP-1受体激动剂，如司美格鲁肽和替西帕肽，通过模拟体内GLP-1的作用，促进葡萄糖依赖性胰岛素分泌、抑制胰高血糖素、延缓胃排空并增加饱腹感，从而有效控制血糖和减轻体重。其核心机制在于通过激活GLP-1受体，对胰腺、胃肠道和中枢神经系统产生多重效应。 研究指出，GLP-1受体激动剂使用者脱发风险显著高于SGLT-2抑制剂（通过肾脏排糖）和DPP-4抑制剂（通过抑制GLP-1降解）使用者。潜在机制可能包括快速体重减轻引发的休止期脱发、营养素（如铁、锌）缺乏以及激素变化，这些因素均可扰乱毛发生长周期。值得注意的是，增加的风险主要与非瘢痕性脱发相关，这意味着毛囊仍保持完整，具有再生潜力。尽管绝对风险较低，这一发现对于临床实践具有重要指导意义，提醒临床医生和患者在制定治疗决策时，应充分考虑并监测脱发这一潜在不良反应，以便在获益与风险之间取得平衡。

Original
This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies . Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pavel Danilyuk from Pexels The use of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists to treat type 2 diabetes and obesity is associated with an increased risk of hair loss (alopecia) in adults with type 2 diabetes, a study published by The BMJ finds. Although the absolute risk is low, awareness of this potential effect may help inform shared treatment decisions, the researchers say. Alopecia has been reported as a possible side effect of GLP-1 receptor agonists, particularly semaglutide and tirzepatide. However, studies assessing the risk of alopecia associated with GLP-1 receptor agonists compared with other diabetes drugs are lacking. To address this, researchers used electronic patient data from the University of Pennsylvania Health System (Penn Medicine) to compare rates of alopecia in adults with type 2 diabetes who started using GLP-1 receptor agonists or other types of diabetes drugs known as SGLT-2 inhibitors or DPP-4 inhibitors. In total, 12,004 patients using GLP-1 receptor agonists were compared with 15,221 using SGLT-2 inhibitors, and a further 11,964 GLP-1 users were compared with 11,233 DPP-4 inhibitor users between January 2019 and September 2024. Compared with SGLT-2 inhibitor users, GLP-1 receptor agonist users were younger (mean age 58 vs. 65), had a higher body mass index (36.2 vs. 32.3), and had lower rates of cardiovascular and chronic kidney diseases. Similarly, GLP-1 users were younger (mean age 58 vs. 67) and had a higher body mass index (36.2 vs. 31.3) than DPP-4 inhibitor users. After adjusting for potentially influential factors, including age, sex, ethnicity, pre-existing conditions, other medication use and body mass index, use of GLP-1 receptor agonists was associated with a 37% higher risk of alopecia than use of SGLT-2 inhibitors (6.91 vs. 5.04 per 1,000 person-years) and a 68% higher risk than use of DPP-4 inhibitors (6.53 vs. 3.89 per 1,000 person-years). Further analyses indicated that the association was specific to nonscarring alopecia (where hair follicles remain intact, leaving the potential for regrowth), with an increased risk of 53% and 72% compared with SGLT-2 inhibitors and DPP-4 inhibitors, respectively. The authors point out that rapid weight loss is a well-established trigger of hair shedding and can also lead to iron or zinc deficiencies, which disrupt the hair growth cycle. Hormonal changes may also be relevant, they note, although further studies are needed to clarify the underlying mechanisms. They also acknowledge several study limitations. For example, a lack of clinical information limited their ability to assess details such as the severity, extent, duration and reversibility of alopecia after stopping treatment. Nor can they rule out the possibility that other unmeasured factors may have influenced their results. However, they say this was a rigorous study based on high-quality data from a large representative cohort, and results were consistent after additional analyses, suggesting they are reliable. As such, they conclude, "Our findings extend previous anecdotal safety signals and provide more systematic evidence to inform clinical awareness of this potential adverse effect." Publication details Risk of hair loss associated with glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists in adults with type 2 diabetes: target trial emulation, The BMJ (2026). DOI: 10.1136/bmj-2026-100077 Journal information: British Medical Journal (BMJ)
