Review highlights essential neurological considerations for gender dysphoria care

本新闻深入探讨了性别焦虑症患者神经外科护理的复杂性。核心在于性别肯定激素治疗（GAHT）对神经系统的具体影响，包括灰质结构变化及脑膜瘤、中风、癫痫等神经系统并发症风险的增加。这揭示了激素在神经病理学中的关键机制作用。围手术期管理因GAHT引起的药物相互作用（如麻醉剂、抗癫痫药、抗病毒药等）而高度复杂，呼吸道和泌尿系统操作也需特殊考量。行业影响方面，该研究凸显了神经外科领域存在知识空白，迫切需要通过多学科合作及前瞻性专业培训来弥补。这旨在提升对该特殊群体的循证、包容性护理水平，减轻社会心理压力，最终推动医疗服务的公平与质量。

英文原文

Original
Gender dysphoria involves distress caused by a mismatch between a person's gender identity and sex assigned at birth. Gender-affirming care may include hormone therapy and surgery. However, neurosurgeons often report limited confidence in treating transgender patients because of gaps in knowledge about the neurological implications of gender dysphoria and hormone therapy. To address this gap, researchers from the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh reviewed existing literature to identify these implications and improve neurosurgical care. Gender dysphoria is a psychiatric condition characterized by distress due to a mismatch between the gender assigned at birth (based on external genitalia) and the self-perception of gender. Meanwhile, transgenderism is a generalized term used to describe a similar condition but may or may not involve the distress component. The incidence of gender dysphoria has gradually increased, especially among children and adolescents. Individuals with gender dysphoria seek various treatments, including gender-affirming hormone therapy (GAHT), psychotherapy, and surgery. However, these treatments are associated with several adverse effects. Neurosurgeons have decreased confidence in managing gender dysphoria owing to their limited knowledge and awareness of neurological implications associated with the condition and its treatment. To improve this awareness, Dr. Rajdeep Brahmachari and Dr. Chandrasekaran Kaliaperumal from the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh reviewed the literature on the neurological considerations of gender dysphoria. This review was published in Volume 12 of the Chinese Neurosurgical Journal in September 9, 2026. Commenting on the rationale for the study, Dr. Kaliaperumal remarked, "Gender dysphoria and its treatment are associated with several neurological issues. Effective management can be achieved by improving the knowledge of neurosurgeons. Therefore, we reviewed the literature to clarify neuroanatomical variations, neurological and neurosurgical risk factors, and perioperative, neuroanesthetic, and neuropsychiatric considerations, enabling evidence-based holistic neurosurgical care." The authors analyzed published articles retrieved from various sources, including public databases. The anatomical features of the brains of transgender individuals resembled the self-identified gender rather than those of the assigned gender, including increased cortical thickness and weak connections in regions involved in processing body perception. Gender-affirming treatment can also lead to physiological changes. For example, GAHT increases gray matter density, volume and the risk of headaches, benign tumors (meningioma and prolactinoma), intracranial hypertension, stroke, and epilepsy. Individuals with gender dysphoria are hesitant to seek surgical treatment owing to stigma, social isolation, and prejudice. To mitigate this hesitancy, neurosurgeons must utilize inclusive language while communicating and document pre-surgical GAHT duration. Patients must be informed about the benefits and risks of the surgery and encouraged to seek advice from a mental health professional for further clarity. Neurosurgeons can increase patient comfort by recording the preferred gender identity and pronouns along with the details of previous or ongoing GAHT before surgery. The preoperative risks of neurosurgery include viral and bacterial infections. The increased prevalence of stress, anxiety, and depression among transgender individuals may predispose them to smoking, alcohol consumption, and drug abuse, increasing perioperative risks. Transgender individuals are also predisposed to metabolic disorders, such as diabetes and insulin resistance. Thus, neurosurgeons must account for preoperative and perioperative risks to mitigate adverse events. Sedation during surgery is an important consideration. Anti-seizure medications, corticosteroids, and neuromuscular blockers may exhibit interactions in individuals undergoing hormonal therapy before surgery. Thus, perioperative neuromuscular monitoring is warranted when the neurosurgery involves the use of neuromuscular blocking agents. Intraoperative risks are especially evident in patients with human immunodeficiency virus infection. Antiviral drugs may interact with hypnotics, anxiolytics, and antibiotics. As GAHT can induce hematological changes, intraoperative blood transfusion must be considered. Surgery may also involve respiratory risks for individuals with gender dysphoria, especially in transmen who use breast-binder garments. Meanwhile, intubation is a concern in transwomen who have undergone laryngeal surgery. Special considerations are warranted for urinary catheterization in individuals who have previously undergone gender-affirming surgery. When preferred, private rooms can enhance patient privacy and comfort after surgery. Healthcare personnel attending the neurosurgical patient can mitigate psychosocial stress by addressing the patient with preferred names and pronouns and effectively managing wounds and scars. The implementation of a multidisciplinary approach for pain management can improve surgical outcomes. Commenting on the importance of this review, Dr. Brahmachari states, "Our review summarizes the medical and psychosocial implications of neurosurgery for individuals with gender dysphoria. Proactive training for neurosurgeons and healthcare personnel can improve the outcomes of neurosurgery in these individuals."

性别焦虑症是指一个人的性别认同与出生时被指定的性别不匹配所引起的痛苦。性别肯定治疗可能包括激素治疗和手术。然而，神经外科医生常常表示对治疗跨性别患者信心不足，因为他们对性别焦虑症和激素治疗的神经系统影响存在知识空白。为了弥补这一空白，爱丁堡皇家医院的研究人员回顾了现有文献，以识别这些影响并改进神经外科护理。 性别焦虑症是一种精神疾病，其特征是由于出生时指定的性别（基于外生殖器）与性别自我认知不匹配而引起的痛苦。与此同时，“跨性别主义”是一个通用术语，用于描述类似的情况，但可能涉及或不涉及痛苦成分。性别焦虑症的发病率逐渐增加，尤其是在儿童和青少年中。患有性别焦虑症的个体寻求各种治疗，包括性别肯定激素治疗（GAHT）、心理治疗和手术。然而，这些治疗与若干不良反应相关。神经外科医生由于对与该疾病及其治疗相关的神经系统影响的知识和认识有限，对管理性别焦虑症的信心有所下降。 为了提高这种认识，爱丁堡皇家医院的Rajdeep Brahmachari医生和Chandrasekaran Kaliaperumal医生回顾了关于性别焦虑症神经系统考量的文献。这篇综述发表在2026年9月9日的《中华神经外科杂志》第12卷上。Kaliaperumal医生在评论这项研究的理由时指出：“性别焦虑症及其治疗与若干神经系统问题相关。通过提高神经外科医生的知识可以实现有效管理。因此，我们回顾了文献，以阐明神经解剖学变异、神经系统和神经外科风险因素，以及围手术期、神经麻醉和神经精神科考量，从而实现基于证据的整体神经外科护理。” 作者分析了从包括公共数据库在内的各种来源检索到的已发表文章。跨性别个体的脑部解剖特征与其自我认同的性别而非指定性别相似，包括皮质厚度增加以及涉及身体感知处理区域的连接较弱。性别肯定治疗也可能导致生理变化。例如，GAHT会增加灰质密度、体积，并增加头痛、良性肿瘤（脑膜瘤和垂体泌乳素瘤）、颅内高压、中风和癫痫的风险。 由于污名、社会孤立和偏见，患有性别焦虑症的个体不愿寻求手术治疗。为了减轻这种犹豫，神经外科医生在沟通时必须使用包容性语言，并记录术前GAHT的持续时间。必须告知患者手术的益处和风险，并鼓励他们向精神卫生专业人员寻求建议以进一步阐明。神经外科医生可以通过在手术前记录偏好的性别认同和代词，以及之前或正在进行的GAHT的详细信息来增加患者的舒适度。 神经外科手术的术前风险包括病毒和细菌感染。跨性别个体中应激、焦虑和抑郁的患病率增加，可能使他们更易吸烟、饮酒和药物滥用，从而增加围手术期风险。跨性别个体也易患代谢紊乱，如糖尿病和胰岛素抵抗。因此，神经外科医生必须考虑术前和围手术期风险以减轻不良事件。手术期间的镇静是一个重要的考虑因素。抗癫痫药物、皮质类固醇和神经肌肉阻滞剂在术前接受激素治疗的个体中可能表现出相互作用。 因此，当神经外科手术涉及使用神经肌肉阻滞剂时，围手术期神经肌肉监测是必要的。术中风险在人类免疫缺陷病毒感染患者中尤为明显。抗病毒药物可能与催眠药、抗焦虑药和抗生素发生相互作用。由于GAHT可能引起血液学变化，必须考虑术中输血。手术还可能涉及性别焦虑症个体的呼吸道风险，尤其是在使用束胸衣的跨性别男性中。同时，对于接受过喉部手术的跨性别女性来说，气管插管是一个值得关注的问题。对于曾接受过性别肯定手术的个体，导尿需要特殊考量。 如果患者偏好，单人病房可以在手术后提高患者的隐私和舒适度。照护神经外科患者的医护人员可以通过使用患者偏好的姓名和代词称呼，并有效管理伤口和疤痕来减轻社会心理压力。实施多学科疼痛管理方法可以改善手术结果。Brahmachari医生在评论这项综述的重要性时指出：“我们的综述总结了神经外科手术对患有性别焦虑症个体的医学和社会心理影响。对神经外科医生和医护人员进行前瞻性培训可以改善这些个体的神经外科手术结果。”
