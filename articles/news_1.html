深度解析

这项研究深入探讨了环境毒理学与公共卫生之间的一个关键交叉点，旨在揭示全氟和多氟烷基物质（PFAS）对蚊子适应性及其传播虫媒病毒能力的影响。PFAS，俗称“永久化学品”，因其极强的化学稳定性和生物累积性，已成为全球性环境污染物，对生态系统和人类健康构成潜在威胁。传统研究多聚焦于PFAS的直接毒性，而本研究则通过蚊子这一生物媒介，探索了其可能导致的间接疾病传播风险。 核心专业概念在于“蚊子适应性”（mosquito fitness）与“媒介传播能力”（vector competence）。研究将评估PFAS暴露如何影响蚊子的存活率、繁殖力、免疫应答等适应性指标，并进一步探究这些变化是否会增强或削弱蚊子获取和传播寨卡、西尼罗、拉克罗斯等虫媒病毒的能力。若PFAS能增强蚊子的媒介传播能力，将预示着这些环境污染物可能间接加剧虫媒传染病的流行风险，为全球公共卫生带来新的挑战。 此研究的行业影响深远。它将为环境科学家提供关于PFAS生态毒理学的新证据，促使更全面的环境风险评估。对公共卫生领域而言，若结果证实PFAS影响病毒传播，将推动疾病监测和控制策略的调整，尤其是在受PFAS污染的地区加强蚊媒疾病预防措施。此外，它也强化了“同一健康”（One Health）理念，强调环境健康、动物健康（蚊子作为媒介）与人类健康之间密不可分的联系，呼吁跨学科合作以应对复杂的全球健康威胁。

英文原文

Original
Kennesaw State University researcher Andrew Haddow has received a three-year grant from the National Institutes of Health (NIH) worth $517,956 to study the effects of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), or "forever chemicals," on mosquitoes. Haddow, an assistant professor of microbiology in the College of Science and Mathematics, is the second KSU researcher to receive a grant from the NIH's National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID) this year, joining associate professor of biology Soon Goo Lee. PFAS are widespread environmental contaminants that can occur in aquatic habitats where mosquitoes develop. The project will explore how exposure to these compounds affects mosquito fitness and whether it influences the ability of medically important species to transmit viruses such as Zika, West Nile, and La Crosse viruses. The study will also provide opportunities for KSU students, both undergraduates and graduates in KSU's Master of Science of Integrative Biology program, to conduct research contributing to discovery. Haddow joined KSU's faculty in 2021 after serving as a senior scientist for General Dynamics based at the U.S. Army Medical Research Institute of Infectious Diseases (USAMRIID). He has studied the effects of environmental changes on arboviruses for nearly two decades. The NIH grant is No. 1R15AI194160-01A1.
