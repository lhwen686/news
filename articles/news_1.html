英文原文

Original
Cambridge researchers have shown how commonly-used sweeteners slow the growth of certain gut bacteria. One sweetener in particular – isosteviol – when combined with the anti-depressant duloxetine significantly impaired two important gut bacteria linked to regulating blood sugar and gut health and may affect the body's immune responses. The scientists say more research is needed to understand the real-world health impacts of this laboratory study, one of the first to assess the direct impact of sweeteners on gut bacteria, particularly when they are combined with other substances. Sweeteners are widely used in a range of food and drinks, including soft drinks, sweets, desserts, snacks and cereals. While marketed as healthier alternatives to sugar, there is increasing evidence of links to diseases such as type 2 diabetes, obesity and cancer. Despite their pervasive use, there have been very few studies that look at the direct interactions between sweeteners and gut bacteria – the vast community of microorganisms that live in the digestive tract and play a crucial role in keeping our bodies healthy. Most of what we know about the potential impact of sweeteners on our health comes from animal research or from population studies. While these studies have indicated involvement of the microbiome in mediating the effect of sweeteners, it's difficult to know how sweeteners act in the body – is it through direct interactions with our gut bacteria?" Professor Kiran Patil, Medical Research Council (MRC) Toxicology Unit, University of Cambridge "Answering this is further complicated by the fact that we rarely ever take sweeteners by themselves – we take them with drinks, in snacks, or even in medication to mask bitterness," added Dr Sonja Blasche, a lead author of the study, also the MRC Toxicology Unit. In research published in Molecular Systems Biology, Dr Blasche and colleagues looked at how artificial and low‑calorie sweeteners affect the bacteria living in our gut, and how these effects change when sweeteners are consumed together with other common substances such as caffeine, flavourings or medicines. The researchers grew each of 25 gut bacterial species – including beneficial, neutral, and potentially harmful bacteria – in the lab. They then exposed each culture individually to 39 common, commercially-used sweeteners, some of which are artificial, others natural, and measured how well the bacteria multiplied. Around three‑quarters of the sweeteners changed how at least one bacterial species grew. Some sweeteners slowed down or stopped the growth of certain bacteria linked to a healthy gut. The researchers then tested each sweetener in combination with common compounds such as caffeine, vanillin (vanilla extract), advantame (an artificial sweetener) and eight commonly-used drugs to assess whether this had any impact on the gut bacteria. They found over 100 interactions where sweeteners acted differently when combined with other substances. In 34 cases, combinations made the effects stronger, while in 68 cases the effects were weaker. Most striking was the combination of a sweetener called isosteviol, widely used in the food and beverage industry, and the antidepressant duloxetine. This combination strongly suppressed Roseburia intestinalis and Parabacteroides merdae, two gut bacteria that play important roles in maintaining a healthy digestive system. In the US in 2023, over 4.2 million patients were prescribed duloxetine. As no gut bacterium exists alone, but rather as part of a 'community' within the gut, the researchers created a synthetic community containing all 25 bacteria. After allowing it to grow over time, they tested the community against a variety of sweetener and drug combinations, looking at which species increased or decreased and whether the overall diversity changed. By mimicking in this simplified way what might happen in the human gut, they showed that the combination of isosteviol and duloxetine reduced microbial diversity. A diverse microbiome is considered important for good gut health. The sweetener-drug combination also altered which bacterial species thrived or declined. Further analysis showed that the effect of the isosteviol-duloxetine combination on the community increased toxicity towards certain host cells and interfered with other cells that play a role in the body's inflammation and immune responses. Dr Blasche said: "Sweeteners are often marketed as metabolically neutral, but our study challenges this idea. We found that they can directly affect gut bacteria, particularly when mixed with other compounds such as medication and food additives. These common combinations could have unintended effects on our gut microbiome." The researchers stress that, as their experiments were carried out in the lab, not tested in humans, more research needs to be done before it is possible to conclude that there will be direct health effects in people. Professor Patil, the study's senior author, added: "Our study suggests that artificial sweeteners don't just pass through the body passively - they can interact with gut microbes, and these effects can be amplified or altered by other substances like medications. These findings can help guide new studies towards understanding how sweeteners might influence health in unexpected ways." The research was funded by the European Union's Horizon 2020 programme and the UK Medical Research Council.

剑桥大学研究人员已证明常用甜味剂如何减缓某些肠道细菌的生长。特别是其中一种甜味剂——异甜菊醇——当与抗抑郁药度洛西汀结合时，显著损害了两种与调节血糖和肠道健康相关的重要肠道细菌，并可能影响身体的免疫反应。 科学家们表示，需要更多研究来了解这项实验室研究的现实世界健康影响，这是首批评估甜味剂直接影响肠道细菌的研究之一，尤其是在它们与其他物质结合时。 甜味剂广泛用于各种食品和饮料中，包括软饮料、糖果、甜点、零食和谷物。尽管它们被宣传为比糖更健康的替代品，但越来越多的证据表明它们与2型糖尿病、肥胖症和癌症等疾病存在关联。 尽管甜味剂普遍使用，但直接研究甜味剂与肠道细菌之间相互作用的研究却非常少。肠道细菌是生活在消化道中的庞大微生物群落，在维持我们身体健康方面发挥着关键作用。 我们对甜味剂潜在健康影响的大部分了解来自动物研究或人群研究。虽然这些研究表明微生物组参与介导甜味剂的作用，但很难知道甜味剂如何在体内发挥作用——它们是否通过与我们的肠道细菌直接相互作用？剑桥大学医学研究理事会(MRC)毒理学部门的Kiran Patil教授说。 该研究的主要作者、同样来自MRC毒理学部门的Sonja Blasche博士补充道：“回答这个问题因一个事实而变得更加复杂，即我们很少单独摄入甜味剂——我们将其与饮料、零食，甚至药物一起摄入以掩盖苦味。” 在《分子系统生物学》杂志发表的研究中，Blasche博士及其同事探讨了人工和低热量甜味剂如何影响我们肠道中的细菌，以及当甜味剂与咖啡因、调味剂或药物等其他常见物质一起食用时，这些影响如何变化。 研究人员在实验室中培养了25种肠道细菌，包括有益菌、中性菌和潜在有害菌。然后，他们分别将每种培养物暴露于39种常见、商业使用的甜味剂中，其中一些是人工的，另一些是天然的，并测量了细菌的繁殖情况。 大约四分之三的甜味剂改变了至少一种细菌的生长方式。一些甜味剂减缓或阻止了与健康肠道相关的某些细菌的生长。 研究人员随后测试了每种甜味剂与常见化合物（如咖啡因、香草醛（香草精）、安赛蜜（一种人工甜味剂）和八种常用药物）结合的情况，以评估这是否对肠道细菌产生任何影响。他们发现，当甜味剂与其他物质结合时，存在超过100种不同的相互作用。在34种情况下，组合使效果更强；而在68种情况下，效果更弱。 最引人注目的是异甜菊醇（一种广泛用于食品和饮料行业的甜味剂）与抗抑郁药度洛西汀的组合。这种组合强烈抑制了肠道罗氏菌（Roseburia intestinalis）和粪便副拟杆菌（Parabacteroides merdae），这两种肠道细菌在维持健康消化系统方面发挥着重要作用。2023年，美国有超过420万患者被开具了度洛西汀。 由于没有肠道细菌是单独存在的，而是作为肠道内“群落”的一部分，研究人员创建了一个包含所有25种细菌的合成群落。在允许其生长一段时间后，他们对该群落测试了多种甜味剂和药物组合，观察了哪些物种增加或减少以及整体多样性是否发生变化。 通过这种简化方式模拟人体肠道中可能发生的情况，他们表明异甜菊醇和度洛西汀的组合降低了微生物多样性。多样化的微生物组被认为是良好肠道健康的重要标志。甜味剂-药物组合还改变了哪些细菌物种繁盛或衰退。 进一步分析表明，异甜菊醇-度洛西汀组合对群落的影响增加了对某些宿主细胞的毒性，并干扰了在身体炎症和免疫反应中发挥作用的其他细胞。 Blasche博士说：“甜味剂通常被宣传为代谢中性，但我们的研究挑战了这一观点。我们发现它们可以直接影响肠道细菌，特别是在与药物和食品添加剂等其他化合物混合时。这些常见的组合可能对我们的肠道微生物组产生意想不到的影响。” 研究人员强调，由于他们的实验是在实验室中进行的，尚未在人体上进行测试，因此在得出甜味剂会对人体健康产生直接影响的结论之前，需要进行更多研究。 该研究的资深作者Patil教授补充道：“我们的研究表明，人工甜味剂并非被动地通过身体——它们可以与肠道微生物相互作用，并且这些影响可以被药物等其他物质放大或改变。这些发现有助于指导新的研究，以了解甜味剂可能如何以意想不到的方式影响健康。” 这项研究由欧盟“地平线2020”计划和英国医学研究理事会资助。
