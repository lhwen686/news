AI模型预测32种实体瘤的癌症亚型、基因突变和生存结局

AI model predicts cancer subtype, genetic mutations, and survival outcomes across 32 solid tumors

深度解析

该研究展示了计算病理学在癌症诊断与精准治疗领域取得的重大突破。核心专业概念在于，利用新型人工智能Vision Transformer模型，对常规苏木精-伊红（H&E）染色的全切片图像（WSI）进行“弱监督学习”，以同时预测多种实体癌症的亚型、基因突变（如关键肿瘤抑制基因TP53的状态及其RNA表达）和生存预后。传统上，精确的分子分型需依赖成本高昂且可及性受限的分子检测。该AI模型通过学习图像形态与分子特征之间的复杂关联，实现了从形态学层面直接推断分子信息，尤其对TP53突变检测展现出高准确度。其行业影响深远，它能作为分子检测的有力补充而非替代，在资源受限地区提供可及的初步筛选和分诊工具，赋能临床医生更高效地获取诊断、分子和预后信息，加速精准癌症护理的普及，并促进早期干预，从而优化患者管理与治疗策略。

英文原文

Original
Researchers have developed a novel AI model that can analyze a routine whole histopathology image and simultaneously predict cancer subtype, specific genetic mutations, and survival outcomes across 32 different solid cancers rather than focusing on a single cancer type. The findings from the study in The American Journal of Pathology, published by Elsevier, highlight the potential of computational pathology to connect routine diagnostic imaging with molecular oncology. Histopathology, the microscopic study of tissue, remains the gold standard for diagnosing cancer and identifying prognostic features across most solid tumors. However, current clinical workflows still depend on additional molecular and genomic assays to identify key alterations, such as those in TP53, which is one of the most frequently altered tumor suppressor genes across human cancers. Because mutations in TP53 influence tumor growth and treatment resistance, accurate diagnosis and molecular profiling are essential for guiding treatment and improving patient outcomes. Standard molecular profiling for TP53 mutations is often costly and inaccessible in underprivileged or remote clinical settings. We wanted to develop a more practical tool for pathologists. Currently, most deep learning-based models are used for single-model concepts; one model for one task. We developed a single model that can generate seven outputs simultaneously from the whole histopathology image, including TP53 mutation status, TP53 RNA expression, tumor type, and survival-related outcomes at the slide level." Alex W. Hewitt, PhD, co-lead investigator, Menzies Institute for Medical Research and School of Medicine, University of Tasmania The AI-based Vision Transformer model analyzed routine hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained whole slide images of human solid tumors. The model was trained on a dataset that included more than 11,000 primary tumor cases retrieved from the Pan-Cancer Atlas, with corresponding somatic mutation, RNA-sequencing, and clinical outcome data. The most significant result was that the model achieved a strong predictive accuracy score (AUROC of 0.766) for TP53 mutation detection across 32 solid tumor types in an independent validation set of 1,729 slides. The model also demonstrated the ability to infer TP53 RNA expression levels and tumor taxonomy directly from whole slide images. Co-lead investigator Abadh K. Chaurasia, PhD, Menzies Institute for Medical Research, University of Tasmania, and Pandani Solutions Pty Ltd, notes, "This approach could help identify patients who may benefit from confirmatory molecular testing, support triage in settings with limited genomic testing, and provide additional decision support to clinicians. Importantly, this method should be viewed as complementary to molecular testing, not a replacement. Its potential impact is strongest as a screening, prioritization, or decision-support tool within broader diagnostic pathways." Because whole slide images are extremely large and complex,and obtaining detailed expert annotations for every relevant tumor region is difficult, costly, and often subjective, the researchers deployed a weakly supervised learning strategy. "In this study, molecular labels such as TP53 mutation status were available at the patch level, but whole slide images containing TP53-associated morphological information were not manually labeled. Weak supervision enabled the model to learn from slide-level labels and identify relevant patterns across image patches without requiring exhaustive pixel- or region-level annotations," notes Dr. Hewitt. He concludes, "Accurate molecular profiling from routine histopathology slides, already widely used in cancer care, could transform clinical oncology. This new AI-based model integrates diagnostic, molecular, and prognostic tasks, and could help clinicians obtain more information from existing pathology workflows, ultimately supporting more accessible precision cancer care and early intervention."

中文翻译

AI Translation
研究人员开发了一种新型人工智能模型，该模型能够分析常规的全组织病理学图像，并同时预测32种不同实体癌症的癌症亚型、特定基因突变和生存结果，而不是仅关注单一癌症类型。这项发表在爱思唯尔旗下的《美国病理学杂志》上的研究成果，强调了计算病理学连接常规诊断影像与分子肿瘤学的潜力。 组织病理学，即对组织进行显微研究，仍然是诊断癌症和识别大多数实体肿瘤预后特征的金标准。然而，当前的临床工作流程仍依赖额外的分子和基因组检测来识别关键变异，例如TP53基因的变异。TP53是人类癌症中最常发生变异的肿瘤抑制基因之一。由于TP53突变影响肿瘤生长和治疗耐药性，准确诊断和分子分析对于指导治疗和改善患者预后至关重要。 塔斯马尼亚大学门席斯医学研究所和医学院的联合首席研究员Alex W. Hewitt博士表示：“针对TP53突变的标准分子分析通常成本高昂，且在医疗条件不足或偏远的临床环境中难以获取。我们希望开发一种更实用的病理学家工具。目前，大多数基于深度学习的模型用于单一模型概念；一个模型只完成一个任务。我们开发了一个单一模型，可以从整个组织病理学图像中同时生成七种输出，包括TP53突变状态、TP53 RNA表达、肿瘤类型以及切片层面的生存相关结果。” 该基于人工智能的Vision Transformer模型分析了人实体肿瘤的常规苏木精-伊红（H&E）染色全切片图像。该模型在一个包含11,000多个原发肿瘤病例的数据集上进行了训练，这些病例来源于泛癌图谱（Pan-Cancer Atlas），并附有相应的体细胞突变、RNA测序和临床结局数据。 最显著的结果是，该模型在1,729张独立验证切片中对32种实体瘤类型的TP53突变检测取得了强劲的预测准确度得分（AUROC为0.766）。该模型还展示了直接从全切片图像推断TP53 RNA表达水平和肿瘤分类的能力。 塔斯马尼亚大学门席斯医学研究所和Pandani Solutions有限公司的联合首席研究员Abadh K. Chaurasia博士指出：“这种方法可以帮助识别可能受益于确证性分子检测的患者，在基因组检测资源有限的环境中支持分诊，并为临床医生提供额外的决策支持。重要的是，这种方法应被视为分子检测的补充，而非替代。其在更广泛的诊断路径中作为筛选、优先级排序或决策支持工具的潜在影响最为强大。” 由于全切片图像极其庞大和复杂，并且为每个相关肿瘤区域获取详细的专家注释既困难、成本高昂，又常常具有主观性，研究人员采用了弱监督学习策略。 Hewitt博士指出：“在这项研究中，TP53突变状态等分子标签在补丁层面可用，但包含TP53相关形态学信息的全切片图像并未进行手动标记。弱监督使模型能够从切片层面的标签中学习，并识别图像补丁中的相关模式，而无需详尽的像素或区域层面的注释。” 他总结道：“从已广泛应用于癌症护理的常规组织病理学切片中进行精确的分子分析，可能会改变临床肿瘤学。这种新型基于人工智能的模型整合了诊断、分子和预后任务，可以帮助临床医生从现有的病理学工作流程中获取更多信息，最终支持更可及的精准癌症护理和早期干预。”
