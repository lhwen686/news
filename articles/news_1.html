吸烟的社交神话遭挑战：一项5万人的研究将其与孤独感联系起来

Smoking's social myth challenged as 50,000-person study links it to loneliness

深度解析

这项欧洲研究深刻揭示了吸烟与孤独感之间被忽视的关联，挑战了将吸烟视为“社交行为”的传统观念。其核心专业概念在于阐明了吸烟如何通过多重机制损害个体的社会心理健康。 从医学机制角度，研究提出吸烟相关的进行性疾病（如慢性阻塞性肺疾病）会显著限制患者的活动能力，使其难以参与社交活动，进而引发社交孤立和孤独感。这是一种直接的生理病理学途径，通过身体机能衰退间接影响社交功能。其次，社会规范的演变，如公共场所禁烟，虽然保护了非吸烟者健康，但也可能减少了吸烟者的社交机会，形成行为和社会环境层面的机制。 对公共卫生的影响深远。首先，公共卫生宣传应重新定位，强调吸烟不仅损害生理健康，更会损害心理和社会福祉，以更全面的角度劝导戒烟。其次，临床医生在戒烟干预时，应将患者的社会心理健康评估纳入考量，提供相应的支持，如转介至心理健康服务。政策制定者则需在推行控烟措施的同时，考虑如何构建更具包容性的社会环境，避免对吸烟者造成进一步的社会隔离。这项研究为理解和应对日益严重的孤独感公共卫生挑战提供了新的视角和有效的干预靶点。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Дмитрий Белов from Pexels Smoking is not social and leads to increased loneliness, according to a large Europe-wide study presented at the European Respiratory Society (ERS) Congress in Barcelona, Spain. Dr. Keir Philip, clinical lecturer in respiratory medicine at the National Heart and Lung Institute, Imperial College London, UK, presented the study. Philip told the Congress, "Although we know smoking is bad for your physical health, we don't know what it does to our social health. Previous research has suggested that loneliness leads to smoking, but there has been very little research asking if smoking leads to loneliness. Smoking has been considered a 'social activity,' but we regularly see people with smoking-related illnesses becoming increasingly socially isolated and lonely. "Our first study in England suggested that people who smoked became lonelier than those who didn't smoke, but it was possible the results were influenced by culture and social norms. We wanted to investigate if we saw the same relationship between smoking and loneliness across other countries too." The researchers analyzed data from more than 50,000 people across 15 countries participating in the Survey of Health and Ageing in Europe (SHARE). They measured how lonely participants felt using the UCLA loneliness scale, which provides a loneliness score based on how often a person feels they lack companionship, feel left out and feel isolated from others. The researchers compared current smokers with people who were not current smokers and followed up two years later using the same questionnaire. The average age of the study participants was 67; 22% were smokers at the start of the research, and 48.5% had more than two chronic illnesses. The researchers found that current smokers were lonelier than non-smokers, even after considering other factors that might influence the results, such as age or gender. People who currently smoked also experienced larger increases in loneliness over time, after considering demographics and their baseline level of loneliness. There was only slight variation between European regions. Philip explained, "Smoking is associated with increasing loneliness in older people, suggesting smoking may damage psychosocial health in addition to physical effects. These findings support and build on previous research and show that the relationship is broadly consistent throughout Europe." Philip believes one likely mechanism for smoking leading to increased loneliness is that progressive smoking-related illnesses limit mobility, making it more difficult for people to access and participate in social activities. He added, "Another potential mechanism relates to social norms around smoking near other people. Smoking bans in public places and informal smoke-free environments in people's homes may make such places less appealing to people who smoke, which could limit the potential for social interactions." Philip believes the next steps are to change the public narrative so that it aligns with the data. "Smoking is not social; our research shows it leads to increased social isolation and loneliness. Spreading the word through various routes will be key, from bars to public policy—the psychosocial impacts of smoking need to be made clear," he added. Professor Des Cox, a member of the European Respiratory Society's Advocacy Council and clinical professor at University College Dublin, Ireland, who was not involved in the research, said, "This is important information for people who smoke. It is vital to support people to quit smoking without stigmatizing them. From individual to policy levels, these findings demonstrate that, as well as damaging physical health, smoking appears to damage aspects of psychosocial health. The idea that smoking might be 'social' should be challenged, as the evidence indicates the opposite. "With loneliness now recognized as one of the key public health challenges of our time, this research identifies smoking as an important risk factor for loneliness, and it's a factor for which effective treatments exist. We need to do all we can to help smokers quit and to make sure young people do not become addicted to nicotine." Key medical concepts SmokingLonelinessSocial Isolation
