WEHI study reveals a new immune pathway for fighting small cell lung cancer

深度解析

这项突破性研究揭示了伽马-德尔塔T细胞（γδ T细胞）在对抗侵袭性小细胞肺癌（SCLC）中的关键作用，为克服该疾病的“免疫隐形”机制提供了新策略。SCLC以其高度恶性、早期转移和对常规免疫疗法响应率低而著称，主要原因是癌细胞能够逃避常规T细胞的识别。γδ T细胞作为一种非MHC限制性T细胞，其独特的识别模式使其能够穿透这种伪装，直接识别并杀灭癌细胞，提供了SCLC免疫治疗的新靶点。 机制上，研究发现通过药物再利用可以显著增强γδ T细胞的抗肿瘤活性。唑来膦酸（一种双膦酸盐药物）能通过作用于甲羟戊酸通路，诱导γδ T细胞增殖并增强其对肿瘤细胞的识别能力。特拉妥单抗（一种靶向Delta-like ligand 3的双特异性T细胞衔接器，已获批用于SCLC）则能将激活的γδ T细胞与表达DLL3的癌细胞紧密连接，从而促进有效的细胞毒性作用。这种协同作用为SCLC提供了强大的新型免疫攻击途径。 其行业影响深远。它为SCLC这种五年生存率不足7%的致命疾病开辟了全新的治疗途径，有望提高免疫疗法的响应率。通过现有药物的重新定位，可以加速实验室发现向临床应用的转化，降低新药研发成本和时间。此外，研究还为γδ T细胞过继性细胞疗法奠定了基础，未来或可从患者体内扩增这些细胞并回输，实现高度个体化的治疗。这不仅对SCLC，对其他常规免疫疗法难以触及的实体瘤也具有借鉴意义，预示着抗癌免疫治疗领域的新突破。

Original
Researchers have uncovered a previously overlooked immune cell that can recognize and kill small cell lung cancer, the most aggressive form of the disease globally. Published in Cancer Cell, the WEHI study found patients with higher levels of these cells lived longer, uncovering a hidden immune defence that could be exploited to attack these hard-to-treat tumors. Researchers also showed existing drugs could be repurposed to boost the cancer-killing activity of these immune cells, opening new opportunities to strengthen current treatments and help more patients benefit from these therapies. At a glance Researchers have found a previously overlooked type of immune cell can recognize and kill small cell lung cancer, revealing a new immune pathway for fighting the deadliest form of the disease. The study found gamma delta T cells could detect and attack these lung cancers, even when the disease has hidden itself from the immune system – overcoming a major treatment challenge. The WEHI team also found existing drugs can boost the cancer-killing abilities of these immune cells, potentially paving the way to enhance therapies for a disease with limited treatment options. Finding a way to boost treatment responses for lung cancer Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) is one of the world's deadliest diseases, with a five-year survival rate of less than 7%. While immunotherapy has transformed outcomes for other cancers, only a rare subset of SCLC patients will experience long-term benefits from the treatment. One major reason is that these cancers are often invisible to the conventional immune cells that immunotherapy treatments rely on, making them difficult to detect and treat. The new Cancer Cell study reveals a previously unrecognized immune pathway that can override these invisibility powers, unlocking a potential new therapeutic avenue for SCLC patients. Professor Kate Sutherland, senior author on the paper and WEHI laboratory head, said the findings could help solve one of the field's most pressing questions: how to boost the number of lung cancer patients who respond to immunotherapy. For decades, we've known that small cell lung cancer is exceptionally good at hiding from the immune system. We've found an immune cell that appears to be able to see through that disguise. Strikingly, patients whose tumors contained more of these specialized immune cells often had better treatment outcomes. This is the strongest evidence yet that gamma delta T cells are present in small cell lung cancers and appear capable of fighting the disease. It suggests these cells may offer an alternative immune attack route for tumors that are able to evade conventional cancer-fighting immune cells, opening up new possibilities for treating a cancer that has seen few advances over the past decades." Professor Kate Sutherland, senior author on the paper and WEHI laboratory head Using mini tumors to uncover a new lung cancer treatment strategy To test whether gamma delta T cells could fight cancer, researchers created miniature tumors in the lab using samples generously donated by patients through the Victorian Cancer Biobank in collaboration with the Royal Melbourne Hospital. These "mini tumors" represent one of the most realistic laboratory models of SCLC developed to date. When the team combined the mini tumors with gamma delta T cells taken from the patients' own blood, they found the immune cells were highly effective at recognizing and killing SCLC cells. The researchers discovered they could amplify this immune response by using two existing drugs – tarlatamab (a cancer treatment approved for SCLC) and zoledronate (approved for osteoporosis and cancer-related bone disease). The team found zoledronate increased the number of gamma delta T cells available and enhanced their ability to recognise SCLC tumors. Tarlatamab acted like a magnetic force that brought the immune cells into closer contact with the cancer. Together, these drugs significantly enhanced the gamma delta T cells' ability to attack the cancer cells, suggesting a potential new way to strengthen anti-cancer immunity in SCLC. Dr Jin Ng, first author on the paper, said the findings also raise the possibility of using existing gamma delta T cells for adoptive transfer therapies – where large numbers of these immune cells are grown outside the body and infused back into patients. "For patients with lower gamma delta T cell levels, therapies such as zoledronate may offer a way to boost their cancer-fighting immune cells and responsiveness to treatments," Dr Ng said. "As therapies that expand and reinfuse gamma delta T cells already exist, our findings raise the exciting possibility of rapidly translating this discovery into new treatment strategies. "This could open an entirely new avenue for personalized treatment approaches in small cell lung cancer and other solid cancers that are often missed by conventional immune cells." Gathering clues from landmark immunotherapy clinical trials The team focused on gamma delta T cells after analyzing data from two practice-changing clinical trials – IMpower133 and CASPIAN – which established immunotherapy as a standard treatment for SCLC. Using advanced genomic and computational analyses led by WEHI's genomics and bioinformatics teams, researchers were able to link a strong gamma delta T cell signature to patients who experienced the greatest benefit from immunotherapy. The team now hopes to clinically translate the findings into a new class of immunotherapy drugs for small cell lung cancer, in an ongoing collaboration with researchers at the Doherty Institute. The research is supported by Cancer Council Victoria, the National Health and Medical Research Council (NHMRC), The CASS Foundation, The International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer.

研究人员发现了一种此前被忽视的免疫细胞，它能够识别并杀灭全球范围内最具侵袭性的疾病形式——小细胞肺癌。 这项发表在《癌细胞》（Cancer Cell）杂志上的WEHI研究发现，体内此类细胞水平较高的患者寿命更长，揭示了一种可以用于攻击这些难治性肿瘤的隐藏免疫防御机制。 研究人员还表明，现有药物可以被重新利用，以增强这些免疫细胞的杀癌活性，从而为强化现有治疗并帮助更多患者从中受益开辟了新机遇。 概览 研究人员发现了一种此前被忽视的免疫细胞类型，它能够识别并杀灭小细胞肺癌，揭示了一种对抗这种最致命疾病的新免疫通路。 研究发现，伽马-德尔塔T细胞（gamma delta T cells）能够检测并攻击这些肺癌，即使当疾病已经成功躲避免疫系统时，也能克服这一主要治疗挑战。 WEHI团队还发现，现有药物可以增强这些免疫细胞的杀癌能力，这可能为改善这种治疗选择有限的疾病的疗法铺平道路。 寻找提高肺癌治疗反应的方法 小细胞肺癌（SCLC）是全球最致命的疾病之一，其五年生存率不足7%。 尽管免疫疗法已彻底改变了其他癌症的治疗结局，但只有极少数SCLC患者能从该治疗中获得长期益处。 一个主要原因是这些癌症常常对免疫疗法所依赖的常规免疫细胞“隐形”，使其难以被检测和治疗。 这项新的《癌细胞》研究揭示了一种此前未被识别的免疫通路，它能够克服这些“隐形”能力，为SCLC患者开启了潜在的新治疗途径。 该论文的资深作者兼WEHI实验室主任凯特·萨瑟兰（Kate Sutherland）教授表示，这些发现可能有助于解决该领域最紧迫的问题之一：如何提高对免疫疗法有反应的肺癌患者数量。 凯特·萨瑟兰教授（论文资深作者兼WEHI实验室主任）表示：“几十年来，我们一直知道小细胞肺癌非常善于躲避免疫系统。我们发现了一种似乎能够识破这种伪装的免疫细胞。令人惊讶的是，肿瘤中含有更多这种特殊免疫细胞的患者，其治疗效果往往更好。这是迄今为止最有力的证据，表明伽马-德尔塔T细胞存在于小细胞肺癌中，并且似乎能够对抗这种疾病。这表明这些细胞可能为那些能够逃避常规抗癌免疫细胞的肿瘤提供了一条替代的免疫攻击途径，为治疗在过去几十年中进展甚微的癌症开辟了新的可能性。” 利用微型肿瘤揭示肺癌新治疗策略 为了测试伽马-德尔塔T细胞是否能够抗癌，研究人员利用维多利亚癌症生物样本库与墨尔本皇家医院合作，由患者慷慨捐赠的样本，在实验室中构建了微型肿瘤。这些“微型肿瘤”代表了迄今为止开发的最逼真的SCLC实验室模型之一。 当研究团队将这些微型肿瘤与从患者自身血液中提取的伽马-德尔塔T细胞结合时，他们发现这些免疫细胞在识别和杀灭SCLC细胞方面非常有效。 研究人员发现，他们可以通过使用两种现有药物来增强这种免疫反应——特拉妥单抗（tarlatamab，一种已获批用于SCLC的癌症治疗药物）和唑来膦酸（zoledronate，已获批用于治疗骨质疏松症和癌症相关骨病）。 团队发现，唑来膦酸能够增加可用的伽马-德尔塔T细胞数量，并增强它们识别SCLC肿瘤的能力。特拉妥单抗则像一种磁力，使免疫细胞与癌细胞更紧密地接触。 结合使用，这些药物显著增强了伽马-德尔塔T细胞攻击癌细胞的能力，提示了一种在SCLC中增强抗癌免疫力的新潜在途径。 该论文的第一作者吴金医生（Dr Jin Ng）表示，这些发现还提出了将现有伽马-德尔塔T细胞用于过继性细胞疗法的可能性——即在体外培养大量这些免疫细胞，然后输回患者体内。 吴医生说：“对于伽马-德尔塔T细胞水平较低的患者，唑来膦酸等疗法可能提供一种增强其抗癌免疫细胞并提高对治疗反应的方法。” “鉴于扩增和回输伽马-德尔塔T细胞的疗法已经存在，我们的发现提出了将这一突破迅速转化为新治疗策略的激动人心的可能性。” “这可能为小细胞肺癌以及其他常常被常规免疫细胞忽视的实体瘤的个性化治疗方法开辟全新的途径。” 从里程碑式免疫疗法临床试验中汲取线索 团队在分析了IMpower133和CASPIAN这两个改变临床实践的临床试验数据后，将重点放在了伽马-德尔塔T细胞上，这两个试验确立了免疫疗法作为SCLC的标准治疗。 通过WEHI基因组学和生物信息学团队主导的先进基因组和计算分析，研究人员能够将强大的伽马-德尔塔T细胞特征与从免疫疗法中获益最大的患者联系起来。 该团队现在希望与多尔蒂研究所（Doherty Institute）的研究人员持续合作，将这些发现临床转化，开发出针对小细胞肺癌的新型免疫疗法药物。 这项研究得到了维多利亚癌症委员会、国家健康与医学研究理事会（NHMRC）、CASS基金会以及国际肺癌研究协会的支持。
