New approach opens rare immune cells to genetic risk research

深度解析

该研究在免疫学和基因组学领域取得了显著进展，揭示了基因风险与疾病之间深层机制的联系。核心概念在于“三维基因组组织”和“远距离DNA相互作用”：基因变异并非总作用于邻近基因，而是通过DNA折叠使远距离调控元件与基因启动子物理接触，从而影响基因表达。研究团队通过优化“启动子捕获Hi-C技术”结合“全基因组关联研究（GWAS）”，成功绘制了罕见第三型先天性淋巴细胞（ILC3s）的三维基因组图谱，克服了传统方法对大量细胞的需求限制。此举不仅识别出逾百个与克罗恩病风险相关的新候选基因，还意外发现了CLN3基因（传统上与神经退行性疾病巴顿病相关）在免疫细胞中的潜在作用，提示了免疫调节与神经发育通路之间可能存在的交叉。这项技术突破使得研究人员能够深入探讨罕见免疫细胞的致病机制，而非仅仅停留在基因关联层面，为炎症性肠病等自身免疫性疾病的诊断和治疗提供了新的靶点和思路，预示着精准医学在复杂疾病领域的广阔前景。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Researchers mapped long-range DNA interactions in rare tonsil-derived ILC3 immune cells to identify regulatory mechanisms linked to autoimmune disease risk. Credit: Cincinnati Children's Much of how genetic risk leads to disease has remained hidden. In many cases, genetic variants linked to disease do not act on nearby genes along the chromosome. Instead, they influence genes located far away along the DNA sequence. This happens because DNA is folded inside the cell, bringing distant regions into physical contact. Capturing these long-distance regulatory connections has been especially difficult in rare immune cells that play important roles in disease. As a result, linking genetic risk to the genes and biological mechanisms that drive disease has remained a major challenge. A study co-led by researchers at Cincinnati Children's and MRC Laboratory of Medical Sciences in London, published in Nature Genetics, uses a new genomic approach to address this challenge. The work links distant DNA regions to the genes they control in type 3 innate lymphoid cells (ILC3s), uncovering previously hidden connections relevant to autoimmune disease. "This work opens the door to studying long-distance DNA interactions in rare immune cells," says Stephen Waggoner, Ph.D., a scientist in the Center of Autoimmune Genomics and Etiology at Cincinnati Children's. "Until now, most methods required millions of cells, which limited what we could learn from the cell types most relevant to disease." Overcoming the barrier of rarity ILC3s are tissue-resident immune cells that play a central role in maintaining barrier integrity and regulating inflammation, particularly in the gut. They are increasingly recognized as contributors to inflammatory bowel disease, but they are difficult to study at scale. This is because ILC3s are rare and don't replicate well outside the body. Most existing methods for mapping genome organization require millions of cells. This means analyses are restricted to studying abundant or mixed populations of cells such as those found in circulating blood. This study overcomes that barrier by applying an approach that works with far fewer cells. Using an optimized promoter capture Hi-C method, the researchers examined how DNA is folded in human ILC3s and how gene promoters physically interact with distant regulatory elements across the genome. The work resulted in a detailed map showing that regulatory DNA elements often act over long distances to influence gene activity. The researchers also found that these promoter contacts can differ between cell types, even though the underlying genetic variants are the same. "While some disease risk variants act on the genes nearest to them, others do not, so if we only look at the nearest gene, we may get the underlying mechanisms wrong," says Mikhail Spivakov, Ph.D., head of the Functional Gene Control Research Group at MRC Laboratory of Medical Sciences. "What is more, the patterns of genome folding differ across cell types, so it is important to study the 3D connections between variants and the genes they control in the cells that are relevant to the disease." Linking 3D genome maps to disease risk To connect these 3D interaction maps to disease risk, the researchers combined their data with large genome-wide association studies (GWAS). They used an analysis framework that considers multiple genetic variants at a single genomic region—improving the ability to link genetic variants associated with disease risk to the genes they regulate. This approach prioritized more than 100 candidate genes linked to Crohn's disease risk in ILC3s, many of which had not previously been associated with inflammatory bowel disease. One of the most notable findings involved CLN3. Mutations in this gene cause Batten disease, a rare neurodegenerative disorder, and CLN3 has not typically been studied in diseases driven by the immune system. Using mouse models, follow-up experiments showed that Cln3 expression (the mouse version of the gene) decreases when ILC3 cells are activated. In contrast, increasing Cln3 levels reduced inflammatory gene activity and cytokine production. These findings do not establish CLN3 as a causal gene in inflammatory bowel disease. However, they raise new questions about how immune regulation and neurodevelopmental disease pathways may intersect. "When you look at how the genome is wired in three dimensions, you start to see that genes linked to rare diseases, like CLN3, can have unexpected roles in immune cells," says Emily Miraldi, Ph.D., a scientist in the divisions of Immunobiology and Biomedical Informatics at Cincinnati Children's. "Now, we have tools to study how genetic risk plays out in other rare cell types that were previously out of reach." Looking ahead With these tools in place, a path is open for a range of next steps: Clarifying the role of CLN3 in immune cell function, including how it shapes inflammatory activity in ILC3s. Exploring potential connections between the immune system and the gut-brain axis, which might link inflammatory bowel disease and neurodevelopmental disease pathways. Applying these approaches to additional rare immune cell types to uncover new disease-associated genes. Using these techniques to compare genetic regulation across cells and disease contexts that have been difficult to study. "Studying genetic regulation in rare cell types allows us to move closer to mechanism, not just association, and that's essential for making genetic findings meaningful across medicine," says Waggoner. 基因风险如何导致疾病的许多机制仍未明晰。在许多情况下，与疾病相关的基因变异并不作用于染色体上附近的基因。相反，它们影响的是DNA序列上远距离的基因。这是因为DNA在细胞内会发生折叠，使远距离区域发生物理接触。 捕捉这些远距离的调控连接在对疾病发挥重要作用的罕见免疫细胞中尤为困难。因此，将基因风险与驱动疾病的基因和生物学机制联系起来，仍然是一个重大挑战。 由辛辛那提儿童医院和伦敦医学科学MRC实验室的研究人员共同领导的一项研究，发表在《自然遗传学》杂志上，利用一种新的基因组学方法来应对这一挑战。该研究将远距离的DNA区域与它们在第三型先天性淋巴细胞（ILC3s）中控制的基因联系起来，揭示了以前隐藏的与自身免疫性疾病相关的连接。 辛辛那提儿童医院自身免疫基因组学和病因学中心的科学家Stephen Waggoner博士说：“这项工作为研究罕见免疫细胞中的长距离DNA相互作用打开了大门。”“直到现在，大多数方法都需要数百万个细胞，这限制了我们从与疾病最相关的细胞类型中获取的信息。” 克服稀有性障碍 ILC3s是组织驻留免疫细胞，在维持屏障完整性和调节炎症方面发挥核心作用，尤其是在肠道中。它们越来越多地被认为是炎症性肠病的促成因素，但由于ILC3s稀有且在体外难以复制，因此很难大规模研究它们。 大多数现有的基因组组织图谱绘制方法都需要数百万个细胞。这意味着分析仅限于研究数量丰富的或混合的细胞群，例如在循环血液中发现的细胞。 这项研究通过应用一种适用于更少细胞的方法克服了这一障碍。研究人员使用优化的启动子捕获Hi-C技术，检查了人类ILC3s中DNA的折叠方式，以及基因启动子如何与全基因组中远距离的调控元件发生物理相互作用。 这项工作绘制了一张详细的图谱，表明调控性DNA元件通常通过长距离作用来影响基因活性。研究人员还发现，即使潜在的基因变异相同，这些启动子接触在不同细胞类型之间也可能存在差异。 医学科学MRC实验室功能基因调控研究组负责人Mikhail Spivakov博士说：“虽然一些疾病风险变异作用于它们最近的基因，但另一些则不然，因此如果我们只关注最近的基因，我们可能会误解潜在的机制。” “更重要的是，基因组折叠模式在不同细胞类型中有所不同，因此研究相关疾病细胞中变异体与其所控制基因之间的三维连接至关重要。” 将三维基因组图谱与疾病风险联系起来 为了将这些三维相互作用图谱与疾病风险联系起来，研究人员将他们的数据与大型全基因组关联研究（GWAS）相结合。他们使用了一种分析框架，该框架考虑了单个基因组区域的多个基因变异——这提高了将与疾病风险相关的基因变异与它们所调控的基因联系起来的能力。 这种方法优先识别了100多个与ILC3s中克罗恩病风险相关的候选基因，其中许多以前并未与炎症性肠病相关联。 最显著的发现之一涉及CLN3基因。该基因的突变会导致巴顿病，一种罕见的神经退行性疾病，CLN3通常未在由免疫系统驱动的疾病中进行研究。 通过使用小鼠模型，后续实验表明，当ILC3细胞被激活时，Cln3的表达（该基因的小鼠版本）会降低。相反，增加Cln3水平会减少炎症基因活性和细胞因子产生。 这些发现并未确定CLN3是炎症性肠病的致病基因。然而，它们提出了关于免疫调节和神经发育性疾病通路之间可能如何交叉的新问题。 辛辛那提儿童医院免疫生物学和生物医学信息学部门的科学家Emily Miraldi博士说：“当你观察基因组的三维连接方式时，你会开始看到像CLN3这样与罕见疾病相关的基因，可能在免疫细胞中扮演意想不到的角色。”“现在，我们有了工具来研究基因风险如何在以前无法触及的其他罕见细胞类型中发挥作用。” 展望未来 有了这些工具，一系列后续步骤的道路已经开启： 阐明CLN3在免疫细胞功能中的作用，包括它如何影响ILC3s中的炎症活性。 探索免疫系统与肠脑轴之间的潜在联系，这可能将炎症性肠病与神经发育性疾病通路联系起来。 将这些方法应用于其他罕见免疫细胞类型，以发现新的疾病相关基因。 使用这些技术比较不同细胞和疾病背景下的基因调控，这些是以前难以研究的。 Waggoner说：“研究罕见细胞类型中的基因调控使我们更接近机制，而不仅仅是关联，这对于使基因发现对医学具有意义至关重要。” 出版详情：Valeriya Malysheva
