受污染违禁酒精在持牌酒铺售予不知情顾客

Contaminated illicit alcohol being sold to unsuspecting customers at licensed bottle shops

该研究揭示了非法酒精产品对公共健康的严重威胁及其行业影响。核心专业概念涉及两种主要污染物：甲醇和塑化剂。甲醇，作为一种常见的非法酒精掺假物，本身毒性较低，但在人体内经醇脱氢酶代谢后会迅速转化为甲醛和甲酸。这些代谢产物具有剧毒，甲醛可直接损伤视网膜细胞和视神经，导致不可逆的失明；甲酸则会引发严重的代谢性酸中毒，损害多器官功能，最终可致昏迷甚至死亡。即使低浓度摄入，长期累积或个体敏感性也可能引发慢性毒性。塑化剂，如邻苯二甲酸酯类，常因不安全的生产、储存或包装过程从塑料中渗入酒精。它们被认为是内分泌干扰物，长期摄入可能对肝脏、肾脏及生殖系统产生负面影响，干扰激素平衡，引发慢性疾病风险。

从行业影响来看，非法酒精市场不仅直接危害消费者健康，更对合法酒类产业造成巨大经济冲击，每年逃避巨额消费税，形成不公平竞争。这对监管部门（如税务局、边境执法局、酒类监管机构）构成了严峻挑战，识别和打击此类犯罪需要跨部门协作。同时，消费者对合法销售产品的信任度也会受到侵蚀。研究强调需加强监管、提升消费者意识，并通过多方合作来遏制这一日益增长的公共卫生和经济问题。

Australians may be drinking contaminated spirits without realizing it, according to new research showing illicit alcohol being sold alongside legal products at bottle shops. A preliminary investigation of licensed retailers in Melbourne revealed that illicit bottles of vodka are being sold—often at a cheaper price point than competitor products—in breach of food safety, liquor and tax regulations.

Chemical testing also showed the bottles contained methanol and plastic contaminants, said the team from the National Drug and Alcohol Research Center (NDARC) at UNSW Sydney and the National Drug Research Institute (NDRI) at Curtin University in Perth.

"Our results are concerning because it shows these products, with the outward appearance of a legal product, are being sold to unsuspecting customers," said lead author Dr. Michala Kowalski, a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at NDARC. "Ingestion of methanol at sufficient levels can be toxic and lead to symptoms ranging from vomiting and abdominal pain to blindness, coma and death."

Dr. Kowalski said typical indicators of illicit alcohol products, such as inexpensive labels and bottles, were not definitive in their own right and can be difficult to spot.

"People have very little to differentiate between cheap and potentially contaminated products when looking between bottles on a store shelf," Dr. Kowalski said. "We have also been told by law enforcement and liquor regulators in several states that these products are increasingly being sold not just in bottle shops but at on-licensed venues like pubs, nightclubs and pubs."

While the true size and reach of the illicit alcohol market is unclear, the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) recently estimated it to be worth at least $767 million annually. This is the equivalent of 14 million standard bottles of vodka, or more than 10% of all spirits consumed legally in Australia.

"Illicit alcohol can be sold much cheaper than legal products by dodging the excise duty, with higher profit margins," said senior author and NDRI Research Fellow, Dr. Nic Taylor. "More importantly, this also has repercussions for public health, particularly among vulnerable drinkers, such as people on limited income. There is reason to believe they are more likely to consume illicit alcohol and be at increased risk of harm."

As part of the study, the team visited four licensed bottle shops in high socioeconomic areas of Melbourne to get a sense of the immediate availability of illicit alcohol.

While this is a small sample, the researchers have since completed larger random audits of bottle shops across several states, which have confirmed the widespread availability of illicit alcohol. They purchased three suspected illicit vodka products and a control product from a premium brand, with all four submitted for chemical analysis.

Each illicit product was the cheapest form of vodka by alcohol content sold by each visited retailer, and all had the appearance of poor-quality labeling and packaging.

For instance, one product had cheap but compliant labeling but without a barcode or resealable cap, while another had the price written in pen on plain paper and contained visible brown flakes in the bottle.

Chemical testing revealed that two products also contained methanol and plastic contaminants—likely the result of unsafe distillation, storage or bottling processes—neither of which are intended for human consumption.

"While the methanol detected was not at the concentration required to cause immediate vision loss or death, it was still in breach of Australian food standards, which raises further questions about the conditions they were produced in," Dr. Kowalski said.

"Ingestion of plasticizers at sufficient levels may also potentially cause negative long-term effects on the liver, kidneys and reproductive system."

Release of the findings in the Drug and Alcohol Review follows the two-day Illicit Alcohol Roundtable event co-hosted by NDARC and NDRI in February to address growing concerns about the emerging illicit alcohol market.

The event brought together the ATO, Australian Border Force, liquor and gaming authorities, health professionals, law enforcement, and researchers to understand the scale of the issue and discuss potential courses of action.

根据一项新研究显示，非法酒精产品与合法产品一同在酒类专卖店销售，澳大利亚人可能在不知不觉中饮用了受污染的烈酒。对墨尔本持牌零售商的初步调查显示，非法伏特加酒正在销售——通常价格比竞争产品更便宜——这违反了食品安全、酒类和税务法规。

新南威尔士大学悉尼校区国家毒品和酒精研究中心（NDARC）以及科廷大学珀斯校区国家毒品研究所（NDRI）的团队表示，化学检测还显示这些酒瓶中含有甲醇和塑料污染物。

“我们的结果令人担忧，因为它表明这些产品，尽管外观上与合法产品无异，却正在出售给毫不知情的顾客，”NDARC的博士后研究员、首席作者Michala Kowalski博士说。“摄入足够剂量的甲醇可能具有毒性，并导致从呕吐、腹痛到失明、昏迷和死亡等症状。”

Kowalski博士表示，非法酒精产品的典型指标，如廉价标签和酒瓶，本身并非决定性因素，而且可能难以发现。

“当人们在商店货架上查看酒瓶时，他们几乎无法区分廉价产品和潜在受污染产品，”Kowalski博士说。“我们还被几个州的执法部门和酒类监管机构告知，这些产品不仅在酒类专卖店销售，而且越来越多地在酒吧、夜总会等持牌经营场所销售。”

虽然非法酒精市场的真实规模和范围尚不清楚，但澳大利亚税务局（ATO）最近估计其每年价值至少达7.67亿澳元。这相当于1400万瓶标准伏特加，或占澳大利亚合法消费烈酒总量的10%以上。

“非法酒精可以通过逃避消费税，以比合法产品便宜得多的价格出售，从而获得更高的利润，”资深作者、NDRI研究员Nic Taylor博士说。“更重要的是，这还会对公共健康造成影响，特别是在弱势饮酒者中，例如收入有限的人群。有理由相信他们更有可能消费非法酒精，并面临更高的危害风险。”

作为研究的一部分，该团队走访了墨尔本高社会经济区域的四家持牌酒类专卖店，以了解非法酒精的即时可获得性。

尽管这只是一个小型样本，但研究人员此后已在多个州完成了更大规模的酒类专卖店随机审计，证实了非法酒精的广泛存在。他们购买了三款疑似非法伏特加产品和一款高端品牌的对照产品，所有四款产品均已送往进行化学分析。

每款非法产品都是所访零售商中按酒精含量计算最便宜的伏特加，并且均具有劣质标签和包装的外观。

例如，其中一款产品标签廉价但符合规定，但没有条形码或可重新密封的盖子，而另一款产品的价格则用笔写在普通纸上，酒瓶中含有可见的棕色薄片。

化学检测显示，其中两款产品还含有甲醇和塑料污染物——这很可能是不安全的蒸馏、储存或装瓶过程造成的——两者均不适用于人体食用。

“尽管检测到的甲醇浓度尚未达到立即导致视力丧失或死亡的程度，但它仍然违反了澳大利亚食品标准，这进一步引发了对其生产条件的问题，”Kowalski博士说。

“摄入足够剂量的塑化剂也可能对肝脏、肾脏和生殖系统造成潜在的长期负面影响。”

相关发现在《药物与酒精评论》上发表，此前NDARC和NDRI于2月份共同主办了为期两天的非法酒精圆桌会议，以应对日益增长的非法酒精市场问题。

此次活动汇集了澳大利亚税务局、澳大利亚边境执法局、酒类和博彩机构、卫生专业人员、执法部门和研究人员，以了解问题的规模并讨论潜在的应对措施。
