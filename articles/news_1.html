注资加强澳大利亚药物发现管线

Funding boost to strengthen Australia's drug discovery pipeline

深度解析

这篇文章详述了澳大利亚政府对WEHI牵头的国家药物发现中心（NDDC）追加投资，旨在通过先进筛选技术加速药物研发。其核心专业概念在于“药物发现管线”前期的“先导化合物”筛选与优化。NDDC的核心能力在于提供“高通量筛选（HTS）”和“虚拟筛选”平台。高通量筛选利用自动化机器人技术，能同时快速测试数百万种物理化合物与特定生物靶点（如蛋白质、酶或细胞受体）的相互作用，从而识别具有潜在治疗活性的“命中化合物”或“先导化合物”。虚拟筛选则是一种计算方法，在计算机模拟环境中，通过分子对接、药效团模型等技术，预测大量化合物与靶点的结合能力，从而在合成和实验前缩小候选范围，显著提高筛选效率并降低成本。这种“小分子筛选”是开发口服药物的关键。 此项投资的行业影响深远。首先，它通过提供世界级基础设施，弥合了基础科学发现与临床应用之间的“转化鸿沟”，加速了创新药物的诞生。其次，增强了澳大利亚在药物发现领域的“主权能力”，减少对国际巨头的依赖，并促进国内生物技术产业发展。通过提升筛选规模和速度，特别是对“未满足医疗需求”疾病的关注，有助于更快地将实验室成果推向临床，提升患者福祉。这不仅巩固了澳大利亚在全球生物医学创新中的领先地位，也为该国带来了潜在的经济效益和全球竞争力。

Original
A WEHI-led project has received $7 million in Australian Government funding to expand the nation’s drug discovery capabilities and accelerate the translation of breakthrough research into new therapies. Professor Guillaume Lessene, Associate Professor Kym Lowes and Professor Jeff Mitchell at the National Drug Discovery Centre, the focal point of a new $7 m MRFF funding grant set to strengthen Australia's drug discovery capacity. Image Credit: WEHI The Medical Research Future Fund (MRFF) investment will give researchers across the country greater access to world-class screening technologies, bridging a critical gap between scientific discovery and new medicines. Delivered through a collaboration between WEHI, the Bio21 Institute and Therapeutic Innovation Australia, the project will expand the National Drug Discovery Centre – Australia’s premier small-molecule screening facility and a critical component of the nation's drug discovery infrastructure. Combined with approximately $3.6 million in co-investment from WEHI, the initiative represents a $10.6 million investment in Australia's future drug discovery capability. At a glance WEHI-led project receives a $7 m MRFF grant to accelerate the development of new treatments across a broad range of diseases and conditions with unmet need. The funding will expand the National Drug Discovery Centre’s capacity to provide Australian researchers with access to its specialized infrastructure, helping to transform promising discoveries into potential new treatments faster. The project is delivered through a collaboration between WEHI, the Bio21 Institute and Therapeutic Innovation Australia. Addressing a critical challenge Identifying viable drug candidates is one of the most important steps in the drug discovery pipeline. They serve as the starting point for developing future treatments that can potentially improve and save lives. Yet this step remains a challenging process in Australian medicine development, as researchers often lack access to the specialized infrastructure needed to efficiently identify potential drug compounds. The WEHI-led project aims to remove that bottleneck by giving researchers unprecedented access to advanced high-throughput and virtual screening technologies capable of rapidly searching millions of drug candidates. Chief Investigator Professor Guillaume Lessene said this would strengthen Australia's sovereign capability to translate our world-class research into health outcomes that could potentially improve lives. Our researchers deserve better access to the advanced tech needed to turn their promising discoveries into future medicines.” Lessene, Professor, Division Lead and Associate Director of Therapeutic Discovery, WEHI “This project will provide researchers with the tools they need to build a stronger national drug discovery pipeline – one that can deliver better outcomes for patients, grow Australia's biotechnology sector and reinforce our position as a global leader in medical innovation. “In doing so, we will empower researchers to pursue ambitious programs that may previously have been beyond reach.” This project received grant funding from the Australian Government. Strengthening a national capability The project will enhance national infrastructure and implement the Discovery Access program at the National Drug Discovery Centre (NDDC) to provide greater access to high-tech screening platforms. Established in 2020 and based at WEHI, the NDDC enables Australian researchers to use its high-capacity robotic drug discovery technologies typically found only in major pharmaceutical companies. Since launching, the center has screened over 10 million compounds across more than 30 research projects. The Discovery Access program will provide researchers from universities, medical research institutes and small-to-medium enterprises with subsidized access to specialized screening technologies. Through the program, researchers will be able to screen more than 20 million virtual compounds and 300,000 physical compounds, dramatically expanding the scale and speed of Australian drug discovery. Joint Head of the NDDC, Associate Professor Jeff Mitchell, said this expansion will cement Australia's position as a global biomedical innovation leader. “It will play a critical role in ensuring groundbreaking discoveries made in Australian laboratories have the best possible chance of turning into treatments made on Australian soil,” Assoc Prof Mitchell said. Associate Professor Kym Lowes, co-head of the NDDC, said: “Our collaborative approach will accelerate the development of treatments for diseases with significant unmet medical need, helping move discoveries from the laboratory towards clinical application more quickly.” This project received grant funding from the Australian Government. The funding has been awarded through the MRFF National Critical Research Infrastructure grant opportunity. Over the next three years, the expanded NDDC is expected to support at least nine nationally selected screening projects, helping Australian researchers uncover the next generation of drug candidates and accelerate the development of future treatments for patients.
