克利夫兰诊所的社区影响力报告深刻揭示了美国非营利医疗系统在复杂医疗生态中的多重角色与战略布局。首先，其高达15.9亿美元的社区投入，特别是弥补联邦医疗补助（Medicaid）资金缺口和提供经济援助，凸显了非营利机构作为社会安全网关键组成部分的职能。这种模式是其免税地位的对价，通过承担政府支付不足、患者无力支付的医疗成本，实质上减轻了公共卫生负担，确保了弱势群体的医疗可及性，但同时也面临巨大的财务压力，如文章提及的联邦医疗保险（Medicare）16亿美元的资金缺口。其次，文章强调对医疗专业人员的教育和医学研究的投入，这不仅是维护高水平临床服务的基础，更是推动医疗科技进步和创新、塑造未来医疗格局的关键动力。这体现了顶级医疗机构前瞻性的发展战略。最后，对社区健康活动（如解决粮食不安全、改善母婴健康、提供稳定住房）的投资，则展现了现代医疗系统从“疾病治疗”向“整体健康管理”的范式转变。这些举措深入干预健康社会决定因素（SDOH），通过改善患者生活环境和健康习惯，从根本上降低发病率，减少未来医疗需求，实现更广义的疾病预防和人群健康促进，对行业具有深远影响。这种综合性的策略是实现可持续公共卫生成果的关键。

Original
Cleveland Clinic contributed $1.59 billion in community impact across Ohio, Florida and Nevada in 2025. The organization's efforts ranged from addressing community health needs and training future healthcare professionals to advancing medical research, providing financial assistance and covering the unpaid costs of care for patients with Medicaid. As a nonprofit, tax-exempt health system, Cleveland Clinic reports its community impact annually to the IRS. The figures are detailed in Cleveland Clinic's 2025 Community Benefit Report. Community impact includes financial assistance for patients, Medicaid shortfall costs, medical education, medical research, subsidized health services and community health programs. 2025 community impact by the numbers Cleveland Clinic's $1.59 billion in community impact in 2025 included: $464.5 million to cover unpaid costs of care for Medicaid patients $400.3 million to educate healthcare professionals $348.5 million in financial assistance $182.3 million for subsidized health services $154.2 million for medical research $44.8 million for community health activities "We are committed to improving health not only through the care we provide, but also through initiatives that strengthen our communities," said Cleveland Clinic CEO and President Tom Mihaljevic, M.D., holder of the Morton L. Mandel CEO Chair. "As a nonprofit health system, we reinvest our resources to improve patient care, advance medical innovation, expand access to care and support the long-term well-being of the communities we serve." Reducing financial barriers to care Cleveland Clinic's financial assistance policy provides free or discounted care to patients with incomes up to 400% of the federal poverty level. In 2025, Cleveland Clinic provided $348.5 million in financial assistance to more than 108,000 patients who were unable to pay some or all of their medical bills. Cleveland Clinic is a leading provider of Medicaid services in Ohio. Medicaid payments do not fully cover the cost of treating Medicaid beneficiaries, resulting in a shortfall. In 2025, the health system spent $464.5 million to help cover this gap for more than 303,000 patients with Medicaid. This investment is less than it was in 2024 due to increased government funding the health system received via supplemental Medicaid payment programs in 2025. Medicare Shortfall, which is not included in community impact, was an additional expense of $1.6 billion for the health system. Training tomorrow's healthcare leaders Preparing the next generation of healthcare professionals remains a core part of Cleveland Clinic's mission. By educating medical professionals, the health system helps ensure the public receives the highest level of medical care and will have access to highly trained health professionals now and in the future. In 2025, the health system invested $400.3 million in programs to educate medical professionals, which supported the training of approximately1,600 residents, physicians, nurses and allied health professionals, and 120 accredited training programs. Cleveland Clinic's commitment to education extends beyond preparing future healthcare professionals. As Ohio's largest employer, we partner with schools and community organizations to create pathways to training and family-sustaining careers while strengthening the healthcare workforce our communities need. Investing in medical discovery Research conducted at Cleveland Clinic is helping shape the future of healthcare by advancing new treatments, technologies and approaches to care. From a community impact standpoint, research includes basic, clinical and community health research, as well as studies on healthcare delivery. In 2025, Cleveland Clinic invested $154.2 million in research, supporting 2,400 research caregivers and trainees and approximately 3,200 active research studies. Meeting critical community health needs In 2025, Cleveland Clinic invested $182.3 million in subsidized health services, including pediatric, behavioral health, obstetric and chronic disease management programs, that are provided at a financial loss because they meet important community needs. The increase over 2024 reflects an updated process for identifying subsidized services and associated community impact. The health system also provided or contributed to community health activities in 2025 that total $44.8 million. These activities aim to impact health outside of clinical care with an emphasis on addressing food insecurity, in addition to improving infant and maternal health, and making stable housing accessible. As part of its commitment to addressing food insecurity, Cleveland Clinic has invested more than $60 million in community-based nutrition programs, including: A teaching kitchen and food pantry recently opened at the Langston Hughes Community Health & Education Center in Cleveland. The resource expands access to nutrition education, healthy cooking skills and food assistance for patients and area residents. Cleveland Clinic Akron General supports healthy food access through its community garden, which was created to provide patients, families and neighborhood residents with free fresh fruits and vegetables. Since its launch, the garden has more than doubled in size. The health system collaborates with local organizations to offer food pantries. Cleveland Clinic Avon Hospital operates an on-site food pantry where eligible patients receive nutritionally tailored food at discharge. Additional food pantries serve patients and community members at Cleveland Clinic South Pointe Hospital and Cleveland Clinic Akron General. In Florida, Cleveland Clinic supports community organizations across five counties that expand access to nutritious food through Meals on Wheels, school and community food pantries, mobile food distribution, fresh produce programs and community gardens. In Nevada, Cleveland Clinic has partnered with The Just One Project to expand community-based food access programs, including farmers markets that bring fresh, nutritious food and other resources directly to Southern Nevada neighborhoods. Together, these programs help improve access to nutritious food, support healthier lifestyles and address one of the most significant social factors affecting health outcomes.
