Study discovers new therapeutic targets for severe preeclampsia

深度解析

这项由伦敦大学学院主导的研究，显著推进了我们对子痫前期这一主要产科并发症的理解。以往研究主要集中于胎盘，但本研究采用了尖端的单细胞基因组检测技术。这项高分辨率技术能够同时分析母婴组织中数千个单个细胞的遗传信息和活性，提供了前所未有的细胞功能障碍细致视图，超越了传统的组织整体分析，精准识别了特定细胞类型的微观变化。 其核心医学机制揭示了三方病理学：表现出缺氧和异常能量代谢的应激胎盘细胞、功能障碍的母体血管重塑细胞，以及全身性的过度活跃免疫反应。这些相互作用的因素共同导致了子痫前期特有的高血压和胎儿血流减少，并重要的是，解释了其全身性影响。胎盘释放的应激分子进一步加剧了这种广泛的炎症反应。 这项研究对行业影响深远。通过将这些特定的生物学过程和细胞行为确定为潜在的治疗靶点，为药物发现和开发开辟了新途径。未来的干预措施可能侧重于改善胎盘功能、恢复正常的血管重塑或调节免疫反应。早期精准干预，特别是在更严重的早发病例中，有望显著改善母婴预后，减少早产和死亡。这项研究将范式转向更全面的、细胞特异性的理解，预示着更有效、更有针对性地治疗这种危及生命的疾病。

英文原文

Original
Unusual behavior in the cells of mothers and babies with preeclampsia may provide new therapeutic targets for the condition, finds a new UCL-led study. Preeclampsia is a leading cause of maternal and fetal mortality, affecting 2 to 4% of pregnancies globally. The condition causes the mother's blood pressure to become too high, which can affect blood flow to the baby and cause symptoms like swelling, headaches, blurred vision and pain under the ribs. If left untreated, it can harm the mother's health, slow the baby's growth and, in severe cases, be a threat to life. However, there is currently no cure. Now, in a new study published in Science Advances, scientists from UCL and University College London Hospitals (UCLH), have discovered that a combination of stressed placental cells, poorly functioning blood vessels, and an overactive immune response all contribute to the condition. Previous research has only focused on the placenta (an organ developed by the mother during pregnancy, that helps support a baby's growth) and not on the tissues surrounding it. The researchers hope that their findings will provide new therapeutic targets for preeclampsia and a potential way to treat those with the condition. Senior author, Professor Sara Hillman (UCL EGA Institute for Women's Health): "We studied individual cells from both the mother and the baby to see how their activity changes in healthy pregnancies compared with preeclampsia. "This helped us to confirm some changes already suspected in the condition and also discover new ones." For their research, the team studied 20 pregnant women – 10 of whom had severe preeclampsia and 10 without – who were recruited at UCLH. They used genomic testing to investigate the individual cells in the placenta and other tissues (myometrium and chorioamniotic membranes) where cells from the developing baby and mother meet. The team then compared these cells to those from healthy and preeclamptic pregnancies at different gestational ages. They did this by using novel technology that helped them to read the genetic information of thousands of individual cells at a time, to understand what each cell was doing and where they sit in a tissue, to build up a detailed picture of the pregnancy. They found that in preeclamptic pregnancies where babies were born premature (before 37 weeks) during the third trimester, the cells in the placenta showed signs of stress and low oxygen and didn't use energy in the normal way. Additionally, they found that some cells responsible for reshaping the mother's blood vessels weren't working properly, which may affect blood flow to the baby. Crucially, there were also signs of an overactive immune response – not just in the placenta but nearby tissues and even in the mother's blood. This, along with other stress molecules released by the placenta, helps to explain why preeclampsia affects the whole body and can become a serious condition. The researchers hope that these findings will help to finally find treatments for the condition and even save lives. Co-lead author, Dr Yara Sanchez Corrales (UCL Great Ormond Street Institute of Child Health) said: "These findings point to specific biological processes that could be targeted with treatments. Acting early in pregnancy, especially in more severe early-onset cases, could help improve outcomes and reduce the high risks associated with severe preeclampsia. "We hope that our findings may set us on the path to reducing premature births and fatalities associated with preeclampsia. " Co-lead Mr Theodoros Xenakis (UCL Great Ormond Street Institute of Child Health), said: "Future studies may provide an even clearer picture of the biological changes linked to the disease by including more participants and using even more precise methods." Both Dr Sanchez Corrales and Mr Xenakis are in Professor Sergi Castellano's lab at the UCL Great Ormond Street Institute of Child Health. The research was funded by the Medical Research Council (MRC) and the NIHR GOSH Biomedical Research Centre (BRC). The research was also supported by the UCLH BRC. Study limitations The researchers were limited by the small number of donors when looking at very subtle gene changes in some rare cell types. Future studies that include more people and use even higher-resolution methods may uncover additional, more detailed biological changes linked to the disease.

伦敦大学学院（UCL）主导的一项新研究发现，子痫前期母婴细胞中的异常行为，或可为该病症提供新的治疗靶点。 子痫前期是导致全球母婴死亡的主要原因，影响约2%至4%的妊娠。该病症会导致孕妇血压过高，进而影响胎儿的血流供应，并引起水肿、头痛、视力模糊和肋骨下方疼痛等症状。 如果不及时治疗，它会损害母亲健康，减缓胎儿生长，在严重病例中，甚至可能危及生命。然而，目前尚无治愈方法。 现在，在《科学进展》（Science Advances）杂志发表的一项新研究中，来自伦敦大学学院（UCL）和伦敦大学学院医院（UCLH）的科学家发现，受应激的胎盘细胞、功能异常的血管以及过度活跃的免疫反应的结合，均导致该病症的发生。 以往的研究只关注胎盘（胎盘是孕期由母亲发育形成，帮助支持胎儿生长的器官），而未关注其周围组织。 研究人员希望他们的发现能为子痫前期提供新的治疗靶点，并为该病症患者提供一种潜在的治疗方法。 资深作者、伦敦大学学院EGA女性健康研究所的Sara Hillman教授表示：“我们研究了来自母亲和胎儿的单个细胞，以观察在健康妊娠与子痫前期妊娠中，其细胞活动如何变化。” “这帮助我们证实了该病症中一些已知的变化，并发现了新的变化。” 为了进行这项研究，该团队在伦敦大学学院医院（UCLH）招募了20名孕妇，其中10名患有严重子痫前期，10名未患病。 他们利用基因组检测，调查了胎盘及其他组织（子宫肌层和绒毛膜羊膜）中的单个细胞，这些是发育中的胎儿和母亲细胞交汇的地方。 随后，该团队将这些细胞与不同孕周的健康妊娠和子痫前期妊娠的细胞进行了比较。 他们通过利用新型技术，一次性读取数千个单个细胞的遗传信息，以了解每个细胞的功能及其在组织中的位置，从而描绘出妊娠的详细图景。 他们发现，在妊娠晚期（妊娠37周前）早产的子痫前期妊娠中，胎盘细胞表现出应激和缺氧迹象，并且未能以正常方式利用能量。 此外，他们发现一些负责重塑母体血管的细胞未能正常工作，这可能影响胎儿的血流供应。 至关重要的是，还存在过度活跃的免疫反应迹象——不仅在胎盘中，还在邻近组织甚至母亲血液中。这一点，连同胎盘释放的其他应激分子，有助于解释为什么子痫前期会影响全身并可能演变为严重疾病。 研究人员希望这些发现将有助于最终找到该病症的治疗方法，甚至挽救生命。 共同主要作者、伦敦大学学院大奥蒙德街儿童健康研究所的Yara Sanchez Corrales博士说：“这些发现指向可通过治疗进行干预的特定生物学过程。在妊娠早期进行干预，尤其是在更严重的早发病例中，有助于改善预后，降低与严重子痫前期相关的高风险。” “我们希望我们的发现能使我们走上减少子痫前期相关早产和死亡的道路。” 共同主要作者、伦敦大学学院大奥蒙德街儿童健康研究所的Theodoros Xenakis先生表示：“未来的研究可能会通过纳入更多参与者和使用更精确的方法，提供与该疾病相关的生物学变化的更清晰图景。” Sanchez Corrales博士和Xenakis先生均在伦敦大学学院大奥蒙德街儿童健康研究所的Sergi Castellano教授实验室工作。 这项研究由医学研究理事会（MRC）和NIHR GOSH生物医学研究中心（BRC）资助。该研究也得到了伦敦大学学院医院生物医学研究中心（UCLH BRC）的支持。 研究局限性 在观察某些稀有细胞类型中非常细微的基因变化时，研究人员受限于捐献者数量少。未来的研究，如果能纳入更多人群并采用更高分辨率的方法，可能会揭示与该疾病相关的更多、更详细的生物学变化。
