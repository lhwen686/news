Early-life environmental exposures linked to multiple health outcomes into adolescence

英文原文

Original
Environmental exposures during pregnancy and childhood can be associated with multiple aspects of health at the same time, with many of these associations persisting into adolescence health, according to a new study led by the Barcelona Institute for Global Health (ISGlobal), a centre supported by the "la Caixa" Foundation, and in collaboration with the French National Institute for Health and Medical Research (Inserm). Published in Science Advances as part of the Human Early Life Exposome (HELIX) project, the study provides an atlas of associations between early-life environmental exposures and health, highlighting factors such as tobacco smoke, indoor air pollution, dietary quality and features of the built environment that may be relevant for prevention and policy action. The exposome refers to the totality of environmental exposures a person experiences throughout life, including factors such as diet, tobacco smoke and air pollution. The researchers used an outcome-wide exposome approach to study their associations with health across four domains: cardiometabolic (Body Mass Index [BMI] and blood pressure); mental health (attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder [ADHD], internalising symptoms, such as anxiety and depression, and externalising symptoms, such as aggression and impulsivity); respiratory (asthma and wheezing); and allergic problems (dermatitis and rhinitis). Early-life exposures linked to multiple health domains "Our approach allowed us to jointly analyze associations between the early-life exposome and multiple health outcomes instead of studying each one separately," explains Augusto Anguita-Ruiz, ISGlobal and University of Barcelona researcher and first author of the study. "We used data on 69 prenatal exposures and 129 childhood exposures, together with 11 health outcomes assessed during childhood (from 6 to 11 years) and adolescence (from 12 to 18 years)." The analysis identified six prenatal and 42 childhood exposures associated with multiple outcomes and health families simultaneously. "Although no exposure was associated with all four domains, around one in three of the selected exposures was linked to more than one, suggesting that certain exposures can have broad and multisystemic effects on health," says Valérie Siroux, Inserm researcher and senior co-author of the study. The factors examined included diet, physical activity, sleep and other individual behaviours, as well as tobacco smoke, indoor and outdoor air pollution, chemical pollutants, metals and characteristics of the built environment. Maternal smoking during pregnancy was associated with higher BMI and more externalising behaviours in children at both early-childhood and adolescence stages, as well as with more ADHD symptoms in adolescence. Higher maternal meat intake during pregnancy was also associated with several mental health outcomes and with asthma in children, as well as with wheezing and dermatitis during childhood. Many of the associations between environmental exposures and health parameters were observed in early childhood and were still visible years later, in adolescence. This suggests that early environmental conditions may leave relatively durable health signatures. An atlas to explore early-life exposome–health associations The study provides an open, interactive atlas of paediatric exposome–health associations, allowing users to explore the relationships identified across environmental factors, health outcomes and developmental stages. The resource can support the generation of new hypotheses and future research. "These findings show that the early-life environment may be associated with different dimensions of health at the same time, and that many of these associations remain observable years later. However, the study does not establish that these exposures cause the observed health outcomes. Further studies in larger and more diverse populations will be needed to better understand these relationships and determine whether modifying some of these exposures can alter health trajectories during childhood and adolescence," says Xavier Basagaña, ISGlobal researcher and senior co-author of the study.

AI Translation
怀孕和儿童期的环境暴露可能同时与健康的多个方面相关联，其中许多关联会持续到青少年时期，这是一项由巴塞罗那全球健康研究所（ISGlobal）（一个由“la Caixa”基金会支持的中心）牵头并与法国国家健康与医学研究所（Inserm）合作进行的新研究发现。该研究作为“人类生命早期暴露组”（HELIX）项目的一部分发表在《科学进展》（Science Advances）杂志上，提供了一份生命早期环境暴露与健康之间关联的图谱，强调了烟草烟雾、室内空气污染、饮食质量和建筑环境特征等可能对预防和政策行动具有重要意义的因素。 暴露组是指一个人在一生中经历的所有环境暴露的总和，包括饮食、烟草烟雾和空气污染等因素。研究人员采用了一种结果全景式暴露组方法，研究这些暴露与四个健康领域的相关性：心血管代谢（体质指数[BMI]和血压）；心理健康（注意力缺陷多动障碍[ADHD]、内化症状，如焦虑和抑郁，以及外化症状，如攻击性和冲动性）；呼吸系统（哮喘和喘息）；以及过敏性问题（皮炎和鼻炎）。 与多个健康领域相关的生命早期暴露 “我们的方法使我们能够联合分析生命早期暴露组与多种健康结果之间的关联，而不是单独研究每一个结果，”巴塞罗那全球健康研究所（ISGlobal）和巴塞罗那大学研究员、该研究的第一作者奥古斯托·安吉塔-鲁伊斯（Augusto Anguita-Ruiz）解释道。“我们使用了关于69种产前暴露和129种儿童期暴露的数据，以及在儿童期（6至11岁）和青少年期（12至18岁）评估的11种健康结果。” 分析确定了六种产前暴露和42种儿童期暴露同时与多种结果和健康家族相关联。“虽然没有一种暴露与所有四个领域都相关，但大约三分之一的选定暴露与一个以上领域相关联，这表明某些暴露可能对健康产生广泛且多系统的影响，”法国国家健康与医学研究所（Inserm）研究员、该研究的资深合著者瓦莱丽·西鲁克斯（Valérie Siroux）说。 所检查的因素包括饮食、体育活动、睡眠和其他个人行为，以及烟草烟雾、室内外空气污染、化学污染物、金属和建筑环境的特征。孕期母亲吸烟与儿童在幼儿期和青少年期更高的体质指数（BMI）和更多的外化行为相关，也与青少年期更多的注意力缺陷多动障碍（ADHD）症状相关。孕期母亲较高的肉类摄入量也与多项心理健康结果和儿童哮喘相关，并与儿童期的喘息和皮炎相关。 许多环境暴露与健康参数之间的关联在幼儿期被观察到，并且在多年后的青少年期仍然可见。这表明早期的环境条件可能留下相对持久的健康印记。 探索生命早期暴露组与健康关联的图谱 该研究提供了一个开放的、交互式的儿科暴露组与健康关联图谱，允许用户探索在环境因素、健康结果和发育阶段之间确定的关系。这一资源可以支持新假设的生成和未来的研究。 “这些发现表明，生命早期环境可能同时与健康的多个维度相关，并且其中许多关联在多年后仍然可观察到。”巴塞罗那全球健康研究所（ISGlobal）研究员、该研究的资深合著者泽维尔·巴萨加纳（Xavier Basagaña）表示：“然而，该研究并未确立这些暴露是观察到的健康结果的根本原因。需要对更大、更多样化的人群进行进一步研究，以更好地理解这些关系，并确定改变其中一些暴露是否能改变儿童期和青少年期的健康轨迹。”
