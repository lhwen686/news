New research presented at the European Congress on Obesity (ECO 2026) in Istanbul, Turkey (12–15 May) and published in the International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health shows that doing around 8,500 steps a day can help people keep weight off after dieting.

Weight management programs frequently include advice to increase the number of steps walked each day. However, there is a lack of evidence to show that it helps with weight loss during dieting.

Moreover, it isn't clear if doing more steps also helps people maintain their new weight and, if it does, how many they should do.

"The most important—and greatest—challenge when treating obesity is preventing weight regain," explains Professor Marwan El Ghoch, of the Department of Biomedical, Metabolic and Neural Sciences, University of Modena and Reggio Emilia, Modena, Italy.

"Around 80% of people with overweight or obesity who initially lose weight tend to put some or all of it back on again within three to five years.

"The identification of a strategy that would solve this problem and help people maintain their new weight would be of huge clinical value."

Professor El Ghoch and researchers from Italy and Lebanon conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of existing research to find out more.

Eighteen randomized controlled trials on the topic were included in the systematic review. Fourteen of these, involving 3,758 individuals (average age of 53 years) with overweight or obesity (average BMI of 31 kg/m2) from countries including the UK, US, Australia and Japan, were included in the meta-analysis.

These trials compared 1,987 patients participating in lifestyle modification (LSM) programs with 1,771 patients who were either dieting alone or not receiving any treatment (control group).

The lifestyle modification programs included dietary recommendations and advice to walk more and count the number of steps done. The programs had a weight loss phase, followed by a weight maintenance phase, where the aim was to keep the weight off in the long term.

Daily number of steps was measured at the start of the trials, at the end of the weight loss phase (average duration of 7.9 months) and at the end of the weight maintenance phase (average duration of 10.3 months).

Both groups of patients had a similar number of steps per day at the start of the trials (7,280 in LSM group vs. 7,180 in control group) indicating that they had similar lifestyles at baseline.

The control group did not increase their number of steps and did not lose weight at any time.

In contrast, the LSM group increased their step count to 8,454 a day by the end of the weight loss phase. They also lost a significant amount of their body weight (4.39% on average, around 4 kg).

They maintained this higher step count and, at the end of the weight maintenance phase, they were doing 8,241 steps daily. They also kept off most of the weight they had lost (average weight loss at end of trials of 3.28%, around 3 kg).

Further analysis showed that there was a clear link between increasing step count and preventing weight regain. Specifically, it was important to increase step count during the weight loss phase and maintain this increase during the weight maintenance phase. Patients who did so regained less weight.

Interestingly, an increase in daily steps was not associated with greater weight loss in the weight loss phase. The researchers speculate this is because other factors, such as reducing calorie intake, play a greater role here.

Professor El Ghoch concludes that lifestyle modification programs can lead to a meaningful amount of weight loss long term.

He adds, "Participants should always be encouraged to increase their step count to approximately 8,500 a day during the weight loss phase and sustain this level of physical activity during the maintenance phase to help prevent them from regaining weight. "Increasing the number of steps walked to 8,500 each day is a simple and affordable strategy to prevent weight regain."

在土耳其伊斯坦布尔举行的欧洲肥胖大会（ECO 2026，5月12日至15日）上发表的一项新研究，同时刊载于《国际环境研究与公共卫生杂志》，显示每天走约8,500步有助于人们在节食后保持体重。

体重管理计划经常包含增加每日步行步数的建议。然而，目前缺乏证据表明这有助于节食期间的体重减轻。

此外，尚不清楚增加步数是否也能帮助人们维持新的体重，如果可以，应该走多少步。

“在肥胖治疗中，最重要且最大的挑战是预防体重反弹，”意大利摩德纳与雷焦艾米利亚大学生物医学、代谢与神经科学系Marwan El Ghoch教授解释道。

“大约80%最初成功减轻体重的超重或肥胖者，在三到五年内会重新增加部分或全部体重。”

“识别出能够解决这一问题并帮助人们维持新体重的策略，将具有巨大的临床价值。”

El Ghoch教授和来自意大利及黎巴嫩的研究人员对现有研究进行了系统回顾和荟萃分析，以了解更多信息。

系统回顾纳入了18项关于该主题的随机对照试验。其中14项试验被纳入荟萃分析，共涉及来自英国、美国、澳大利亚和日本等国的3,758名超重或肥胖个体（平均年龄53岁，平均BMI为31 kg/m2）。

这些试验比较了1,987名参与生活方式干预（LSM）计划的患者，与1,771名仅进行节食或未接受任何治疗的患者（对照组）。

生活方式干预计划包括饮食建议以及增加步行和计算步数的指导。这些计划设有一个减重阶段，随后是体重维持阶段，旨在长期保持体重不反弹。

每日步数在试验开始时、减重阶段结束时（平均持续7.9个月）和体重维持阶段结束时（平均持续10.3个月）进行测量。

试验开始时，两组患者的每日步数相似（LSM组为7,280步，对照组为7,180步），表明他们在基线时的生活方式相似。

对照组没有增加步数，并且在任何时候都没有减轻体重。

相比之下，LSM组在减重阶段结束时将每日步数增加到8,454步。他们还显著减轻了体重（平均4.39%，约4公斤）。

他们保持了这种较高的步数，在体重维持阶段结束时，他们每天步行8,241步。他们也成功保持了大部分已减轻的体重（试验结束时平均减重3.28%，约3公斤）。

进一步分析表明，增加步数与预防体重反弹之间存在明确关联。具体而言，在减重阶段增加步数并在体重维持阶段保持这种增加至关重要。这样做患者体重反弹较少。

有趣的是，每日步数的增加与减重阶段更大幅度的体重减轻无关。研究人员推测这是因为其他因素，如减少卡路里摄入，在此阶段发挥了更大的作用。

El Ghoch教授总结道，生活方式干预计划可以带来长期有意义的体重减轻。

他补充说：“应始终鼓励参与者在减重阶段将每日步数增加到大约8,500步，并在维持阶段保持这一运动水平，以帮助他们预防体重反弹。将每日步行步数增加到8,500步是一种简单且经济实惠的预防体重反弹策略。”
