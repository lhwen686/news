Genetic risk for PTSD varies across different types of trauma

深度解析

这项开创性研究在创伤后应激障碍（PTSD）的病因学中引入了精准医学的视角。其核心专业概念在于基因与环境的相互作用（GxE），特别是揭示了童年创伤（尤其是忽视）对个体PTSD易感性的遗传学影响更为显著。研究机制表明，基因组的广泛效应和特定基因的影响会因创伤类型而异，例如FAM120A基因（参与大脑恐惧和压力处理）与童年情感忽视的强相互作用。这强调了PTSD并非单一疾病，而是基于个体遗传背景和创伤暴露异质性的复杂综合征。从医学机制上看，童年时期是大脑神经发育和心理社会建构的关键窗口，基因在此阶段对创伤反应的调节作用尤为突出，可能通过影响神经回路塑性、应激反应轴（HPA轴）调控等生物学过程来增加风险。 该研究对行业的影响深远。首先，它推动了PTSD诊断和风险评估向个性化方向发展，可能通过遗传筛查识别高危人群，从而实现更早期的预防性干预。这有助于将PTSD管理从反应式治疗转向主动式预防。其次，揭示特定基因-创伤相互作用为开发新型靶向药物和非药物治疗策略提供了潜在方向。例如，针对FAM120A通路或与神经元通讯、细胞蛋白清除相关的基因，可能开发出更有效的精准疗法。这将显著改善患者预后，降低PTSD带来的痛苦和功能障碍，最终优化医疗资源的分配，提升公共卫生效益。

英文原文

Original
A groundbreaking study reveals that your genes may make you more vulnerable to Post Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) from certain types of trauma-particularly childhood neglect-than from others. The peer-reviewed findings, published today in the European Journal of Psychotraumatology, suggest that both what happens to you and your genetic makeup together affect your risk of developing the disorder. PTSD; and who might be more vulnerable? In the wake of a life-threatening event (trauma), many people experience strong emotional or physical reactions. These reactions can be overwhelming, but for most people they gradually ease over time. When they endure and begin to interfere with everyday life, they may evolve into PTSD. Common symptoms of PTSD can include re-experiencing a traumatic event (e.g. through flashbacks, nightmares or intrusive thoughts), doing things to avoid being reminded of that event, and ongoing feelings of stress or negative emotions. Not everyone who experiences trauma develops PTSD, with risk for the condition being shaped by both environmental and genetic factors. The largest genetic study of PTSD - a separate paper, which looked at 150,000 people with the condition - identified 95 genetic loci (a specific region on a chromosome) associated with PTSD and 43 genes with strong evidence for a causal effect. The broad effect of the genome on PTSD differs across different experiences Differences in people's DNA have previously been linked to PTSD, but research on this topic has rarely considered how different types of traumatic experiences might affect these genetic links. We found that the broad effect of the genome on PTSD does indeed differ across different experiences, and that this is also sometimes true when we look at the effects of specific genes." Dr. Jonathan Coleman, senior author, Senior Lecturer in Statistical Genetics at King's College London and expert in psychiatric genomics The study team - funded by the Medical Research Council (a part of UKRI) – included a diverse range of experts from King's, including Dr Coleman and lead author Jacob Knyspel, a PhD student in psychiatric genetics and developmental psychology, as well as Professor Chérie Armour from Queen's University Belfast. The researchers analysed mental health and genetic data from a total of 144,702 individuals in the UK Biobank and three studies in the National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR) BioResource and NIHR Biomedical Research Centre: Maudsley: Genetic Links to Anxiety and Depression (GLAD), Eating Disorders Genetic Initiative (EDGI) UK and COVID-19 Psychiatry and Neurological Genetics (COPING). Individuals were assessed for PTSD symptoms and asked whether they had experienced eleven different traumatic experiences. These included five childhood traumas: emotional abuse or neglect, physical abuse or neglect, and sexual abuse; and six adulthood traumas: serious accident, serious illness, sexual assault, violent crime, war exposure, or witnessing death. The researchers looked at whether the influence of a trauma on PTSD differs among people based on their genetics. They did this at three different scales: polygenic risk scores (that is, at a genome-wide level); individual genes; and specific genetic variants. Childhood trauma often linked to PTSD symptoms and gene-trauma interaction Overall, the researchers found that childhood traumas tended to be more strongly associated with PTSD symptoms and have greater levels of gene–trauma interaction than those experienced during adulthood. This suggests that genetics might have been particularly important in shaping how people responded to childhood trauma. Childhood abuse was more strongly associated with PTSD symptoms than childhood neglect; the association with childhood neglect, although more moderate, was more likely to be influenced by genetic factors. "Childhood is a very important time - a lot of changes are happening both biologically, in terms of your brain maturing, and also socially, in the formation of relationships and personal identity. Disruption to that can have long lasting effects, and that might help us explain particularly strong relationships between genetic risk, childhood trauma and PTSD," adds Dr Coleman, who co-leads the MSc in Developmental Psychology and Psychopathology at King's. At the same time, he cautions, the findings might be influenced by the ways in which the researchers studied different types of traumatic experience: "Childhood is briefer than adulthood and we examined a narrower set of experiences in childhood - these differences might make childhood look more important than it really is." The researchers also found that some of the main genes that have been linked to PTSD interacted particularly strongly with certain trauma types. For example, FAM120A - a gene involved in the brain's processing of fear and stress - was found to interact significantly more strongly with childhood emotional neglect than any of the other traumas studied. Other genes, including ones involved in clearing old cellular proteins, neuronal communication, and bringing proteins to DNA, interacted strongly with a broad range of other traumas. "Broadly, this fits with the biology of PTSD being based in how the brain develops, how the cells of the brain communicate, and how the body responds to stress," lead author Jacob Knyspel, a PhD student at King's, says. "There were also outliers - one gene with strong trauma interactions, SGCD, is involved in muscle function, making its role in PTSD less clear. "When we looked across genes, we didn't identify any particular biological process that could explain why certain traumas seemed more strongly linked to genetics, so more research is needed." The future of PTSD and genetics in research – how treatment might improve According to the researchers, their findings highlight the importance of factoring trauma exposure information into future PTSD genetics research. "We might get a better understanding of how genetic changes affect people's risk of PTSD if we include information on the full range of experiences a person has had," Dr Coleman says. In the future, this understanding could help to improve PTSD treatment. "Ultimately, we would like to be able to measure people's DNA and use this to help screen people exposed to - or at risk of - particular traumatic events. That would help to ensure that the people most at risk of developing PTSD get support as early as possible, reducing the distress and disablement that PTSD can bring," Dr Coleman concludes.

AI Translation
一项开创性研究揭示，相比其他类型的创伤，某些创伤（特别是童年时期的忽视）可能更易使基因层面的人群对创伤后应激障碍（PTSD）表现出更高的脆弱性。 这项经同行评审的研究成果今日发表于《欧洲心理创伤学杂志》，表明个体的经历与其遗传构成共同影响着罹患该障碍的风险。 创伤后应激障碍（PTSD）；以及谁可能更易受其影响？ 在经历危及生命的事件（创伤）之后，许多人会产生强烈的情绪或身体反应。这些反应可能令人难以承受，但对大多数人而言，它们会随着时间推移逐渐缓解。然而，当这些反应持续存在并开始干扰日常生活时，它们就可能演变为PTSD。 PTSD的常见症状包括反复体验创伤事件（例如通过闪回、噩梦或闯入性思维）、采取措施避免回忆该事件，以及持续的压力感或负面情绪。 并非所有经历创伤的人都会发展为PTSD，罹患此病的风险由环境和遗传因素共同塑造。迄今为止规模最大的PTSD遗传学研究（一项独立论文，涵盖了15万名患者）识别出95个与PTSD相关的遗传位点（染色体上的特定区域），以及43个具有强有力因果效应证据的基因。 基因组对PTSD的广泛影响因不同经历而异。 “人类DNA的差异此前已被证实与PTSD相关，但此前对此领域的研究鲜少考虑不同类型的创伤经历可能如何影响这些基因关联。我们发现，基因组对PTSD的广泛影响确实因不同的经历而异，有时当我们审视特定基因的影响时，情况亦是如此。”伦敦国王学院统计遗传学高级讲师、精神病基因组学专家、本研究的资深作者Jonathan Coleman博士表示。 该研究团队——由医学研究理事会（英国研究与创新局UKRI的一部分）资助——成员包括来自国王学院的各领域专家，如Coleman博士和主要作者Jacob Knyspel（精神病遗传学与发展心理学博士生），以及贝尔法斯特女王大学的Chérie Armour教授。 研究人员分析了来自英国生物样本库（UK Biobank）以及国家健康与护理研究所（NIHR）生物资源和NIHR生物医学研究中心三项研究（Maudsley：焦虑与抑郁遗传关联研究GLAD、英国饮食障碍遗传倡议EDGI UK、COVID-19精神病学与神经遗传学研究COPING）的共计144,702名个体的心理健康和遗传数据。 研究对象接受了PTSD症状评估，并被询问是否经历过十一种不同的创伤经历。其中包括五种童年创伤：情感虐待或忽视、身体虐待或忽视以及性虐待；以及六种成年期创伤：严重事故、严重疾病、性侵犯、暴力犯罪、战争暴露或目睹死亡。 研究人员探讨了创伤对PTSD的影响是否因个体的遗传背景而异。他们从三个不同层面进行了分析：多基因风险评分（即全基因组水平）、单个基因以及特定的遗传变异。 童年创伤常与PTSD症状及基因-创伤相互作用相关。 总体而言，研究人员发现童年创伤与PTSD症状的关联性更强，且基因-创伤相互作用水平高于成年期创伤。这表明遗传因素可能在塑造个体对童年创伤的反应中发挥了特别重要的作用。 童年虐待与PTSD症状的关联性强于童年忽视；尽管与童年忽视的关联性较为温和，但它更可能受到遗传因素的影响。 “童年是一个非常重要的时期——在生物学上，大脑正在发育成熟；在社会学上，人际关系和个人身份正在形成，许多变化正在发生。这一过程中的中断可能产生持久影响，这或许有助于我们解释遗传风险、童年创伤与PTSD之间特别强烈的关联。”Coleman博士补充道，他同时也是国王学院发展心理学与精神病理学硕士课程的联合负责人。 与此同时，他提醒道，研究结果可能受到研究人员研究不同类型创伤经历方式的影响：“童年时期比成年期短，我们在童年期考察的经历范围也更窄——这些差异可能使得童年期的重要性看起来比实际更大。” 研究人员还发现，一些已被证实与PTSD相关的主要基因与特定类型的创伤表现出特别强的相互作用。 例如，FAM120A（一个参与大脑恐惧和压力处理的基因）被发现与童年情感忽视的相互作用显著强于所研究的其他任何创伤。其他基因，包括那些参与清除衰老细胞蛋白、神经元通讯以及将蛋白质转运至DNA的基因，则与广泛的其他创伤表现出强烈的相互作用。 国王学院博士生、主要作者Jacob Knyspel表示：“总体而言，这与PTSD的生物学基础相吻合，即其根植于大脑如何发育、脑细胞如何交流以及身体如何应对压力。” “当然也存在例外——一个与创伤有强烈相互作用的基因SGCD，它与肌肉功能有关，这使得它在PTSD中的作用尚不明确。” “当我们审视所有基因时，我们未能识别出任何特定的生物学过程，可以解释为什么某些创伤似乎与遗传学联系更紧密，因此还需要更多的研究。” PTSD与遗传学研究的未来——治疗如何改进。 研究人员指出，他们的发现强调了在未来的PTSD遗传学研究中，将创伤暴露信息纳入考量的重要性。 Coleman博士表示：“如果我们能纳入一个人所经历的全部信息，我们或许能更好地理解基因变化如何影响人们患PTSD的风险。” 未来，这种理解有助于改善PTSD的治疗。 Coleman博士总结道：“最终，我们希望能够测量个体的DNA，并利用这些信息帮助筛查那些暴露于特定创伤事件或有风险的人群。这将有助于确保患PTSD风险最高的人群尽早获得支持，从而减少PTSD可能带来的痛苦和功能障碍。”
