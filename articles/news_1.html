Air pollution just isn't what it used to be. While levels of lead and sulfur dioxide have dropped due to environmental regulations, other threats to air quality persist.

Particle pollution, ozone and wildfire smoke all taint the air you breathe and put your health at risk.

"Nearly everyone on the planet is exposed to unhealthy air at least some of the time," said Dr. Stavros Garantziotis, a researcher at the National Institutes of Health.

"The same air pollution exposure doesn't affect everyone the same way," he added in the April issue of NIH News in Health.

The risks are higher for folks with asthma, which makes it hard to breathe. Children, older adults and folks with diabetes or heart disease may also struggle.

In fact, News in Health notes, high-pollution days are often followed by a spike in hospital visits for heart attacks and strokes in older adults. Some pollutants also increase cancer risk.

Particle pollution from industrial processes, vehicles and power plants is a major threat. Tiny airborne particles can be inhaled deep into the lungs.

Ozone, a gas that forms when sunlight reacts with certain pollutants, can also damage airways.

And wildfire smoke can affect people hundreds of miles from the source. These blazes, which are happening more often than ever, release chemicals, tiny particles and metals into the air.

"Smoke doesn't stay where the fire is," Garantziotis said. "It goes wherever the wind takes it."

In other words, even if the fire happened hundreds of miles away, byproducts can seep into your home, adding to the health impact posed by pollutants from indoor sources such as fireplaces, gas stoves, mold, dust, pet dander and pests.

Garantziotis and his team are investigating how lung cells repair themselves after exposure to polluted air.

They discovered that pollution can break apart supportive material around lung cells, making it harder to breathe.

The finding suggests that giving people a healthy form of this material, called hyaluronan, may help restore lung health.

Scientists are also trying to learn why some folks are more susceptible to pollution effects. Learning why could lead to new ways to prevent and treat related illnesses, News in Health said.

In the meantime, you can take these steps:

Ask your doctor how polluted air at home, school or work may affect your health.

When pollution levels are high, limit outdoor activity and keep windows closed.

When cooking, especially with gas appliances, always use exhaust fans.

Change HVAC filters quarterly.

Avoid smoking indoors and being around secondhand smoke.

For rooms with higher pollution sources, consider portable HEPA air cleaners.

Open windows when outdoor air quality is good to ventilate your home.

他在NIH《健康新闻》的四月刊中补充道：“同样的空气污染暴露对每个人的影响方式不同。”

对于患有哮喘、呼吸困难的人群，风险更高。儿童、老年人以及患有糖尿病或心脏病的人群也可能受影响更严重。

事实上，《健康新闻》指出，高污染日之后，老年人因心脏病发作和中风而就诊医院的人数通常会激增。某些污染物还会增加患癌风险。

来自工业流程、车辆和发电厂的颗粒物污染是一个主要威胁。微小的空气颗粒物可被吸入肺部深处。

臭氧，一种当阳光与某些污染物发生反应时形成的气体，也会损害气道。

野火烟雾甚至能影响距离火源数百英里的人群。这些火灾的发生频率比以往任何时候都高，会将化学物质、微小颗粒物和金属释放到空气中。

加兰佐蒂斯表示：“烟雾不会停留在火源地。它会随风飘散。”

换言之，即使火灾发生在数百英里之外，其副产品也可能渗入您的家中，加剧壁炉、燃气灶、霉菌、灰尘、宠物皮屑和害虫等室内污染源对健康的危害。

加兰佐蒂斯和他的团队正在研究肺细胞在暴露于污染空气后如何自我修复。

这一发现表明，向人们提供这种物质的一种健康形式，即透明质酸，可能有助于恢复肺部健康。

《健康新闻》称，科学家们还在努力研究为什么有些人对污染影响更敏感。了解其原因可能为预防和治疗相关疾病提供新途径。

与此同时，您可以采取以下措施：

咨询您的医生，家庭、学校或工作场所的污染空气可能如何影响您的健康。

当污染水平较高时，限制户外活动并关好门窗。

烹饪时，特别是使用燃气设备时，务必使用排风扇。

每季度更换暖通空调过滤器。

避免在室内吸烟并避免二手烟。

对于污染源较多的房间，可以考虑使用便携式HEPA空气净化器。

当室外空气质量良好时，开窗通风。
