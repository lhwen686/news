探究政治分歧对疫苗安全性与有效性认知的影响

Examining political differences in perceptions of vaccine safety and effectiveness

深度解析

本文深入探讨了影响疫苗接受度和接种率的关键因素，揭示了公众对疫苗和科学的认知复杂性。核心专业概念在于“疫苗犹豫”（vaccine hesitancy）的深层驱动因素，即它不仅仅是对科学家能力或可靠性的不信任，更延伸至对科学家“公正性”的感知以及对科学整体“有益性”的信念。文章通过对美国不同政治派别（共和党与民主党）的对比分析，揭示了政治立场如何显著影响对特定疫苗（如COVID-19疫苗）的安全性与有效性评价。 这一研究对公共卫生和医疗行业具有重要影响。它强调，在制定疫苗推广策略时，不能仅停留在强调疫苗的科学证据，而必须采纳更精细、更有针对性的“定制沟通策略”。这意味着公共卫生信息应根据不同受众的政治背景和担忧点，着重强调科学的透明度、科学家的公正性，以及疫苗带来的具体、可感知的健康益处。例如，对于对科学家公正性有疑虑的群体，沟通重点应放在科研过程的公开透明性；对于普遍对科学有负面看法的群体，则需强调科学在日常生活中带来的实际正面影响。这种策略转变有助于更有效地化解疫苗犹豫，提升全社会疫苗接种率，从而更好地应对未来的公共卫生危机。

Dr. Dror Walter, with the Department of Communication at Georgia State University in Atlanta, and colleagues examined these perceptions using data from a national probability sample of 1,763 U.S. adults surveyed in November 2024. The study was published online in the American Journal of Public Health on Sept. 10, 2026. "Previous research examining associations with vaccine hesitancy has primarily measured perceptions of science and scientists in terms of trust. However, analyses of contemporary arguments on vaccine hesitancy suggest that resistance extends beyond mistrust or doubts about scientists' competence," says Walter. Notably, respondents rated the MMR vaccine as the safest and most effective, while the COVID-19 vaccine received the lowest ratings for both safety and effectiveness. Republicans generally reported less favorable perceptions of vaccine safety and effectiveness than Democrats, particularly for the COVID-19 vaccine. The findings showed that positive views of science and scientists were associated with higher perceived safety and effectiveness of the MMR, flu and COVID-19 vaccines. Explaining the value of this approach, Walter says, "Our analysis shows that perceiving scientists as unbiased mattered more in Republicans' assessments of the COVID-19 vaccine's effectiveness but less so (compared with Democrats) in the case of the MMR's effectiveness. This difference is likely the result of attacks on the integrity of scientists during the COVID-19 pandemic from the right, as opposed to prepandemic concerns about the MMR vaccine from the left." Perceiving science as beneficial was the factor most consistently associated with positive vaccine evaluations across all three vaccines. This pattern suggests that communication emphasizing the tangible benefits of science and vaccines may be particularly effective, especially when tailored to audience concerns and political context. Walter and colleagues conclude that understanding vaccine perceptions may benefit from examining both political affiliation and perceptions of science and scientists. This may offer practical insights for addressing vaccine concerns by guiding tailored communication strategies. Provided by American Public Health Association Who's behind this story?
