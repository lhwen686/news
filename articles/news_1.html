深度解析

这篇研究揭示了D-丝氨酸在胃癌（GC）进展和免疫逃逸中的关键作用，并提供了潜在的生物标志物和治疗靶点。其核心机制在于D-丝氨酸能够重塑肿瘤微环境，使其向免疫抑制和抗炎方向发展。具体而言，D-丝氨酸促进M2样巨噬细胞等抗炎免疫细胞的数量和活性，这些细胞进而分泌纤连蛋白1（FN1）和分泌性磷蛋白1（SPP1）等免疫抑制分子。这些分子协同作用，大幅抑制了抗肿瘤免疫中至关重要的CD8+细胞毒性T细胞的功能和数量，从而加速肿瘤生长并导致肿瘤对免疫检查点抑制剂（ICI）疗法产生抵抗。 从行业影响来看，这项发现具有多重临床意义。首先，血清D-丝氨酸浓度可作为胃癌诊断和ICI治疗抵抗的预测性生物标志物，有助于临床医生优化患者分层，筛选出可能对ICI反应不佳的患者，避免不必要的治疗毒性。其次，D-丝氨酸及其代谢途径，包括其肠道微生物来源，为开发新型抗癌疗法提供了全新的靶点。通过靶向抑制D-丝氨酸的合成或其信号通路，有望增强现有免疫疗法的疗效，尤其对于ICI抵抗的胃癌患者，这代表着免疫肿瘤学领域潜在的突破性进展。该研究也强调了癌症代谢，特别是氨基酸代谢，在肿瘤免疫逃逸中的重要性。

英文原文

Original
Researchers from Keio University have found that D-serine increases the growth of gastric cancer by creating an immunosuppressive and anti-inflammatory tumor microenvironment. Higher levels of D-serine in serum correlated with greater immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy resistance in tumors. Suppressing D-serine in circulation could improve the efficacy of immunotherapies against gastric cancer. These findings position D-serine within the broader field of cancer amino acid metabolism and point to it as a possible target for tumor evasion. One of the crucial steps in cancer proliferation is evading killer immune cells. Cancer cells use a variety of signaling molecules to evade the immune system. Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy interferes with some of these evasion tactics, improving the immune system's ability to suppress tumors. However, the success of ICI therapy depends on the specific signals being secreted by the tumor. Recent research has identified D-amino acids (D-AAs) as a new class of signaling molecules. D-AAs are enantiomers, or "mirror images" of L-amino acids that make up proteins. D-AAs are normally present in human biological fluids and can be derived from the diet, microbiota, and metabolic byproducts. Our previous research showed that oral administration of D-AAs improved colitis in a mouse model." Shohei Suzuki, Assistant Professor, Keio University School of Medicine Could D-AAs play a role in tumor immunity? That is the question Dr. Suzuki sought to answer. With support from Assistant Professor Kai Tsugaru and Associate Professor Tomohisa Sujino, also from the School of Medicine, the research team studied the effects of D-AAs, specifically D-serine (D-ser), in mouse models of GC and in human patients. Their findings were made available online in the journal eBioMedicine on July 31, 2026. The team induced gastric cancer (GC) in healthy mice and injected the tumors with various solutions of D-AAs and L-AAs. Only mice that received D-ser showed significantly faster tumor growth compared to controls. Further analysis showed that D-ser had major immunosuppressive effects. D-ser administration increased both the numbers and activity of anti-inflammatory immune cells in the tumor microenvironment, particularly M2-like macrophages. As a result, CD8+ cytotoxic T cells, which would otherwise kill GC tumor cells, were in much smaller numbers and their activity was greatly reduced. Further studies showed that tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) in D-ser-injected tumors secreted excessive amounts of two immunosuppressive molecules, fibronectin 1 (FN1) and secreted phosphoprotein 1 (SPP1), both known to suppress CD8+ T cells. When anti-SPP1 antibodies were injected into the D-ser-enhanced tumors, tumor growth rate decreased, approaching that of the low-D-ser phenotype. The researchers then examined clinical records across multiple human cohorts. "We discovered that the concentration of D-ser in the serum of patients with GC was higher than that of healthy control," says Dr. Suzuki. What's more, the concentration of D-ser in serum was correlated with resistance to ICI therapy; patients with stage IV, ICI-resistant GC tumors had the highest D-ser concentrations in their bloodstream. "Although ICIs have become a first-line treatment option for GC, better predictors of treatment response are needed due to the risk of immune-related adverse events," says Dr. Suzuki. These findings show that serum D-ser could serve as a biomarker for both GC itself and the likelihood of ICI resistance in patients with the disease. Encouraged by these results, Dr. Suzuki identified directions for future research in this field. "We are now investigating whether D-ser levels in blood and feces can help predict how patients respond to ICI therapy. Ultimately, we hope to develop new treatments that target D-ser and the gut bacteria that produce it," says Dr. Suzuki. Dr. Sujino adds, "A strategy to reduce the concentration of D-ser in the serum of patients with tumors might regulate immune activity against cancer," indicating that D-ser and its downstream signaling molecules may be potential new targets for anti-GC immunotherapies.

AI Translation
庆应义塾大学的研究人员发现，D-丝氨酸通过创造免疫抑制和抗炎的肿瘤微环境，促进胃癌的生长。血清中较高水平的D-丝氨酸与肿瘤对免疫检查点抑制剂（ICI）治疗更强的抵抗力相关。抑制循环中的D-丝氨酸可能提高免疫疗法对抗胃癌的疗效。这些发现将D-丝氨酸置于更广泛的癌症氨基酸代谢领域中，并将其指向一个可能的肿瘤免疫逃逸靶点。 癌症增殖的关键步骤之一是逃避杀伤性免疫细胞。癌细胞利用多种信号分子来逃避免疫系统。免疫检查点抑制剂（ICI）疗法通过干扰其中一些逃避策略，提高免疫系统抑制肿瘤的能力。然而，ICI疗法的成功取决于肿瘤分泌的特定信号。 最近的研究已将D-氨基酸（D-AAs）识别为一类新型信号分子。D-AAs是组成蛋白质的L-氨基酸的对映异构体，或“镜像”分子。D-AAs通常存在于人体生物体液中，可来源于饮食、微生物群和代谢副产物。 庆应义塾大学医学院助理教授铃木昭平表示：“我们之前的研究表明，口服D-氨基酸在小鼠模型中改善了结肠炎。” D-氨基酸在肿瘤免疫中是否发挥作用？这是铃木博士试图回答的问题。在同样来自医学院的助理教授津轻开和副教授筋野智久的支持下，研究团队在胃癌（GC）小鼠模型和人类患者中研究了D-氨基酸，特别是D-丝氨酸（D-ser）的作用。他们的研究结果于2026年7月31日在eBioMedicine杂志在线发表。 该团队在健康小鼠中诱导了胃癌（GC），并向肿瘤注射了各种D-氨基酸和L-氨基酸溶液。只有接受D-丝氨酸的小鼠表现出比对照组显著更快的肿瘤生长。进一步分析表明，D-丝氨酸具有显著的免疫抑制作用。D-丝氨酸的给药增加了肿瘤微环境中抗炎免疫细胞的数量和活性，特别是M2样巨噬细胞。结果，原本会杀伤胃癌肿瘤细胞的CD8+细胞毒性T细胞数量大大减少，其活性也大幅降低。 进一步研究表明，注射D-丝氨酸的肿瘤中的肿瘤相关巨噬细胞（TAMs）分泌过量的两种免疫抑制分子：纤连蛋白1（FN1）和分泌性磷蛋白1（SPP1），这两种分子均已知能抑制CD8+ T细胞。当抗SPP1抗体被注射到D-丝氨酸增强的肿瘤中时，肿瘤生长速度下降，接近于低D-丝氨酸表型的肿瘤生长速度。 随后，研究人员检查了多个人类队列的临床记录。铃木博士说：“我们发现胃癌患者血清中D-丝氨酸的浓度高于健康对照组。”更重要的是，血清中D-丝氨酸的浓度与对ICI疗法的抵抗力相关；患有IV期ICI抵抗性胃癌肿瘤的患者血液中D-丝氨酸浓度最高。 铃木博士指出：“尽管ICI已成为胃癌的一线治疗选择，但由于存在免疫相关不良事件的风险，需要更好的治疗反应预测指标。”这些发现表明，血清D-丝氨酸可以作为胃癌本身以及患者对ICI抵抗可能性的生物标志物。 受这些结果的鼓舞，铃木博士指出了该领域未来的研究方向。“我们现在正在研究血液和粪便中的D-丝氨酸水平是否能帮助预测患者对ICI疗法的反应。”铃木博士说：“最终，我们希望开发出针对D-丝氨酸及其产生D-丝氨酸的肠道细菌的新疗法。”筋野博士补充道：“降低肿瘤患者血清中D-丝氨酸浓度的策略可能调节抗癌免疫活性，”这表明D-丝氨酸及其下游信号分子可能是抗胃癌免疫疗法的潜在新靶点。
