只有三分之一符合条件的乳腺癌患者接受基因表达检测

Only a third of eligible breast cancer patients receive a gene expression test

💡 AI 深度解析 (AI Insight)

基因表达谱（GEP）检测是精准肿瘤学中的关键工具，通过分析肿瘤组织中特定基因的表达水平，为早期乳腺癌患者提供个体化的预后和预测信息。核心机制在于，这些检测（如MammaPrint、Oncotype DX和Prosigna）能够量化肿瘤的生物学侵袭性，评估远处转移复发的风险，并预测患者从辅助化疗中获益的可能性。例如，Oncotype DX通过21基因复发评分将激素受体阳性、HER2阴性乳腺癌患者分为低、中、高风险组，从而指导是否需要化疗，有效避免了低风险患者不必要的化疗毒副作用，如骨髓抑制、心脏毒性及继发性癌症风险。

然而，文章揭示，尽管GEP检测具有显著的临床价值，其在荷兰的普及率仍受限，即使在引入报销政策后也仅达37%。这反映了医疗技术采纳中的普遍挑战：除经济障碍外，还可能存在临床认知不足、检测流程整合困难以及医生对新技术的接受度差异等实施壁垒。这种低使用率导致部分符合条件的患者可能面临过度治疗（即接受本可避免的化疗）的风险。从行业角度看，GEP检测的推广不仅优化了患者护理，提升了生活质量，也推动了诊断产业发展，并促使医疗系统更加注重成本效益，减少不必要的治疗开支。未来的重点在于通过多学科协作，制定全国性实施计划，确保这项重要技术能普惠更多患者。

A gene expression test can help prevent unnecessary chemotherapy in breast cancer patients, yet its use in the Netherlands remains limited. This is shown by new research from the Netherlands Comprehensive Cancer Centre (IKNL), led by Sabine Siesling, principal investigator at IKNL and professor at the University of Twente. Even after reimbursement was introduced, only about one in three eligible patients receives a test. The work is published in the International Journal of Cancer.

Gene expression tests, such as MammaPrint and Oncotype DX, provide doctors with additional information about the risk of metastases in women with early-stage breast cancer. This information supports decision-making on whether chemotherapy is beneficial. About 13% of all breast cancer patients in the Netherlands are eligible for such a test. Based on data from the Dutch Cancer Registry (NKR), IKNL studied the use of these tests in Dutch clinical practice.

Reimbursement increases use, but remains insufficient

Researcher Joyce Meijer, a Ph.D. student at the University of Twente and IKNL, analyzed the effect of the reimbursement policy on the use of gene expression tests. Before reimbursement, only 9% of eligible patients received a test. Since October 2023, when reimbursement was introduced in the Netherlands, this percentage has increased to 37%. This could mean that in 2024, more than 1,000 eligible women may not have received the test.

Siesling, principal investigator at IKNL and professor at the University of Twente, leader of this study, said: "It is good to see that reimbursement clearly stimulates use. But the use of gene expression tests still falls short of what can be expected. About two-thirds of the patients who are eligible for it still do not receive a test. And that's a shame, because this can mean that women receive chemotherapy unnecessarily." The use of the test is still increasing each year; by now, the total percentage will be higher again.

Test results often decisive in chemotherapy decisions

The research results show that gene expression test outcomes are strongly related to the final treatment decision. Among patients classified as high risk based on the test results, 83% (MammaPrint) and 85% (Oncotype DX) received chemotherapy. In contrast, chemotherapy was not administered in 93% and 97% of cases among patients classified as low risk, respectively. This highlights the value of these tests in preventing overtreatment and avoiding unnecessary side effects.

Many patients without a gene expression test still receive chemotherapy

Notably, of the patients who were eligible for a gene expression test but did not receive it, more than half (52%) received chemotherapy. Siesling said: "This suggests that the lack of test information may lead to a broader use of chemotherapy than may be necessary." This is an important finding because chemotherapy is associated with well-known side effects. At the same time, it is not possible to determine what the test result would have been in these patients because these data are lacking.

More insight is needed into barriers

The results show that gene expression tests are an important tool for appropriate care for breast cancer, but their use is not yet optimal. To gain a better understanding of the barriers to the use of these tests, IKNL is starting an implementation project together with the University of Twente and a group of clinicians and researchers. The aim is to better identify obstacles to use and to further support appropriate care. This project is made possible by a grant from ZonMw.

The goal is to develop a national implementation plan for the appropriate use of this type of test across all regional oncology networks. This plan can also give direction to the possible future use of new tests, such as Prosigna. Recently, research by University College London showed that about two-thirds of breast cancer patients can be treated with hormone therapy instead of chemotherapy. The Prosigna test distinguishes between the groups that benefit from this and those that do not.

基因表达检测有助于避免乳腺癌患者接受不必要的化疗，然而其在荷兰的应用仍然有限。这是由荷兰综合癌症中心（IKNL）的最新研究表明的，该研究由IKNL首席研究员兼特文特大学教授Sabine Siesling领导。即使在引入报销政策之后，大约只有三分之一符合条件的患者接受了这项检测。这项工作发表在《国际癌症杂志》上。

MammaPrint和Oncotype DX等基因表达检测为早期乳腺癌女性提供了关于转移风险的额外信息。这些信息有助于医生决定化疗是否有利。荷兰约有13%的乳腺癌患者符合此类检测的条件。IKNL根据荷兰癌症登记中心（NKR）的数据，研究了这些检测在荷兰临床实践中的使用情况。

报销增加了使用，但仍不充分

特文特大学和IKNL的博士生研究员Joyce Meijer分析了报销政策对基因表达检测使用情况的影响。在报销政策出台之前，只有9%的符合条件的患者接受了检测。自2023年10月荷兰引入报销以来，这一比例已增至37%。这意味着在2024年，可能仍有超过1000名符合条件的女性未能接受该检测。

IKNL首席研究员兼特文特大学教授、本研究的负责人Siesling表示：“很高兴看到报销政策明显刺激了使用。但基因表达检测的使用仍未达到预期水平。约三分之二符合条件的患者仍未接受检测。这令人遗憾，因为这可能意味着女性接受了不必要的化疗。”检测的使用率仍在逐年增加；截至目前，总比例将再次提高。

检测结果常在化疗决策中起决定性作用

研究结果表明，基因表达检测结果与最终治疗决策密切相关。在根据检测结果被归类为高风险的患者中，83%（MammaPrint）和85%（Oncotype DX）接受了化疗。相比之下，在被归类为低风险的患者中，分别有93%和97%的病例未进行化疗。这凸显了这些检测在预防过度治疗和避免不必要副作用方面的价值。

许多未接受基因表达检测的患者仍接受化疗

值得注意的是，在那些符合基因表达检测条件但未接受检测的患者中，超过一半（52%）接受了化疗。Siesling表示：“这表明检测信息的缺失可能导致化疗的使用范围比实际需要更广。”这是一个重要的发现，因为化疗与众所周知的副作用相关。同时，由于缺乏数据，无法确定这些患者的检测结果会是什么。

需要更多地了解障碍

结果显示，基因表达检测是乳腺癌适当护理的重要工具，但其使用尚未达到最佳状态。为了更好地了解这些检测使用中的障碍，IKNL正与特文特大学以及一群临床医生和研究人员合作，启动一项实施项目。目标是更好地识别使用障碍，并进一步支持适当的护理。该项目得到了ZonMw的资助。

目标是为所有区域肿瘤网络中此类检测的适当使用制定一项全国性实施计划。该计划还可以为未来可能使用Prosigna等新型检测提供方向。伦敦大学学院最近的研究表明，大约三分之二的乳腺癌患者可以接受激素治疗而非化疗。Prosigna检测能够区分哪些群体从中受益，哪些群体不受益。
