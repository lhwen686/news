基于干细胞构建的类脑器官揭示一种治疗毁灭性儿童疾病的有前景新疗法

Creating mini-brains from stem cells reveals a new, promising treatment for a devastating childhood disease

该研究揭示了一种罕见神经退行性疾病DHDDS变异的深层医学机制及其潜在治疗方案。核心机制在于DHDDS基因缺陷导致聚戊烯醇（dolichol）合成不足，这种脂质是聚糖（glycans）合成的关键“锚”。聚戊烯醇的减少不仅造成聚糖构建错误，还干扰脂质代谢，导致星形胶质细胞中胆固醇积累。胆固醇积累进一步引发线粒体功能障碍，最终降低细胞能量产生，解释了疾病的进行性特点。

研究团队创新性地利用“迷你大脑”模型，模拟了疾病的病理进展，并筛选出烟酰胺单核苷酸（NMN）作为潜在疗法。NMN作为维生素B3的一种形式，在细胞水平上通过改善能量代谢、减轻线粒体功能障碍发挥作用，与疾病机制高度契合。其行业影响深远：它为罕见病的机制研究和药物筛选提供了高效且伦理友好的新范式；通过“旧药新用”的策略，将一种易得、廉价且安全的维生素转变为有效疗法，显著降低了罕见病治疗的门槛和成本，为患者带来了福音，也为其他类似罕见病的治疗开发提供了宝贵经验。此外，该案例彰显了患者、慈善机构和学术界协同合作在推动罕见病治疗中的关键作用。

Variants in the DHDDS gene cause a severe neurodegenerative condition, characterized by tremors, seizures, coordination and learning difficulties, usually manifesting in early childhood. This Parkinson's-like condition is extremely rare, and until recently, parents were told there was nothing that could be done to slow its progression. But now, researchers from the Netherlands and the U.S. who created "mini brain" models from patients' own cells to test new therapies have found not only the mechanism of the disease, but also that a naturally occurring form of vitamin B3 (nicotinamide mononucleotide, or NMN) holds significant promise in slowing disease progression.

Presenting the results to the annual conference of the European Society of Human Genetics today, Dr. Irena Muffels, a clinical genetics resident at the Wilhelmina Children's Hospital, Utrecht, the Netherlands, described how two parents contacted researchers at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, New York, U.S., where she was working at the time in the Morava-Kozicz lab. They had been told that the only hope for their two children, who had been diagnosed with DHDDS-related disease, was to wait for researchers to take an interest in the rare disorder.

"But they didn't want to wait," says Dr. Muffels. "They didn't want their children to become wheelchair dependent and unable to take care of themselves due to their movement problems. So they contacted Professor Eva Morava, for whom I was then working. We started creating mini-brains—tiny blobs of brain tissue grown in the lab from patients' own cells*—thus avoiding the need to take samples directly from the children's brains."

The patient-derived mini-brains allowed the researchers to find the DHDDS disease mechanism as well as why it was progressive. After four months, the mini-brains showed clear signs of deterioration, mirroring what happens in real patients.

Normal DHDDS helps produce dolichol, a small lipid "anchor" that carries sugar. In studying the DHDDS mini-brains, the researchers found that this anchor was severely reduced. Sugar also helps build glycans, a kind of antenna that helps proteins perform their correct functions. In the mini-brains, the researchers could see that there were mistakes in the building of these antennae.

Another problem with defective DHDDS is that reduced dolichol affects lipid metabolism as a whole and can lead to significant cholesterol buildup in astrocytes, brain cells involved in neuroprotection. "This accumulation builds over time, and this is why we think the disease progresses," says Dr. Muffels, "since the accumulation of cholesterol leads to mitochondrial dysfunction, leading in turn to reduced energy production."

The researchers collaborated with the biotech company Perlara to identify potential new therapies by screening FDA-approved drugs and vitamins, and found that NMN was able to rescue a yeast model of DHDDS-related disease. They tested the vitamin in the mini-brains and noticed striking improvements. Since this form of vitamin B3 can be bought without a prescription, people started ordering it online before the experiments were completed.

"Within a month we had noticed that these patients' walking improved and that they were more energetic, less shaky, and their movements became more fluid. It really seemed to slow down progression of the disease," says Dr. Muffels.

NMN has been shown to improve molecular mechanisms in muscle cells of patients with mitochondrial disease, a common and devastating pediatric metabolic disorder, and it is currently being tested clinically in these patients. High doses of the vitamin have also been shown to slow progression in Parkinson's disease patients and reduce symptom burden. Due to the plethora of positive effects that vitamin B3 and NMN can induce at the cellular level, other genetic metabolic disorders that affect energy production in the brain could potentially benefit from the treatment.

"The word about NMN reached more DHDDS patients, and we currently have 12 patients taking it. We are starting an international trial for NMN supplementation in DHDDS-related disease," says Dr. Muffels. "Patients will take NMN for a year and will be evaluated every three months. Although I have now left the U.S., I hope that the Wilhelmina Hospital in Utrecht will be one of the official sites and that I may continue to work with these patients."

"There is still a long way to go, but it was encouraging to see how well the creation of the mini-brains helped us mimic the progression of the disease in patients—we could literally see the brains falling apart under the microscope. We were surprised by how fast and how well NMN has worked, and particularly pleased since it is widely available, cheap, and has no known side effects. In some patients, you couldn't even see that they were affected by DHDDS disease after treatment. Now, the first four patients are enrolled in the trial and we are looking forward to being able to help them and their families further in the future."

Chair of the conference, Professor Alexandre Reymond, who was not involved in the research, said, "This study is a perfect example of how rapid progress in genetic diagnosis can lead to new treatments for rare diseases. Because rare diseases such as DHDDS affect so few people, it is usually very difficult to get industry interested. It is impressive that a united front of parents, charities and academics was able to find this promising therapy that is also cheap and widely available."

DHDDS基因变异会导致一种严重的神经退行性疾病，其特征是震颤、癫痫发作、协调和学习困难，通常在幼儿期发病。这种类似帕金森病的疾病极为罕见，直到最近，家长们仍被告知对此疾病的进展束手无策。但现在，来自荷兰和美国的科学家们，利用患者自身的细胞创建了“迷你大脑”模型来测试新的疗法，他们不仅发现了疾病的机制，还发现一种天然形式的维生素B3（烟酰胺单核苷酸，NMN）在减缓疾病进展方面具有显著前景。

今天，在欧洲人类遗传学学会年会上，荷兰乌得勒支威廉敏娜儿童医院的临床遗传学住院医生伊雷娜·马弗尔斯博士（Dr. Irena Muffels）介绍了研究结果。她描述了两位家长如何联系到纽约西奈山伊坎医学院的研究人员，当时她正在莫拉瓦-科齐茨实验室工作。此前，他们被告知，其两个被诊断出DHDDS相关疾病的孩子的唯一希望是等待研究人员对这种罕见疾病产生兴趣。

“但他们不想等，”马弗尔斯博士说。“他们不想让他们的孩子因为运动问题而变得依赖轮椅，无法自理。所以他们联系了伊娃·莫拉瓦教授，我当时正为她工作。我们开始创建迷你大脑——在实验室中利用患者自身细胞培养出来的微小脑组织块*——从而避免了直接从孩子大脑中取样的需要。”

这种患者来源的迷你大脑使研究人员能够发现DHDDS疾病的机制以及其进行性特点。四个月后，迷你大脑显示出明显的退化迹象，这与真实患者的病理进展相吻合。

正常的DHDDS有助于产生聚戊烯醇（dolichol），这是一种携带糖的小型脂质“锚”。在研究DHDDS迷你大脑时，研究人员发现这种“锚”显著减少。糖还有助于构建聚糖（glycans），一种帮助蛋白质执行其正确功能的“天线”。在迷你大脑中，研究人员观察到这些“天线”的构建存在错误。

缺陷DHDDS的另一个问题是，聚戊烯醇的减少会影响整体脂质代谢，并可能导致星形胶质细胞（参与神经保护的脑细胞）中胆固醇的显著积累。“这种积累会随着时间推移而增加，这就是我们认为疾病会进展的原因，”马弗尔斯博士说，“因为胆固醇的积累会导致线粒体功能障碍，进而导致能量产生减少。”

研究人员与生物技术公司Perlara合作，通过筛选FDA批准的药物和维生素来寻找潜在的新疗法，并发现NMN能够拯救DHDDS相关疾病的酵母模型。他们在迷你大脑中测试了这种维生素，并注意到了显著的改善。由于这种形式的维生素B3无需处方即可购买，所以在实验完成之前，人们就已经开始在线订购了。

“在一个月内，我们注意到这些患者的行走能力有所改善，他们精力更充沛，颤抖减少，动作变得更流畅。这似乎确实减缓了疾病的进展，”马弗尔斯博士说。

NMN已被证明能够改善线粒体疾病患者肌肉细胞的分子机制。线粒体疾病是一种常见且毁灭性的儿科代谢性疾病，NMN目前正在这些患者中进行临床测试。高剂量的这种维生素也被证明能减缓帕金森病患者的进展并减轻症状负担。鉴于维生素B3和NMN在细胞水平上能诱导多种积极效应，其他影响大脑能量产生的遗传性代谢紊乱也可能从该治疗中受益。

“NMN的消息传到了更多DHDDS患者耳中，我们目前有12名患者正在服用。我们正在启动一项针对DHDDS相关疾病的NMN补充剂国际试验，”马弗尔斯博士说。“患者将服用NMN一年，并每三个月进行一次评估。尽管我现在已经离开了美国，但我希望乌得勒支的威廉敏娜医院能成为官方站点之一，我也能继续与这些患者合作。”

“前方仍有漫长的道路，但令人鼓舞的是，创建迷你大脑如何帮助我们模拟患者的疾病进展——我们真的可以在显微镜下看到大脑正在瓦解。我们惊讶于NMN的作用如此之快、效果如此之好，尤其令人高兴的是它易于获得、价格低廉且没有已知的副作用。在一些患者中，治疗后甚至看不出他们受到DHDDS疾病的影响。现在，首批四名患者已入组试验，我们期待未来能够进一步帮助他们及其家人。”

未参与这项研究的会议主席亚历山大·雷蒙德教授（Professor Alexandre Reymond）表示：“这项研究完美地展示了基因诊断的快速进展如何能够为罕见病带来新疗法。由于DHDDS等罕见病影响的人数很少，通常很难引起行业的兴趣。令人印象深刻的是，家长、慈善机构和学术界组成的统一战线能够找到这种有前景的、同时又廉价且易于获得的疗法。”
