癌症患者在塑造肿瘤学生成式人工智能方面需要更有力的话语权，一项综述发现。

People affected by cancer need a stronger voice in shaping generative AI for oncology, review finds

深度解析

这篇新闻深度探讨了生成式人工智能（Generative AI）在肿瘤学领域应用的现状与未来发展方向。核心概念在于，尽管生成式AI在提升癌症患者健康信息可及性和可读性方面展现出潜力，但目前的临床研究与应用实践存在显著的“患者参与设计”空白。文章强调，将AI工具融入癌症护理，不仅仅是技术层面的实现，更需从患者、幸存者及照护者的视角出发，共同塑造其设计、治理与使用框架。 目前的研究未能充分评估患者对AI的隐私担忧、期望契合度、安全性、透明度、公平性及自主权维护等关键伦理和社会维度。这种缺失可能导致AI工具与实际临床需求脱节，影响其采纳率和有效性。对行业的深远影响在于，它警示了在推广AI医疗应用时，必须超越单纯的技术效能评估，转而采纳以人为本的“共创”模式。未来的研究和产品开发应将患者知情同意、情感适宜性、个性化反馈、以及健全的问责和监管机制纳入考量，确保生成式AI不仅能优化临床路径，更能尊重并赋能患者，从而实现真正负责任且有效的AI医疗转型。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain Flinders University researchers were part of an international team that conducted a scoping review that identified 2,441 records and found that just 32 studies examined how cancer patients, survivors and caregivers feel about generative AI being used in their care. The study is published in the European Journal of Cancer. People affected by cancer were generally favorable when generative AI improved the accessibility and readability of health information. However, most studies focused on providing cancer information or simplifying clinical reports, leaving many other potential uses of generative AI in cancer care unexplored. "Designing AI for patients is not the same as designing it with them," says Dr. Bradley Menz, a research fellow in the College of Medicine and Public Health at Flinders University. "If these tools are going to influence cancer care, then patients, survivors and caregivers need a meaningful role in shaping how they are designed, governed and used. "In addition, future research needs to broaden the application contexts for responsible and effective AI implementation." The research revealed that important aspects of generative AI engagement were assessed only intermittently in previous studies, including cancer patients' willingness to use AI and concerns regarding privacy, alignment with expectations and familiarity. The review found that trust in generative AI often depended on whether its responses were personalized, emotionally appropriate and perceived as accurate. Major gaps in what researchers had asked people affected by cancer were also revealed. Of the 32 studies, accountability perspectives were examined in only three studies, and no studies assessed perspectives on safety, transparency, equity or the preservation of human autonomy. "Gaining perspectives from cancer patients might help inform the design and implementation of generative AI that best serves oncology patients, including improved interface design, patient education, consent processes, workflow integration, governance, monitoring and feedback mechanisms," says Associate Professor Ashley Hopkins from the College of Medicine and Public Health at Flinders University. "Future studies need to assess perspectives with greater conceptual breadth and depth. The current literature focused most often on comprehension, usefulness, relevance, trust, accuracy and clarity." The researchers hope their review paves the way for more specific and detailed examination of generative AI in oncology—including how the perspectives of people affected by cancer may influence uptake, engagement, effectiveness and concerns about the broader impacts of AI use.
