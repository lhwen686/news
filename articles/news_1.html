Shared meiosis proteins show surprising functional differences across animals

深度解析

这篇研究深入探讨了减数分裂（meiosis）这一生殖细胞形成核心过程的分子机制及其在进化中的保守性与柔性。减数分裂是确保遗传物质正确传递的关键，其异常会导致人类不孕不育、流产和出生缺陷。文章的核心发现是，尽管不同物种（如人类与海葵）间减数分裂的分子机器——特别是联会复合体（synaptonemal complex）——在结构上高度相似，但其特定蛋白质的功能却可能在进化过程中发生显著分化。例如，海葵中某些联会复合体蛋白（如syce2）的突变仅导致雌性不育，揭示了减数分裂调控的性别特异性差异和功能柔性。这项研究的专业意义在于：首先，它挑战了“结构相似则功能相似”的传统观念，为理解蛋白质功能进化提供了新视角。其次，通过引入透明的活体模式生物海葵（Nematostella），研究人员得以直接在活体组织中观察减数分裂过程，克服了传统哺乳动物模型在实时动态观察上的局限，为深入探究染色体行为和细胞机制提供了强大的技术平台。这些发现不仅深化了我们对基础生物学的认识，也为未来临床上诊断和治疗人类生殖障碍（如不明原因不孕）提供了新的思路，强调在开发干预措施时需考虑减数分裂蛋白功能在不同物种甚至不同性别间的细微差异，为精准医疗奠定基础。

英文原文

Original
Every egg cell and sperm cell depends on meiosis, a tightly choreographed process in which chromosomes find their partners, exchange genetic material, and then divide. When meiosis goes awry in humans, the consequences can include infertility, miscarriage, and birth defects. A new study from the Stowers Institute for Medical Research, published in Science Advances, shows that the molecular machinery guiding this process is more widely shared across animals than expected, and more flexible. The surprise was that familiar-looking parts were not always doing the same jobs, revealing an unexpected flexibility in how meiosis works across evolution. "That was one of the broader insights from the paper," said Stowers Investigator and Graduate School President Matt Gibson, Ph.D. "Proteins and protein complexes may look very much the same, but nevertheless function in different ways." The project was led by Stefanie "Steffi" Williams, Ph.D., a Stowers Graduate School alum who is currently a postdoctoral fellow at the European Molecular Biology Laboratory. At the Institute, her work grew from a collaboration between Gibson's lab and the lab of the late R. Scott Hawley, Ph.D., a former Stowers Investigator and a pioneer in the field of meiosis. In this latest study, Williams found that the sea anemone assembles a ladder-like structure called the synaptonemal complex between paired chromosomes, much as humans do. This structure contains eight of the ten proteins known to make up the synaptonemal complex in humans. But when Williams tested what some of those proteins actually do, the similarities began to break down. "Despite their essential function, many of the proteins that coordinate meiosis show signatures of rapid evolution, changing much faster than normal housekeeping genes do," said Williams. "So, I was fascinated to find older research showing that cnidarian genomes encode proteins clearly similar to those in humans, and I had to know whether they truly function in the same way." When Williams disabled three of these proteins, the results were unexpected. Mutations in two of them, sycp1 and syce2, caused female sterility. Males carrying the same mutations remained fertile, and loss of syce2 disrupted the stabilization of paired chromosomes only in females. "When I first sat at the microscope and saw that some components are not required to synapse chromosomes, I thought I must have made a mistake because it didn't make any sense," said Williams. "Finding out that the architectural functions of synaptonemal complex proteins must have changed was totally unexpected." Until now, functional studies of these proteins in animals were largely limited to mice, fruit flies, and roundworms. Because Nematostella is transparent and its eight gonads can be observed directly in live animals, it offers researchers something those traditional models often cannot: the potential to watch meiosis unfold in living tissue. That could make it possible to ask questions about chromosome behavior that are difficult or impossible to address in mammals. "This work establishes Nematostella as a good experimental system for understanding meiotic processes at a deeper level," said Gibson. "The potential for live imaging could reveal new biology that's difficult to observe in mammals, particularly at the cellular level." As a member of the Stowers Graduate School, Williams first rotated in the Hawley Lab, studying meiosis in the fruit fly Drosophila. Williams then rotated into the Gibson Lab and began to work with Nematostella, a sea anemone and a relative of corals and jellyfish that is the focus of Gibson's research. "Scott inspired Steffi's passion for meiosis," said Gibson. "When she began working on sea anemones, she became interested in how it worked in these organisms where it hadn't been explored before. This led to a collaboration between labs, and I felt lucky to benefit all along the way from Scott's immense knowledge and Steffi's excitement about the topic, which ultimately led to this new area of research." "Scott taught me to follow my passion regardless of what others may think of it, as well as the importance of teaching and good mentorship," said Williams. "To this day, Scott inspires me to become a better scientist and human being."

每个卵细胞和精子细胞都依赖于减数分裂，这是一个严密编排的过程，其中染色体寻找配对，交换遗传物质，然后分裂。当人类减数分裂出现异常时，可能导致不孕不育、流产和出生缺陷。 斯托尔斯医学研究所（Stowers Institute for Medical Research）发表在《科学进展》（Science Advances）杂志上的一项新研究表明，指导这一过程的分子机制在动物界中的共享程度比预期更广泛，并且更具柔性。令人惊讶的是，那些看似熟悉的组分并非总是在执行相同的功能，这揭示了减数分裂在进化过程中运作方式的意想不到的柔性。 “这是论文中更广泛的见解之一，”斯托尔斯研究所研究员兼研究生院院长马特·吉布森博士（Matt Gibson, Ph.D.）说，“蛋白质和蛋白质复合物可能看起来非常相似，但其功能却截然不同。” 该项目由斯特凡妮·“斯特菲”·威廉姆斯博士（Stefanie "Steffi" Williams, Ph.D.）领导，她是斯托尔斯研究生院的校友，目前在欧洲分子生物学实验室担任博士后研究员。在研究所，她的工作源于吉布森实验室与已故R.斯科特·霍利博士（R. Scott Hawley, Ph.D.）实验室之间的合作，霍利博士曾是斯托尔斯研究所的研究员，也是减数分裂领域的先驱。 在这项最新研究中，威廉姆斯发现海葵会在配对染色体之间组装一个梯状结构，称为联会复合体（synaptonemal complex），这与人类的情况非常相似。这种结构包含了已知构成人类联会复合体的十种蛋白质中的八种。但当威廉姆斯测试其中一些蛋白质的实际功能时，相似性开始瓦解。 威廉姆斯说：“尽管它们具有基本功能，但许多协调减数分裂的蛋白质都表现出快速进化的特征，其变化速度远快于正常的持家基因。”“因此，我着迷于发现早期研究表明刺胞动物基因组编码的蛋白质与人类蛋白质明显相似，我必须知道它们是否真的以相同的方式发挥作用。” 当威廉姆斯使这三种蛋白质失活时，结果出乎意料。其中两种蛋白质，sycp1和syce2的突变导致雌性不育。携带相同突变的雄性仍保持生育能力，而syce2的缺失仅在雌性中破坏了配对染色体的稳定性。 威廉姆斯说：“当我第一次坐在显微镜前，看到有些组分不需要与染色体联会时，我以为自己一定搞错了，因为这完全不合常理。”“发现联会复合体蛋白质的结构功能一定发生了改变，这是完全出乎意料的。” 迄今为止，动物中这些蛋白质的功能研究主要限于小鼠、果蝇和线虫。由于海葵（Nematostella）是透明的，其八个生殖腺可以直接在活体动物中观察到，它为研究人员提供了传统模型通常无法提供的优势：在活体组织中观察减数分裂展开的可能性。这使得研究染色体行为中那些在哺乳动物中难以或不可能解决的问题成为可能。 吉布森说：“这项工作将海葵（Nematostella）确立为一个良好的实验系统，用于更深入地理解减数分裂过程。”“活体成像的潜力可以揭示在哺乳动物中难以观察到的新生物学现象，尤其是在细胞层面。” 作为斯托尔斯研究生院的一员，威廉姆斯最初在霍利实验室轮转，研究果蝇（Drosophila）中的减数分裂。随后，威廉姆斯轮转到吉布森实验室，并开始研究海葵（Nematostella）。海葵是珊瑚和水母的近亲，也是吉布森研究的重点。 吉布森说：“斯科特激发了斯特菲对减数分裂的热情。”“当她开始研究海葵时，她对这种以前从未被探索过的生物体中的减数分裂机制产生了兴趣。这促成了实验室间的合作，我感到很幸运，一路受益于斯科特渊博的知识和斯特菲对这个主题的热情，最终开辟了这个新的研究领域。” 威廉姆斯说：“斯科特教导我，要跟随自己的热情，不顾他人的看法，也教导了我教学和良好指导的重要性。”“至今，斯科特仍激励我成为一名更优秀的科学家和更优秀的人。”
