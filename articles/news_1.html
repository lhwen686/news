深度解析

AlphaGenome Atlas代表了人工智能在基因组学和基因调控领域的一个里程碑式进步。该工具由谷歌DeepMind与斯托尔斯医学研究所等机构合作开发，旨在通过整合并预测数十亿个单碱基DNA变异的分子效应，加速对人类基因组的理解。其核心专业概念在于，利用先进的AI框架（如BPNet和PISA）解析复杂的DNA序列，识别被称为“调控基序”的功能元件，并预测它们如何通过转录因子调控基因的开启与关闭。这解决了传统实验方法在分析基因组海量变异和调控模式时所面临的时间和资源限制。通过提供一个可搜索的、涵盖了数百万细胞类型数据的资源，AlphaGenome Atlas能够帮助科学家快速识别潜在的致病变异，理解基因调控的普遍规则，并生成如AVI评分这样的综合指标，用于评估变异对基因表达、RNA剪接和蛋白质功能等关键分子过程的影响。其行业影响深远，不仅能极大加速基础生物学研究，更能赋能精准医疗，通过优先筛选最有前景的变异和生物机制，从而优化药物研发、疾病诊断及治疗策略，最终改善人类健康。

英文原文

Original
The human genome contains approximately 3 billion DNA letters, creating more than 9 billion possible single-letter changes. Testing the effects of each change in a laboratory would be practically impossible. Google DeepMind's new AlphaGenome Atlas, available beginning today, gives scientists a comprehensive, searchable resource designed to accelerate understanding of the human genome. Stowers Institute for Medical Research Investigator Julia Zeitlinger, Ph.D., partnered with the Google DeepMind team led by Vice President of Science and Chief Scientist Žiga Avsec, Ph.D., to map and interpret the patterns in DNA that regulate biological processes inside cells. At the same time, additional scientific collaborators from leading institutions across the United States and England helped test how the new resource could be used to identify impactful genetic variation in humans. The work is now available as a preprint on bioRxV. The one-petabyte resource contains artificial intelligence-generated predictions for the molecular effects of more than 9 billion possible changes, creating what Google DeepMind describes as the most comprehensive catalogue of its kind. Zeitlinger has made significant contributions to the field of gene regulation and computational biology. In 2019, in an international collaboration that included Avsec, Zeitlinger and her team at the Stowers Institute developed a powerfulAI framework, BPNet.This framework is now widely used to extract and dissect the DNA sequences that explain genome-wide biological data. Just last month, her lab unveiled anew AI method, PISA,which generates high-resolution visualizations of what AI models have learned from DNA. Stowers Institute Bioinformatics Scientist and Zeitlinger Lab member, Melanie Weilert, served as a lead author on the AlphaGenome project. With her deep expertise in interpretating AI models, she helped build the AlphaGenome Atlas resource to ask one of biology's biggest questions: How does a cell know which genes to turn on and off? "This is a very difficult problem because every cell type speaks a slightly different language, making it hard to know which rules are general," Zeitlinger said. "With AlphaGenome, we can quickly query many cell types and look for general patterns by which genes are activated and repressed." Google DeepMind developed the technology behind the Atlas. Zeitlinger, who also leads the Stowers Institute's AI Initiative, helped connect its predictions to the biological processes that give cells their identities and allow them to function. AlphaGenome Atlas is a powerful example of how AI can expand human knowledge and advance scientific discovery. By making this resource widely available, we hope scientists around the world can use it to better understand the language of life and what happens when individual letters in the human genome change." Pushmeet Kohli, Ph.D., VP Science, Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist, Google Cloud Scientists can access the resource through a web browser without writing code, allowing more researchers to explore genetic variation at a scale that was not previously possible. "AlphaGenome Atlas is foundational research with the potential to have an impact across multiple areas of biology," said Avsec. "We worked with experts in the field, including Julia, whose biological insight helped us explore how the resource can map functional elements in the genome and reveal their roles at the molecular level." How Stowers scientists helped reveal the regulatory "words" of the genome Every cell in the human body contains essentially the same DNA, yet different cells use that information in very different ways. Short DNA sequences called motifs act as regulatory instructions, helping control which genes are active, when they are activated and how strongly they operate. Zeitlinger and her team used AlphaGenome Atlas to analyze regulatory motifs across the genome and determine what they reveal about the proteins, called transcription factors, that control gene activity. The researchers categorized these regulatory signals by function, distinguishing transcription factors that change whether DNA is accessible from those that also activate or repress genes. Conducting this type of analysis experimentally across thousands of sites and many different cell types would require enormous time and resources. By making genome-wide predictions available in one searchable resource, the Atlas allowed Zeitlinger's team to identify broader patterns in how genes are regulated and begin defining the general rules underlying the regulatory language of DNA. "Having these motifs mapped at base-pair resolution across the genome and in many cell types gives us a searchable dictionary for non-coding DNA," Zeitlinger said. "By giving the scientific community access to these predictions, AlphaGenome Atlas can accelerate how we identify potentially disease-causing variants while helping us understand the fundamental rules by which genes are regulated." "This collaboration demonstrates how Stowers scientists are helping shape emerging technologies, not simply adopting them," said Stowers Institute President and Chief Scientific Officer Alejandro Sánchez Alvarado, Ph.D. "By pairing deep biological knowledge with the capabilities of AI, researchers can ask questions at a scale that was not previously possible and create new opportunities to more clearly understand human health and disease." The Atlas does not replace laboratory research. Instead, it can help scientists determine which variants and biological mechanisms should be investigated first, focusing experimental time and resources on the most promising questions. "Tools such as AlphaGenome Atlas become most valuable when their predictions can be connected to meaningful biological questions," said Stowers Institute Scientific Director Kausik Si, Ph.D." Julia's work brings together deep expertise in gene regulation and computational biology to help move us from simply reading DNA sequence toward understanding the rules that control gene activity." From billions of variants to focused biological questions AlphaGenome Atlas contains thousands of molecular-effect predictions for each variant across hundreds of human cell types and tissues. These predictions contribute to the new AlphaGenome Variant Impact, or AVI, score. They also enabled researchers to identify and map recurring DNA motifs, short sequences where transcription factors bind to help control gene activity. The AVI score brings together predictions from AlphaGenome, AlphaMissense and evolutionary conservation data. It gives researchers a single measure for ranking variants by their potential impact across protein-coding and non-coding regions of the genome. Researchers can then examine which molecular processes, including gene expression, RNA splicing and protein function, are predicted to be affected. The collaborating institutions explored how the resource could support several areas of human genetic research. Scientists at the Broad Institute used the AVI score to prioritize a previously overlooked non-coding variant associated with an unsolved rare disease case. At the University of Exeter, researchers applied Atlas to genomic data from more than 54,000 UK Biobank participants, uncovering additional associations between rare noncoding variants and protein levels.

人类基因组包含大约30亿个DNA碱基对，可能产生超过90亿种单碱基变化。在实验室中测试每种变化的影响几乎是不可能完成的任务。谷歌DeepMind今天发布的全新AlphaGenome Atlas，为科学家提供了一个全面、可搜索的资源，旨在加速对人类基因组的理解。 斯托尔斯医学研究所研究员Julia Zeitlinger博士与谷歌DeepMind团队（由科学副总裁兼首席科学家Žiga Avsec博士领导）合作，绘制并解释了调节细胞内生物过程的DNA模式。与此同时，来自美国和英国顶尖机构的其他科学合作者也协助测试了该新资源如何用于识别人类中具有影响力的遗传变异。这项工作现已作为预印本在bioRxV上发布。 这份1拍字节（petabyte）的资源包含了人工智能对超过90亿种可能变化的分子效应的预测，构建了谷歌DeepMind所称的同类中最全面的目录。 Zeitlinger博士在基因调控和计算生物学领域做出了重大贡献。2019年，在与Avsec博士的国际合作中，Zeitlinger博士和她在斯托尔斯研究所的团队开发了一个强大的人工智能框架BPNet。该框架现已广泛用于提取和剖析解释全基因组生物学数据的DNA序列。就在上个月，她的实验室推出了一种新的人工智能方法PISA，能够生成人工智能模型从DNA中学到的内容的高分辨率可视化。 斯托尔斯研究所生物信息学科学家、Zeitlinger实验室成员Melanie Weilert担任AlphaGenome项目的主要作者。凭借她在解释人工智能模型方面的深厚专业知识，她帮助构建了AlphaGenome Atlas资源，旨在解决生物学领域最大的问题之一：细胞如何知道哪些基因应该开启和关闭？ “这是一个非常困难的问题，因为每种细胞类型都使用略有不同的‘语言’，这使得很难了解哪些规则是通用的，”Zeitlinger博士说。“有了AlphaGenome，我们可以快速查询许多细胞类型，寻找基因激活和抑制的普遍模式。” 谷歌DeepMind开发了Atlas背后的技术。Zeitlinger博士同时也是斯托尔斯研究所人工智能计划的负责人，她帮助将这些预测与赋予细胞特性并使其发挥功能的生物过程联系起来。 “AlphaGenome Atlas是人工智能如何拓展人类知识和推动科学发现的一个强大范例。通过广泛提供这一资源，我们希望世界各地的科学家能够利用它更好地理解生命的语言，以及人类基因组中单个‘字母’发生变化时会发生什么。”谷歌DeepMind科学副总裁兼谷歌云首席科学家Pushmeet Kohli博士表示。 科学家可以通过网络浏览器访问该资源，无需编写代码，这使得更多研究人员能够以前所未有的规模探索遗传变异。 “AlphaGenome Atlas是一项基础性研究，有潜力对生物学的多个领域产生影响，”Avsec博士说。“我们与该领域的专家（包括Julia）合作，她的生物学洞察力帮助我们探索了该资源如何绘制基因组中的功能元件并揭示其在分子水平上的作用。” 斯托尔斯科学家如何帮助揭示基因组的调控“词汇” 人体内的每个细胞都含有基本相同的DNA，但不同的细胞以非常不同的方式使用这些信息。被称为基序的短DNA序列作为调控指令，帮助控制哪些基因活跃、何时被激活以及作用强度。 Zeitlinger博士和她的团队使用AlphaGenome Atlas分析了基因组中的调控基序，并确定它们揭示了什么关于控制基因活性的蛋白质，即转录因子。研究人员根据功能对这些调控信号进行了分类，区分了改变DNA可及性的转录因子和那些同时激活或抑制基因的转录因子。 在数千个位点和许多不同细胞类型中进行这类实验分析，将需要大量时间和资源。通过将全基因组预测汇集到一个可搜索的资源中，Atlas使Zeitlinger博士的团队能够识别基因调控的更广泛模式，并开始定义DNA调控语言背后的普遍规则。 “在全基因组和许多细胞类型中以碱基对分辨率绘制这些基序，为我们提供了一本可搜索的非编码DNA词典，”Zeitlinger博士说。“通过让科学界访问这些预测，AlphaGenome Atlas可以加速我们识别潜在致病变异的速度，同时帮助我们理解基因调控的基本规则。” “这项合作表明斯托尔斯科学家如何帮助塑造新兴技术，而不仅仅是采用它们，”斯托尔斯研究所所长兼首席科学官Alejandro Sánchez Alvarado博士说。“通过将深厚的生物学知识与人工智能的能力相结合，研究人员能够以以前不可能的规模提出问题，并创造新的机会更清晰地理解人类健康和疾病。” Atlas不能取代实验室研究。相反，它可以帮助科学家确定应该首先研究哪些变异和生物学机制，从而将实验时间和资源集中在最有前景的问题上。 “当像AlphaGenome Atlas这样的工具的预测能够与有意义的生物学问题联系起来时，它们才能发挥最大的价值，”斯托尔斯研究所科学主任Kausik Si博士说。“Julia的工作汇集了基因调控和计算生物学的深厚专业知识，帮助我们从简单地读取DNA序列转向理解控制基因活性的规则。” 从数十亿个变异到聚焦的生物学问题 AlphaGenome Atlas包含数百种人类细胞和组织中每个变异的数千个分子效应预测。这些预测构成了新的AlphaGenome变异影响（AVI）评分。它们还使研究人员能够识别和绘制重复出现的DNA基序，即转录因子结合以帮助控制基因活动的短序列。 AVI评分整合了来自AlphaGenome、AlphaMissense和进化保守性数据中的预测。它为研究人员提供了一个单一指标，用于根据变异对基因组中蛋白质编码区和非编码区的潜在影响进行排序。研究人员随后可以检查哪些分子过程（包括基因表达、RNA剪接和蛋白质功能）预计会受到影响。 合作机构探索了该资源如何支持人类遗传学研究的几个领域。博德研究所的科学家使用AVI评分优先研究了一个先前被忽视的、与一个未解决的罕见疾病病例相关的非编码变异。在埃克塞特大学，研究人员将Atlas应用于来自54,000多名英国生物样本库参与者的基因组数据，揭示了罕见非编码变异与蛋白质水平之间的额外关联。
