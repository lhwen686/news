Targeted lead placement is no better than standard placement in cardiac resynchronization therapy

深度解析

DANISH-CRT试验的研究结果对心力衰竭患者心脏再同步治疗（CRT）的临床实践具有重要指导意义。CRT是一种通过精确同步心室收缩来改善左束支传导阻滞（LBBB）引起的心脏功能障碍的治疗方法。其核心医学机制在于通过双心室起搏纠正异常的心室激动顺序，优化心室同步性，从而提高射血分数，缓解心力衰竭症状。本研究的焦点在于左心室导线的置入策略。此前，观察性研究提示将导线置入心室最晚激动部位可能带来更好的临床结局，这引发了关于电生理标测辅助靶向置入导线的探讨。 然而，DANISH-CRT这项大型随机对照试验（RCT）——医学研究中最高级别的证据——明确指出，与标准置入方法相比，靶向置入策略并未改善死亡或因心力衰竭导致的非计划住院这一主要临床终点。更重要的是，靶向组还出现了数量上更多的导线相关并发症。 这一发现的行业影响是深远的。首先，它可能改变临床指南，不再常规推荐在CRT植入过程中进行复杂的电生理标测以靶向置入左心室导线，从而简化手术流程，可能降低手术时间和相关并发症风险。其次，对于医疗器械研发而言，这意味着未来CRT装置的创新方向可能不再侧重于更精密的导线置入定位技术，而可能转向优化起搏算法、开发新型导线材料或非侵入性调整方案，以提高整体应答率。最后，这项研究也为未来的科研指明了方向，即需要更深入地探索CRT无应答患者的异质性，以识别出能从CRT中最大程度获益的特定患者亚组，而不是统一地改进导线置入技术。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain Targeted placement of the left ventricular lead did not improve outcomes in patients with heart failure undergoing cardiac resynchronization therapy, according to results presented in a Hot Line session at ESC Congress 2026 and published simultaneously in The Lancet. Cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) is a type of pacemaker treatment for some people with heart failure who have conduction abnormalities such as left bundle branch block (LBBB). It sends carefully timed electrical signals to the heart so that the heart's lower chambers beat together more effectively. "While CRT improves symptoms, quality of life and survival in the majority of patients, up to one-third do not experience any benefit," explained principal investigator Jens Cosedis Nielsen of Aarhus University Hospital, Denmark. "Observational data have shown that measuring a late electrical signal from the left ventricular lead is associated with better outcomes. However, whether a strategy of targeting placement of the left ventricular lead at the latest activated site improves outcomes has never been investigated in a large randomized controlled trial," he noted. The DANISH-CRT trial was an investigator-initiated, double-blind trial conducted in all five Danish university centers performing CRT device implantations. The trial included 1,000 patients who had heart failure and LBBB on guideline-directed medication and were referred for biventricular pacing. Patients were randomized to left ventricular lead placement targeted to the site of latest electrical activation within the coronary sinus branches or to standard lead placement (in the posterolateral, nonapical coronary sinus). After a median of 45.8 months of follow-up, the primary endpoint of death or first unplanned hospitalization for heart failure occurred in similar proportions of patients with targeted lead placement and standard lead placement (27.9% vs. 25.5%; hazard ratio 1.10; 95% confidence interval 0.86 to 1.39; p=0.45). Consistently similar findings were observed across secondary endpoints, including structural changes to the left ventricle, quality of life, functional status and physical capacity, as well as in subgroup analyses of the primary endpoint. Numerically, more lead-related complications were observed in the targeted lead placement group. "Our findings do not support routine electrical mapping to target left ventricular lead placement in contemporary CRT practice," Cosedis Nielsen said. "We will continue to analyze data from the DANISH-CRT trial to investigate which patient subgroups benefited most from CRT." Publication details Jens Cosedis Nielsen et al, Targeted left ventricular lead placement in biventricular pacing for heart failure: a national, multicentre, double-blind, randomised controlled trial in Denmark, The Lancet (2026).
