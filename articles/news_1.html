在实际就诊前与AI医生交流，可提高癌症患者的理解并减轻压力

Meeting an AI doctor before a real-life consultation can improve cancer patients' understanding and reduce stress

Cancer patients who interact with an artificial intelligence (AI) avatar doctor before they meet their real-life consultant feel more knowledgeable and less stressed, according to research presented at the Congress of the European Society for Radiotherapy and Oncology (ESTRO 2026).

The study was presented by Dr. Adam Raben, Chair of Radiation Oncology at the Helen F Graham Cancer Center & Research Institute at Christiana Care in Newark, Delaware, U.S.

He said, "We know that patients' understanding of cancer treatments, like radiotherapy, is essential in making sure they can give informed consent. It also improves patient satisfaction and makes it more likely that they will stick to and complete their course of treatment. Despite doctors' best efforts, patients often arrive at consultations overwhelmed, anxious and unable to comprehend and retain complex information—particularly in radiation oncology, where treatment concepts can be technically complex.

"We wanted to explore whether meeting an AI avatar who looked and sounded like a doctor before their main consultation could allow patients to be more prepared to meet their doctor in real life, helping them to make better decisions about their treatment and reducing their stress."

The team worked with a digital technology company to create an AI avatar of a doctor designed to help patients understand about radiation treatment options before meeting their real doctors.

They recruited 1,464 patients; one group of 506 (34.6%) watched an educational video and the other 958 (65.4%) watched an avatar-based video that used personalized scripts and illustrations to explain the concepts. All patients then completed a multiple-choice quiz with feedback to confirm that they had understood and retained the material, followed by a standardized satisfaction survey.

The patients who watched the personalized AI avatar videos showed better understanding of their treatment plan, a greater ability to engage with health care decisions, and reduced stress compared to those who watched the standard educational video. Their hospital satisfaction scores also dramatically improved.

Dr. Raben explains, "We found that patients were very willing to engage with digital educational material before their first radiation oncology visit. Their satisfaction and understanding scores were high overall, but particularly high among those who viewed the AI-avatar-based videos. All the patients completed the quiz, which shows that they were actively engaged with the material, not just watching it."

The team now plans to expand the use of the avatar throughout the treatment journey and assess its impact on anxiety, patients' confidence in decision making, and how consultations can be run most efficiently.

ESTRO President Professor Matthias Guckenberger, from University Hospital Zurich, Switzerland, who was not involved in the research, said, "AI is already being used to plan and deliver radiotherapy, reducing the burden on health care systems, and we know that patients are already using AI to ask about their condition and treatment. Dr. Raben's study is one of the earliest implementations of AI-avatar-based patient education being tested out in the clinic, not in a simulated or academic-only environment.

"For people with cancer, the study suggests that engaging with an AI doctor enables patients to arrive at their consultation with a real doctor better prepared, less anxious and more confident in asking informed questions. This should mean that meetings between doctors and their patients will be more productive and more focused on the patient's individual concerns and decision-making. The research also suggests that patients will feel happier overall with the care they receive."

根据在欧洲放射治疗与肿瘤学学会大会（ESTRO 2026）上发布的研究，癌症患者在会见其真实医生之前，如果与人工智能（AI）虚拟医生互动，会感到知识更丰富、压力更小。

这项研究由美国特拉华州纽瓦克市克里斯蒂安娜护理机构海伦·F·格雷厄姆癌症中心与研究所放射肿瘤科主任亚当·拉本博士发表。

他表示：“我们深知，患者对放射治疗等癌症疗法的理解，对于确保他们能够给出知情同意至关重要。这也能提升患者满意度，并增加他们坚持并完成治疗疗程的可能性。尽管医生们已尽力而为，但患者在就诊时常常感到不堪重负、焦虑不安，难以理解和记住复杂的信息——尤其是在放射肿瘤学领域，治疗概念可能涉及技术复杂性。”

“我们希望探索，在主要咨询前，与一个外形和声音都像医生的AI虚拟医生会面，是否能让患者为与真实医生的会面做好更充分的准备，从而帮助他们就治疗做出更好的决策并减轻压力。”

该团队与一家数字技术公司合作，创建了一个AI虚拟医生，旨在帮助患者在会见真实医生之前了解放射治疗方案。

他们招募了1,464名患者；其中一组506名（占34.6%）观看了教育视频，另一组958名（占65.4%）观看了基于虚拟医生的视频，该视频使用个性化脚本和插图来解释相关概念。所有患者随后都完成了一项带有反馈的多项选择题测验，以确认他们已理解并记住了这些材料，之后还完成了一项标准化满意度调查。

与观看标准教育视频的患者相比，观看个性化AI虚拟医生视频的患者对治疗计划表现出更好的理解，参与医疗决策的能力更强，并且压力更小。他们的医院满意度评分也显著提高。

拉本博士解释说：“我们发现患者非常乐意在首次放射肿瘤科就诊前参与数字教育材料的学习。他们的整体满意度和理解度评分都很高，尤其是在那些观看了基于AI虚拟医生视频的患者中得分更高。所有患者都完成了测验，这表明他们是积极主动地参与学习这些材料，而不仅仅是观看。”

该团队现在计划在整个治疗过程中推广使用该虚拟医生，并评估其对患者焦虑、决策信心以及如何最有效地进行咨询的影响。

欧洲放射治疗与肿瘤学学会主席、来自瑞士苏黎世大学医院的马蒂亚斯·古肯伯格教授（未参与此项研究）表示：“人工智能已被用于规划和实施放射治疗，从而减轻了医疗系统的负担，我们也知道患者已经在利用人工智能询问他们的病情和治疗情况。拉本博士的研究是AI虚拟医生辅助患者教育在临床环境中进行测试的最早实践之一，而非仅仅在模拟或学术环境中。”

“对于癌症患者而言，这项研究表明，与AI医生互动能够让患者在与真实医生会诊时准备更充分、焦虑感更低，并更有信心地提出知情问题。这意味着医生与患者之间的会面将更具成效，更专注于患者的个体关注点和决策过程。研究还表明，患者将对他们所获得的护理整体上感到更满意。”
