酸味如何盖过其他味道

How sourness overpowers other kinds of tastes

深度解析

这项研究显著提升了我们对味觉感知的理解，表明味蕾并非简单的信息传递器，而是具备主动整合和调节味觉信号能力的复杂单元。核心专业概念在于揭示了“外周抑制”机制：酸味刺激通过味蕾内特定细胞释放神经递质血清素，能在味觉信息传递至大脑之前，对甜味、苦味和咸味信号产生抑制效应。这意味着味觉感知在末梢层面就已进行初步筛选和调制。 这一发现具有深远的行业影响和临床意义。在食品科学领域，它可以指导开发创新的风味调节剂，例如通过精确调控酸度或靶向血清素通路，在不牺牲感官体验的前提下降低食品中的糖和盐含量。对于临床医学和营养学，理解血清素在味觉抑制中的作用，为治疗味觉障碍（如化疗引起的味觉改变）和管理饮食偏好提供了新的干预策略。此外，味蕾将味觉信息初步分类为“愉悦”或“不愉悦”的发现，以及对相关神经元“基因特征”的探索，将为基础神经生物学研究提供新方向，并可能为药物研发开辟治疗味觉失调或影响食物选择的新靶点。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain When organisms consume foods or drinks with a mixture of tastes, like sweet and sour, how do their taste buds communicate this taste information to the brain? In a new study in JNeurosci, Stephen Roper and colleagues at the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine advanced understanding by measuring how signals pass from the tongue to the brain in mice during exposure to mixed tastes. By comparing neural responses under different taste conditions, the researchers found that sourness triggered neural activity that dampened responses to sweetness, bitterness and saltiness. The research team further discovered that suppression of neural responses to other tastes by sourness was not limited to a subset of neurons in the pathway from the tongue to the brain and that these neurons essentially categorized tastes into two bins: pleasant or unpleasant. Probing the mechanism for sourness suppression of other tastes, the researchers manipulated levels of a neurotransmitter called serotonin that sour-responsive cells release within each taste bud. Increasing serotonin in taste buds strengthened sourness suppression of other tastes, while depleting serotonin weakened the effect. These findings suggest that sweetness, bitterness and saltiness may be dampened by sourness before taste information reaches neurons. Says Roper, "Taste buds not only respond to and transduce taste compounds; they also integrate and manipulate gustatory signals before transmitting them to the brain. The neurons that collect signals produced in taste buds are themselves organized into two large groups or clusters—neurons that convey gustatory signals associated with pleasure and neurons that convey unpleasant taste signals." According to the researchers, these data may inform future studies probing the "genetic signatures" of these neurons and whether altering these genes can manipulate taste. Publication details Sour Suppresses Sweet, Salty, and Bitter: Implications for Signal Processing in Taste Buds, JNeurosci (2026).
