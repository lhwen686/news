Study uncovers brain mechanism that drives sustained motivation

深度解析

本研究深入剖析了食欲素神经元在调节动机行为中的核心作用，揭示了其在驱动个体持续努力实现目标过程中的神经机制。研究团队巧妙运用化学遗传学、光纤光度测量和光遗传学等前沿神经科学技术，在基因改造的“食欲素-Cre”大鼠模型上进行了精确的神经元操作与实时监测。核心发现表明，食欲素神经元的激活能显著增强动机强度，促使大鼠付出更多努力；而其功能受损则导致动机减退。尤为关键的是，研究揭示了食欲素神经元活动随努力程度增加而增强，并在预期奖励未及时出现时保持活跃，这提供了一个将奖励预期转化为持续行动的神经生物学机制。研究中观察到的不对称性——即抑制食欲素神经元损害动机，但人为过度兴奋它却未能进一步提升动机——具有重要的临床启示。这提示食欲素神经元是维持动机的必要条件，但并非简单的“越多越好”。这一细致入微的理解为抑郁症、成瘾和注意力缺陷多动障碍等神经精神疾病中的动机缺陷提供了潜在的新治疗靶点，可能引导开发出更精确地恢复而非简单刺激特定神经元活动模式的干预策略，从而提升治疗的有效性。

英文原文

Original
What enables us to sustain effort toward goals, even when the task becomes increasingly difficult? A recent study by researchers at Nagoya University in Japan revealed the underlying brain mechanism, showing that orexin neurons play a crucial role in driving and regulating motivated behavior. The findings were published in Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America (PNAS). Motivational deficits, including loss of motivation, are often seen in mental disorders such as depression, addiction and ADHD. However, the brain mechanisms behind these problems remain largely unclear. The research team, led by Hiroyuki Mizoguchi, associate professor, and Kiyofumi Yamada, professor emeritus, at Nagoya University's Graduate School of Medicine, focused on orexin neurons. These neurons regulate essential physiological functions such as sleep, appetite and energy expenditure. Although recent studies suggest that orexin neurons also influence motivation, their exact role has remained unclear. This study used rats to examine how changes in orexin neuron activity influence motivation to obtain food rewards. While most previous studies used mice, rats offer superior learning abilities and are better suited for complex behavioral experiments. Because of technical challenges in targeting specific neurons in rats, research in this area has been limited. To address this limitation, the team developed genetically modified "orexin-Cre" rats, allowing precise targeting and manipulation of orexin-producing neurons. This model was used to investigate how these neurons influence motivation. First, using chemogenetics, the researchers activated the rats' orexin neurons and had them perform a progressive ratio test where the number of touches required to earn a food reward increased with each trial. The point at which a rat gave up (the breakpoint) measured motivation intensity. Rats with activated orexin neurons showed higher breakpoints, meaning they worked harder for the reward. Conversely, in a model where orexin neurons were selectively degenerated, breakpoints were lower, indicating reduced motivation. Next, using fiber photometry, the team recorded real-time activity of orexin neurons as the rats anticipated and received their reward. Activity increased before the reward was obtained, decreased once it was received, and remained elevated when an expected reward failed to arrive. Notably, the more effort required, the stronger the orexin neuron activity became. The researchers say this pattern may reflect how the brain links reward expectation to the effort required to pursue them. To test this causally, the researchers used optogenetics to control orexin neuron activity at the moment a reward was anticipated. When orexin neuron activity was suppressed using an inhibitory protein, the rats' motivated behavior decreased - they took longer to complete effort-based tasks, and their breakpoints dropped. By contrast, when the team attempted to boost orexin neuron activity at that moment using an excitatory protein, no further increase in motivated behavior was observed, even though the stimulation reliably activated the neurons. In other words, suppressing orexin neurons impaired motivation, but artificially exciting them beyond natural levels did not enhance it. The researchers say this asymmetry suggests orexin neurons are necessary for sustaining motivated behavior, though simply raising their activity may not be sufficient to increase it. Further studies are needed to determine what governs this effect, such as the duration or pattern of orexin neuron activity. Our study demonstrated significant changes in orexin neuron activity depending on expected rewards and the effort required, suggesting a potential mechanism for translating expectations into sustained action." Hiroyuki Mizoguchi, associate professor, Nagoya University's Graduate School of Medicine Future research will investigate the input and output circuits connected to orexin neurons. A deeper understanding of orexin function may inform new approaches to addressing motivational deficits, including loss of motivation or challenges in sustaining goal-directed behavior.

AI Translation
我们如何能够在任务变得越来越困难时，仍然持续努力实现目标？日本名古屋大学研究人员最近的一项研究揭示了其潜在的大脑机制，表明食欲素神经元在驱动和调节动机行为中发挥着关键作用。该研究成果发表在《美国国家科学院院刊》（PNAS）上。 动机缺陷，包括动机丧失，常在抑郁症、成瘾和注意力缺陷多动障碍（ADHD）等精神障碍中出现。然而，这些问题背后的大脑机制在很大程度上仍不清楚。 由名古屋大学医学研究生院的副教授沟口博之和名誉教授山田清文领导的研究团队，将研究重点放在食欲素神经元上。这些神经元调节睡眠、食欲和能量消耗等重要的生理功能。尽管近期研究表明食欲素神经元也影响动机，但其确切作用仍不明确。 这项研究使用大鼠来考察食欲素神经元活动的变化如何影响获得食物奖励的动机。虽然大多数先前的研究使用小鼠，但大鼠具有更卓越的学习能力，更适合复杂的行为实验。由于靶向大鼠特定神经元存在技术挑战，该领域的研究一直受到限制。 为解决这一局限性，研究团队开发了基因改造的“食欲素-Cre”大鼠，从而能够精确靶向和操控产生食欲素的神经元。该模型被用于探究这些神经元如何影响动机。 首先，研究人员利用化学遗传学激活了大鼠的食欲素神经元，并让它们进行渐进比率测试，即每次试验中获得食物奖励所需的触碰次数都会增加。大鼠放弃的那个点（拐点）衡量动机强度。食欲素神经元被激活的大鼠显示出更高的拐点，意味着它们为获得奖励付出了更多努力。反之，在食欲素神经元选择性退化的模型中，拐点较低，表明动机减弱。 接下来，研究团队利用光纤光度测量技术，实时记录了大鼠在预期和接收奖励时的食欲素神经元活动。在获得奖励前，活动增加；一旦接收到奖励，活动便减少；当预期奖励未能到来时，活动则保持升高。值得注意的是，所需努力越多，食欲素神经元的活动就越强。研究人员表示，这种模式可能反映了大脑如何将奖励预期与追求奖励所需的努力联系起来。 为了验证其因果关系，研究人员利用光遗传学在预期奖励的那一刻控制食欲素神经元的活动。当使用抑制性蛋白抑制食欲素神经元活动时，大鼠的动机行为减少——它们完成基于努力的任务所需时间更长，且拐点下降。相比之下，当研究团队试图在那一刻使用兴奋性蛋白提升食欲素神经元活动时，尽管刺激可靠地激活了神经元，却未观察到动机行为的进一步增加。 换句话说，抑制食欲素神经元会损害动机，但人为地将其兴奋水平提高到自然水平以上并不能增强动机。研究人员表示，这种不对称性表明食欲素神经元对于维持动机行为是必需的，尽管仅仅提高它们的活动可能不足以增加动机。需要进一步的研究来确定决定这一效应的因素，例如食欲素神经元活动的持续时间或模式。 “我们的研究证明了食欲素神经元活动根据预期奖励和所需努力存在显著变化，这表明了一种将预期转化为持续行动的潜在机制。”名古屋大学医学研究生院副教授沟口博之说。 未来的研究将探究与食欲素神经元相连的输入和输出回路。对食欲素功能更深入的理解，可能为解决动机缺陷提供新方法，包括动机丧失或难以维持目标导向行为的问题。
