Maternal house cleaning behaviors during pregnancy associated with child developmental screening outcomes

深度解析

本研究探讨了妊娠期母亲与室内灰尘相关的清洁行为与儿童早期（18个月至3岁）发育问题之间的关联。核心发现指出，更频繁的清洁行为与儿童发育问题（如沟通、大运动、精细运动、解决问题和个人社交领域）的风险降低存在统计学关联，这在广义线性模型分析中通过校正比值比（AORs）得以量化。然而，文章严谨强调，这仅是一种关联，而非因果关系。 在医学和公共卫生领域，区分关联与因果至关重要。研究人员将清洁行为作为家庭环境管理的“替代指标”，并未直接测量儿童对特定环境污染物（如邻苯二甲酸酯、过敏原、内毒素或颗粒物）的暴露水平。这意味着我们无法确切了解是哪些具体的环境因素或清洁行为背后的机制（例如，减少有害物质暴露、或与清洁行为相关的其他健康生活习惯）导致了这种关联。此外，校正比值比接近1，表明这种关联相对温和。这一限制提示，在没有更具体、客观的环境测量数据和机制解释之前，不能将清洁行为直接等同于降低发育风险的干预措施。未来的研究需通过生物监测或灰尘取样等客观方法，结合多因素分析，以探明潜在的因果路径，从而为制定精确的临床指南和公共卫生建议提供更有力的证据。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: More frequent house dust–related cleaning during pregnancy was associated with lower odds of developmental concerns from 18 months to 3 years, although causality remains uncertain. Credit: Assistant Professor Kanako Shimada, University of Toyama, Japan House dust can contain chemical and biological components, including phthalates, flame retardants, allergens, endotoxins and microorganisms. Because pregnant women spend time indoors, household environmental conditions during pregnancy may be relevant to child development. Earlier research linked maternal cleaning behaviors with infant development up to 12 months, but evidence beyond the first year was limited. The researchers therefore examined whether these behaviors were associated with developmental screening outcomes from 18 months to 3 years of age. To address this gap, a research team led by specially appointed assistant professor Kanako Shimada of the Toyama Regional Center for JECS at the University of Toyama, Japan, conducted the study in collaboration with researchers from the university's Department of Public Health. They analyzed data from 81,125 mother–child pairs participating in the Japan Environment and Children's Study, a nationwide birth cohort. Mothers reported how often they vacuumed living room floors and futons and aired futons during pregnancy, as well as whether they used anti-mite covers for their futons or bedding. Child development was assessed at 1.5, 2.0, 2.5 and 3.0 years using the Japanese Ages and Stages Questionnaires, Third Edition (J-ASQ-3), covering five developmental domains: communication, gross motor, fine motor, problem-solving and personal–social. Generalized linear models were used to estimate adjusted odds ratios (AORs); Model 1 accounted for maternal factors, and Model 2 additionally accounted for socioeconomic and dwelling environment factors. Their findings were published online in the journal BMC Pediatrics on Sept. 3, 2026. Across all four assessment ages, higher frequencies of house dust–related cleaning behaviors were associated with lower odds of screening positive for developmental concerns. In Model 1, adjusted odds ratios ranged from 0.74 to 0.97, while Model 2 produced a range of 0.68 to 0.98. A graded association was observed, with higher frequencies of cleaning-related behaviors associated with progressively lower odds of screening positive for developmental concerns. "Our previous study found an association between maternal cleaning behaviors during pregnancy and child development up to 1 year of age. However, it remained unclear whether these associations extended beyond infancy," says Shimada. "We therefore examined the associations between these behaviors and developmental screening outcomes from 18 months to 3 years of age." The findings may be relevant to household environmental management during pregnancy, particularly behaviors such as regular vacuuming, airing futons and using anti-mite covers. However, the study did not directly measure house dust or environmental contaminants. The cleaning behaviors were used as proxy indicators of household environmental management, so the researchers could not determine whether the associations reflected reduced exposure to chemical contaminants, allergens, particulate matter, microbial components or other aspects of the indoor environment. The observed associations were also modest, with adjusted odds ratios close to unity. "Further research is needed to better understand the factors underlying these associations. Studies using objective measures of the home environment will be particularly important in determining what may explain the observed associations," says Shimada. Importantly, the findings show an association, not a causal relationship, between maternal cleaning behaviors and developmental screening outcomes. Overall, the study extends previous findings by showing that maternal house dust–related cleaning behaviors during pregnancy were associated with developmental screening outcomes across multiple time points from 18 months to 3 years. The results should not be interpreted as evidence that cleaning itself directly reduces developmental risk. Differences in caregiving, health-related behaviors, socioeconomic circumstances, postnatal environments and other unmeasured factors may contribute to the associations. The researchers emphasize the need for studies incorporating objective environmental measurements, such as dust sampling or biomonitoring, as well as replication in diverse cultural and environmental settings, to clarify the underlying mechanisms and generalizability. Publication details Kanako Shimada et al, Maternal house dust–related cleaning behaviors during pregnancy and neurodevelopment from 18 months to 3 years of age in the Japan Environment and Children's Study, BMC Pediatrics (2026).
