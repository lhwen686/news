Original Text
In a new National Institutes of Health (NIH)-funded study, scientists at Cleveland Clinic discovered that hormones associated with male development may play a key role in limiting the growth of brain tumors in men. The research team found that the loss of androgen hormones, such as testosterone, in a preclinical model of glioblastoma drove tumor growth by inducing local inflammation and triggering the production of stress hormones. In an analysis of data from more than 1,300 men with glioblastoma, the authors found that supplemental testosterone was significantly associated with improved survival, which was consistent with their preclinical experiments.

This outcome is a welcome surprise and may potentially offer a lead for new treatments for a kind of cancer that is deadlier in men." Anthony Letai, M.D., Ph.D., director of NIH's National Cancer Institute (NCI)

As glioblastoma and androgens are simultaneously of higher prevalence in men, many researchers have suspected that these hormones are part of the problem. However, previous studies have not investigated the effects of androgens on tumor growth in the unique environment of the brain.

"The brain has evolved to keep stuff out and that includes immune cells from elsewhere in the body. It's a delicate tissue that often doesn't want huge immune reactions," said corresponding author Justin Lathia, Ph.D., a professor of cancer sciences and scientific director of the Brain Tumor Center at Cleveland Clinic.

Lathia and his colleagues discovered that androgens in the brain play a crucial role in regulating the organ's security systems, unlike other places in the body. Reducing androgens in mouse models of glioblastoma put a neuroendocrine system called the hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis into overdrive. This caused a spike in stress hormones that subsequently drove a subset of cells to further insulate the brain from the rest of the body.

The tightened security created an immunosuppressive environment in the brain, meaning fewer immune cells could reach the growing threat and thus, tumors progressed mostly unchecked. The authors found that testosterone did not produce the same effect in female mice.

The researchers identified that the HPA axis is likely triggered by inflammation in the hypothalamus caused by tumors in androgen-deficient mice. In future work, they intend to pin down exactly how tumors can induce this reaction in an entirely separate region of the brain.

Seeking to explore the relationship between androgens and brain cancer in humans, the researchers analyzed existing clinical data made available through the NIH/NCI Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database. They found that men with glioblastoma who were receiving supplemental testosterone for reasons unrelated to cancer demonstrated a 38% lower risk of death compared to patients not taking the same supplements.

Though not establishing a causal relationship, Lathia and his colleagues believe this observational finding together with their preclinical results warrant clinical trials for further investigation in humans.

"An obvious follow-up study would be to find out whether androgen deprivation, which is a common treatment for cancer, is actually detrimental for glioblastoma," Lathia said.

在美国国立卫生研究院（NIH）资助的一项新研究中，克利夫兰诊所的科学家们发现，与男性发育相关的激素可能在限制男性脑肿瘤的生长中发挥关键作用。研究团队发现，在胶质母细胞瘤的临床前模型中，睾酮等雄激素的缺失通过诱导局部炎症并触发应激激素的产生，从而促进了肿瘤的生长。对来自1300多名男性胶质母细胞瘤患者的数据分析显示，补充睾酮与改善生存期显著相关，这与他们的临床前实验结果一致。

“这个结果是一个令人惊喜的发现，它可能为治疗一种在男性中更致命的癌症提供新疗法的线索。”美国国立卫生研究院国家癌症研究所（NCI）主任、医学博士安东尼·莱泰（Anthony Letai）说道。

由于胶质母细胞瘤和雄激素在男性中同时具有更高的患病率，许多研究人员曾怀疑这些激素是问题的一部分。然而，此前的研究并未调查雄激素对大脑独特环境中肿瘤生长的影响。

“大脑进化出屏障以阻止物质进入，其中包括来自身体其他部位的免疫细胞。它是一种脆弱的组织，通常不希望产生巨大的免疫反应。”通讯作者、克利夫兰诊所癌症科学教授兼脑肿瘤中心科学主任贾斯汀·拉西亚（Justin Lathia）博士说。

拉西亚和他的同事们发现，与身体其他部位不同，大脑中的雄激素在调节该器官的安全系统方面发挥着关键作用。在胶质母细胞瘤小鼠模型中减少雄激素，会使一个名为下丘脑-垂体-肾上腺（HPA）轴的神经内分泌系统过度活跃。这导致应激激素急剧升高，随后促使一部分细胞进一步将大脑与身体其他部分隔离。

这种强化的安全屏障在大脑中创造了一个免疫抑制环境，意味着更少的免疫细胞能够到达日益增长的威胁（肿瘤），因此肿瘤几乎不受抑制地进展。作者发现，睾酮在雌性小鼠中没有产生相同的效果。

研究人员确定，HPA轴可能由雄激素缺乏小鼠肿瘤引起的下丘脑炎症触发。在未来的工作中，他们打算精确确定肿瘤如何能在大脑中一个完全独立的区域诱导这种反应。

为了探索雄激素与人类脑癌之间的关系，研究人员分析了通过NIH/NCI监测、流行病学和最终结果（SEER）数据库提供的现有临床数据。他们发现，因非癌症相关原因接受补充睾酮治疗的胶质母细胞瘤男性患者，与未服用相同补充剂的患者相比，死亡风险降低了38%。

尽管尚未建立因果关系，但拉西亚和他的同事们认为，这项观察性发现以及他们的临床前结果，值得进行临床试验以在人类中进行进一步研究。

“一个显而易见的后续研究是，确定雄激素剥夺（一种常见的癌症治疗方法）是否真的对胶质母细胞瘤有害。”拉西亚说。
