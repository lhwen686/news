双生子研究将艺术创造力（而非科学创造力）与较差的心理健康关联

Twin study links artistic, but not scientific, creativity to poorer mental health

深度解析

本研究通过采用对数千对瑞典双胞胎进行纵向和遗传学信息分析的严谨方法，为创造力与心理健康之间复杂关系提供了细致入微的洞见。研究的核心专业概念在于其利用“同卵双胞胎对照设计”，这使得研究者能够有效分离基因遗传因素与共享环境因素对创造力与心理健康表型关联的影响，从而深入探讨遗传度在其中的作用。传统研究常将创造力视为单一构念，而本文的关键突破在于区分了“艺术创造力”与“科学创造力”，并发现前者与更差的心理健康状况（如双相情感障碍、重度抑郁症风险增加）显著关联，且基因在此关联中发挥重要作用，而后者则未显示此种负面关联。 该研究的行业影响深远。首先，它推动了精神病学和心理学领域对“创造力”的定义和测量向更精细化、多维度方向发展，避免了对不同创造力形式的泛化处理。其次，揭示了艺术创造力与心理健康之间潜在的遗传联系，为未来探索具体的基因位点、神经生物学机制以及可能的生物标志物提供了方向。这有助于开发更精准的风险评估工具和早期干预策略，尤其对于那些具有高艺术天赋但同时面临心理健康脆弱性的个体。这项研究为理解创造性精神障碍的遗传基础和发展提供了宝贵的临床线索，并强调了在临床实践中对不同类型创造性个体进行差异化评估和支持的必要性。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash.com, Dragos Gontariu. The relationship between creativity and mental health has attracted the interest of many psychologists over the past decades. Some past studies have found that creativity is sometimes linked to mental health difficulties, while others collected opposing or inconsistent results. Researchers at the Max Planck Institute for Empirical Aesthetics, Karolinska Institutet and other institutions recently set out to further explore the relationship between creativity and mental health by analyzing questionnaire results collected from thousands of twins living in Sweden. Their findings, published in Molecular Psychiatry, suggest that this link is greatly influenced by how creativity is measured, which could explain why earlier studies reached conflicting conclusions. "There is a longstanding idea that genius and 'madness' go hand in hand, particularly in creative fields, with many famous artists believed to have struggled with mental health problems," Miriam A. Mosing, co-senior author of the paper, told Medical Xpress. "However, the empirical evidence for this association remains mixed. We examined this question for the first time using a highly comprehensive data set, combining longitudinal and genetically informative data with multiple indicators of creativity and both self-reported and registry-based measures of mental health." Analyzing data collected from thousands of Swedish twins Mosing and her colleagues analyzed data collected as part of two surveys run in 2012–2013 and 2022–2023, respectively. These data were collected from a total of 14,658 individual twins living in Sweden, 6,419 of whom participated in both surveys. The surveys assessed the twins' achievement in various artistic fields, including music, writing, dance, theater and visual arts, as well as science and invention. In addition, the surveys asked participants to report their occupations and psychiatric symptoms and assessed their openness to experience. The second survey also assessed the respondents' divergent thinking, the ability to come up with various ideas or possible solutions when presented with a problem. Specifically, participants were asked to name 10 words as different in meaning from one another as possible. "We first explored the longitudinal association between creativity and mental health, or in simpler terms, if earlier creativity predicts later mental health, and if earlier mental health predicts later creativity," explained Mosing. "We also examined the association in identical twins (i.e., twins sharing almost all genes and rearing environment) to test if the association would remain after excluding the genetic and environmental effects shared within each pair of identical twins." In their analyses, the researchers looked at differences in the creativity, self-reported mental health symptoms and psychiatric diagnoses (recorded in Swedish medical records) of identical twins. The researchers measured creativity in both artistic and scientific domains, while also looking at participants' occupations, openness to experience and scores on the divergent thinking test. "To assess mental health, we considered the diagnoses of multiple psychiatric disorders (e.g., bipolar disorder and major depressive disorder) and self-reported questionnaires," said Penghao Xia, first author of the paper. "We then tried to understand if genes play a role in this association. For this purpose, we tested if participants' genetic risk of mental disorders could predict their creativity scores, and if their genetic propensity of creativity could predict their mental health." A complex relationship beyond cause and effect The researchers found that the Swedish twins' artistic creativity was linked to poorer mental health. Scientific creativity, on the other hand, was not significantly linked to most mental health conditions, with only some measures linked to fewer mental health difficulties. "We found higher artistic creativity to be associated with somewhat more mental health problems, and genes play an important role in this association," said Xia. "More studies are needed to understand how artistic creativity and mental health are linked. Though our study suggested the importance of genes, it doesn't mean environment is not important. Therefore, we still need to investigate other explanations of the association." The researchers found that influences genetically inherited by the twins and shared across the examined traits contributed to the associations they observed. They also observed that earlier depressive symptoms predicted greater openness to experience at a later stage, even in comparisons between identical twins. This suggests a possible causal relationship between depressive symptoms and openness, although future studies are needed to confirm this. Overall, the team's results suggest that research focusing on the link between creativity and mental health should not treat all creative inclinations the same way. This is because combining different forms of creativity, such as artistic and scientific creativity, could obscure meaningful differences between the two. "The role of genes in the association we observed needs more exploration," added Mosing. "As part of future research, we need to figure out which genes, through which mechanisms, drive the association between artistic creativity and mental health." Mosing）告诉Medical Xpress：“长期以来一直有一种观点认为，天才与‘疯狂’是并存的，尤其是在创意领域，许多著名艺术家都被认为曾与心理健康问题作斗争。” “然而，支持这种关联的实证证据仍然混杂不清。我们首次使用了一个高度全面的数据集来研究这个问题，该数据集结合了纵向和遗传学信息数据，并采用了多项创造力指标以及自我报告和基于登记的心理健康测量方法。” 分析数千对瑞典双胞胎的收集数据 莫辛和她的同事分析了分别在2012–2013年和2022–2023年进行的两次调查所收集的数据。这些数据来自居住在瑞典的共计14,658名双胞胎个体，其中6,419人参与了两项调查。 这些调查评估了双胞胎在各种艺术领域（包括音乐、写作、舞蹈、戏剧和视觉艺术）以及科学和发明方面的成就。此外，调查还要求参与者报告他们的职业和精神症状，并评估了他们对经验的开放性。 第二次调查还评估了受访者的发散性思维能力，即在面对问题时提出各种想法或可能解决方案的能力。具体而言，参与者被要求说出10个在意义上尽可能彼此不同的词语。 莫辛解释说：“我们首先探讨了创造力与心理健康之间的纵向关联，或者简单来说，是早期的创造力能否预测后期的心理健康，以及早期的心理健康能否预测后期的创造力。我们还在同卵双胞胎（即基因和成长环境几乎完全相同的双胞胎）中检查了这种关联，以测试在排除每对同卵双胞胎共有的遗传和环境影响后，这种关联是否仍然存在。” 在他们的分析中，研究人员考察了同卵双胞胎在创造力、自我报告的心理健康症状和精神病诊断（记录在瑞典医疗记录中）方面的差异。研究人员测量了艺术和科学领域的创造力，同时还考察了参与者的职业、对经验的开放性以及发散性思维测试得分。 论文的第一作者夏鹏豪（Penghao Xia）说：“为了评估心理健康，我们考虑了多种精神疾病（例如双相情感障碍和重度抑郁症）的诊断以及自我报告的问卷。然后，我们试图了解基因在这种关联中是否发挥作用。为此，我们测试了参与者的精神疾病遗传风险是否能预测他们的创造力得分，以及他们的创造力遗传倾向是否能预测他们的心理健康。” 超越因果的复杂关系 研究人员发现，瑞典双胞胎的艺术创造力与较差的心理健康状况相关。另一方面，科学创造力与大多数心理健康状况没有显著关联，仅有少数衡量指标与较少的心理健康问题相关。 夏鹏豪说：“我们发现较高的艺术创造力与更多的心理健康问题存在一定关联，并且基因在这种关联中扮演着重要角色。需要更多的研究来理解艺术创造力与心理健康是如何关联的。尽管我们的研究表明了基因的重要性，但这并不意味着环境不重要。因此，我们仍需探究这种关联的其他解释。” 研究人员发现，双胞胎遗传的、并在所考察特质之间共享的影响因素促成了他们观察到的关联。他们还观察到，即使在同卵双胞胎之间的比较中，早期的抑郁症状也能预测后期对经验的更高开放性。这表明抑郁症状与开放性之间可能存在因果关系，尽管未来需要进一步研究来证实这一点。 总的来说，该团队的研究结果表明，关注创造力与心理健康之间关系的研究不应将所有创造倾向一概而论。这是因为将不同形式的创造力，如艺术创造力和科学创造力，混为一谈可能会掩盖两者之间有意义的差异。 莫辛补充说：“我们观察到的这种关联中基因的作用需要更多探索。作为未来研究的一部分，我们需要弄清楚是哪些基因通过何种机制驱动了艺术创造力与心理健康之间的关联。” 本文由我们的作者英格丽德·法德利（Ingrid Fadelli）撰写，加比·克拉克（Gaby Clark）编辑，并由罗伯特·伊根（Robert Egan）进行事实核查和审阅——这是一项精心的人工工作成果。我们依赖像您这样的读者来维持独立科学新闻的生命力。如果这份报道对您很重要，请考虑捐款（尤其是月度捐款）。作为感谢，您将获得一个无广告账户。 出版详情：夏鹏豪（Penghao Xia）等人，《利用基因分型双胞胎的纵向分析揭示创造力与心理健康之间的多方面关系》，发表于《分子精神病学》（2026）。DOI: 10.1038/s41380-026-03925-5。期刊信息：《分子精神病学》。 关键词：抑郁症，情绪 © 2026 Science X Network
