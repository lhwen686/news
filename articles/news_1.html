A new research study published in the Journal of the American College of Radiology (JACR) has found that unlike other modalities, linens and disposable supplies account for the vast majority of ultrasound's annual carbon emissions—not equipment energy use. The study, titled "Harmonizing Diagnostic Ultrasound Practice with Environmental Sustainability: A Life Cycle Assessment of Diagnostic Ultrasound in a Single Adult University Hospital," determined that linens (35%) and other single-use supplies (34%) accounted for most of ultrasound's greenhouse gas emissions, whereas production (7%) and energy use (3%) of the ultrasound equipment were minor contributors.

The article encourages the use of alternative materials and products with lower environmental impacts, such as sustainably sourced organic cotton for linens, and compostable alternatives to plastic products.

"We are excited to share this work as the results highlight both the low energy use of ultrasound and the potential impact of contentious supply use," said lead author Katherine Frederick-Dyer, MD, Director of Body MRI, Vanderbilt Health. "While linens and disposable supplies were the greatest contributors to the carbon footprint of ultrasound, strategies to optimize their use can be implemented across radiology department modalities for an even greater impact. By identifying supplies such as linens as a major driver of environmental impact in ultrasound imaging, our study highlights the need to look beyond equipment and take a more comprehensive, systems-based approach to sustainability."

This study is part of the JACR's April Focus on Sustainability issue, which looks at a variety of topics, including starting points on energy management and clean energy sourcing, supply chain stewardship, and the broader question of appropriate utilization.

"We need to think more about how our technological processes, from end to end, contribute to health care emissions, because most of the technology in a health system lives with us in radiology—MRI machines, CT machines and now AI," added guest editor Florence X. Doo, MD, MA, Director of Innovation at the University of Maryland Medical Intelligence Imaging Center.

"Sustainability is starting to be framed more as a quality improvement and operational efficiency problem. We need to do this right to reduce waste, improve our energy and optimize our workflow so we can do better as radiologists."

美国放射学院杂志 (JACR) 发表的一项新研究发现，与其他影像模式不同，床单和一次性用品在超声检查的年度碳排放中占绝大部分，而非设备能耗。这项题为“将诊断性超声实践与环境可持续性相协调：单一成人大学医院诊断性超声的生命周期评估”的研究确定，床单（35%）和其他一次性用品（34%）构成了超声检查温室气体排放的大部分，而超声设备的生产（7%）和能耗（3%）则贡献较小。

文章鼓励使用环保影响较低的替代材料和产品，例如可持续来源的有机棉床单，以及塑料产品的可堆肥替代品。

“我们很高兴分享这项工作，因为研究结果突出显示了超声设备的低能耗以及高耗材使用的潜在影响，”范德堡健康中心身体磁共振成像主任、主要作者Katherine Frederick-Dyer医学博士说。“尽管床单和一次性用品是超声检查碳足迹的最大贡献者，但优化其使用的策略可以在放射科的各种影像模式中实施，以产生更大的影响。通过将床单等耗材识别为超声成像中环境影响的主要驱动因素，我们的研究强调了需要超越设备本身，采取更全面、基于系统的方法来实现可持续性。”

这项研究是JACR四月可持续性专题的一部分，该专题探讨了各种议题，包括能源管理和清洁能源采购的起点、供应链尽责管理以及适当利用的更广泛问题。

“我们需要更多地思考我们的整体技术流程如何从始至终地导致医疗碳排放，因为医疗系统中大部分技术，如磁共振成像仪、CT成像仪以及现在的AI，都存在于我们的放射科，”马里兰大学医疗智能影像中心创新主任、客座编辑Florence X. Doo医学博士、文学硕士补充道。

“可持续性正越来越多地被视为一个质量改进和运营效率问题。我们需要正确处理这个问题，以减少浪费，提升能源效率并优化我们的工作流程，从而使我们作为放射科医生做得更好。”
