Chronic stress and inflammation cause silent structural heart damage

深度解析

这项医学新闻深度揭示了慢性炎症作为心血管疾病的隐匿驱动因素及其复杂的病理生理机制。研究通过大规模生物样本库数据，识别出糖蛋白乙酰化产物（GlycA）这一新型生物标志物，其高水平预示着持续的全身性炎症，并与心脏结构性改变（如心室壁增厚、心腔缩小、充盈功能障碍）和主要心血管事件风险显著相关。文章强调，这些早期心脏重塑可能在临床症状出现前数年悄然发生，提示了亚临床疾病的重要性。此外，研究特别指出了社会经济劣势、心理困扰与慢性炎症之间的紧密联系，揭示了社会因素通过生物学途径影响心血管健康的深层机制。更重要的是，研究锁定了白细胞介素-1（IL-1）和肿瘤坏死因子（TNF）家族的关键炎症蛋白为靶点，为开发新型抗炎药物以早期干预和预防心血管疾病提供了潜在方向，预示着精准医疗在心血管领域的巨大潜力。这些发现将推动心血管疾病预防策略从传统风险因素干预扩展至炎症生物标志物筛查与靶向治疗，对临床实践、药物研发和公共卫生政策都具有深远影响。

英文原文

Original
Chronic stress from people's everyday lives may be causing lasting structural changes to the heart, harming health and reducing life expectancy. The findings come from a study of almost half a million adults in the UK, believed to be the largest of its kind, led by researchers at the MRC Laboratory of Medical Sciences (LMS) and Imperial College London. It finds that chronic inflammation, associated with people's socioeconomic situation and lifestyles, is linked to damaging structural changes in the heart, as well as to a higher risk of heart attacks and stroke. The researchers explain that these changes may appear years before people experience any cardiovascular symptoms, remaining a hidden health threat. Professor Declan O'Regan, British Heart Foundation Chair of Cardiovascular AI at Imperial College London and Head of the Computational Cardiac Imaging Group at the LMS, said: "Our study, which is the largest of its kind, suggests that millions of people could be living with hidden inflammation, which is slowly changing their heart and causing long term damage - increasing the risk of heart attack and stroke. Chronic inflammation is complicated, but we know it's tied to our health and driven by a range of lifestyle and economic factors – meaning people may be at more risk just because of their surroundings, their economic status, their family's health and their lifestyle." "But while tackling health inequalities remains an issue, there are things that we can do about inflammation, including reducing risk factors like smoking and obesity." Chronic inflammation is a long-term low-level activation of the body's immune system. It has been linked with a range of diseases, including cancer and diabetes, and is increasingly recognized as a hidden driver of heart disease. In the latest study, researchers analyzed UK Biobank data from nearly 480,000 UK adults. They used a blood marker known as glycoprotein acetyls (GlycA), alongside heart imaging and genetic data. They found that people with the highest levels of inflammation (the top 20%) had a 43% higher risk of heart attack and stroke than those with the lowest levels (the bottom 20%). But in addition, those with higher levels also showed more evidence of structural changes in their hearts, including thickened heart walls, smaller heart chambers and poorer heart filling – all changes which may develop quietly for years before progressing to heart failure. Higher levels of inflammation were strongly associated with measures of socioeconomic disadvantage and psychological distress, as well as the more established risk factors including smoking and excess body fat. People with persistently higher levels of inflammation in the study faced a 43% greater risk of heart attack and stroke, even if they had no previously existing heart disease. The findings suggest that chronic inflammation may be one biological pathway through which everyday life stresses, including poverty and mental health issues can have biological effects that may increase cardiovascular risk. The surprising thing was how much social factors and mental health are linked to inflammation and damage to the heart - as well as more well-known risk factors like smoking and inactivity. There was also a strong genetic factor, with some people being naturally more resilient or susceptible to the inflammatory damage that comes from different lifestyles." Professor Declan O'Regan, British Heart Foundation Chair of Cardiovascular AI at Imperial College London and Head of the Computational Cardiac Imaging Group at the LMS However, the researchers stress that people who are genetically susceptible or exposed to adverse circumstances are not destined to develop heart disease, and much can be done to prevent long term inflammation. The study identified key inflammatory proteins from the interleukin-1 and TNF families as likely drivers of this damage. Several of these proteins are already being targeted by drugs in clinical trials, raising hopes that anti-inflammatory treatments could help prevent cardiovascular disease before symptoms appear. The findings suggest that combining inflammation blood tests with genetic risk scores could help identify those most likely to benefit from early intervention. Professor Bryan Williams, Chief Scientific and Medical Officer at the British Heart Foundation, said: "Inflammation is part of the body's healing process, but there is also a darker side to it. This large-scale study found people with higher levels of inflammation experienced silent changes to the structure of their hearts and had a higher risk of major cardiac events including heart attacks and strokes." "The research provides valuable insights into how our genes and lifestyle factors, such as smoking, poor mental health and socioeconomic status, can converge to trigger inflammation. It also identifies several inflammatory proteins that appear to play a key role in the heart changes linked to chronic inflammation, and it is to be hoped that anti-inflammatory medicines could become an important tool in preventing cardiovascular disease. "These findings reinforce the importance of tackling the causes of inflammation and poor cardiovascular health, while also supporting people to make lifestyle changes that can help protect their hearts." The study was funded by the Medical Research Council and British Heart Foundation. The work was also supported by the NIHR Imperial Biomedical Research Centre, a translational research partnership between Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust and Imperial College London.

人们日常生活中的慢性压力可能会对心脏造成持久的结构性改变，损害健康并缩短预期寿命。 这项研究对英国近五十万名成年人进行了调查，被认为是同类研究中规模最大的一次，由医学科学研究委员会（LMS）和帝国理工学院的研究人员主导。 研究发现，与人们的社会经济状况和生活方式相关的慢性炎症，与心脏的破坏性结构改变以及更高的心脏病发作和中风风险有关。 研究人员解释说，这些改变可能在人们出现任何心血管症状之前几年就已出现，仍然是一个隐匿的健康威胁。 帝国理工学院英国心脏基金会心血管人工智能主席兼医学科学研究委员会计算心脏成像小组负责人Declan O'Regan教授表示：“我们的研究是同类研究中规模最大的一次，它表明数百万人可能生活在隐匿性炎症中，这种炎症正在缓慢地改变他们的心脏并造成长期损害——增加了心脏病发作和中风的风险。慢性炎症很复杂，但我们知道它与我们的健康息息相关，并受到一系列生活方式和经济因素的驱动——这意味着人们可能仅仅因为他们的环境、经济状况、家庭健康和生活方式而面临更高的风险。” “尽管解决健康不平等问题仍是一项挑战，但我们仍可以采取措施应对炎症，包括减少吸烟和肥胖等风险因素。” 慢性炎症是身体免疫系统长期低水平的激活。它与包括癌症和糖尿病在内的多种疾病相关，并日益被认为是心脏病的隐匿驱动因素。 在最新研究中，研究人员分析了英国生物样本库中近48万名英国成年人的数据。他们使用了一种名为糖蛋白乙酰化产物（GlycA）的血液标志物，并结合心脏影像学和遗传数据。 他们发现，炎症水平最高的人群（前20%）患心脏病和中风的风险比炎症水平最低的人群（后20%）高出43%。此外，炎症水平较高的人群还表现出更多心脏结构改变的证据，包括心壁增厚、心腔缩小和心脏充盈功能变差——所有这些改变都可能在数年内悄无声息地发展，最终进展为心力衰竭。 较高的炎症水平与社会经济劣势和心理困扰的衡量指标密切相关，也与吸烟和过度体脂等更已确定的风险因素相关。 研究中炎症水平持续较高的人群，即使没有既往存在的心脏病，也面临着43%的心脏病发作和中风的更大风险。 研究结果表明，慢性炎症可能是一种生物学途径，通过这种途径，包括贫困和心理健康问题在内的日常生活压力可以产生生物学效应，从而增加心血管风险。 “令人惊讶的是，社会因素和心理健康与炎症及心脏损害之间的关联程度——以及吸烟和不运动等更为人熟知的风险因素。此外，还存在强大的遗传因素，有些人天生对不同生活方式带来的炎症损害更具韧性或更易感。”帝国理工学院英国心脏基金会心血管人工智能主席兼医学科学研究委员会计算心脏成像小组负责人Declan O'Regan教授说。 然而，研究人员强调，基因易感或暴露于不利环境的人并非注定会患心脏病，可以通过许多措施来预防长期炎症。 该研究确定了白细胞介素-1和肿瘤坏死因子（TNF）家族中的关键炎症蛋白是造成这种损害的可能驱动因素。其中一些蛋白已被作为药物靶点进入临床试验，这燃起了人们的希望，即抗炎治疗可能有助于在症状出现前预防心血管疾病。 研究结果表明，将炎症血液检测与遗传风险评分相结合，可能有助于识别最有可能从早期干预中获益的人群。 英国心脏基金会首席科学和医学官Bryan Williams教授表示：“炎症是身体愈合过程的一部分，但它也有其负面作用。这项大规模研究发现，炎症水平较高的人群心脏结构发生了无声变化，并且发生包括心脏病发作和中风在内的主要心脏事件的风险更高。” “这项研究为我们的基因和生活方式因素，如吸烟、不良心理健康和社会经济状况，如何共同作用引发炎症提供了宝贵的见解。它还识别出几种似乎在与慢性炎症相关的心脏变化中起关键作用的炎症蛋白，我们希望抗炎药物能够成为预防心血管疾病的重要工具。” “这些发现再次强调了解决炎症和心血管健康不良根源的重要性，同时支持人们改变生活方式以帮助保护心脏。” 这项研究由医学研究理事会和英国心脏基金会资助。 这项工作也得到了NIHR帝国生物医学研究中心的支持，该中心是帝国理工学院医疗保健NHS信托和帝国理工学院之间的转化研究合作伙伴关系。
