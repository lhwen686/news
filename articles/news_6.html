Thrive Capital引领风投进军职业体育所有权；Collaborative Fund刚刚加码

Thrive Capital led VCs into pro sports ownership; Collaborative Fund just upped that play

深度解析

这篇文章揭示了风险投资（VC）进入职业体育所有权领域的新趋势，这与传统的个人财富或私募股权投资方式形成显著区别。其核心概念在于将体育特许经营权视为“终极消费产品”，而不仅仅是升值资产。Thrive Capital通过设立永久资本工具以长期持有“奖杯资产”，而Collaborative Fund则更进一步，将体育俱乐部及其球场视为一个基础设施，利用其巨大的消费者触达能力，为其投资组合中的其他消费品牌（如Whoop和Olipop）提供一个“活生生的展示平台”和分销渠道。 这种商业逻辑的核心在于通过体育资产的广阔受众基础和现场体验的独特价值，创造投资组合公司之间的协同效应。在一个日益“合成化”的数字时代，现场体验的稀缺性使其成为连接消费者与品牌的宝贵介质。 对行业的影响是多方面的：首先，它拓展了VC的投资边界，将高增长科技之外的“体验经济”纳入视野；其次，它将推动职业体育进一步的商业化和创新，通过引入更多品牌整合和粉丝互动模式，提升球队和球场的经济价值；最后，VC的涌入可能进一步推高体育资产的估值，使得这一领域的竞争更加激烈，并促使其他投资者重新审视体育资产的战略价值。这种模式有望重新定义体育俱乐部作为一种商业实体和消费者平台的潜力。

英文原文

Original
Collaborative Fund, the 15-year-old, New York-based generalist venture firm that has roughly $1 billion under management and which made early bets on Lyft, Reddit, Sweetgreen, and Olipop, among others, is taking a stake in the soccer club D.C. United and its stadium, Audi Field. It’s the latest — and smallest — firm to try something that Thrive Capital opened the door to just months ago: turning venture money into pro sports ownership. To recap, Joshua Kushner’s Thrive launched a new vehicle, Thrive Eternal, explicitly built to hold “iconic franchises and cultural institutions” for decades, funded by many of the same investors already in Thrive’s venture and growth funds. The firm kicked things off by announcing a stake in the San Francisco Giants. Months later, the same vehicle — with former Disney CEO Bob Iger, a Thrive partner, joining as co-owner — bought the Lakers outright for a record $12.5 billion. That’s new. Historically, money has poured into pro sports two other ways: individual tech fortunes, and private equity. For example, Vinod Khosla and his family agreed this summer to buy the Seattle Seahawks for a record $9.6 billion soon after the Khosla family also took a stake in the San Francisco 49ers alongside OpenAI chairman Bret Taylor. That was a personal-wealth play, the kind we’ve seen over and over. Private equity firms have also been at this for years, including Sixth Street, which holds stakes in the Boston Celtics, the New England Patriots, and MLB’s San Francisco Giants; Ares, which owns a piece of the Miami Dolphins outright and separately financed Chelsea’s stadium plans through a $500 million preferred-equity deal; RedBird, which owns AC Milan outright and holds a minority stake in Fenway Sports Group, the holding company behind Liverpool and the Red Sox; and Arctos, with minority positions scattered across MLB, the NFL, the NBA, and European soccer. (Apollo, the newest entrant, has mostly stuck to sports financing deals so far rather than ownership stakes.) Thrive and Collaborative are doing neither of those things. At the same time, the two firms’ approaches to sports ownership look very different. Thrive built a standalone, permanent-capital vehicle specifically to hold trophy assets. Collaborative is investing out of the same early-stage fund it uses to write seed and Series A checks, and treating the deal less like something to buy and hold and almost more like infrastructure. In a memo shared with TechCrunch, Collaborative Fund founder and managing partner Craig Shapiro framed the deal as an extension of what the firm already does. “A franchise is the ultimate consumer product,” he wrote, arguing that D.C. United’s status as one of Major League Soccer’s original clubs gives Collaborative access to an institution with a decades-long fan base to build on. He pointed to the tailwinds around American soccer specifically (a World Cup just behind the sport, the LA Olympics ahead of it, soaring youth participation numbers in the U.S.) as well as D.C.’s ownership of Audi Field in Washington, D.C., plus a talent-development pipeline through Loudoun County, Virginia, and rights to a future Baltimore expansion team. Indeed, the thesis Shapiro laid out at a TechCrunch StrictlyVC event Thursday night in New York is less about owning a piece of an appreciating asset – the sports team itself – and more about what the team makes possible. Collaborative wants to turn Audi Field into what he describes as a living showcase for its own portfolio. As a backer of both fitness band maker Whoop and the beverage brand Olipop, for example, Collaborative Fund is imagining a WHOOP wearables activation for fans, or Olipop drinks woven into game-day concessions. He’s thinking about the stadium’s foot traffic — tens of thousands of people showing up on a predictable schedule — as a distribution channel at a time when, because AI is making more of daily life feel synthetic, live experiences are becoming more valuable. Shapiro doesn’t dwell on this, but it surely helped sell Collaborative’s investors that team valuations have been soaring, so the stake could pay off on its own. Soccer valuations in particular have been on a tear. Inter Miami’s franchise value has roughly doubled in the two years since Lionel Messi arrived, MLS’s average club value is up roughly 134% since 2019, and D.C. United’s own valuation has climbed from $35 million in 2008 to $785 million today, factoring in its ownership of Audi Field and the surrounding real estate. If Shapiro is right that a franchise is also “the ultimate consumer product,” it could be a pretty good place to park money. Time will tell. The deal is subject to MLS approval.

AI Translation
Collaborative Fund，这家拥有15年历史、总部位于纽约的综合性风险投资公司，管理着约10亿美元的资产，并曾早期投资Lyft、Reddit、Sweetgreen和Olipop等公司，目前正入股足球俱乐部D.C.联队及其主场奥迪球场。 这是最新一家——也是规模最小的一家——尝试Thrive Capital数月前开创的模式：将风险投资资金转化为职业体育所有权的公司。 回顾一下，Joshua Kushner的Thrive公司推出了一款名为Thrive Eternal的新型投资工具，明确旨在长期持有“标志性特许经营权和文化机构”数十年，其资金来源与Thrive现有风投和成长基金的许多投资者相同。该公司最初宣布入股旧金山巨人队。几个月后，该工具——在Thrive合伙人、前迪士尼首席执行官鲍勃·伊格尔作为共同所有人加入后——以创纪录的125亿美元价格全盘收购了湖人队。 这是一种新趋势。从历史上看，资金流入职业体育主要有两种其他方式：个人科技财富和私募股权。例如，今年夏天，维诺德·科斯拉及其家人同意以创纪录的96亿美元收购西雅图海鹰队，此前科斯拉家族还与OpenAI董事长布雷特·泰勒一同入股了旧金山49人队。这属于个人财富的投资，是我们反复见到的那种。 私募股权公司也涉足该领域多年，其中包括第六街（Sixth Street），该公司持有波士顿凯尔特人队、新英格兰爱国者队以及美国职棒大联盟旧金山巨人队的股份；阿瑞斯（Ares），该公司全资拥有迈阿密海豚队的一部分，并另行通过一笔5亿美元的优先股交易为切尔西的球场计划提供资金；红鸟（RedBird），该公司全资拥有AC米兰，并持有芬威体育集团（利物浦和红袜队背后的控股公司）的少数股权；以及阿尔克托斯（Arctos），该公司在MLB、NFL、NBA和欧洲足球领域拥有零散的少数股权。（最新的入局者阿波罗（Apollo）迄今主要专注于体育融资交易，而非所有权股权。） Thrive和Collaborative都没有采用上述两种方式。与此同时，这两家公司在体育所有权方面的做法看起来也大相径庭。Thrive建立了一个独立的、永久资本的工具，专门用于持有“奖杯资产”。Collaborative则从其用于种子轮和A轮投资的同一个早期基金中进行投资，并将这笔交易视为不那么像买入并持有，而更像是基础设施建设。 在一份与TechCrunch分享的备忘录中，Collaborative Fund创始人兼管理合伙人克雷格·夏皮罗将这笔交易描述为公司现有业务的延伸。他写道：“特许经营权是终极消费品”，并认为D.C.联队作为美国职业足球大联盟（MLS）创始俱乐部之一的地位，让Collaborative得以接触到一个拥有数十年球迷基础的机构，可以此为基础进行发展。 他特别提到了美国足球所面临的有利条件（世界杯刚刚过去，洛杉矶奥运会即将来临，美国青少年参与人数飙升），以及D.C.联队在华盛顿特区拥有奥迪球场，并通过弗吉尼亚州劳登县拥有人才发展渠道，以及未来巴尔的摩扩建队的权利。 事实上，夏皮罗周四晚在纽约TechCrunch StrictlyVC活动上阐述的观点，与其说是在拥有一个不断升值的资产——体育队本身——的一部分，不如说更多地是关于这支球队所能实现的可能性。Collaborative希望将奥迪球场打造成他所描述的、展示其自身投资组合的活生生平台。 例如，作为健身手环制造商Whoop和饮料品牌Olipop的投资者，Collaborative Fund正在构想为球迷提供WHOOP可穿戴设备激活服务，或将Olipop饮料融入比赛日特许经营商品中。他正在思考球场的人流量——数万人按可预测的时间表出现——作为一个分销渠道，尤其是在人工智能让日常生活更多地感到“合成化”的当下，现场体验正变得越来越有价值。 夏皮罗并没有着重强调这一点，但这无疑帮助说服了Collaborative的投资者，即球队估值一直在飙升，因此这笔股权本身就能带来回报。特别是足球队的估值一直高涨。自莱昂内尔·梅西到来以来的两年里，迈阿密国际队的特许经营价值大致翻了一番；自2019年以来，美国职业足球大联盟的平均俱乐部价值上涨了约134%；D.C.联队自身的估值从2008年的3500万美元攀升至今天的7.85亿美元，这其中包含了其对奥迪球场及周边房地产的所有权。 如果夏皮罗关于特许经营权也是“终极消费品”的观点是正确的，那么这可能是一个相当不错的投资去处。时间会证明一切。 该交易尚需美国职业足球大联盟的批准。
