深度解析

当前新闻机构起诉OpenAI和微软的案件，核心揭示了生成式人工智能（Generative AI）技术在商业应用中面临的深刻伦理和法律挑战。生成式AI，如ChatGPT，其技术原理是基于海量现有数据（包括新闻报道、文章等）进行训练，学习语言模式和内容结构，从而生成看似原创的新文本。然而，这种“学习”过程并未获得内容原创者的明确授权，这便触及了版权保护的核心。新闻业的商业逻辑依赖于原创内容的创作与发行，而AI直接利用这些内容进行商业化产出，无疑是对其生存模式的巨大冲击。这种利用原创内容实现商业目标的模式，一方面推动了AI技术进步，另一方面却可能瓦解内容生产的经济基础。其行业影响是深远的：它不仅引发了关于知识产权的全球性辩论，更迫使传统内容产业重新思考其商业模式与AI共存的策略，并促使监管机构探索新的法律框架，以平衡技术创新与内容创作者的权益保护。这场诉讼不仅是法律战，更是关于未来数字内容生态构建方向的理念之争。

英文原文

Original
In Brief Two more news organizations are suing OpenAI and Microsoft over the supposed use of their journalism to train AI. A lawsuit filed by The Seattle Times and Newsday argued that with the advent of AI, the journalism industry could become “broken beyond repair.” The lawsuit described generative AI as “a snake eating its own tail” that could “destroy the very organizations” that produce the content it’s trained on. “AI products like ChatGPT and CoPilot are touted as producers of content, but in fact they are rapacious consumers, devouring human-authored content and delivering back to the world copies and derivative imitations of that same original content they consumed to achieve their commercial objectives,” the lawsuit said. Back in 2023, The New York Times sued OpenAI and its partner/investor Microsoft over alleged copyright infringement; other publications have followed suit as the case continued. The Seattle Times’ lawsuit is particularly notable because Microsoft and OpenAI have funded some of the organization’s journalism projects and fellowships. A Microsoft spokesperson told GeekWire that the company is “surprised by the lawsuit” but is “always happy to sit down and explore solutions to this type of dispute.”
