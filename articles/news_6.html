深度解析

这篇文章揭示了企业安全领域一个被忽视但至关重要的方面：由个人脆弱性放大的“内部威胁”。它深入探讨了外国情报机构如何利用个人关系，特别是涉及高管的关系，来获取敏感信息或政策洞察的可能性。 文章讨论的核心专业概念是“人力情报（HUMINT）对人类脆弱性的利用”。与侧重技术黑客攻击的传统网络安全不同，这种机制利用个人联系、婚外情或利益冲突。这个过程通常始于“培养”——建立长期关系（如文章所述，通常持续数年而非数周），而非快速打击。这使得情报机构能够巧妙地提取信息，影响决策，或识别组织内的“症结所在”（阻碍）。 其行业影响是深远的。首先，它暴露出企业安全的一个重大盲区。许多公司将安全狭隘地定义为设施保护、网络防御和物理威胁，却忽视了复杂的人为因素。这种疏忽导致安全团队资金不足，未能充分关注主动的内部威胁检测。其次，对于涉及关键技术或国家安全利益的行业，如拥有先进芯片的英伟达，这种脆弱性可能导致巨大的知识产权损失和地缘政治影响，正如对Megaspeed的怀疑以及芯片可能被输送到中国的情况所示。这篇文章敲响了警钟，强调了在当今复杂的威胁环境中，仅限于“合规，一分钱都不能多”的心态是远远不够的。

Original
Outsiders had little reason to notice when Nvidia’s longtime chief security officer and head of product security stepped down in August. Just two and a half months later, CEO Jensen Huang himself left the company over growing questions about his extramarital relationships — starting with an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore data center company that U.S. investigators suspect of funneling Nvidia chips to China, then widening to reports about two separate affairs with Nvidia employees. At the time, David Reber, who spent four years as CSO and seven at Nvidia overall, said he was leaving for health reasons. “This wasn’t a pivot I had planned. But I know what needs to come first: family and health,” he wrote on LinkedIn in late August, disclosing that he’d been diagnosed with young-onset Parkinson’s more than two years ago. Reber’s replacement, Saša Zdjelar, previously spent 17 years at ExxonMobil in Houston, leaving as a senior IT manager before spending one year at Salesforce and several years at the cybersecurity company ReversingLabs. Zdjelar didn’t respond to questions Thursday about the transition or what the security team knew. Nothing public connects Reber’s or Zdjelar’s role changes to the Megaspeed matter, but it seems fair to wonder what Nvidia’s security team knew about the government’s scrutiny of Megaspeed and about Huang’s relationships, and whether anyone raised concerns. Reber didn’t respond to questions Thursday. Nvidia also has not answered our related questions. Not all of those relationships are the business of the security team. Relationships with subordinates typically fall to HR, legal, and the board. But an affair with an executive at a company under federal investigation is a different animal. (Megaspeed has denied any wrongdoing.) Security specialists say affairs can create vulnerabilities that foreign intelligence services look to exploit. Catching that kind of exposure isn’t easy. The way intelligence services collect information, much of it through everyday conversation rather than hacking, makes it especially difficult for security teams to identify risks, according to retired senior intelligence officer Nicholas Eftimiades, who says he has trained more than 1,000 corporate security professionals. Some people inside Nvidia clearly knew parts of the picture. Nvidia’s top lawyer was engaged with the government’s Megaspeed inquiry more than a year ago, The New York Times reported earlier this week. (Nvidia has said it regularly contacts officials.) Reports about Huang’s relationships with employees suggest colleagues also knew of them, even if the behavior is not expressly forbidden by Nvidia’s code of conduct. The Times cited five Nvidia employees who said he was involved with a vice president who reported directly to him, and Reuters, citing two people, later reported a second relationship. What’s unclear is whether any of that reached security. Even if it had reached Nvidia’s security team, Eftimiades, who wrote Chinese Espionage Operations and Tactics in 2020, said a security team’s resources are often limited. For one thing, they’re often underfunded because, unlike some other cost centers, they don’t drive revenue. It’s astonishingly common for CEOs to answer a security team’s warnings with “not a nickel more than compliance,” he said. Many companies also define security very narrowly, specialists say. In an opinion piece in The Hill, Don Aviv, CEO of the corporate investigations firm Interfor International, and author Jeremy Hurewitz, wrote, “Too many companies still approach security primarily as a matter of protecting facilities, preventing cyberattacks, and responding to physical threats.” The two argued that “[s]ecurity teams need to pay greater attention to insider threats, including the cultivation of executives and employees by foreign intelligence services, suspicious business relationships, conflicts of interest and attempts to obtain sensitive information through trusted personal contacts.” Still, some of it is beyond what any team can do. Speaking generally, not about Huang, Eftimiades offered a hypothetical in which a CEO complains that he can’t do something because “I’m fighting a board who doesn’t want to do this.” That tells an intelligence asset who the obstacle is. The service then asks, “Who’s the sticking point here?” and looks for ways into that person’s email and phone. What a rival intelligence service wants is often less technology than policy insight, including who’s pushing to change export rules and who the biggest blocker is. “Intel world tends to be like cancer,” he said. “Once it gets into the body, it looks to spread and looks to reach out.” The playbook isn’t new, Eftimiades added, “particularly for China.” Any executive dealing with the country “will tell you that they’ve been approached.” He said there are also “tons of diplomat stories” of U.S. diplomats walking into a hotel room to find a woman on the bed. He said what’s newer compared with Soviet era spying is the relationships these services cultivate now often last years, not weeks. Technology can augment that human intelligence, Eftimiades said, pointing to IMSI catchers are one example. These devices mimic a cell tower so that nearby phones connect to them. A basic version can cost about $1,000 and fit in a suitcase, he said, though government-grade models cost far more. Eftimiades says no one knows yet if these were used against Huang, whose phone was presumably hardened such that compromises could be detected, if not stopped altogether. But either way, what Nvidia’s security team knew, and whether it could have acted on it, should become clearer as the board’s independent investigation and federal inquiries proceed. In the meantime, Eftimiades said he hopes the whole episode is “a wake-up call” for U.S. industry, which he said is mostly “clueless” about China’s tactics. “We have lost trillions [of dollars] in intellectual property,” is his guess. “Whatever we think we’re doing isn’t working.”

外界鲜有关注的理由，当英伟达（Nvidia）长期任职的首席安全官兼产品安全主管于八月离职时。仅仅两个半月后，首席执行官黄仁勋本人也离开了公司，原因是他婚外情的问题日益增多——最初是一名Megaspeed公司的高管，该公司是一家新加坡数据中心企业，美国调查人员怀疑其将英伟达芯片输送至中国；随后又传出他与两名英伟达员工有染的报道。 当时，在英伟达担任了四年首席安全官，总共七年的大卫·雷伯（David Reber）表示他因健康原因离职。“这并非我计划中的转变。但我知道什么才是第一位的：家庭和健康。”他在八月下旬的领英（LinkedIn）上写道，并透露自己在两年多前被诊断出患有早发性帕金森症。 雷伯的继任者，萨沙·兹德耶拉（Saša Zdjelar），此前曾在休斯顿的埃克森美孚（ExxonMobil）工作17年，离职时担任高级IT经理，之后在Salesforce工作了一年，并在网络安全公司ReversingLabs工作了数年。兹德耶拉周四未回应有关此次过渡或安全团队所知情况的问题。 公开信息并未将雷伯或兹德耶拉的职位变动与Megaspeed事件联系起来，但我们有理由怀疑英伟达安全团队对政府审查Megaspeed以及黄仁勋的关系了解多少，以及是否有人提出过担忧。雷伯周四未回应提问。英伟达也未回答我们提出的相关问题。 并非所有这些关系都属于安全团队的职责范畴。与下属的关系通常由人力资源、法务部门和董事会处理。但与一家正接受联邦调查的公司高管发生婚外情则性质不同。（Megaspeed否认有任何不当行为。）安全专家表示，婚外情可能制造出外国情报机构试图利用的漏洞。 发现这类泄密并不容易。据已退休的高级情报官员尼古拉斯·埃夫蒂米亚德斯（Nicholas Eftimiades）称，情报机构收集信息的方式，其中很多是通过日常对话而非黑客手段，这使得安全团队尤其难以识别风险。他表示曾培训过1000多名企业安全专业人士。 英伟达内部的一些人显然了解部分情况。《纽约时报》本周早些时候报道称，英伟达的首席律师一年多前就已参与政府对Megaspeed的调查。（英伟达曾表示会定期与官方接触。）关于黄仁勋与员工关系的报道表明，同事们也知晓这些关系，即便这种行为并未被英伟达的行为准则明确禁止。《纽约时报》援引五名英伟达员工的话说，黄仁勋与一名直接向他汇报的副总裁有染；路透社随后援引两人的话报道了第二段关系。不清楚的是，这些信息是否传达到了安全部门。 即使这些信息传达到了英伟达的安全团队，埃夫蒂米亚德斯，他曾在2020年撰写《中国间谍行动与战术》一书，表示安全团队的资源通常有限。首先，他们通常资金不足，因为与其他一些成本中心不同，他们不产生收入。他说，首席执行官们对安全团队的警告做出“除了合规，一分钱都不能多”的回复，这种情况非常普遍。 专家们表示，许多公司对安全的定义也非常狭隘。在《国会山报》的一篇评论文章中，企业调查公司Interfor International的首席执行官唐·阿维夫（Don Aviv）和作家杰里米·赫雷维茨（Jeremy Hurewitz）写道：“太多公司仍然主要将安全视为保护设施、预防网络攻击和应对物理威胁的问题。”两人认为，“安全团队需要更加关注内部威胁，包括外国情报机构对高管和员工的策反、可疑的商业关系、利益冲突以及通过可信的个人联系获取敏感信息的企图。” 然而，有些事情超出了任何团队的能力范围。埃夫蒂米亚德斯一般性地（并非特指黄仁勋）提出了一个假设情景：一位首席执行官抱怨他无法做某事，因为“我正在与一个不愿做此事的董事会抗争。”这就告诉了情报人员谁是阻碍。情报机构随后会问：“症结在哪？”并寻找方法入侵那个人的电子邮件和手机。 竞争情报机构想要的往往不是技术，而是政策洞察，包括谁在推动改变出口规定，以及谁是最大的阻碍者。他说：“情报界就像癌症。一旦进入体内，它就会寻求扩散并向外伸展。” 埃夫蒂米亚德斯补充说，这种策略并非新鲜事，“尤其是对于中国而言。”任何与该国打交道的高管“都会告诉你他们曾被接触过。”他说，还有“大量外交官的故事”，讲述美国外交官走进酒店房间，发现床上有一名女性。他说，与苏联时期的间谍活动相比，更新颖的是，这些机构现在培养的关系通常持续数年而非数周。 埃夫蒂米亚德斯表示，技术可以增强这种人力情报，他以IMSI捕捉器为例。这些设备模仿手机信号塔，以便附近的手机连接到它们。他说，一个基本版本可能花费约1000美元，并能装进手提箱，尽管政府级别的型号要昂贵得多。 埃夫蒂米亚德斯说，目前尚无人知晓这些技术是否曾被用于对付黄仁勋，他的手机可能经过加固，即使无法完全阻止入侵，也能检测到。但无论如何，随着董事会的独立调查和联邦调查的推进，英伟达安全团队的所知情况以及他们是否能采取行动，应该会变得更加明朗。 同时，埃夫蒂米亚德斯表示，他希望整个事件能给美国工业界“敲响警钟”，他说，美国工业界对中国的策略大多“一无所知”。他猜测：“我们已经损失了数万亿美元的知识产权。”“无论我们认为自己正在做什么，都行不通。”
