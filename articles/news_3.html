深度解析

此次刚果埃博拉疫情，由罕见的邦迪布焦病毒引起，其近50%的高病死率凸显了该病毒的极度凶险。文章揭示，疫情难以控制的关键在于其复杂的公共卫生挑战。首先，80%的新病例来自非监测传播链，表明社区传播广泛且隐匿，传统的接触者追踪策略形同虚设。其次，三分之一的死亡发生在治疗中心外且未经检测，这意味着大量感染者未能及时识别、隔离和获得专业医疗干预，不仅延误了救治良机，也加剧了疾病在社区的扩散。 从医学机制上看，埃博拉病毒通过直接接触体液传播，病变机制可导致严重的出血热，多器官衰竭，若无早期支持性治疗，预后极差。然而，邦迪布焦病毒尚无特异性疫苗或治疗药物，仅能进行对症支持治疗，这使得疫情控制难度倍增。行业影响方面，疫情严重挤兑了刚果脆弱的医疗卫生系统，医护人员罢工、公共不信任及武装冲突等社会政治因素，进一步削弱了疫情应对能力和全球卫生安全防护网。此次疫情的跨区域蔓延，警示国际社会需加强对新兴和再现传染病的全球监测、快速响应机制和疫苗药物研发投入，以应对此类公共卫生危机。

Credit: AP Photo/Dieudonne Dirole Deaths in Congo's Ebola outbreak have topped 5,000 as the country grapples with the relentless spread of the disease and cases are recorded in other African countries, the latest government data on Saturday shows. Data from the Congolese Ministry of Health showed that the disease has killed 5,026 people out of 10,351 infected, at a nearly 50% lethality rate. The surge is sparking concerns among experts regarding the authorities' ability to contain the deadly virus. The latest outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo virus, was declared in mid-May, catching authorities by surprise. They have since struggled to manage it. It is already the second-deadliest Ebola outbreak in history, second only to a two-year-long West Africa outbreak between 2014 and 2016, which killed 11,000 people across multiple countries. "It is a very tragic milestone because it is just not a number," said Dr. Patrick Otim, a program area manager with the World Health Organization. "It is families and communities that have been devastated by this outbreak. The number also shows the severity of this outbreak." So far, 2,788 people have recovered from the virus. The Bundibugyo virus has no known vaccine or treatment, but health workers treat patients based on symptoms. A health care worker is disinfected after taking part in a burial for a victim of the Ebola virus, in North Kivu, Congo, Friday, Oct. 30, 2026. Credit: AP Photo/Dieudonne Dirole One-third of people who have died did not get to receive any care Since May, the disease has spread to 67 out of the country's 178 health zones in eight of 26 provinces. Fifty of the affected 67 health zones have active cases. The virus has been concentrated in the eastern region of Congo, especially in Ituri and North Kivu, which have emerged as the epicenters with 9,782 total confirmed cases and 4,792 total deaths. Authorities are still struggling with tracing contacts and testing, key components in the race to curb the outbreak. According to the Africa Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, about 80% of new cases are coming from outside the chain of monitored contacts, meaning an overwhelming share of infections are coming from unknown chains of transmission to health authorities. Also, one-third of recorded deaths are victims who have died outside of treatment centers, before being tested for Ebola, WHO's Otim said. These issues, health officials say, contribute to community transmission outside of the immediate purview of health workers who can effectively manage infection in accordance with medical protocols. "These are people we never had opportunity to try to save them and it is one of the biggest concerns for us in this outbreak. How do we try to reach people early to get them to come to health facilities to get a fighting chance to survive?" Otim said. Health care workers take part in a burial for Joseline Ngonji Kale, a 55-year-old woman who was killed by the Ebola virus, in Bunia, Congo, Friday, Oct. 30, 2026. Credit: AP Photo/Dieudonne Dirole 'The outbreak is stretching everyone responding to it' The explosive number of infections in Congo comes on the heels of a long list of issues that have hamstrung efforts to contain the outbreak. The eastern region of Congo, where the outbreak is concentrated, plays host to a conflict between the government and an array of armed groups, most prominently the Rwanda-backed M23 rebel group, and there is also an Islamist insurgency in the border areas with Uganda, where the Allied Democratic Forces carry out attacks, limiting the reach of health care workers. Also, there is widespread disbelief among the population, which has led to a series of attacks on health workers and health facilities. A nurse was killed while responding to an Ebola alert last week, and a politician from the ruling party was killed in September after advocating for health measures on the radio. Health workers have gone on multiple strikes, shutting down facilities where they treat infected people, citing long delays in payments by the government. On Friday, workers at an Ebola treatment center in Tchomia, in Ituri, downed tools, demanding four months of unpaid wages. At the center, there are currently five confirmed Ebola patients and 10 suspected cases under care. "We are tired of requesting our salaries. If they want to pay us, they will. ... Today, being able to feed our families has become a real problem," Moise Mugenyi, one of the striking workers, told the AP. Dr. Justin Mukonkole, acting head of response operations in Ituri, said the workers should be patient while the government responds to their demands. Similar situations have played out in other health zones like Beni in North Kivu, where services in some treatment centers have ground to a halt. Alain Siwako, an elected public official in Beni, said cases are spiraling in Beni because of a lack of fuel and financial resources from the central government. "Across Ituri and North Kivu, this outbreak is stretching everyone responding to it, including the communities themselves," Rose Tchwenko, country director for the DRC, Prosper Global (formerly Mercy Corps), said. In October, both Kenya and Nigeria confirmed cases of Ebola. Kenya has confirmed eight cases and two deaths, while Nigeria's sole confirmed case had been evacuated to Switzerland. The confirmation of cases in both countries, and earlier in Uganda, made the latest outbreak the first recorded in three African regions and the continent's most geographically widespread outbreak.
