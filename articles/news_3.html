抗体能选择性地抑制有害T细胞，而不削弱整体免疫系统。

Antibodies can selectively shut down harmful T cells without weakening whole immune system

大阪大学团队发现的免疫诱导T细胞受体样抗体（iTab）揭示了一种精妙的免疫调节机制。iTab通过模仿T细胞受体（TCR）的识别模式，选择性地结合到肽-抗原-主要组织相容性复合体II类（MHC class II）复合物上，从而阻断特定T细胞的活化，避免了自身免疫反应。与传统的广谱免疫抑制剂不同，iTab的特点在于其高度选择性，它能精准调控异常活跃的T细胞应答，而不会削弱整体免疫系统对病原体的防御能力，这在自身免疫疾病治疗领域具有革命性意义。该研究在多发性硬化症（MS）小鼠模型中证实，iTab可显著减轻疾病严重程度并延缓发作，甚至通过诱导iTab产生的疫苗也能达到预防效果。这为自身免疫疾病的精准治疗开辟了新途径，有望实现靶向干预，同时降低感染等副作用风险。此外，理解iTab的生成机制也有助于优化疫苗设计，在需要强大免疫应答时避免其诱导，从而提升疫苗效力。这项成果标志着免疫学领域向更安全、更有效的个体化免疫治疗迈出了重要一步。

Regulation of T cell response by Immune-induced TCR-like antibody (iTab). Credit: 2026, Kazuki Kishida et al., Immuno-induced TCR-like antibodies regulate specific T cell response in mice, Nature Communications

The immune system is the frontline protection against infection, continually searching for and destroying unknown pathogens. While typical operation of the immune system scans for threats, some systems attack the body's own healthy cells, leading to autoimmune diseases, like multiple sclerosis. Treatment of autoimmune diseases is a difficult balance, and one that has not yet been successfully achieved.

Now, a multi-institutional team led by the University of Osaka has discovered a previously unknown immune regulatory mechanism mediated by antibodies that selectively shut down overactive immune responses at their source. The findings have been published in Nature Communications.

The immune system relies on T cells, which respond to threats found on fragments of proteins, called antigens, on other cells. However, some T cells perceive threats on healthy cells and respond to them as if they were harmful invaders. This can lead to autoimmune disorders such as multiple sclerosis.

"We discovered a new immune regulatory mechanism driven by a novel antibody called an 'immune-induced TCR-like antibody' or iTab," says Kazuki Kishida, lead author of the study. "These antibodies selectively suppress the activity of T cells, blocking the immune response to specific antigens."

Immune-induced TCR-like antibody (iTab) specifically recognizes peptide-antigen-MHC complexes. Credit: Hisashi Arase, The University of Osaka

The researchers found that in mice, iTabs are produced naturally during the immune response. They work by mimicking a receptor on the T cell, latching onto a molecule called MHC class II. This prevents the T cell from "plugging in" and activating the immune response. To uncover the mechanism, the team studied how mice responded to different protein fragments.

They discovered that when antigens included extra pieces at the ends of the protein, or flanking regions, the immune system generated iTabs that bind the proteins. This prevented the T cells from recognizing them and avoided the unnecessary attack on healthy cells.

Importantly, the researchers showed that iTabs could reduce harmful immune responses in a mouse model of autoimmune disease, similar to multiple sclerosis. Treatment with iTabs reduced disease severity and delayed its onset. Even more striking, vaccination with peptides designed to induce iTabs also protected against disease.

This opens the door to a new therapeutic approach: instead of suppressing the whole immune system, like many current immunosuppressants, treatments could be designed to generate iTabs that target only disease-causing immune responses.

"By designing vaccines or therapies that promote iTab production," explains Hisashi Arase, senior author, "it may be possible to treat conditions driven by overactive T cells while leaving the rest of the immune system intact. Conversely, by designing vaccines that avoid inducing iTabs, we may be able to increase vaccine effectiveness."

Itab improves experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE). Credit: 2026, Kazuki Kishida et al., Immuno-induced TCR-like antibodies regulate specific T cell response in mice, Nature Communications

These findings suggest that when the immune system can work against itself, iTabs may offer a way to safely calm down an overactive system, focusing only on ensuring the well-being of the host.

免疫诱导T细胞受体样抗体（iTab）对T细胞应答的调节作用。图片来源：2026年，Kazuki Kishida 等人，《免疫诱导T细胞受体样抗体调节小鼠特异性T细胞应答》，《自然通讯》

免疫系统是抵御感染的第一道防线，它不断搜索并摧毁未知病原体。虽然免疫系统的典型运作是扫描威胁，但有些系统会攻击身体自身的健康细胞，导致自身免疫疾病，如多发性硬化症。自身免疫疾病的治疗是一个艰难的平衡，而且至今尚未成功实现。

现在，由大阪大学领导的一个多机构团队发现了一种以前未知的免疫调节机制，该机制由抗体介导，能够选择性地关闭源头处过度活跃的免疫应答。这些发现已发表在《自然通讯》杂志上。

免疫系统依赖于T细胞，它们对其他细胞上称为抗原的蛋白质片段所发现的威胁作出反应。然而，一些T细胞会将健康细胞上的威胁误认为是有害入侵者并对其作出反应。这可能导致自身免疫性疾病，如多发性硬化症。

“我们发现了一种由一种新型抗体——‘免疫诱导T细胞受体样抗体’（iTab）驱动的新免疫调节机制，”该研究的主要作者Kazuki Kishida说，“这些抗体选择性地抑制T细胞的活性，从而阻断对特定抗原的免疫应答。”

免疫诱导T细胞受体样抗体（iTab）特异性识别肽-抗原-MHC复合物。图片来源：Hisashi Arase，大阪大学

研究人员发现，在小鼠体内，iTabs在免疫应答过程中自然产生。它们通过模仿T细胞上的受体，附着在一种称为主要组织相容性复合体II类（MHC class II）的分子上发挥作用。这阻止了T细胞的“插入”并激活免疫应答。为了揭示这一机制，该团队研究了小鼠对不同蛋白质片段的反应。

他们发现，当抗原包含蛋白质末端的额外片段或侧翼区域时，免疫系统会生成结合这些蛋白质的iTabs。这阻止了T细胞识别它们，从而避免了对健康细胞不必要的攻击。

重要的是，研究人员表明iTabs可以减少自身免疫疾病小鼠模型（类似于多发性硬化症）中的有害免疫应答。iTabs治疗减轻了疾病严重程度并延缓了其发作。更引人注目的是，接种旨在诱导iTabs的肽也能预防疾病。

这为一种新的治疗方法打开了大门：与许多当前的免疫抑制剂那样抑制整个免疫系统不同，新的治疗方法可以设计成产生只针对致病性免疫应答的iTabs。

“通过设计促进iTab产生的疫苗或疗法，”资深作者Hisashi Arase解释说，“有可能在保持免疫系统其余部分完整的同时，治疗由过度活跃T细胞驱动的疾病。相反，通过设计避免诱导iTab的疫苗，我们或许能够提高疫苗的有效性。”

这些发现表明，当免疫系统可能“内讧”时，iTabs或许能提供一种安全地平息过度活跃系统的方法，只专注于确保宿主的健康。
