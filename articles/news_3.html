迷幻药能否平息过度活跃的免疫系统？一项新的荟萃分析正在调查。

Can psychedelics soothe an overactive immune system? New meta-analysis investigates

深度解析

这篇文章深入探讨了血清素能致幻剂（如裸盖菇素和LSD）作为潜在治疗药物，在精神健康和免疫调节方面的作用机制及其临床前景。核心专业概念在于这些物质通过作为血清素受体（特别是5-HT2A受体和部分sigma-1受体）的激动剂来发挥其生物学效应。它们的化学结构与内源性神经递质血清素相似，使其能特异性结合并激活神经元和免疫细胞表面的这些受体。 其治疗潜力体现在两方面：首先，在中枢神经系统，通过激活血清素受体，致幻剂能显著增强神经可塑性，促进神经元回路的重塑，从而有助于纠正与抑郁、焦虑等精神疾病相关的僵化思维模式和功能失调的连接。其次，在免疫系统层面，研究表明这些化合物在免疫系统过度活跃、炎症水平升高的情况下，能选择性地发挥抗炎和免疫调节作用，降低促炎细胞因子水平。值得注意的是，这种抗炎作用并非普遍性免疫抑制，而是在病理状态下的一种校正性反应。 该领域的发展对精神病学和免疫学领域具有深远影响。它推动了对神经炎症与精神疾病之间复杂关系的理解，并为开发基于致幻剂作用机制的新型治疗药物提供了靶点。然而，目前的人体临床数据仍显不足且结果不一，尤其是在活动性炎症患者中的疗效和安全性尚待大规模、严格的随机对照试验验证。未来的研究需更精准地筛选适用人群，并深入解析其作用的细胞和分子通路，以期将其转化为安全的临床应用。

Credit: Pexels. The same substances that can alter perception and shift moods are now drawing scientific interest for their potential therapeutic abilities. Researchers across the globe are investigating whether psychedelics, such as psilocybin from magic mushrooms or LSD, could help treat mental health and brain-related disorders. In a recent study, researchers gathered many such studies and carried out a systematic review and meta-analysis focusing on the anti-inflammatory and immune-modulating properties of serotonergic psychedelics. Serotonergic psychedelics act by targeting the same receptors that respond to serotonin, a chemical messenger involved in regulating mood, communication between neurons and immune responses. The psychoactive drugs have chemical structures similar to serotonin, allowing them to bind directly to serotonin receptors in the brain and body. Out of the 61 studies analyzed, most of the lab-grown cell studies and animal studies found that psychedelics reduce inflammation. The human results were more mixed, with only three of the 10 trials showing reduced inflammation. When the researchers combined the data from mouse studies, they found that serotonergic psychedelics show strong anti-inflammatory properties, but only when baseline inflammation is already elevated due to an overactive immune system. They had no effect in healthy mice. The findings are published in Brain, Behavior, and Immunity. Hallucinogenic helpers For diseases that affect the central nervous system, researchers are always on the lookout for therapeutics that can be used alongside conventional medication. Psychedelics are emerging as one such possibility, with studies reporting benefits across a range of psychiatric and neurological conditions, including depression, PTSD, Parkinson's disease and migraines. By binding to serotonin receptors in the central nervous system, psychedelics activate neuroplasticity, helping the brain adapt, forge new connections and break free from the rigid neural patterns that fuel depression, anxiety and addiction. Some studies suggest that psychedelics might influence the immune system, reducing elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines in preclinical models with an overactive immune response. Since chronic inflammation is linked to mental health disorders, calming this inflammatory activity could be another way these substances impart their therapeutic effects. A systematic dataset that brings together evidence from cell studies, animal research and human trials on psychedelics' effects on the immune system could help health care professionals assess whether psychedelic therapy has a place in clinical treatment. To address this need, the researchers searched six major databases for studies published up to Feb. 9, 2026. Of the 9,195 studies they screened, 61 met the requirements: 24 were done in cell culture, 30 in animals and seven in humans, covering 10 separate trials. Associations of immune markers in blood and serotonergic psychedelic drug exposure in human studies. Credit: Brain, Behavior, and Immunity (2027). DOI: 10.1016/j.bbi.2026.107033 What does the evidence say? Across the trials, researchers found that 19 out of 24 laboratory cell studies and 25 out of 30 animal studies reported anti-inflammatory effects of serotonergic psychedelics, particularly when cells were exposed to an inflammatory threat or immune challenge. The data also showed that psychedelics did not suppress immune function across the board. In healthy animal models, the compounds did not significantly change cytokine levels. In studies that reported anti-inflammatory effects, the team found that psychedelics acted through specific biological targets that help regulate immune activity. For instance, DOI and psilocybin primarily acted through the serotonin 5-HT2A receptor, while DMT acted through the sigma-1 receptor. While animal studies show promising anti-inflammatory effects, there isn't enough evidence to confirm that these benefits translate to humans. Seven out of the 10 human clinical trials did not include people with active inflammatory diseases. Whether psychedelics can tackle inflammation when the immune system is overactive remains an open question that future studies involving people with active inflammatory conditions can address.
