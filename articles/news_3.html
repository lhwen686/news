强化血压控制降低脑出血幸存者的卒中复发风险

Intensive blood pressure control lowers stroke recurrence risk in ICH survivors

深度解析

这项荟萃分析揭示了脑出血（ICH）幸存者卒中二级预防策略的关键性转变。其核心机制在于，强化且长期的血压管理，通过将平均收缩压（SBP）进一步降低至127毫米汞柱（相较于常规的138毫米汞柱），显著减轻了脑血管壁的剪切应力。脑出血后，受损血管结构更脆弱，高血压是复发性脑出血的主要危险因素。持续的低血压状态有助于稳定这些脆弱的血管，降低破裂风险，从而实现61%的脑出血复发率降低，并最终使总卒中复发风险降低38%。其临床意义重大：首先，研究挑战了当前指南的常规血压目标（低于130/80毫米汞柱），揭示即使基线血压已达标的患者，通过更强化降压仍能显著获益，这可能促使未来指南更新。其次，在不增加严重不良事件的前提下，强化降压的安全性得到证实，为临床实践提供了更积极干预的信心。行业影响包括对新型降压药物（特别是固定剂量复方制剂）的需求增加，以及医疗机构需推行更系统化的血压管理方案，例如结构化滴定和患者依从性干预，以降低全球范围内，尤其在中低收入国家高发的脑出血负担。

英文原文

Original
Intensive, long-term blood pressure-lowering treatment reduced the risk of recurrent stroke by 38%, without increasing serious adverse events, in people who have survived an intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH). The findings of the major meta-analysis were published today in The Lancet Neurology. Researchers analyzed data from four randomised controlled trials involving 2,944 adults with ICH. Two studies investigated fixed-dose antihypertensive therapies and two evaluated treatment strategies based on defined blood pressure targets. During a follow-up period of up to six years, recurrent stroke of any type occurred in 6.5% of patients receiving intensive blood pressure-lowering compared with 10.4% of those receiving less intensive treatment or standard care (the control arm). This translated to a 38% reduction in recurrent stroke risk, with consistent benefits observed across all studies. Professor Craig Anderson, senior author and Senior Professorial Fellow at The George Institute said the findings provide compelling evidence for more intensive blood pressure management after ICH. "Intracerebral hemorrhage is one of the deadliest forms of stroke, with limited treatment options and a high risk of recurrence among survivors. Our analysis shows that intensive blood pressure treatment can prevent 16 recurrent strokes for every 1,000 patients treated within the first year alone. These findings reinforce the central role of blood pressure control in secondary stroke prevention." During follow-up, average systolic blood pressure (SBP) was 127 mm Hg in the intensive treatment group and 138 mm Hg in the control group, representing a mean difference of 11.2 mm Hg. The reduction in recurrent stroke was mainly driven by an approximately 61% lower risk of ICH among patients receiving intensive treatment. Importantly, there was no observed increase in serious adverse events, which occurred in approximately 29% of patients in the intensive treatment group and 33% of patients receiving standard care. The findings have important global implications. More than three million people experience ICH each year and there's an estimated 17 million survivors. Among these survivors, approximately one quarter will later die from recurrent stroke or cardiovascular disease. The ICH burden falls disproportionately on low- and middle-income countries, where high blood pressure is often inadequately controlled. Rates of ICH are almost twice as high in these countries compared with high-income nations. Subgroup analysis showed that the benefits of intensive blood pressure lowering were consistent regardless of how much time had passed since the initial ICH event. Effects were consistent across different baseline blood pressure levels. Notably, patients whose baseline blood pressure was already at conventional target remained at considerable risk and benefitted from blood pressure lowering. For example, among those with systolic blood pressure ≤130 mm Hg at baseline, recurrent stroke occurred in 13.9% of control patients compared with 6.2% of patients receiving intensive treatment. Professor Anderson added that the findings challenge assumptions made by current guidelines. "Generally, guidelines recommend treatment to a target of less than 130/80 mm Hg, but our study showed that patients at or below this threshold can still face a significant risk of recurrent stroke." "Preventing stroke recurrence requires us to address the persistent barriers to blood pressure control, such as poor treatment adherence and therapeutic inertia. To achieve reliable control, we need to continue pursuing approaches that have shown promise, including fixed-dose combination therapies and structured treatment titration protocols." The TRIDENT study, led by The George Institute for Global Health, was the only trial in this analysis that exclusively enrolled patients with spontaneous ICH. The remaining trials included people with either ischemic stroke or ICH at baseline.

对于脑出血（ICH）幸存者，强化、长期降压治疗可使卒中复发风险降低38%，且不增加严重不良事件的发生。这项重要的荟萃分析结果今日发表在《柳叶刀·神经病学》杂志上。 研究人员分析了涉及2944名脑出血成年患者的四项随机对照试验数据。其中两项研究调查了固定剂量降压治疗方案，另两项则评估了基于特定血压目标的治疗策略。 在长达六年的随访期内，接受强化降压治疗的患者中，任何类型卒中复发的发生率为6.5%，而接受非强化治疗或标准护理（对照组）的患者中，这一比例为10.4%。这意味着卒中复发风险降低了38%，且所有研究均观察到了一致的获益。 该研究的资深作者、乔治全球健康研究院资深教授研究员Craig Anderson表示，这些发现为脑出血后更强化的血压管理提供了令人信服的证据。 “脑出血是卒中致死率最高的类型之一，治疗选择有限，幸存者复发风险高。我们的分析显示，仅在治疗的第一年内，每1000名接受强化降压治疗的患者中，可预防16例卒中复发。这些发现再次强调了血压控制在卒中二级预防中的核心作用。” 随访期间，强化治疗组的平均收缩压（SBP）为127毫米汞柱，而对照组为138毫米汞柱，平均差值为11.2毫米汞柱。 卒中复发风险的降低主要得益于强化治疗组患者脑出血复发风险约61%的显著下降。重要的是，未观察到严重不良事件的增加，强化治疗组和接受标准护理的患者中，严重不良事件的发生率分别约为29%和33%。 这些发现具有重要的全球意义。每年有超过三百万人发生脑出血，估计有1700万幸存者。在这些幸存者中，约有四分之一将因卒中复发或心血管疾病而死亡。 脑出血的负担不成比例地落在中低收入国家，这些国家的高血压往往控制不足。与高收入国家相比，这些国家的脑出血发生率几乎是两倍。 亚组分析显示，无论距离首次脑出血事件发生多久，强化降压的益处均保持一致。 无论基线血压水平如何，其效果均保持一致。值得注意的是，即使基线血压已达到常规目标值的患者，仍面临相当大的风险，并从降压治疗中获益。例如，基线收缩压≤130毫米汞柱的患者中，对照组的卒中复发率为13.9%，而接受强化治疗的患者为6.2%。 Anderson教授补充说，这些发现对当前指南的假设提出了挑战。 “通常，指南建议将治疗目标设定为低于130/80毫米汞柱，但我们的研究表明，即使血压达到或低于此阈值的患者，仍可能面临显著的卒中复发风险。” “预防卒中复发需要我们解决血压控制中的持续性障碍，例如依从性差和治疗惰性。为实现可靠的控制，我们需要继续探索已显示出前景的方法，包括固定剂量复方制剂和结构化治疗滴定方案。” 由乔治全球健康研究院主导的TRIDENT研究，是本次分析中唯一一项专门招募自发性脑出血患者的试验。其余试验则纳入了基线时患有缺血性卒中或脑出血的患者。
