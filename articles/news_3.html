该研究揭示了一项针对眼科诊断的重大技术突破，通过创新性地利用光学相干断层扫描（OCT）技术，旨在解决传统角膜生物力学评估的局限性。核心概念是圆锥角膜等疾病中角膜局部机械性减弱往往早于宏观形态学变化。传统方法多评估整体响应或单点/单截面，易忽略这些早期、局部的病理改变。 ICTER团队开发的新型多点、单次、深度编码复用OCT系统是关键。其技术原理在于，通过“深度编码”技术，系统能同步测量角膜在九个不同位置对气流脉冲刺激的变形响应。这克服了传统顺序扫描在捕捉快速、瞬态（约20毫秒）生物力学事件时的时间误差，极大地提高了测量精度和可靠性。系统以极高时间分辨率（100,000次/秒）工作，确保了对角膜快速动态变化的精确捕捉。 研究引入了“不对称向量”这一核心临床生物标志物。该向量通过量化角膜在不同测量点变形程度的差异，提供了一个简洁的参数来指示生物力学失衡的大小和方向，直接指向角膜最脆弱的区域。 该技术对行业影响深远：它有望实现圆锥角膜的更早期诊断和更精准的疾病进展及治疗效果（如角膜胶原交联术）监测。在屈光手术规划中，更详细的生物力学信息能帮助医生更准确地筛选患者，降低术后并发症风险。此外，这种同步多点高速成像原理还可推广至其他软组织生物力学研究，具有广泛的应用前景。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Comparison of conventional OCT approaches with a new multi-spot method that simultaneously measures air-puff-induced corneal deformation at nine locations. This enables assessment of corneal asymmetry, relevant particularly to keratoconus. Credit: Biomedical Optics Express Researchers at the International Centre for Translational Eye Research (ICTER) have developed a prototype imaging system that can simultaneously track the cornea's mechanical response at nine locations. The technology marks an important step toward more accurate, noncontact assessment of corneal biomechanics and could improve early detection and monitoring of disorders such as keratoconus. The cornea is the transparent front surface of the eye and the first structure that bends incoming light. Its shape and optical properties are essential for sharp vision. Yet the cornea is much more than a transparent window. It is a complex, layered tissue whose mechanical properties influence how it responds to pressure, external forces and even subtle changes in its own structure. Understanding these biomechanical properties has become increasingly important in modern ophthalmology. They can reveal changes that appear before visible structural abnormalities develop and help clinicians assess disease progression or treatment outcomes. However, accurately measuring corneal biomechanics remains technically challenging. Researchers from ICTER, operating at the Institute of Physical Chemistry of the Polish Academy of Sciences, have developed a new optical coherence tomography (OCT) system designed specifically for this purpose. Their study, "Single-shot, depth-encoded multiplexed OCT for multi-spot tracking of induced transient corneal dynamics," published in Biomedical Optics Express, describes a prototype capable of recording how the cornea deforms after a short air pulse—all within a single measurement. The ICTER authors of the publication, both current and former members of the Centre, are Karol Karnowski, Jadwiga Milkiewicz, Onur Cetinkaya, Angela Pachacz, Andrea Curatolo, Kamil Liżewski, Dawid Borycki and Maciej Wojtkowski. The project was carried out in collaboration with researchers from Nicolaus Copernicus University in Toruń, the Collegium Medicum of Nicolaus Copernicus University in Bydgoszcz, the University of Liverpool, the Instituto de Óptica Daza de Valdés in Madrid and the University of Rochester. Optomechanical design of the system. Credit: Biomedical Optics Express (2026). DOI: 10.1364/boe.596342 Looking beyond conventional corneal measurements Modern ophthalmic instruments routinely measure corneal thickness, curvature and overall shape. Many also assess how the cornea responds to a gentle mechanical stimulus. One of the best-known examples is the noncontact air-puff test, widely used to measure intraocular pressure. During the examination, a brief pulse of air causes a slight deformation of the cornea, which is then analyzed by the instrument. While this approach has become a clinical standard, it has an important limitation. Mechanical weakening of the cornea is not always uniform. In keratoconus—a progressive disease that causes the cornea to thin and bulge outward—localized areas of tissue may become mechanically weaker long before conventional imaging reveals obvious structural changes. As the disease progresses, patients may experience worsening vision, increasing astigmatism, greater sensitivity to light and growing difficulty achieving good visual correction with glasses or contact lenses. Advanced cases may ultimately require specialized treatment or even corneal transplantation. Data processing and results visualization. Credit: Biomedical Optics Express (2026). DOI: 10.1364/boe.596342 Existing biomechanical assessment methods typically evaluate the overall corneal response or analyze deformation within a single cross-sectional plane. Although these approaches provide valuable clinical information, they may overlook subtle, localized abnormalities occurring away from the central measurement axis. The ICTER team set out to overcome this limitation. Instead of analyzing the cornea one location at a time, their system measures its response simultaneously at nine positions—one central point and eight surrounding peripheral locations. This makes it possible not only to determine how much the cornea deforms but also to identify precisely where the tissue responds most strongly and whether that pattern may indicate local biomechanical weakening. "The cornea is one of the eye's primary optical elements responsible for focusing light onto the retina, so not only its shape but also its mechanical properties are crucial. It is believed that the mechanical state of the cornea changes in certain conditions, such as keratoconus, before any alterations in its shape become apparent. By simultaneously measuring the cornea's response to an air-puff stimulus, our system can assess this mechanical asymmetry, offering the potential to detect pathological changes earlier than methods based solely on corneal geometry," says Karnowski from ICTER. Capturing nine measurement points in a single shot The new technology builds on optical coherence tomography (OCT), one of the most widely used imaging techniques in ophthalmology. OCT provides high-resolution cross-sectional images of the eye using light rather than sound, making it the optical counterpart of ultrasound. It has become indispensable in retinal imaging and anterior segment diagnostics because it can visualize ocular tissues quickly, noninvasively and with micrometer-scale resolution. In this project, however, the ICTER researchers pushed the technology beyond conventional OCT imaging. Rather than illuminating the cornea with a single probing beam, they designed an optical system in which several beams strike the tissue simultaneously. Each beam corresponds to a different measurement location. Instead of acquiring these locations sequentially, the signals are separated using depth encoding, allowing information from multiple points on the cornea to be recorded within a single OCT acquisition. The advantage is straightforward but significant. Conventional scanning systems collect data point by point, meaning that measurements from different locations are acquired at slightly different moments in time. For relatively static tissues, this is rarely a problem. Corneal deformation following an air puff, however, is a rapid, transient event lasting only a few tens of milliseconds. Even tiny eye movements, blinking, fluctuations in the tear film or the inherent delay of sequential scanning can introduce measurement errors. By recording all nine locations simultaneously, the ICTER system eliminates these temporal inconsistencies. Every point is measured during the same mechanical event, providing a much more faithful representation of how the cornea responds to external force. The prototype operates at exceptionally high speed. It records corneal motion every 10 μs, corresponding to 100,000 measurements per second across all nine locations simultaneously. The researchers also investigated what would happen if the temporal resolution were reduced. When sampling was degraded to 250 μs, the error in estimating the magnitude of the asymmetry vector reached approximately 20%. At 1 ms, the error approached 70%. The effect was even more pronounced when estimating the direction of the asymmetry vector: The angular error increased to 17.5° at 250 μs and as much as 47.5° at 1 ms. These findings demonstrate that temporal resolution is not merely a technical specification—it fundamentally determines measurement accuracy. Slower acquisition rates may not only underestimate or overestimate the extent of biomechanical abnormalities but also misidentify the region where the cornea is weakest. "The corneal deformation we aim to capture to assess its mechanical asymmetry lasts only about 20 milliseconds, and the measurement cannot be repeated. This makes the simultaneous acquisition of the corneal response at multiple locations the key feature of our method. Achieving this requires very high temporal resolution—much like a camera capable of capturing an extremely high number of frames per second. Existing techniques have either lacked sufficient temporal resolution or have been limited in their spatial coverage," explains Karnowski. For this reason, simultaneous acquisition represents one of the key innovations of the new system. Instead of reconstructing corneal behavior from measurements taken fractions of a millisecond apart, the technology captures the entire biomechanical event as it unfolds. The result is a more reliable representation of corneal mechanics and a significantly improved ability to detect subtle local biomechanical differences that may otherwise remain hidden. The asymmetry vector: Turning complex motion into a clinically meaningful biomarker Recording corneal deformation at nine locations simultaneously generates a wealth of biomechanical information. To make these data clinically useful, the researchers developed a parameter called the asymmetry vector. The concept is straightforward. The system compares corneal deformation at eight peripheral locations with the response at the center and between opposing measurement points. If one region deforms more than another, this indicates biomechanical imbalance within the tissue. The asymmetry vector describes this imbalance using two parameters. Its magnitude reflects how large the biomechanical difference is, while its direction indicates where the greatest deformation occurs. Together, they provide a concise representation of corneal behavior that is easy to compare between patients and across follow-up examinations. One way to think about the asymmetry vector is as a biomechanical compass. Rather than simply indicating that the cornea behaves asymmetrically, it points toward the region where that asymmetry is most pronounced. This could prove particularly valuable in keratoconus. In keratoconus, tissue weakening, thinning and protrusion rarely occur exactly in the center of the cornea. Instead, the disease typically develops locally, affecting only part of the tissue in its earliest stages. As a result, the average biomechanical response of the entire cornea may appear relatively normal even though one region has already become mechanically compromised. The researchers demonstrated that the asymmetry vector consistently pointed toward the area affected by keratoconus. In these patients, the vector direction corresponded to the region where the cornea was thinner, steeper and showed abnormal posterior elevation. The authors note that the vector did not perfectly overlap with maps generated by commercially available corneal tomography systems. This was expected because the two techniques measure different aspects of the tissue. Conventional topography and tomography describe corneal shape and anatomy. The new OCT-based approach measures how the tissue behaves mechanically when subjected to a brief air pulse. Rather than competing with existing diagnostic methods, the researchers see these measurements as complementary. "The asymmetry vector provides a way to translate the cornea's rapid and complex motion into a single parameter that can be compared across patients and between successive examinations. From a clinical perspective, the key question is whether it can identify the predominant direction of biomechanical weakening. In the future, such a parameter could support risk assessment and disease monitoring," explains Milkiewicz, M.S., from ICTER. One air pulse, two distinct corneal deformations While validating the prototype, the researchers observed an unexpected phenomenon. Instead of deforming only once after a single air pulse, the cornea occasionally exhibited two distinct deformation events. The team named this previously unreported behavior the dual-indentation response. The discovery emerged after the researchers modified the air-delivery system. To allow measurements not only at the corneal apex but also across the peripheral measurement points, they increased the diameter of the air nozzle from 1.5 mm to 3.7 mm. The broader nozzle produced a wider air pulse while reducing its peak force by approximately half. Importantly, the pulse duration remained unchanged at 8 ms. Although the stimulus became weaker, the biomechanical response became more complex. Instead of a single indentation followed by recovery, the cornea developed two clearly distinguishable deformation phases after the same air pulse. This suggests that widening the air stream changes how mechanical force is distributed across the corneal surface, revealing dynamic behavior that conventional measurements may fail to capture. Because all nine locations were recorded simultaneously and at extremely high temporal resolution, the researchers were able to observe this phenomenon directly rather than infer it retrospectively from sequential scans. "Before the data can be analyzed, they must be carefully prepared—cleaned, standardized and corrected for measurement artifacts. The reliability of the conclusions depends heavily on the quality of this preprocessing stage, which is why we devote as much attention to it as to the measurement itself," says Borycki. Although the biological mechanisms underlying this response remain to be investigated, the observation illustrates the value of high-speed, simultaneous biomechanical imaging. By capturing the entire event rather than reconstructing it from sequential measurements, the system can reveal subtle dynamic phenomena that might otherwise remain hidden. What could this mean for patients? Although the current study is primarily a technical proof of concept, the technology has clear clinical potential. Because the system combines optical coherence tomography with a brief air pulse—both already familiar in ophthalmic practice—it could be integrated into future diagnostic devices without fundamentally changing the patient experience. The innovation lies not in the examination itself but in how the corneal response is recorded and analyzed. One of the most promising applications is the earlier detection of keratoconus. In many patients, biomechanical changes precede visible structural abnormalities. A system capable of identifying subtle, localized weakening of the cornea could therefore detect disease at an earlier stage than methods that rely solely on morphology. The technology could also support long-term monitoring of disease progression and provide an objective way to assess treatment outcomes. This may be particularly valuable following corneal cross-linking, a procedure designed to strengthen corneal tissue and slow the progression of keratoconus. Another potential application is refractive surgery planning. Procedures such as LASIK and other forms of laser vision correction require careful assessment of corneal biomechanics to minimize the risk of postoperative complications. More detailed biomechanical information could help clinicians identify patients who are not suitable candidates or refine treatment planning for those who are. The authors emphasize, however, that the present study focused on demonstrating the capabilities of the technology rather than validating its clinical performance. A separate clinical dataset has already been collected, including healthy volunteers as well as patients with early and mild forms of keratoconus. The results of this validation study will be presented in a future publication. Like any emerging technology, the current prototype also has limitations. At present, analysis requires manual segmentation of the anterior corneal surface in OCT images. While this approach is robust and appropriate for research, it is too time-consuming for routine clinical use. The ICTER team is therefore developing automated analysis pipelines, including machine learning-based algorithms that could substantially accelerate image processing. Accurate eye positioning remains another technical challenge. Small eye movements, incomplete alignment or blinking can reduce signal quality and affect measurement accuracy. Future versions of the system may therefore incorporate pupil monitoring, real-time alignment feedback and eye-tracking technology to improve robustness during clinical examinations. Beyond the cornea Although the study focuses on corneal biomechanics, the researchers believe that the underlying measurement principle could have applications far beyond ophthalmology. The work demonstrates that simultaneous multi-point OCT imaging can capture extremely rapid, nonrepeatable mechanical events in light-scattering biological tissues—situations in which conventional sequential scanning may introduce significant temporal errors. This concept could ultimately prove valuable for studying other soft tissues where fast mechanical processes are difficult to capture reliably with existing imaging techniques. The prototype described in this study represents an important step toward that broader vision. By combining ultrafast acquisition with simultaneous multi-point measurements, the ICTER team has shown that OCT can provide not only detailed structural images but also new insights into tissue biomechanics. As the technology continues to mature and undergo clinical validation, it could become a valuable addition to the ophthalmic diagnostic toolbox, enabling clinicians to detect biomechanical abnormalities earlier, monitor disease progression more precisely and make better-informed treatment decisions. 他们的系统不是一次分析一个位置的角膜，而是同时测量其在九个位置的响应——一个中心点和八个周围的周边位置。这不仅可以确定角膜变形的程度，还可以精确识别组织响应最强的区域，以及该模式是否可能表明局部生物力学减弱。 “角膜是眼睛的主要光学元件之一，负责将光线聚焦到视网膜上，因此不仅其形状，其机械特性也至关重要。据信，在某些情况下，例如圆锥角膜，角膜的机械状态会在其形状发生任何明显改变之前发生变化。通过同时测量角膜对气流脉冲刺激的响应，我们的系统可以评估这种机械不对称性，从而有望比仅基于角膜几何形状的方法更早地检测出病理变化，”ICTER的Karnowski说。 单次捕获九个测量点 这项新技术建立在光学相干断层扫描（OCT）的基础上，这是眼科学中最广泛使用的成像技术之一。OCT使用光而不是声波提供眼睛的高分辨率横截面图像，使其成为超声波的光学对应物。它在视网膜成像和眼前节诊断中变得不可或缺，因为它能够快速、非侵入性地以微米级分辨率可视化眼部组织。 然而，在这个项目中，ICTER研究人员将这项技术推向了超越传统OCT成像的界限。 他们没有用单一的探测光束照射角膜，而是设计了一个光学系统，其中多束光束同时击中组织。每束光束对应一个不同的测量位置。这些信号不是按顺序采集，而是使用深度编码进行分离，从而允许在单次OCT采集中记录角膜上多个点的信息。 其优点是显而易见的，但意义重大。传统的扫描系统逐点收集数据，这意味着来自不同位置的测量是在时间上略有不同的时刻采集的。对于相对静态的组织来说，这很少成为问题。然而，气流脉冲后的角膜变形是一个快速、瞬态的事件，仅持续几十毫秒。即使是微小的眼球运动、眨眼、泪膜波动或顺序扫描固有的延迟都可能引入测量误差。 通过同时记录所有九个位置，ICTER系统消除了这些时间上的不一致。每个点都在相同的机械事件中进行测量，提供了角膜如何响应外力的更忠实表现。 该原型系统以极高的速度运行。它每10微秒记录一次角膜运动，相当于同时在所有九个位置每秒进行100,000次测量。 研究人员还调查了如果降低时间分辨率会发生什么。当采样降至250微秒时，估计不对称向量大小的误差约为20%。在1毫秒时，误差接近70%。在估计不对称向量方向时，这种影响更为显著：在250微秒时，角度误差增加到17.5°，而在1毫秒时，高达47.5°。 这些发现表明，时间分辨率不仅仅是一项技术规格——它从根本上决定了测量精度。较慢的采集速率不仅可能低估或高估生物力学异常的程度，还可能错误地识别角膜最薄弱的区域。 “我们旨在捕获的角膜变形以评估其机械不对称性仅持续约20毫秒，并且该测量无法重复。这使得同时获取多个位置的角膜响应成为我们方法的关键特征。实现这一点需要非常高的时间分辨率——就像一台能够以极高帧率拍摄的相机一样。现有技术要么缺乏足够的时间分辨率，要么在空间覆盖方面受到限制，”Karnowski解释道。 因此，同步采集代表了新系统的主要创新之一。该技术不是从相隔几分之一毫秒的测量数据中重建角膜行为，而是在生物力学事件发生时将其完整捕获。 其结果是对角膜力学更可靠的表征，以及显著提高检测可能隐藏的细微局部生物力学差异的能力。 不对称向量：将复杂运动转化为具有临床意义的生物标志物 同时记录九个位置的角膜变形会产生大量的生物力学信息。为了使这些数据在临床上可用，研究人员开发了一个名为不对称向量的参数。 这个概念很简单。系统将八个周边位置的角膜变形与中心点以及相对测量点之间的响应进行比较。如果一个区域比另一个区域变形更多，这表明组织内部存在生物力学失衡。 不对称向量使用两个参数描述这种失衡。其大小反映了生物力学差异的程度，而其方向则指示了最大变形发生的位置。它们共同提供了一个简洁的角膜行为表示，便于在患者之间和连续的随访检查中进行比较。 可以将不对称向量视为一个生物力学“指南针”。它不仅仅是简单地指示角膜行为不对称，而是指向这种不对称最明显的区域。 这在圆锥角膜中可能尤其有价值。 在圆锥角膜中，组织减弱、变薄和突出很少精确地发生在角膜中心。相反，疾病通常是局部发展的，在早期阶段仅影响部分组织。因此，即使某个区域的机械性能已经受损，整个角膜的平均生物力学响应也可能显得相对正常。 研究人员证实，不对称向量始终指向受圆锥角膜影响的区域。在这些患者中，向量方向与角膜较薄、曲率更陡峭并显示异常后表面隆起的区域相对应。 作者指出，该向量与市售角膜地形图系统生成的图谱并未完全重叠。这是预料之中的，因为这两种技术测量的是组织的不同方面。 传统的角膜地形图和断层扫描描述的是角膜的形态和解剖结构。这种新的基于OCT的方法测量的是组织在受到短暂气流脉冲时机械性地如何反应。研究人员认为这些测量是现有诊断方法的补充，而非竞争。 “不对称向量提供了一种方法，能将角膜快速复杂的运动转化为一个单一参数，可在患者之间以及连续检查之间进行比较。从临床角度来看，关键问题是它能否识别生物力学减弱的主要方向。未来，这样一个参数可以支持风险评估和疾病监测，”ICTER的硕士研究员Milkiewicz解释道。 一次气流脉冲，两种不同的角膜变形 在验证原型系统时，研究人员观察到一个意想不到的现象。角膜在单次气流脉冲后，偶尔会表现出两次明显的变形事件，而不是仅变形一次。团队将这种此前未曾报道的行为命名为双重凹陷响应。这一发现是在研究人员修改了送气系统后出现的。 为了不仅在角膜顶点，而且在周边测量点进行测量，他们将气流喷嘴的直径从1.5毫米增加到3.7毫米。较宽的喷嘴产生了更宽的气流脉冲，同时将其峰值力减小了大约一半。重要的是，脉冲持续时间保持不变，为8毫秒。尽管刺激减弱了，但生物力学响应变得更加复杂。 角膜在相同的气流脉冲后，没有出现单一的凹陷后恢复，而是发展出两个清晰可辨的变形阶段。这表明，拓宽气流改变了机械力在角膜表面的分布方式，揭示了传统测量可能无法捕捉的动态行为。 由于所有九个位置都是同步以极高时间分辨率记录的，研究人员能够直接观察到这一现象，而不是从顺序扫描中回顾性地推断。 “在数据分析之前，必须对其进行仔细的准备——包括清洗、标准化和校正测量伪影。结论的可靠性在很大程度上取决于这个预处理阶段的质量，这也是为什么我们对此投入的关注不亚于测量本身的原因，”Borycki说。 尽管这种响应背后的生物学机制仍有待研究，但这一观察结果说明了高速同步生物力学成像的价值。通过捕获整个事件而不是从顺序测量中重建，该系统可以揭示可能被隐藏的微妙动态现象。 这对患者意味着什么？ 尽管目前的研究主要是技术概念验证，但这项技术具有明确的临床潜力。 由于该系统将光学相干断层扫描与短暂气流脉冲相结合——两者在眼科实践中都已为人熟知——它可以在不从根本上改变患者体验的情况下，集成到未来的诊断设备中。创新之处不在于检查本身，而在于角膜响应的记录和分析方式。 最有前景的应用之一是更早地检测圆锥角膜。在许多患者中，生物力学变化早于可见的结构异常。因此，一个能够识别角膜细微局部弱化的系统，可以比仅依赖形态学的方法更早地检测出疾病。 该技术还可以支持疾病进展的长期监测，并提供客观评估治疗结果的方法。这在角膜胶原交联术（一种旨在加强角膜组织并减缓圆锥角膜进展的程序）后可能特别有价值。 另一个潜在应用是屈光手术规划。LASIK和其他形式的激光视力矫正等手术需要仔细评估角膜生物力学，以最大程度地降低术后并发症的风险。更详细的生物力学信息可以帮助临床医生识别不适合的患者，或为适合的患者优化治疗方案。 然而，作者强调，本研究的重点是展示该技术的能力，而非验证其临床性能。 目前已收集了一组独立的临床数据集，包括健康志愿者以及早期和轻度圆锥角膜患者。这项验证研究的结果将在未来的出版物中公布。 像任何新兴技术一样，目前的原型系统也存在局限性。 目前，分析需要对OCT图像中的角膜前表面进行手动分割。虽然这种方法可靠且适用于研究，但对于常规临床使用来说过于耗时。因此，ICTER团队正在开发自动化分析流程，包括基于机器学习的算法，可以显著加速图像处理。 精确的眼球定位仍然是另一个技术挑战。微小的眼球运动、不完全对准或眨眼会降低信号质量并影响测量精度。因此，未来版本的系统可能会整合瞳孔监测、实时对准反馈和眼球追踪技术，以提高临床检查期间的鲁棒性。 超越角膜 尽管这项研究的重点是角膜生物力学，但研究人员认为其基本测量原理在眼科学之外也可能具有广泛应用。 这项工作表明，同步多点OCT成像能够捕获光散射生物组织中极快、不可重复的机械事件——在这些情况下，传统的顺序扫描可能会引入显著的时间误差。 这一概念最终可能对研究其他软组织具有价值，因为这些组织中快速的机械过程难以用现有成像技术可靠地捕捉。 本研究中描述的原型系统代表了实现这一更广阔愿景的重要一步。通过将超高速采集与同步多点测量相结合，ICTER团队展示了OCT不仅可以提供详细的结构图像，还可以提供对组织生物力学的新见解。 随着这项技术的不断成熟和临床验证，它有望成为眼科诊断工具箱中的宝贵补充，使临床医生能够更早地检测出生物力学异常，更精确地监测疾病进展，并做出更明智的治疗决策。 发表详情：Karol Karnowski
