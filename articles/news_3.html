Fractional exhaled nitric oxide elevated in pediatric chest tightness-variant asthma

深度解析

本研究深入探讨了儿童胸闷变异性哮喘（CTVA）这一特殊的哮喘亚型，其主要症状为胸闷而非典型的喘息，导致诊断常具挑战性。研究核心发现揭示了两个关键的诊断辅助指标：呼出气一氧化氮分数（FeNO）升高和小气道功能受损。FeNO作为一种非侵入性生物标志物，反映了嗜酸性粒细胞性气道炎症，这是哮喘的典型特征。CTVA患者FeNO水平升高，即便缺乏经典的喘息症状，也提示存在活跃的炎症过程。同时，MEF25、MEF50、MEF75等参数显示的小气道功能减退，强调了评估这些通常是哮喘炎症和气道重塑初始部位的远端气道的重要性，而传统肺活量测定法（主要关注大气道）常会忽略这一点。 该研究的临床意义深远。通过结合FeNO测量与详细的小气道功能测试，临床医生能更精确地诊断和表型分类CTVA，从而减少误诊，促进早期干预，并指导更个性化的治疗方案，尤其是在哮喘可能严重影响儿童发育的情况下。此外，识别肥胖和过敏性疾病等相关危险因素，强调了采取综合管理措施的必要性，包括环境控制和合并症治疗。这项研究推动了哮喘诊断范式的演变，超越传统方法，以期捕捉疾病更精细的病理生理学特征，最终改善患者预后。

英文原文

Original
This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies . Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Children with chest tightness variant asthma (CTVA) have higher fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) levels and lower measures of small airway function, according to a study published online Aug. 10 in Frontiers in Pediatrics. Ruo-Yu Cao, from Cangzhou Central Hospital in China, and colleagues examined pulmonary function characteristics and risk factors for pediatric CTVA in a retrospective case-control study. They analyzed clinical data from 39 children diagnosed with CTVA from February 2022 to February 2024 and compared them with 50 healthy children undergoing physical examinations during the same period. At initial diagnosis or physical examination, all participants underwent FeNO measurement and pulmonary ventilation function tests. The researchers found that, compared with the healthy control group, FeNO levels were significantly higher in the CTVA group. The CTVA group also had significantly lower forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1)/forced vital capacity (FVC), peak expiratory flow, and maximal expiratory flow at 25%, 50% and 75% of FVC. No significant differences were seen in FEV1, FVC or maximal mid-expiratory flow. Significant intergroup differences were observed for obesity, inhalant allergen sensitization, allergic rhinitis, atopic dermatitis, history of recurrent respiratory infections and family history of allergic diseases. These factors were confirmed as significantly associated with pediatric CTVA in a univariate logistic regression analysis. "The combination of FeNO and small airway indicators may warrant further investigation in the diagnostic evaluation of CTVA, but formal diagnostic accuracy studies are needed," the authors write. More information Ruo-Yu Cao et al, Pulmonary function and risk factors in pediatric chest tightness variant asthma: a case-control study, Frontiers in Pediatrics (2026).
