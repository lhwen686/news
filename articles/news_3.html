居家耳迷走神经刺激对儿童和青少年偏头痛患者初显希望

Home-based auricular vagus nerve stimulation shows early promise for kids and teens with migraines

深度解析

该研究深入探讨了经皮耳迷走神经刺激（taVNS）作为儿童和青少年急性偏头痛的非药物治疗方案。其核心专业概念在于迷走神经的生理机制及其神经调控潜力。迷走神经是副交感神经系统的重要组成部分，负责调节心率、消化和生理稳态等内脏功能。在偏头痛发作时，中枢疼痛处理和自主神经调节功能常常失衡。taVNS通过向外耳（特别是左耳甲腔，该区域迷走神经支配丰富）的迷走神经耳支施加温和电脉冲，旨在调节异常的自主神经活动并激活大脑中的内源性疼痛抑制通路，从而达到缓解偏头痛的目的。 这项研究的行业影响深远。目前，儿科偏头痛的非药物治疗方案极度匮乏，taVNS作为一种居家可行的、非侵入性且耐受性良好的干预手段，有望填补这一临床空白，为年轻患者提供一种安全、有效的替代疗法，减少对药物的依赖。尽管本次单臂探索性研究存在局限性，例如缺乏对照组，但其成功验证了taVNS在真实世界环境中的安全性和可行性，并为未来开展更具统计学效力的假刺激对照随机试验奠定了坚实基础。这标志着向开发经临床验证的非药物治疗工具迈出了关键一步，预示着儿科偏头痛管理领域未来可能迎来重大变革。

DOI: 10.1016/j.neurom.2026.10.002 As clinicians and researchers, we often see how heavily migraine attacks weigh on children and adolescents, yet non-pharmacological, child-friendly treatment options remain strikingly scarce. For years, our team at Nanfang Hospital and collaborating institutes have been looking into ways to bridge this gap. Recently, our team set out to explore whether a gentle, at-home wearable technology—transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS)—could offer a safe and feasible way to help young patients manage acute migraine episodes. Targeting the vagus nerve through the ear To understand how this works, you first need to know about the vagus nerve. It is one of the most extensive cranial nerves in our body, acting as a major highway between the brain and the internal organs. It plays a key role in the parasympathetic nervous system to regulate involuntary functions such as heart rate, digestion, and physiological homeostasis. During a migraine attack, central pain processing and autonomic regulation can become disrupted. Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS) delivers mild electrical pulses to the auricular branch of the vagus nerve—specifically at the left cymba conchae of the outer ear, a region rich in vagal innervation. This non-invasive stimulation is thought to modulate autonomic activity and activate central pain-inhibitory pathways in the brain. Anatomical distribution of the auricular branch of the vagus nerve (ABVN) in the external ear, highlighting target areas such as the cymba conchae used for non-invasive neuromodulation. Credit: Adapted from Frontiers in Psychiatry (2018). DOI: 10.3389/fpsyt.2018.00020, licensed under CC BY 4.0 Encouraging signals in pain relief and safety In our prospective, single-arm exploratory study recently published in Neuromodulation: Technology at the Neural Interface, we evaluated 11 pediatric and adolescent patients (ages 7 to 18) who self-administered 30-minute taVNS sessions at home over a 4-week period, tracking a total of 72 treated attacks. First and foremost, the approach proved to be highly feasible and well-tolerated. Out of 72 treatment sessions, we recorded only two mild, transient local skin reactions at the stimulation site, with no serious adverse events reported. Furthermore, visual analog scale (VAS) pain scores showed a consistent downward trajectory within attacks, and medication-free 2-hour pain freedom was observed in 36.11% of the treated episodes. When comparing the 4-week intervention period to retrospective baseline data, we also noted reductions in average attack duration and monthly migraine days. Scientific caution and paving the way for controlled trials These encouraging results build directly upon our team's earlier, step-by-step groundwork in non-invasive neuromodulation. Previously, we established a rigorous methodological and technical framework for auricular vagus nerve stimulation through a published study protocol on closed-loop taVNS for stroke motor recovery, while simultaneously capturing direct early clinical signals in a case report on taVNS for pediatric chronic migraine. Combining that technical rigor with real-world pediatric observations paved a solid foundation for this prospective, single-arm exploratory study. Even with this solid groundwork in place, we believe it is crucial as scientists to interpret the findings of this early-stage trial with a high degree of caution. Because our study was designed as an open-label, single-arm trial without a control group, we cannot definitively attribute the observed pain relief or longitudinal changes solely to the taVNS intervention. Pediatric migraines can feature high variability and natural remission, and psychological expectancy or placebo effects always play a role. Additionally, our exploratory analysis of heart rate variability (HRV) did not yield statistically significant changes after correcting for multiple testing, indicating that HRV cannot currently serve as a reliable biomarker for treatment response. Despite these limitations, our findings provide a vital stepping stone. They demonstrate that home-based neuromodulation can be safely administered by children and families in a real-world setting. More importantly, they validate the operational framework needed to design our next major milestone: an adequately powered, sham-controlled randomized trial. By establishing safety and feasibility now, we hope to move closer to offering young migraine sufferers a validated, nondrug therapeutic tool for the future.
