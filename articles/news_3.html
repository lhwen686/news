研究显示，GLP-1治疗更显著减重有益于肥胖相关疾病

Study shows benefits in obesity-linked conditions of losing more weight with GLP-1 treatment

New research presented at the European Congress on Obesity (ECO 2026, Istanbul, Turkey, 12–15 May) shows that, following treatment for obesity or diabetes or both, using incretin-based drugs, losing more weight versus less weight, and losing a little weight versus gaining weight, both lead to a relatively lower risk of obesity-related conditions. The study is by Professor John Wilding of the University of Liverpool, UK, and colleagues.

In randomized trials, glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1)-based treatments (semaglutide/liraglutide) and GLP-1/GIP (Glucose-dependent Insulinotropic Polypeptide) dual agonists (tirzepatide) lead to weight reduction and decreased risk for adverse clinical outcomes.

In real-world settings, discontinuation of GLP-1-based treatments is common and there is substantial heterogeneity in weight change. Whether real-world weight change following GLP-1–based treatment initiation is associated with subsequent risk of adverse clinical outcomes is not well established. As such, the authors investigated this risk.

Using Optum Market Clarity, a US-based electronic health record and claims database, the authors calculated first-year BMI change after initiation of GLP-1-based treatment (liraglutide, semaglutide, tirzepatide) between January 2021 and June 2024 and assessed associations with subsequent clinical outcomes through June 2025.

First-year BMI change was defined as the change from baseline to the mean BMI of all measurements from 275 to 455 days following initiation.

They then assessed the association of BMI change with the risk of incident osteoarthritis, chronic kidney disease (CKD), obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), and heart failure, and estimated hazard ratios (HRs) after adjustment for demographic and clinical characteristics. Patients who experienced an outcome prior to their follow-up BMI measurements were excluded.

The study included 67,841 (75.6%) patients who initiated semaglutide, 15,661 (17.5%) tirzepatide, and 6,216 (6.9%) liraglutide. Upon treatment initiation, the mean age was 57.5 years and BMI was 34.7 kg/m2, and 61% had type 2 diabetes.

Overall, half (50.1%) of patients discontinued GLP-1-based treatment, defined by a 60-day or longer gap without medication, within one year of initiation. The study analyzed the whole cohort based on how much weight was lost, regardless of whether they continued or discontinued therapy.

In the year following treatment initiation, 27.0%, 22.4%, 14.1%, and 15.8% of patients had reductions in BMI of <5%, 5% to <10%, 10% to <15%, and ≥15%, respectively, while 20.8% experienced an increase in BMI. The incidence rate per 1,000 person-years over the subsequent mean follow-up of 11 months (meaning the 11 months after the initial year of treatment) was 21.4 for osteoarthritis, 21.1 for CKD, 20.3 for OSA, and 3.9 for heart failure.

Compared to patients with a BMI reduction of 0 to <5%, the odds for those who experienced a BMI reduction of at least 15% were 37% lower for osteoarthritis, 30% lower for CKD, 69% lower for OSA, and 32% lower for heart failure (with all results statistically significant except that for heart failure).

The odds for patients with an increase in BMI having these outcomes versus a person with 0 to <5% BMI reduction were 10% higher for osteoarthritis (a trend, not statistically significant), 14% higher for CKD (borderline significant), 22% higher for OSA, and 69% higher for heart failure (both statistically significant).

The authors conclude, "In this real-world study where half of patients discontinued GLP-1-based treatment within a year after initiation, not losing weight was associated with worse clinical outcomes while larger reductions were associated with decreased risks. These findings highlight the potential clinical importance of achieving and maintaining weight loss after GLP-1-based treatment initiation."

在欧洲肥胖大会（ECO 2026，土耳其伊斯坦布尔，5月12日至15日）上发布的新研究表明，无论是在治疗肥胖症、糖尿病或两者兼有时，使用肠促胰素类药物，相比于减轻较少体重，减轻更多体重，以及相比于体重增加，减轻少量体重，均能相对降低肥胖相关疾病的风险。这项研究由英国利物浦大学的John Wilding教授及其同事完成。

在随机试验中，基于胰高糖素样肽-1（GLP-1）的治疗（司美格鲁肽/利拉鲁肽）和GLP-1/GIP（葡萄糖依赖性促胰岛素多肽）双受体激动剂（替西帕肽）可导致体重减轻并降低不良临床结局的风险。

在真实世界环境中，GLP-1类药物的停用很常见，且体重变化存在显著异质性。基于GLP-1的治疗启动后，真实世界的体重变化是否与随后不良临床结局的风险相关，尚未得到充分证实。因此，作者们调查了这一风险。

作者们利用美国电子健康记录和索赔数据库Optum Market Clarity，计算了2021年1月至2024年6月期间启动GLP-1类治疗（利拉鲁肽、司美格鲁肽、替西帕肽）后的第一年BMI变化，并评估了其与2025年6月之前后续临床结局的关联。

第一年BMI变化定义为从基线到启动治疗后275至455天所有测量值平均BMI的变化。

随后，他们评估了BMI变化与新发骨关节炎、慢性肾脏病（CKD）、阻塞性睡眠呼吸暂停（OSA）和心力衰竭风险的关联，并在调整了人口统计学和临床特征后，估算了风险比（HR）。在随访BMI测量之前发生结局的患者被排除在外。

该研究纳入了67,841名（75.6%）启动司美格鲁肽治疗的患者，15,661名（17.5%）启动替西帕肽治疗的患者，以及6,216名（6.9%）启动利拉鲁肽治疗的患者。治疗启动时，患者平均年龄为57.5岁，平均BMI为34.7 kg/m2，其中61%患有2型糖尿病。

总体而言，半数（50.1%）患者在启动治疗一年内停用了GLP-1类药物，停用定义为连续60天或更长时间未用药。该研究根据体重减轻程度分析了整个队列，无论他们是否持续或停用治疗。

在治疗启动后的一年内，27.0%、22.4%、14.1%和15.8%的患者BMI分别降低<5%、5%至<10%、10%至<15%和≥15%，而20.8%的患者BMI有所增加。在随后的平均11个月（即治疗初始年份之后的11个月）随访期间，每1,000人年的发病率，骨关节炎为21.4例，CKD为21.1例，OSA为20.3例，心力衰竭为3.9例。

与BMI降低0至<5%的患者相比，BMI降低至少15%的患者，骨关节炎的发生几率降低37%，CKD降低30%，OSA降低69%，心力衰竭降低32%（除心力衰竭外，所有结果均具统计学意义）。

与BMI降低0至<5%的患者相比，BMI增加的患者发生这些结局的几率，骨关节炎高10%（趋势，无统计学意义），CKD高14%（临界显著），OSA高22%，心力衰竭高69%（两者均具统计学意义）。

作者总结道：“在这项真实世界研究中，半数患者在启动GLP-1类治疗一年内停药，未减轻体重与更差的临床结局相关，而体重减轻幅度越大则与风险降低相关。这些发现强调了在启动GLP-1类治疗后实现并维持体重减轻的潜在临床重要性。”
