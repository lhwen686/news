Suicide is a major public health concern. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), it is responsible for more than 700,000 deaths a year. In Spain, it is the leading cause of death from non-natural causes, above traffic accidents. The number of suicides in recent years has reached record highs, especially since the COVID-19 pandemic. In 2022, 4,227 people (74% of them men and 26% women) died from this cause. In other words, an average of 12 people a day died by suicide.

Suicide has traditionally been taboo, bringing stigma both to those taking their own lives and to their families, who suffer shame and guilt in addition to the grief of losing a loved one. According to a study by the Universitat Oberta de Catalunya (UOC) analyzing discourse on suicide in the press, and specifically the metaphors used to discuss it, there has been a paradigm shift: Compared with the past, when newspaper style guides prohibited mentioning it (to avoid emulation suicides), nowadays suicide is discussed openly, to remove the stigma around it.

"The use of metaphors in the press responds to a cognitive mechanism; they are not neutral and reveal the type of representation transmitted," said the researchers.

Marta Coll-Florit, Ph.D. in the information and knowledge society, and researcher at the GRIAL research group of the UOC-TRÀNSIC research center, led a study titled Suicide Metaphors in the Press: Breaking the Taboo, published in Metaphor and Symbol, presenting the findings of an analysis of discourse on suicide in the El País, La Vanguardia and ABC newspapers between 2020 and 2023.

Coll-Florit was motivated to carry out the study by a combination of personal experience and scholarly interest.

"Having unfortunately experienced the impact of suicide in my own family, I'm very aware of how powerful the taboo around it is. Although suicide has been studied at length in disciplines such as psychology and sociology, it's still practically uncharted territory in the field of cognitive linguistics," she said.

Individual phenomenon versus collective responsibility: two contrasting approaches

Coll-Florit used corpus linguistics methods to analyze 243 news items about suicide in three newspapers with divergent ideologies. The study identified 509 metaphors and found that the political ideology of the newspaper did not affect the approach to suicide. The most common metaphor for suicide was war, an enemy to be fought. Associations with illness were also used, with words such as epidemic, pandemic or contagion. The researcher believes that these terms were influenced by the pandemic that was ongoing at the time. Other metaphors referred to suicide as a journey, a criminal offense or a hidden object.

"The metaphors used most often in news stories about suicide serve to confront the silence. For example, the war metaphor can portray the taboo as an enemy to be beaten, and hidden object metaphors can condemn the fact that suicide has been covered up. Also, talking about it is seen as an antidote, within the illness metaphor, or as a necessary path for prevention, with the journey metaphor. The silence is even personified as a living organism that has been silenced, reinforcing the idea that breaking the taboo is both liberating and beneficial for public health," said Coll-Florit, who is also a member of the UOC's Faculty of Arts and Humanities.

The press's use of metaphors when referring to suicide is not a matter of style or ornamentation. It actually relates to a cognitive mechanism. The metaphors used are not neutral, and analysis shows the type of depiction of suicide they convey.

The UOC researcher found that suicide is sometimes presented as a purely individual phenomenon. For example, when it is described as an escape or a crime: "This places all the focus on the individual's decision. An example of this is the use of metaphors for suicide as homicide against oneself, with no mention of the shortcomings of the system. Furthermore, they evoke ideas of cowardice, weakness or selfishness. This stigmatizes victims and leaves their families burdened with a devastating sense of guilt," said Coll-Florit.

Other metaphors—fortunately, the majority—focus on collective responsibility and can have a positive effect: "Framing suicide as a collective challenge or a shared journey of prevention, rather than an individual process, shifts the attribution of blame away from the people directly affected by it," said the researcher.

Coll-Florit has extensive experience in analyzing how conceptual metaphors can help understand mental disorders such as depression or schizophrenia. In this field, she coordinated the first Mental Health Metaphor Dictionary, a UOC initiative to raise public awareness of these disorders.

A tragedy that can be avoided with everyone's support

The researcher believes that the focus must shift away from individual responsibility toward collective responsibility: "We must stop viewing suicide as an individual struggle and reframe it as a public health issue that requires a social and health support network. Responsible journalism must avoid presenting suicide as a way out for mental suffering."

This can be achieved by providing context and expert opinions and, above all, by "conveying the idea that, with the right help and resources, suicide can be prevented."

自杀是一个重大的公共卫生问题。据世界卫生组织（WHO）称，每年有超过70万人死于自杀。在西班牙，它是非自然原因导致的头号死亡原因，高于交通事故。近年来，自杀人数达到了历史新高，尤其是在COVID-19大流行之后。2022年，有4,227人（其中74%为男性，26%为女性）死于此因。换言之，平均每天有12人死于自杀。

自杀传统上一直是一个禁忌话题，给自杀者及其家人带来了耻辱。除了失去亲人的悲痛之外，家人还承受着羞耻和内疚。根据加泰罗尼亚开放大学（UOC）一项分析媒体对自杀的讨论，特别是所使用的隐喻的研究，范式已经发生了转变：与过去报纸风格指南禁止提及自杀（以避免模仿性自杀）相比，如今自杀被公开讨论，以消除其周围的耻辱感。

研究人员表示：“媒体中隐喻的使用回应了一种认知机制；它们并非中立，而是揭示了所传达的表征类型。”

信息与知识社会学博士、UOC-TRÀNSIC研究中心GRIAL研究小组的研究员Marta Coll-Florit领导了一项题为《媒体中的自杀隐喻：打破禁忌》的研究，该研究发表在《隐喻与符号》杂志上，呈现了2020年至2023年间《国家报》、《先锋报》和《ABC报》对自杀讨论的分析结果。

Coll-Florit开展这项研究的动机是个人经历和学术兴趣的结合。

她说：“由于不幸在我的家庭中经历过自杀的影响，我非常清楚围绕它的禁忌有多么强大。尽管自杀已在心理学和社会学等学科中得到了广泛研究，但在认知语言学领域，它仍然几乎是一片未开垦的领域。”

个体现象与集体责任：两种对比鲜明的方法

Coll-Florit使用语料库语言学方法，分析了三家意识形态不同的报纸中243篇关于自杀的新闻报道。该研究识别出509个隐喻，发现报纸的政治意识形态并未影响对自杀的看法。最常见的自杀隐喻是“战争”，一个需要与之搏斗的敌人。还使用了与疾病相关的联想，例如“流行病”、“大流行”或“传染”。研究人员认为，这些术语受到了当时正在进行的大流行的影响。其他隐喻则将自杀称为“旅程”、“刑事犯罪”或“隐藏的物体”。

“新闻报道中最常使用的自杀隐喻有助于对抗沉默。例如，战争隐喻可以将禁忌描绘成一个需要被击败的敌人，而隐藏物体隐喻则可以谴责自杀被掩盖的事实。此外，谈论它在疾病隐喻中被视为一种解药，或在旅程隐喻中被视为预防的必要途径。沉默甚至被拟人化为一个被压制着的活生生的有机体，这强化了打破禁忌既是解放性的又对公共卫生有益的观点，”同时也是UOC艺术与人文学院成员的Coll-Florit说。

媒体在提及自杀时使用隐喻并非风格或修饰问题。它实际上与一种认知机制相关。所使用的隐喻并非中立，分析表明它们传达了何种类型的自杀描绘。

这位UOC研究员发现，自杀有时被呈现为纯粹的个体现象。例如，当它被描述为“逃避”或“犯罪”时：“这把所有焦点都放在了个体的决定上。一个例子就是将自杀隐喻为自杀性杀人，却没有提及系统存在的缺陷。此外，它们还唤起了懦弱、软弱或自私的观念。这使受害者蒙羞，并给他们的家人带来了毁灭性的内疚感，”Coll-Florit说。

另一些隐喻——幸运的是，大部分——侧重于集体责任，并能产生积极影响：“将自杀框定为一个集体挑战或共同的预防之旅，而不是一个个体过程，可以将责任归因从直接受影响的人身上转移开，”研究人员说。

Coll-Florit在分析概念隐喻如何帮助理解抑郁症或精神分裂症等精神障碍方面拥有丰富的经验。在该领域，她协调了首部《精神健康隐喻词典》，这是UOC旨在提高公众对这些障碍认识的一项倡议。

一个通过大家的支持可以避免的悲剧

研究人员认为，必须将重点从个体责任转移到集体责任上：“我们必须停止将自杀视为一场个体斗争，并将其重新定义为一个需要社会和健康支持网络的公共卫生问题。负责任的新闻业必须避免将自杀呈现为摆脱精神痛苦的出路。”

这可以通过提供背景信息和专家意见来实现，最重要的是，“传达这样的理念：通过正确的帮助和资源，自杀是可以预防的。”
