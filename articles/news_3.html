唾液皮质醇模式与11年间的认知衰退相关

Salivary cortisol patterns linked to cognitive decline over 11 years

深度解析

本研究揭示了唾液皮质醇水平及昼夜节律模式与晚年认知衰退之间存在关联，并突出了不同种族间皮质醇模式的差异。核心医学机制在于，皮质醇作为一种由下丘脑-垂体-肾上腺（HPA）轴调节的糖皮质激素，是机体应对生理和心理应激的关键介质。它能穿透血脑屏障，与脑内，特别是海马体和前额叶皮层等参与认知功能的区域的特异性受体结合。长期或慢性高水平皮质醇暴露可能导致神经元损伤、突触可塑性受损以及神经炎症，进而加速认知功能衰退。研究发现黑人参与者皮质醇水平较低且昼夜变化平坦，这可能反映了慢性应激导致的HPA轴功能障碍或适应性改变，即所谓的“异体负荷”，这种生理负荷最终仍与认知衰退风险相关。这项研究对于行业具有深远影响：一是将唾液皮质醇作为潜在的认知衰退生物标志物，为早期风险评估提供了新思路；二是提示在临床实践中，应关注慢性应激管理和个体化的皮质醇模式评估，尤其是在不同种族群体中，以制定更精准的预防和干预策略，促进健康认知老龄化。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: CC0 Public Domain In a study published in the journal JAMA Network Open, an irregular pattern and consistently higher levels of the stress hormone cortisol in saliva were associated with cognitive decline later in life. Black participants specifically had lower overall cortisol levels and less change in their levels during the day than white participants, according to newly published research. "We tested salivary cortisol since it's actively circulating in the body and interacting with organs, including the brain," said Ted K.S. Ng, Ph.D., study author and translational gerontologist and neuroscientist at Rush. "The study design shows us that there are racial differences in cortisol patterns, which may reflect regular exposure to stress and its lasting impact on communities that face social and economic conditions." Over a 24-hour period, cortisol was measured at three points during the day: morning, afternoon and evening. "The cortisol patterns differed between Black and white participants," Ng said. "Black participants had lower overall levels, less change during the day and a flatter pattern from morning to night. But even with these differences, cortisol patterns were linked to cognitive decline in similar ways in both groups. People with more variation during the day had slower cognitive decline, while those with the highest overall cortisol levels had faster decline." The study followed 3,895 people with an average age of 76.7. It included participants from the Chicago Health and Aging Project (CHAP): 2,503 Black participants and 1,392 white participants. The CHAP participants in this study were from four neighborhoods on Chicago's South Side and were followed for up to 11 years. "A strength of this study is that more than 60% of the study participants were Black older adults," Ng said. "Many studies have been conducted primarily in white populations. This gives us an opportunity to examine differences in cortisol in saliva across racial groups while examining how cortisol patterns affect cognitive decline." Releasing cortisol is the body's natural way of responding to stress. Cortisol crosses the blood-brain barrier and binds to receptors in areas of the brain responsible for cognition. This research highlights the potential long-term effects of stress on brain function. "These findings suggest that this stress-related biomarker may one day identify people who may be at higher risk for cognitive decline," Ng said. "There are some disadvantages to studies that examine cortisol levels, so there would need to be more studies to examine what's happening over a longer period of time in order to find new ways of supporting healthy cognitive aging." The study did not find that cortisol patterns predicted who developed Alzheimer's disease during the follow-up period. Publication details Ted K. S. Ng et al, Salivary Cortisol and Cognitive Decline and Alzheimer Disease in Older Adults, JAMA Network Open (2026).
