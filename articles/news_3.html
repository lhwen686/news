深度解析

此项研究深入揭示了肝脏生物钟在代谢调控中的关键作用，特别是其如何通过精准的时间依赖性蛋白质分泌来协调全身代谢。核心机制在于，肝脏的昼夜节律钟（内部时钟）并非简单地调节蛋白质的合成与分解，而是精确控制了特定蛋白质（如肝因子）的释放时间点。这种时序性交流使得肝脏能与脂肪组织等其他代谢器官在一天中的特定时刻进行优化互动，从而影响脂肪分解（lipolysis）等生理过程。当这种时间协调机制紊乱时，个体罹患肥胖症、糖尿病等代谢性疾病的风险便会增加。 这项发现对临床医学和药物研发具有深远影响。首先，它强调了“时间医学”或“时辰药理学”的重要性，提示未来针对代谢性疾病的治疗方案，如内皮抑素相关疗法，其给药时机可能与疗效密切相关。其次，深入理解这些定时分泌的肝因子，有望发现新型的治疗靶点和生物活性多肽（如文中所提的“下一个GLP-1类药物”），为代谢紊乱提供创新干预策略。此外，该研究也为优化饮食模式和生活作息提供了科学依据，倡导顺应身体生物钟的健康习惯，以期更有效地预防和管理代谢性疾病。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain What if one of the keys to a healthier metabolism is understanding how and when the liver communicates with the rest of the body? Researchers at UT Health San Antonio, the academic health center of The University of Texas at San Antonio (UT San Antonio), have uncovered how the liver's internal clock coordinates the release of proteins that help regulate metabolism. The study, published June 2 in Nature Communications, found that many of these proteins are released according to a precise daily schedule, allowing the liver to communicate with organs such as fat tissue at specific times of day. The findings could help guide future research on metabolic disease, as well as the timing of meals and medication. How the body keeps time For many biological functions, timing is everything. Nearly every cell in the body contains its own molecular clock, helping coordinate daily processes such as metabolism. Researchers are working to better understand how these clocks communicate with one another. "Any cell with a nucleus is likely to express the molecular clock. We are interested in how cells work together and coordinate their activities," said Kevin Koronowski, Ph.D., assistant professor in the Department of Biochemistry and Structural Biology at the Joe R. and Teresa Lozano Long School of Medicine and the Sam and Ann Barshop Institute for Longevity and Aging Studies. "The clock is one way the body can organize physiology in an efficient and appropriate manner." Researchers have long known that disrupted sleep schedules and irregular eating patterns increase the risk of obesity, diabetes and other metabolic disorders. Because the liver plays a central role in metabolism, scientists wanted to better understand how its internal clock regulates the release of proteins that communicate with other organs. Protein secretion during acute incubation of liver tissue ex vivo. Credit: Nature Communications (2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-73840-4 Christopher Litwin, a fourth-year Ph.D. student in Koronowski's lab and first author of the study, said that while researchers have known the liver regulates the secretion of some proteins, they lacked a well-developed measure of how and when proteins were released. "We thought the time-dependent release of proteins may be important for coordinating metabolism in tissues like fat or muscle," said Litwin. "The core idea is that the clock regulates protein secretion and can influence metabolism across the day and in other tissues." When endostatin works best One of the study's biggest discoveries was that a liver protein called endostatin was most effective at helping break down fat when it was released on the liver's natural daily schedule. The finding suggests that future treatments based on this protein may work best if they're given at the right time of day. The research team is continuing to investigate new approaches that could lead to future therapeutic benefits. "We are interested in developing and characterizing novel peptides. We want to find the next GLP-1 drug, or even something better that is waiting to be discovered," said Koronowski.
