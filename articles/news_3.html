蛋白质“开关”决定脂肪肉瘤细胞是否会变得具有侵袭性

Protein 'switch' determines whether liposarcoma cells will become aggressive

深度解析

这项研究揭示了脂肪肉瘤异质性及肿瘤进展的关键分子机制。核心概念在于，肿瘤内部存在类似干细胞的细胞群，具备高度的细胞状态可塑性，能够向高分化（WD）或去分化（DD）两种不同侵袭性表型分化。通过单细胞核RNA测序技术，研究人员精准地识别了这些具有可塑性的肿瘤细胞，并发现过氧化物酶体增殖物激活受体γ（PPAR-γ）蛋白作为决定细胞命运的关键“开关”。PPAR-γ的高表达促使细胞向更成熟、脂肪样的高分化状态发展，而其调控失衡则可能导致细胞停留在不成熟、快速增殖的去分化状态。这一发现对肿瘤学领域具有深远影响。它不仅解释了脂肪肉瘤治疗后复发和肿瘤内部不同区域侵袭性差异的生物学基础，更重要的是，将PPAR-γ或相关干细胞样细胞群确定为潜在的治疗靶点。未来可能开发出通过调控PPAR-γ活性，诱导去分化肿瘤细胞再分化，或抑制其恶性转化的靶向药物，从而为高复发率的脂肪肉瘤患者提供更精准、更有效的治疗策略，改善临床预后。

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aea6516 New research from an expert in cancer biology explains what triggers liposarcoma cells to become more or less aggressive and identifies potential targets that could keep aggressive tumors in check. A study led by Blake Wilde, Ph.D., of Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center highlights how this discovery may pave the way for new treatment options for this cancer. The findings are published in the journal Science Advances. More than half of all people with liposarcoma will see their cancer return after treatment, underscoring the need for new and better treatment options. The two most common types of this cancer, which begins in fat tissue, are well-differentiated (WD) and dedifferentiated (DD) liposarcoma, explains the study's first author, Blake Wilde, Ph.D., assistant professor of oncology in the Departments of Urology and Cell Stress Biology at Roswell Park. While WD liposarcoma tends to grow slowly and usually can be managed with surgery, DD liposarcoma grows faster and is more likely to spread and resist treatment. One tumor can contain both types in different regions, with the dominant type affecting how the cancer behaves. In some cases, WD tumor cells rapidly become DD cells, while some DD tumors begin to grow more slowly. At the same time, some tumors are found to have changed from one type to another when they reappear after treatment. What causes these transformations? A switch between tumor states Using single-nucleus RNA sequencing, Wilde and a team of colleagues analyzed tissue samples from patients with liposarcomas that contained both WD and DD regions. They discovered tumor cells similar to stem cells that can transform into either WD or DD liposarcoma cells to resupply different regions of the tumor. "We found that a protein called PPAR-gamma acts as a switch that helps determine whether these stem-like cells will develop into the more mature, fat-like state of well-differentiated liposarcoma or remain in the immature, rapidly growing state of dedifferentiated, or poorly differentiated, liposarcoma," he says. Targets for slowing progression "This discovery explains how different forms of liposarcoma can arise and be maintained within the same tumor," Wilde says. "It also points to the stem-like cells and biological programs that may need to be targeted to prevent a manageable tumor from progressing into a more dangerous form." Wilde's work on the study began when he was a postdoctoral scholar at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) and was conducted with co-authors from UCLA. Publication details Blake R.
