An investigational targeted therapy designed to block one of the most common genetic drivers of pancreatic cancer has shown promising early results when combined with standard first-line chemotherapy, according to research presented today at the ESMO Gastrointestinal Cancers Congress 2026.

The phase I/II multicenter study, led by researchers from Dana-Farber Cancer Institute in Boston, evaluated zoldonrasib in patients with metastatic pancreatic cancer whose tumors carried a KRAS G12D mutation. The early findings showed high rates of tumor shrinkage, substantial reductions in cancer DNA detected in the bloodstream, and no unexpected safety concerns.

Pancreatic cancer is one of the world's deadliest cancers and remains one of the most difficult to treat. Since pancreatic cancer often causes few specific symptoms in its early stages, many patients are diagnosed only after the disease has become metastatic, when curative surgery is usually no longer possible. Chemotherapy remains the standard first-line treatment for metastatic pancreatic cancer, but its ability to control the disease is limited, and the five-year survival rate for metastatic disease remains approximately 3%.

For decades, scientists have known that pancreatic cancer is driven by mutations in the KRAS gene, which produces a protein that regulates normal cell growth. When KRAS is mutated, it continuously signals cancer cells to grow and divide. Around 90% of pancreatic cancers harbor a KRAS mutation, and approximately 40% carry the KRAS G12D subtype, the most common KRAS mutation found in the disease.

Until recently, KRAS G12D was widely regarded as an "undruggable" target. Zoldonrasib is one of a new generation of investigational medicines designed to selectively inhibit this mutation. Unlike chemotherapy, which affects rapidly dividing cells throughout the body, zoldonrasib acts directly on the abnormal KRAS G12D protein that drives tumor growth. Researchers are investigating whether combining this targeted approach with chemotherapy can improve patient outcomes compared with chemotherapy alone.

Dr. Teresa Macarulla, head of the medical oncology department at Hospital Clínic Barcelona in Spain and congress co-chair, who was not involved in the study, commented: "The emergence of therapies designed specifically against KRAS G12D represents one of the most promising areas of research in pancreatic cancer today. For many years, this mutation was considered impossible to target, making these early findings particularly important."

The study enrolled 81 patients with previously untreated metastatic pancreatic cancer harboring a KRAS G12D mutation at multiple cancer centers in the United States. Of these, 41 received zoldonrasib in combination with modified FOLFIRINOX, and 40 received zoldonrasib plus gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel.

Among patients with sufficient follow-up for efficacy assessment, objective response rates reached 82% in patients receiving zoldonrasib plus modified FOLFIRINOX and 61% in those receiving zoldonrasib plus gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel. Disease control was achieved in 96% and 90% of patients, respectively.

Strong molecular responses were also observed. Every evaluable patient experienced at least a 50% reduction in circulating tumor DNA carrying the KRAS G12D mutation. Complete clearance of detectable mutant circulating tumor DNA was achieved in 47% of evaluable patients receiving modified FOLFIRINOX and 71% of those receiving gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel.

The safety profile was consistent with known chemotherapy-related adverse events, with no additional toxicities identified. The most common treatment-related adverse events were diarrhea, nausea and fatigue with zoldonrasib plus modified FOLFIRINOX, and fatigue, nausea and decreased neutrophil counts with zoldonrasib plus gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel. Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events occurred in 61% and 80% of patients, respectively, and no treatment-related deaths were reported.

"These findings are encouraging because they suggest that targeting KRAS G12D can be combined with standard chemotherapy without introducing unexpected safety concerns," Macarulla said. "However, this remains an early study involving a relatively small number of patients, and it will now be important to confirm these results in the ongoing randomized phase III trial."

These findings supported the launch of RASolute 305, a global, randomized phase III trial comparing zoldonrasib plus chemotherapy with placebo plus chemotherapy in patients with previously untreated KRAS G12D metastatic pancreatic cancer.

Looking ahead, Macarulla said, "If these findings are confirmed, this approach could represent an important advance toward more personalized treatment for pancreatic cancer. It could also be explored in earlier stages of disease, where greater tumor shrinkage before surgery may improve patient outcomes."

根据今天在2026年ESMO胃肠道癌症大会上公布的研究，一项旨在阻断胰腺癌最常见遗传驱动因素之一的在研靶向疗法，与标准一线化疗联合使用时，已显示出有希望的早期结果。

这项由波士顿达纳法伯癌症研究所研究人员主导的I/II期多中心研究，评估了zoldonrasib在肿瘤携带KRAS G12D突变的转移性胰腺癌患者中的疗效。早期结果显示，肿瘤缩小率高，血液中检测到的癌细胞DNA显著减少，并且未出现意料之外的安全问题。

胰腺癌是世界上最致命的癌症之一，并且仍然是最难治疗的癌症之一。由于胰腺癌在早期通常只引起少量特异性症状，许多患者只有在疾病发生转移后才被诊断出来，此时根治性手术通常已不再可能。化疗仍然是转移性胰腺癌的标准一线治疗方法，但其控制疾病的能力有限，转移性疾病的五年生存率仍约为3%。

几十年来，科学家们已经知道胰腺癌是由KRAS基因突变驱动的，KRAS基因产生一种调节正常细胞生长的蛋白质。当KRAS发生突变时，它会持续向癌细胞发出信号，促使其生长和分裂。大约90%的胰腺癌携带KRAS突变，其中约40%携带KRAS G12D亚型，这是该疾病中最常见的KRAS突变。

直到最近，KRAS G12D仍被广泛认为是“不可成药”的靶点。Zoldonrasib是新一代在研药物之一，旨在选择性地抑制这种突变。与影响全身快速分裂细胞的化疗不同，zoldonrasib直接作用于驱动肿瘤生长的异常KRAS G12D蛋白。研究人员正在探讨将这种靶向方法与化疗相结合是否能比单独化疗更好地改善患者预后。

西班牙巴塞罗那临床医院肿瘤内科主任兼大会联合主席Teresa Macarulla博士（未参与本研究）评论道：“专门针对KRAS G12D的疗法的出现，代表了当今胰腺癌研究中最有前景的领域之一。多年来，这种突变一直被认为无法作为靶点，这使得这些早期发现尤为重要。”

该研究在美国多个癌症中心招募了81名既往未接受治疗的、携带KRAS G12D突变的转移性胰腺癌患者。其中，41名患者接受了zoldonrasib联合改良FOLFIRINOX方案治疗，40名患者接受了zoldonrasib联合吉西他滨和白蛋白紫杉醇治疗。

在有足够随访数据进行疗效评估的患者中，接受zoldonrasib联合改良FOLFIRINOX方案治疗的患者客观缓解率达到82%，接受zoldonrasib联合吉西他滨加白蛋白紫杉醇治疗的患者客观缓解率为61%。疾病控制率分别达到96%和90%。

研究还观察到强烈的分子学应答。每位可评估患者的携带KRAS G12D突变的循环肿瘤DNA均减少至少50%。在接受改良FOLFIRINOX方案治疗的可评估患者中，47%实现了可检测突变循环肿瘤DNA的完全清除；在接受吉西他滨加白蛋白紫杉醇治疗的患者中，这一比例为71%。

安全性特征与已知的化疗相关不良事件一致，未发现额外毒性。Zoldonrasib联合改良FOLFIRINOX方案最常见的治疗相关不良事件是腹泻、恶心和疲劳；zoldonrasib联合吉西他滨/白蛋白紫杉醇方案最常见的是疲劳、恶心和中性粒细胞计数下降。3级或更高级别的治疗相关不良事件分别发生在61%和80%的患者中，未报告治疗相关死亡。

Macarulla表示：“这些发现令人鼓舞，因为它们表明靶向KRAS G12D可以与标准化疗联合使用，而不会引入意想不到的安全问题。然而，这仍然是一项涉及相对少量患者的早期研究，现在重要的是在正在进行的随机III期试验中确认这些结果。”

这些发现支持了RASolute 305的启动，这是一项全球性、随机III期试验，旨在比较zoldonrasib联合化疗与安慰剂联合化疗在既往未接受治疗的KRAS G12D转移性胰腺癌患者中的疗效。

展望未来，Macarulla表示：“如果这些发现得到证实，这种方法可能代表着胰腺癌个性化治疗的重要进展。它也可能在疾病的早期阶段进行探索，例如术前更大程度的肿瘤缩小可能会改善患者预后。”
