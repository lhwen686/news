As the oldest person elected president of the United States, Donald Trump has long faced questions about his health. But is it appropriate for doctors to comment publicly on a president's mental health? In an opinion article published in The BMJ, David Nicholl and Trisha Greenhalgh examine the ethical tensions involved. While heads of state are entitled to medical confidentiality, their decisions can have far-reaching consequences, raising the question of whether professional norms against public commentary should ever be set aside.

The authors emphasize that professional standards generally prohibit clinicians from offering diagnoses without direct assessment. They caution strongly against attempts to diagnose specific conditions—such as neurodegenerative disorders—on the basis of media reports or publicly available material.

In particular, they note that conditions such as behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD) require detailed clinical evaluation and appropriate imaging, and cannot be reliably inferred from observed behavior alone.

They acknowledge that some commentators have interpreted public statements and behaviors as evidence of cognitive decline, but stress that such claims fall well short of the standards required for diagnosis.

"In our view, it would be impossible to make a diagnosis of probable bvFTD without full clinical assessment and appropriate imaging," they write.

At the same time, the authors distinguish between making a clinical diagnosis and expressing broader, clinically informed concerns. They note that in 2016, senior psychiatrists raised concerns about Donald Trump's mental fitness for office while explicitly refraining from diagnosis, and called for an impartial medical assessment.

On 29 November 2016, three US psychiatrists wrote to then-president Barack Obama about their concerns about the mental health of Donald Trump. They said that symptoms that had been widely reported had led them to question his fitness for office.

"We strongly recommend that, in preparation for assuming these responsibilities, he receive a full medical and neurological evaluation by an impartial team of investigators," they wrote.

Nicholl and Greenhalgh agree that such an assessment remains an appropriate course of action.

The article concludes that greater clarity is needed between clinical judgment and public commentary, and that doctors should exercise caution, humility, and adherence to professional standards when engaging in debates about the health of public figures.

作为美国当选总统中年龄最大者，唐纳德·特朗普的健康状况长期以来一直备受质疑。但医生公开评论总统的精神健康是否恰当？在大英医学杂志（The BMJ）发表的一篇评论文章中，大卫·尼科尔（David Nicholl）和特丽莎·格林哈尔格（Trisha Greenhalgh）审视了其中涉及的伦理困境。虽然国家元首享有医疗保密权，但他们的决策可能产生深远影响，这就引发了一个问题：反对公开评论的职业规范是否应该在某些情况下被搁置。

作者们强调，职业标准通常禁止临床医生在未经直接评估的情况下做出诊断。他们强烈警告不要试图仅凭媒体报道或公开可获取的材料诊断特定疾病，例如神经退行性疾病。

他们特别指出，诸如行为变异型额颞叶痴呆（bvFTD）等疾病需要详细的临床评估和适当的影像学检查，不能仅仅通过观察到的行为可靠推断。

他们承认，一些评论员将公开声明和行为解读为认知能力下降的证据，但强调此类主张远未达到诊断所需的标准。

他们写道：“在我们看来，如果没有全面的临床评估和适当的影像学检查，就不可能诊断出可能的行为变异型额颞叶痴呆（bvFTD）。”

与此同时，作者们区分了做出临床诊断和表达更广泛、具有临床依据的担忧。他们指出，在2016年，资深精神科医生对唐纳德·特朗普担任公职的精神状况提出了担忧，同时明确避免做出诊断，并呼吁进行公正的医学评估。

2016年11月29日，三名美国精神科医生致信时任总统巴拉克·奥巴马，表达了他们对唐纳德·特朗普精神健康的担忧。他们表示，被广泛报道的症状让他们质疑特朗普是否适合担任总统职务。

他们写道：“我们强烈建议，在准备承担这些职责之前，他应由一个公正的调查团队进行全面的医学和神经学评估。”

尼科尔和格林哈尔格同意，这种评估仍然是一种适当的行动方案。

文章总结说，临床判断与公开评论之间需要更清晰的界限，医生在参与有关公众人物健康的辩论时，应保持谨慎、谦逊并遵守职业标准。
