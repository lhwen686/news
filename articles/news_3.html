深度解析

此新闻揭示了西尼罗病毒（WNV）在欧洲的扩散趋势及其公共卫生挑战。西尼罗病毒是一种由库蚊传播的黄病毒科病毒，多数感染者表现为轻微流感样症状，但少数病例可发展为神经侵袭性疾病，如脑炎和脑膜炎，严重者可致命。这种神经并发症源于病毒对中枢神经系统的直接损害和炎症反应。文章强调了WNV传播地域的扩大和入侵性蚊种（如亚洲虎蚊）的蔓延，后者不仅能传播WNV，还是登革热和基孔肯雅热等其他虫媒病的宿主，极大增加了疾病爆发的风险。鉴于目前尚无针对WNV的特异性疫苗和治疗药物，预防措施和病媒控制成为核心的公共卫生策略。这包括加强蚊虫监测、提高社区参与度以及实施环境友好的靶向灭蚊方案。这一状况凸显了全球气候变化和人类活动对病媒传播疾病流行病学的影响，并对各国卫生系统提出了跨区域合作和综合防控的严峻要求。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Jimmy Chan from Pexels Twelve countries in Europe have reported locally acquired cases of West Nile virus so far this year, the EU health agency said Friday, urging the continent to boost its control efforts. The mosquito-borne virus can cause symptoms similar to the flu, but in extreme cases, it can result in tremors, fever, comas and a lethal swelling of brain tissue known as encephalitis. It can also cause meningitis. The 12 countries are Albania, Austria, France, Germany, Greece, Italy, Kosovo, the Netherlands, North Macedonia, Romania, Serbia and Spain, with infections reported in 99 regions, according to the Stockholm-based European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC). Italy and Greece had the highest numbers of cases—311 and 157, respectively—while 21 regions across Europe reported their first-ever cases. By comparison, 67 regions in nine European countries had reported cases during the corresponding period in August 2025. "The expansion of West Nile virus transmission as well as the spread of invasive mosquito species demonstrate why Europe needs to strengthen its mosquito-control capacity," Celine Gossner, ECDC's head of food-, water-, vector-borne and zoonotic diseases, said in a statement. "For effective mosquito control, we need close cooperation in Europe and an integrated approach that brings together mosquito monitoring, community engagement and targeted mosquito control solutions that take environmental impact into account." In Europe, most human infections occur during mosquito season, which generally runs from late spring to autumn, with a peak in transmission between July and September. There are no approved human vaccines and no specific treatments for the virus, according to the WHO. Care is limited to managing symptoms and providing supportive hospital treatment for severe cases. While the mosquitoes that spread West Nile virus (Culex mosquitoes) are already widely present in Europe, the Asian tiger mosquito (Aedes albopictus), which can transmit the dengue and chikungunya viruses, is now established in 16 European countries, double the number from 12 years ago, the ECDC said.
