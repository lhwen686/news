Intracranial local field potentials offer clues to prolonged disorders of consciousness

本文深入探讨了严重脑损伤后长期意识障碍（pDOC）诊断的挑战及其解决方案。核心专业概念在于局部场电位（LFPs）作为一种精确的生物标志物，通过直接测量颅内电信号来评估患者的意识水平。从医学机制上看，脑损伤损害了神经细胞和网络，特别是中断了电信号流向丘脑等深层脑区，导致关键脑区通信减弱，大脑功能受损。健康大脑维持着激活性慢信号和镇静性快信号的平衡与协调流动，而pDOC患者则表现为信号减弱、失衡和混乱，从而降低了意识和反应能力。技术原理方面，LFPs通过分析脑电信号的复杂性、协调性和对刺激（尤其是丘脑刺激）的反应，揭示患者“隐藏的意识”。其行业影响深远：LFP的临床应用有望显著降低pDOC的误诊率，实现更精准的诊断和个性化治疗方案，这将极大提升神经危重症患者的护理质量，并解决由此引发的伦理困境。未来在更大患者群体中进行研究，并制定严格的伦理指南，对于推动LFPs成为常规临床诊断工具至关重要。

英文原文

Original
Prolonged disorders of consciousness (pDOC) represent states in which patients fail to show reliable evidence of awareness after severe brain injury. Despite the availability of validated tools, the misdiagnosis rate associated with pDOC is considerably high. Local field potentials (LFPs) provide data for calibrating non-invasive biomarkers and help in understanding mechanistic substrates of consciousness. The study presents current evidence on intracranial LFP features in pDOC. The accurate detection of pDOC is challenging in patients with severe brain injuries, and the incorrect detection limits treatment effectiveness. Current evidence indicates a 40% false diagnosis even with the use of advanced detection tools. This is mainly due to patient conditions - exhaustion, sedation, or unresponsive conditions. At present, doctors depend on various brain scanning techniques as the confirmatory procedure for identifying consciousness in patients. The researchers from Hebei Medical University reviewed several scientific research databases to overview the current evidence on pDOC management methods. Their major focus was on the studies that directly measured the brain's electrical signaling in pDOC patients, termed "local field potential." The study led by Dr. Conghui Li and Dr. Yi Yang was published in Volume 12, article number 22, on July 13, 2026, in the Chinese Neurosurgical Journal. Dr. Conghui Li said, "We aim to analyze how the brain electrical signal recordings support the pDOC diagnosis and study the possible outcomes and limitations of this technique." This review analysis proposes a brain communication network model to explain how brain injury leads to long-term unconsciousness. According to this model, the brain injury damages or destroys many nerve cells, disrupting the flow of electrical signals to the deep brain areas like the thalamus. As a result, the communication between important brain regions is weakened, thereby reducing the brain functioning. Supporting this theory, four major brain electrical signaling activity measures are identified that help the researchers to understand the level of consciousness. The healthy conscious brain maintains a constant, organized, and balanced flow of activating slow and calming fast signals of both the rhythmic and non-rhythmic signal types. Interestingly, different areas of the active brain keep communicating with each other in a controlled, nonchaotic manner. According to another scientific theory, an active brain does multitask - each part of the brain processes its own information as well as keeps its communication network functional. The severe brain injury disrupts all these balanced signaling pathways and communication networks: the fast, active brain waves shift to weaker brain waves, while slower signals become more dominant. Furthermore, the coordinated functioning of multiple brain areas is impacted, and instead of a continuous flow, the brain sends short, simple, and less organized signals. The ability of the brain to process information is also declined during the state of unconsciousness. Collectively, these changes reduce the person's awareness and responsiveness. Current studies comparing the healthy brain with the severely injured brain identify poorer communication between the thalamus and the outer layer of the brain in patients with pDOC. Measuring how complex this signaling activity is - particularly after brain stimulation - helps the doctors to estimate the person's level of consciousness. The research based on animal studies, anesthesia-used studies, and brain injury patient studies has identified five more important brain signaling activities that may help to improve the understanding, diagnosis, and treatment of pDOC. In patients with severe brain injury, there is a lack of coordination between different brain areas and an interrupted flow of electrical signals as short bursts are observed. A lack of coordinated firing of individual brain cells in response to the rhythmic signals, along with disrupted balance in brain signaling activity, is also observed in pDOC patients. Mild stimulation to the brain, particularly the thalamus, restores its normal activity, so measuring how the brain responds to stimulation is a reliable way to detect hidden awareness. Dr. Li commented, "There are also important ethical challenges identified regarding the studies, such as obtaining consent from family members, equality in obtaining treatment, and clearly explaining uncertain test results to the family." However, more detailed research should be conducted in the future. The reviewed studies included only a small number of patients with different types of brain injuries, and the recordings were made under different conditions. Hence, the findings cannot be applied confidently to individual patients. In conclusion, measuring brain electrical signals directly from inner brain areas helps to provide more accurate diagnoses and more personalized treatments. According to Dr. Li, "Further studies on large patient populations, collaboration between hospitals, and strong ethical guidelines are necessary to make this diagnostic test more reliable."

AI Translation
长期意识障碍（pDOC）是指患者在遭受严重脑损伤后未能表现出可靠意识证据的状态。尽管已有经过验证的工具可用，但与pDOC相关的误诊率仍然相当高。局部场电位（LFPs）为校准无创生物标志物提供了数据，并有助于理解意识的机制基础。本研究旨在介绍pDOC中颅内局部场电位特征的现有证据。 在严重脑损伤患者中，准确检测pDOC具有挑战性，且不正确的检测会限制治疗效果。现有证据表明，即使使用先进的检测工具，仍有高达40%的误诊率。这主要是由于患者的身体状况——精疲力尽、镇静或无反应状态。目前，医生依赖各种脑部扫描技术作为识别患者意识的确诊程序。 河北医科大学的研究人员查阅了多个科学研究数据库，以概述当前pDOC管理方法的现有证据。他们的主要关注点是那些直接测量pDOC患者大脑电信号的研究，即“局部场电位”研究。这项由李从惠博士和杨毅博士领导的研究于2026年7月13日发表在《中华神经外科杂志》第12卷第22篇文章中。李从惠博士表示：“我们的目标是分析大脑电信号记录如何支持pDOC诊断，并研究这项技术的可能结果和局限性。” 这项综述分析提出了一种大脑通信网络模型，以解释脑损伤如何导致长期无意识。根据该模型，脑损伤会损害或破坏许多神经细胞，从而中断电信号流向丘脑等深部脑区。结果，重要脑区之间的通信减弱，进而降低大脑功能。为支持这一理论，研究人员确定了四种主要的大脑电信号活动测量指标，这些指标有助于理解意识水平。 健康的有意识大脑维持着激活性慢信号和镇静性快信号（包括节律性和非节律性信号类型）的持续、有序且平衡的流动。有趣的是，活跃大脑的不同区域以受控、非混乱的方式持续相互通信。根据另一项科学理论，活跃的大脑会执行多任务处理——大脑的每个部分既处理自己的信息，又维持其通信网络的正常运作。 严重的脑损伤会破坏所有这些平衡的信号通路和通信网络：快速、活跃的脑电波转变为较弱的脑电波，而较慢的信号则变得更加主导。此外，多个脑区的协调功能受到影响，大脑不再持续流畅地发送信号，而是发出短促、简单且组织性较差的信号。在无意识状态下，大脑处理信息的能力也随之下降。总的来说，这些变化降低了个体的意识和反应能力。 当前比较健康大脑与严重受损大脑的研究发现，pDOC患者的丘脑与大脑外层之间的通信较差。测量这种信号活动的复杂程度——特别是在脑刺激之后——有助于医生评估患者的意识水平。 基于动物研究、麻醉研究和脑损伤患者研究的成果，已经确定了另外五种重要的大脑信号活动，这些活动可能有助于提高对pDOC的理解、诊断和治疗。在严重脑损伤患者中，观察到不同脑区之间缺乏协调性，电信号流表现为间歇性爆发。此外，pDOC患者还存在单个脑细胞对节律性信号的协调放电不足，以及大脑信号活动平衡失调。对大脑，特别是丘脑进行轻微刺激，可以恢复其正常活动，因此测量大脑对刺激的反应是检测隐藏意识的可靠方法。 李博士评论道：“研究中还发现了一些重要的伦理挑战，例如如何获得家属的同意、治疗机会的公平性，以及如何向家属清楚解释不确定的检测结果。” 然而，未来还需要进行更详细的研究。此次综述的研究仅包含了少数不同类型脑损伤的患者，且记录是在不同条件下进行的。因此，这些发现不能自信地应用于个体患者。 总之，直接测量大脑内部区域的电信号有助于提供更准确的诊断和更个性化的治疗。李博士表示：“为了使这项诊断测试更加可靠，有必要在大量患者群体中进行进一步研究，加强医院间的协作，并制定强有力的伦理指导方针。”
