人工智能记录员可能无法捕捉患者的体验。

AI scribes may fail to capture patients' experiences

医疗
Source

深度解析

环境式AI记录员（ambient AI scribes）结合语音转文本技术与人工智能，旨在自动化医生问诊记录，从而减轻临床医生的行政负担。然而，这项技术在带来效率的同时，也引发了多方面的专业顾虑。其核心原理是通过AI算法识别并总结口语信息，但此过程可能遗漏非语言线索（如面部表情、手势和情绪状态）及患者的个体叙事，使记录偏向纯粹的临床数据，牺牲了患者体验的完整性。在医学机制层面，文章强调了“认知卸载”（cognitive offloading）现象，即临床医生将部分精神努力外包给AI，这可能在短期内提升沟通质量和复杂任务的专注度，但长期而言却可能削弱其记忆召回能力和临床推理技能。手动记录笔记对于医生巩固患者信息、进行深度反思至关重要，AI的介入可能剥夺了这些关键的技能习得机会，导致“技能退化”（deskilling）。行业影响方面，该技术虽有望提高医疗效率，但其推广面临患者隐私（不愿透露敏感信息）和信任挑战。此外，AI模型在不同医疗文化和系统中的适应性、潜在偏见及中长期风险尚待深入研究。这要求技术开发者在设计时需更加注重保留“患者的声音”，确保技术能真正支持以患者为中心的护理，而非简单的数据采集工具。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: CC0 Public Domain The use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools to take notes can change the nature of interactions between patients and clinicians, sometimes missing vital information, according to the results of a new review. The report also states that the use of these tools, referred to as ambient AI scribes, may lead to deskilling and deprive clinicians of opportunities to acquire clinical reasoning skills. While ambient AI scribes can help with administrative work, more research needs to be done to understand how these tools work in specific health care settings and how their design supports both patients and clinicians, experts say. Some 40% of GPs in the UK said they used ambient scribes, which integrate speech-to-text technology with AI to assist with capturing patient notes during a consultation. Researchers from the University of Edinburgh conducted a review of 27 articles in scientific and medical literature to examine AI scribes' adoption and the implications of scaling up their use. One of the key findings of their analysis was that although the AI tools can help capture what patients say, they can sometimes miss important aspects of consultations, such as facial expressions, gestures and emotional states. AI summaries tend to prioritize clinical information, which can come at the expense of patients' stories and experiences of their illness. The review also found that patients may be hesitant to reveal sensitive information, such as substance abuse, domestic abuse or mental health struggles, when they know the consultation is being recorded and processed by AI. The team also describes how manual note-taking serves important functions for clinicians in clinical reasoning and reflection on what the patient has said. Using AI scribes could result in clinicians outsourcing mental effort—known as cognitive offloading. This can have benefits, such as creating the opportunity for more meaningful conversations with patients and more time to focus on complex tasks. By contrast, these tools can also reduce memory recall and skill development. Clinicians report instances in which they do not recognize their own notes or remember the patient the next time they see them. The researchers conclude that these systems must preserve the patient's voice rather than overwrite it. They also say more work needs to be done to understand the medium- and long-term use of these AI tools in specific settings and emerging risks, particularly for tools used in countries with health care systems different from those where the AI tools were built. The findings have been published in BMJ Digital Health and AI. Dr. Lucas Seuren, GAIL Fellow and research fellow at the University of Edinburgh's Centre for Biomedicine, Self and Society, said, "Many clinicians are excited about ambient AI scribes because they promise to cut down on paperwork. But the experiences of patients are poorly considered, and there are real risks that patients' stories are lost. This can further disadvantage people who already face marginalization in health and social care services."
