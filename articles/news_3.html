深度解析

本文揭示了全球健康领域的一个变革性趋势，主张将艺术和文化参与系统性地整合到医学框架中。这一在《柳叶刀》杂志上阐述的倡议，通过强调对健康的生物-心理-社会-文化综合理解，超越了传统的生物医学模式。其核心专业概念在于，将艺术参与视为一种基础性的健康促进行为，其重要性与体育活动、饮食和睡眠等同，而非仅仅是可选的辅助手段。 其深层机制在于艺术活动对多种生理和心理通路的显著影响。例如，音乐和舞蹈能调节神经内分泌反应，降低应激激素并改善情绪；视觉艺术参与则能培养认知灵活性和情绪调节能力。通过合作艺术进行的社会参与可以缓解孤独感并构建社区韧性，这对于心理健康至关重要。这种视角与多学科患者护理方法相契合，其中非药物干预措施发挥着日益重要的作用。 其行业影响是深远的。首先，它呼吁医疗服务模式的范式转变，促进结合临床治疗与艺术疗法的新型整合护理模式。这为在慢性病管理、康复、姑息治疗和心理健康支持等不同临床环境中应用循证艺术干预创造了机会。其次，它要求加大对严谨科学研究的投入，以建立特定艺术形式及其健康结果的可靠证据基础，从而推动政策改革，支持艺术与健康倡议的广泛采纳和资金支持。第三，它通过倡导人群层面可及的艺术参与来促进健康公平，这可能减少健康差距并促进文化敏感的健康促进。这重新定义了“医学”的范畴，使其涵盖更广阔的人文维度，从而丰富患者体验并促进整体社会福祉。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain Researchers have developed a roadmap for integrating the arts across health and well-being systems globally, from population health and prevention to community pathways and clinical care. Based on a wealth of international evidence, the roadmap is outlined in the Jameel Arts & Health Lab–Lancet Global Collection on Arts and Health. Professor Agustín Ibáñez, of the Global Brain Health Institute at Trinity College Dublin, is a co-author of both cornerstone papers in the collection, alongside an international group of researchers, health experts and leaders in arts and culture. The papers appear in The Lancet. The first paper, "The arts as a global health resource: key priorities for future research," synthesizes evidence showing that engagement with music, dance, visual arts, theater and other cultural activities can contribute to mental, physical and social health across the life course, from health promotion and prevention to the management and treatment of illness. The authors argue that the field has now reached a critical stage requiring strategic, systems-level integration. The second paper, "Developing roadmaps for advancing practice, research, and policy in the arts and health," moves from evidence to action. It calls for equitable access to arts engagement, its inclusion in population-level prevention, stronger community pathways, evidence-based arts interventions in clinical care, and sustained cross-sector investment and research. "This is a historic moment for medicine," Ibáñez said. "For the first time, arts and cultural engagement are being brought together within a global medical framework spanning health promotion, prevention, treatment and care. "We are moving from seeing the arts as an optional complement to health toward recognizing cultural engagement as part of how we understand, protect and improve human health." The collection goes further by proposing that arts engagement should be recognized as a health-enhancing behavior, comparable in importance to other behaviors such as physical activity, diet and sleep, while stressing that implementation must be culturally grounded and locally adapted. "For global brain health, this is particularly important," Ibáñez added. "Health emerges from the interaction of biological, psychological, social and cultural environments. Arts and culture are part of those processes. The challenge now is to build the evidence, policies and health systems that recognize this." The collection will be presented in New York during Healing Arts at UNGA, NYC 2026, held alongside the United Nations General Assembly.
