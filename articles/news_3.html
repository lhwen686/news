体育机构表示，应优先对精英运动员的心理健康进行常规筛查和定期监测。

Prioritize routine screening and regular monitoring of elite athletes' mental health, says sports body

深度解析

国际奥委会的共识声明深刻揭示了精英运动员心理健康面临的严峻挑战，并提出了系统性解决方案。文章核心专业概念在于将运动员心理健康视为一个动态的“连续统一体”，而非仅仅局限于可诊断的精神障碍。这意味着即使没有达到临床诊断标准，运动员也可能经历与竞技压力、伤病、高强度训练及生涯转折相关的短暂性应激反应或心理困扰。该声明强调了“生物心理社会生态模型”在理解和管理运动员心理健康中的关键作用。运动员的福祉受多种相互作用的因素影响，包括其生理状态、心理特质、社会支持系统以及所处的体育生态环境。因此，心理健康支持必须从传统的个体治疗转向涵盖训练环境、领导行为、保障系统及组织文化的“全生态系统”干预。行业影响深远：它要求体育机构将心理健康从医疗护理的辅助部分提升为治理、保障和高水平表现体系的核心要素。这将促使运动医学、运动心理学和体育管理领域在政策制定、筛查监测、危机干预及教育培训方面进行重大改革，从而建立更安全、更道德、更可持续的精英体育发展模式。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain Sports bodies should prioritize routine screening and regular monitoring of elite athletes' mental health to tackle the high prevalence of mental health issues among this group, concludes an International Olympic Committee (IOC) consensus statement, published online in the British Journal of Sports Medicine. These activities should be done at predefined time points and after significant events, and should address the full range of contributing factors in training and competition, says the statement, which updates a previous statement on the topic, published in 2019. And they should be part of an organizational culture that fosters good mental health and well-being among elite athletes across the board, it says. The new statement draws on a synthesis of current scientific evidence reviewed by an international panel of 31 people with lived, clinical and scientific expertise. It aims to advance a more standardized, evidence-based approach to the management of mental health in elite athletes. Mental ill health among elite athletes is common; may be triggered by, and manifest during, sport; and can affect performance, says the statement, adding that it exists on a continuum and is changeable. "While recognition of overt mental health disorders in elite athletes remains essential, situating athlete well-being within a dynamic continuum offers an additional framework for understanding the spectrum of psychological functioning in this population," suggests the statement. "For example, elite athletes may experience transient stress reactions related to competitions, various temporary setbacks that are a normal and common part of elite athletes' lives, or intense training periods. Thus, there are important mental health considerations even if an athlete does not have a diagnosable disorder," it points out. The statement covers a broad spectrum of topics, including detection and general management of mental ill health; types of treatment; specific mental health issues; influential stressors and environmental factors; cultural considerations; barriers to help-seeking; fostering mental well-being and resilience; social media; the intersection of injury, performance and mental health; and mental health emergencies. Key recommendations: Mental health support for elite athletes should extend beyond individual management to making sure training environments, leadership behaviors, safeguarding systems and organizational culture support elite athlete mental health and well-being. Clinicians and sports bodies should proactively address stigma, confidentiality concerns, fear of deselection and low mental health literacy as part of routine elite athlete care. Sports bodies should implement routine screening and monitoring of elite athlete mental health at predefined time points and after significant events. All elite sport settings should establish, rehearse and regularly review mental health emergency action plans with defined roles and escalation pathways. Management of mental health symptoms and disorders should be tailored, evidence-based and culturally responsive, integrating psychotherapy, drug treatment and other contextually appropriate therapies, as indicated. Psychological responses to injury should be routinely assessed, and return-to-sport for elite athletes with mental ill health should incorporate athlete safety, stability and function as guiding principles. Athletes should receive targeted mental health screening and support before, during and after major transitions in sport. Mental health assessment and care for elite Para athletes must account for disability-specific stressors, accessibility barriers, classification systems and heightened transition risks. Mental health screening and support for elite adolescent athletes should reflect developmental stage, educational demands, family dynamics and the early onset of many common mental health disorders. Coaches, support staff and health professionals for elite athletes should receive role-appropriate mental health education to support early recognition, safe referral and psychologically informed practice. "The responsibility for prevention, recognition and management of mental health symptoms and disorders and for promoting well-being extends across the entire elite sport ecosystem," highlights the statement. "Multiple interacting biopsychosocial and ecological factors influence athlete well-being, necessitating system-level attention and coordinated strategies. Elite athletes experience mental health symptoms and disorders at rates similar to, or higher than, the general population, and their presentations and care needs may differ from those of non-athletes. "Ensuring that clinicians, coaches and sport organizations are prepared to recognize, respond to and proactively support mental health is essential to safe, ethical and sustainable elite sport participation," it concludes. In a linked editorial, Dr. Brian Hainline of New York University and international colleagues point out, "Emerging evidence also highlights a substantial mental health burden among members of the athlete entourage, including coaches and medical professionals. "These findings reinforce the view that mental health in elite sport is an ecosystem issue rather than one borne solely by athletes. "In parallel, all athletes ultimately transition out of sport, and difficult transitions are strongly associated with the development of mental health symptoms and disorders." The time has come for action, insist the authors, "For international federations and national governing bodies, this second IOC consensus statement demands decisive action. Mental health must be embedded as a core element of governance, safeguarding and high-performance systems, and not treated as an adjunct to medical care or athlete resilience." And they conclude, "This consensus provides a roadmap; our hope is that leadership, accountability and sustained investment will follow."
