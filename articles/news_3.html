Graphical abstract. Credit: Cell Host & Microbe (2026). DOI: 10.1016/j.chom.2026.04.015

Most pandemics start when a pathogen spreads from animals to humans. It's a leading explanation for the COVID-19 pandemic: The SARS-CoV-2 virus, which causes COVID-19, is a cousin of coronaviruses that live in bats.

Now, researchers at the UCSF Quantitative Biosciences Institute, Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, Institut Pasteur and Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center report that a single amino acid change alters how a coronavirus protein interacts with the human and bat immune systems, shifting the body's response to infection.

It helps explain how benign animal viruses can adapt to humans and cause severe disease.

The study appears in Cell Host & Microbe.

Researchers looked at SARS-CoV-2 and a related coronavirus called RaTG13, which infects only bats, and compared how each virus interacted with immune proteins in bat and human lung cells. The experiments relied on the first laboratory-grown lung cell line from the greater horseshoe bat.

A viral protein called OrfB9 emerged as a key factor. The SARS-CoV-2 and RaTG13 versions of OrfB9 differ by one amino acid out of roughly 100. In human cells, the SARS-CoV-2 version disabled an immune alarm system, allowing the virus to multiply. In bat cells, the RaTG13 version activated an immune protein that helped suppress the virus.

"The difference between a virus that stays in bats and one that spills over into humans and causes catastrophic disease can come down to remarkably small genetic changes," said Nevan J. Krogan, Ph.D., director of QBI and senior author of the study.

"By mapping these interactions at the protein level—across two viruses and two species—we can read the molecular signatures that predict spillover risk. It's the kind of early warning system the world needs."

大多数大流行病始于病原体从动物传播到人类。这是COVID-19大流行的一个主要解释：导致COVID-19的SARS-CoV-2病毒，是蝙蝠体内冠状病毒的近亲。

目前，加州大学旧金山分校定量生物科学研究所、西奈山伊坎医学院、巴斯德研究所和弗雷德哈钦森癌症中心的研究人员报告称，一个单个氨基酸的变化会改变冠状病毒蛋白质与人类和蝙蝠免疫系统互作的方式，从而改变机体对感染的反应。

这有助于解释良性动物病毒如何适应人类并引发严重疾病。

该研究发表在《细胞宿主与微生物》期刊上。

研究人员观察了SARS-CoV-2和一种名为RaTG13的相关冠状病毒（只感染蝙蝠），并比较了这两种病毒如何与蝙蝠和人类肺细胞中的免疫蛋白质互作。这些实验依赖于首个从大马蹄蝠体内培养的实验室肺细胞系。

一种名为OrfB9的病毒蛋白质成为关键因素。SARS-CoV-2和RaTG13版本的OrfB9在大约100个氨基酸中，仅有一个氨基酸不同。在人类细胞中，SARS-CoV-2版本的OrfB9抑制了免疫警报系统，从而使病毒得以增殖。而在蝙蝠细胞中，RaTG13版本的OrfB9激活了一种有助于抑制病毒的免疫蛋白质。

“一种停留在蝙蝠体内，而另一种溢出到人类并引起灾难性疾病的病毒，其差异可能归结为微小的基因变化，”加州大学旧金山分校定量生物科学研究所（QBI）主任兼该研究的通讯作者Nevan J. Krogan博士表示。

“通过在蛋白质水平上——跨越两种病毒和两个物种——绘制这些互作图谱，我们可以解读预测溢出风险的分子特征。这是世界所需要的一种早期预警系统。”
