心理学家不应以人工智能取代思维，研究人员警告

Psychologists shouldn't replace thinking with AI, researchers warn

For some psychologists, it's becoming more common to use AI systems to replace human thinking in research. That's a very risky choice based on misconceptions, warn Iris van Rooij and Olivia Guest in a new paper published today in Current Directions in Psychological Science. "Research based on artificial intelligence models will never be an adequate substitute for understanding and replicating human thought."

The technology companies behind the latest popular artificial intelligence models often make big promises, such as claiming to have developed artificial minds that can rival human brains. "That's a promise that can be very alluring to psychologists," explains van Rooij, professor of computational cognitive science at Radboud University. "It's easy to think you can do psychological experiments with artificial participants, but you simply can't automate science."

"Following the replication crisis [where it was found that many results in peer-reviewed studies in psychology could not be reproduced], some researchers are pushing for a more methodological, statistical approach to psychology research, something that can be proceduralized and maybe even automated," says Guest, associate professor of computational cognitive science at Radboud University. "But the whole point of doing science is to produce knowledge. That's why it's so important to emphasize AI can never meaningfully replicate human cognition."

Identifying traps

In their paper, van Rooij and Guest point out three traps for fellow researchers in their field to avoid. "First, AI systems are not minds. As we've discussed in our previous research, AI systems can never be sufficiently trained to reach human-level cognition. Even if tech companies continue to use astronomical levels of resources to train them, they won't even be able to get close," says van Rooij. "At best, you'll be able to produce a decoy: something that may look impressive and trick you into thinking it can act like a human, but is in no way a replacement for the real thing."

Guest adds, "Furthermore, these systems are based on predicting; that's not a basis for actual new theories. Just because an AI system can predict what a human would say or do doesn't mean it can explain what humans do. Compare it, for example, to the tides. Long before humans understood what caused the tides, we created tide tables to predict when ebb and flood would happen. But no one would argue that those tables explain the tides—yet that's what some people are claiming AI models do."

Science is slow

Finally, the researchers warn that it's a fallacy to think cognitive science can be automated. Guest said, "Doing theoretical work is very difficult, and sometimes researchers might fall for instant gratification, or maybe they haven't been taught how to do it. But if you ask AI to take over, you run into many risks, from getting stuck in existing theories to deskilling future scientists. It creates inconsistencies, which run the risk of introducing pseudoscientific ideas into the field."

If we truly want to advance the study of cognition, the authors argue, we can't rely on AI models to take shortcuts. van Rooij said, "Part of the problem is inherent to the system: We ask researchers to write as many papers as possible, while good science is slow. But we can't rely on AI as if it's a cheat code, and the best way to avoid the traps of AI is to be aware of them. Only once we've acknowledged that can we expect to move cognitive science forward."

对于一些心理学家来说，在研究中使用AI系统取代人类思维正变得越来越普遍。伊里斯·范·罗伊（Iris van Rooij）和奥利维亚·格斯特（Olivia Guest）在今天发表于《心理科学当前方向》（Current Directions in Psychological Science）的一篇新论文中警告称，这是一个基于误解的、风险极高的选择。“基于人工智能模型的研究永远无法充分替代对人类思维的理解和复制。”

最新流行人工智能模型背后的科技公司常常做出宏伟的承诺，例如声称已经开发出可以与人类大脑媲美的人工智能。拉德堡德大学计算认知科学教授范·罗伊解释说：“这个承诺对心理学家来说可能非常诱人。人们很容易认为可以用人工参与者进行心理实验，但科学是无法简单地自动化的。”

“在‘重复性危机’[指心理学领域许多同行评审研究的结果无法重现]之后，一些研究人员正在推动一种更具方法论、统计学倾向的心理学研究方法，这种方法可以程序化，甚至可能自动化，”拉德堡德大学计算认知科学副教授格斯特说。“但做科学的全部意义在于产生知识。这就是为什么强调人工智能永远无法有意义地复制人类认知如此重要的原因。”

识别陷阱

在他们的论文中，范·罗伊和格斯特指出了同行研究人员应避免的三个陷阱。“首先，AI系统不是心智。正如我们在之前的研究中讨论过的，AI系统永远无法得到充分训练以达到人类水平的认知。即使科技公司继续投入天文数字般的资源来训练它们，它们也无法接近，”范·罗伊说。“充其量，你只能制造出一个诱饵：一个可能看起来令人印象深刻，并欺骗你以为它能像人类一样行事的东西，但它绝不能替代真实的事物。”

格斯特补充道：“此外，这些系统是基于预测的；这并非建立实际新理论的基础。仅仅因为AI系统能够预测人类会说什么或做什么，并不意味着它能够解释人类为什么这样做。例如，可以将其与潮汐进行比较。早在人类理解潮汐原因之前，我们就创建了潮汐表来预测潮涨潮落的时间。但没有人会争辩说这些表格解释了潮汐——然而，有些人却声称AI模型能够做到这一点。”

科学是缓慢的

最后，研究人员警告说，认为认知科学可以自动化的想法是一个谬误。格斯特说：“从事理论工作非常困难，有时研究人员可能会追求即时满足，或者他们可能没有被教导如何进行理论工作。但如果你让AI接管，你将面临许多风险，从陷入现有理论到使未来科学家技能退化。这会产生不一致之处，并有将伪科学思想引入该领域的风险。”

如果我们真的想推进认知研究，作者们认为，我们就不能依赖AI模型走捷径。范·罗伊说：“部分问题是系统固有的：我们要求研究人员尽可能多地发表论文，而优秀的科学研究是缓慢的。但我们不能依赖AI，仿佛它是一个作弊代码；避免AI陷阱的最佳方式就是认识到它们。只有当我们承认这一点后，我们才能期望推动认知科学向前发展。”
