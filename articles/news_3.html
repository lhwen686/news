深度解析

本研究深入探讨了普惠性免费学校供餐计划取消后对学生营养、行为及学业表现的深远影响。核心概念在于公共卫生政策与儿童健康发展的交叉作用。普惠性供餐作为一项重要的社会支持机制，旨在通过提供稳定、可及的营养来源，缓解儿童饥饿，减轻家庭经济压力，并促进学生在校期间的专注力与整体福祉。研究发现，该计划的实施显著降低了学生的饥饿感和家庭压力。然而，计划的终止导致学生饥饿、焦虑和行为问题显著增加，对垃圾食品的摄入量上升，并直接影响了课堂注意力，这凸显了营养状况与认知功能及情绪调节之间的紧密联系。 从行业影响来看，这项研究对公共卫生、教育和政策制定领域具有重要启示。它强调了持续性营养干预对儿童身心健康和学业成就的关键作用。政策制定者需重新评估此类计划的社会效益与成本效益，认识到其不仅是食物保障，更是提升教育公平性和国家未来人力资本的关键投资。同时，研究也揭示了教师在政策变化中承受的额外负担，提示需完善支持系统，以确保教育工作者能更好地专注于教学任务，而非弥补政策缺口。该研究为未来制定更具韧性和可持续性的儿童营养政策提供了重要的实证依据。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain How did students' diets and behavior change after the expiration of a pandemic-era universal free school meal program in 2022? Researchers asked teachers for their observations of the impact on students and found that teachers perceived more student hunger and stress, increased consumption of junk food and reduced focus in the classroom among the negative outcomes. "Teachers are firsthand observers of the impact school meal policies have on children. Participation data can tell us that fewer students ate lunch, but teachers can tell us why a student did or did not eat and how it presented in the classroom," says Deborah Olarte, the study's lead author and assistant professor of nutrition at NYU Steinhardt. "Universal free school meals removed a worry from the school day. When they went away, the worry came back, and teachers watched it play out in their classrooms." Researchers interviewed 25 teachers in the Anchorage School District in Alaska, asking them to compare the 2021–2022 year, when their schools participated in the universal meal program—a federal program that provided meals to all students, regardless of economic need—with 2022–2023, after the program ended. They asked questions focused on the benefits and challenges of offering free meals to students. The article is published in the Journal of Nutrition Education and Behavior. During the universal meal program, teachers perceived the following: Reduced student and family stress Increased food waste among students "I think the kids who would be shy about saying that they're hungry or don't have lunch didn't have to worry about it because it was just provided," one teacher shared. After the program's expiration, teachers shared these observations: Increased student hunger More frequent behavioral issues among students Students had more difficulty concentrating Increased student anxiety about paying for meals Teachers were more likely to provide food for students Increased consumption of sugary snacks and beverages and ultra-processed foods Decreased meal participation, particularly among students eligible for free meals According to one teacher, "Some kids ... they're just hungry all afternoon and can't focus, and again disruptive behaviors happen." "Teachers gave us one window into this policy shift. We should also include the voices of students, parents, administrators and food service staff across different districts and states," Olarte says. "But what stayed with me from these interviews is how much of school meal policy lands on teachers who were buying snacks, cleaning up, etc. I think it's important to focus on how the burden school meal programs place on educators may shape families' decisions to participate in school meals." Publication details Deborah A. Olarte et al, When School Meals Are No Longer Free: A Qualitative Exploration of Elementary School Teachers' Experiences and Perceptions, Journal of Nutrition Education and Behavior (2026).
