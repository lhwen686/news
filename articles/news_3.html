EULAR大会聚焦风湿病患者的创新支持策略

EULAR Congress highlights innovative support strategies for rheumatology patients

Original Text
Rheumatic and musculoskeletal diseases (RMD) profoundly impact daily life, limiting both work ability and participation in everyday activities. At the 2026 annual Congress of EULAR - The European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology - a series of abstracts examined strategies to deal with fatigue, as well as the lived experiences of caregivers, cost-effectiveness of patient education, and access to rheumatology care. These were part of the Congress dedicated to EULAR Health Professionals in Rheumatology (HPR) - the network of national organisations of HPR across Europe, which serves as platform for collaboration and shared information among the different health professionals working with rheumatology.

Fatigue is a common and burdensome symptom for people with inflammatory arthritis. Tailored physical activity and cognitive behavioural approaches are known to help - and are recommended by EULAR - but little is known about the impact of combining the two. COMFI - the COMbined Fatigue Intervention - has been designed to integrate these two approaches. At the 2026 EULAR Congress in London, Astrid Damgaard presented findings from a feasibility study involving 29 people with inflammatory arthritis in Denmark and Sweden. The study showed that COMFI could be delivered successfully in both countries. Recruitment was acceptable, adherence rates varied across groups, and delivery fidelity was satisfactory. At 6-month follow-up, the results indicated a tendency toward reduced fatigue, with observed changes meeting the minimal clinically important difference in both countries in all fatigue measurements including the subscales for fatigue severity, impact, and coping. The authors concluded that the approach was feasible to deliver to people with inflammatory arthritis and fatigue. Points to consider for future studies are the unexpected limited use of the individual sessions, support line and involvement of relatives, but this combined support system warrants further investigation of in a larger, randomised controlled trial to evaluate effectiveness and cost-effectiveness.

Being able to self-manage is important for people with an RMD, and patient education can be useful in this regard. Results were shared from a trial designed to evaluate the 12-month cost-effectiveness of digital patient education compared to a standard face-to-face approach for 175 patients with newly diagnosed rheumatoid arthritis. The findings showed that the digital approach had lower mean healthcare costs, and slightly greater QALY gains, and the digital intervention was dominant in both adjusted and unadjusted models. Hospital care was the primary driver of cost differences between groups, with lower mean hospital costs in the digital patient education group. Importantly, bootstrap analyses indicated improved outcomes at lower costs - supporting the hypothesis of resource savings for the healthcare system.

A secondary outcome was achieving a minimal important change in self-efficacy score from baseline to 12 months; for this, the intervention was cost-saving, but effects were small and inconsistent across adjusted and unadjusted analyses.

Speaking at the congress, Line Raunsbæk Knudsen said "While confirmation in broader populations is warranted, these findings suggest that structured digital patient education may be integrated into routine care to support efficient resource use without compromising on quality."

RMD often require sustained support from caregivers to enable daily functioning and disease management. But despite their central role, caregivers experiences and needs are insufficiently explored in rheumatology research - and they are often invisible. A team in Italy set out to change this by exploring the lived experiences of a group of family members and primary informal caregivers, and identifying needs around targeted supportive interventions. Presenting the work, Valentina Bressan described the qualitative phenomenological study, and the methods used. Overall, 25 caregivers particpated - mostly spouses and cohabiting partners. The onset of illness emerged as a major turning point, with emotional distress and feelings of helplessness and guilt. Many people reported difficult choices and compromises, with 26.7% citing major life changes or sacrifices, and 52% reporting predominantly negative emotions, including sadness, anxiety, fatigue, and emotional overload - often limiting their ability to plan for the future.

The results suggest caregivers experience complex and multifaceted challenges that significantly affect their wellbeing and daily lives, and highlight the need for systematic recognition within rheumatology care pathways. Developing caregiver-focused assessments and tailored psychosocial and educational interventions may improve their wellbeing, enhance patient outcomes, and support more holistic and person-centred care.

There is a global mean delay to diagnosis for axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) of 7.4 years. A team in Canada presented preliminary results from the FASTRAX study - an interdisciplinary model to improve access to rheumatology care and promote early identification. This approach uniquely leverages trained extended scope providers and rheumatology fellows to quickly screen patients with chronic low back pain. To date, 179 patients have completed the screening process across three study sites with a median wait time to assessment of 28 days. Of those, 22.9% were diagnosed with axSpA, and they had a mean time to diagnosis of 12.1 years. Overall, the number needed to screen across the province was five patients with chronic back pain to identify one patient with axSpA, although this varied slightly between sites; however, of note, historically axSpA represents approximately 5% of those with chronic back pain, suggesting a need to screen approximately 20 people to detect one axSpA case.

Lead author Laura Passalent said "The model is valid and delivers quick access to care, with referral wait times much less than the provincial median. Time to diagnosis remains prolonged, but this is expected to decrease as the model gains awareness amongst referring physicians and matures over time, allowing for more targeted rheumatology referrals."

疲劳是炎症性关节炎患者常见且负担沉重的症状。量身定制的体育活动和认知行为疗法已知有效，并由EULAR推荐，但关于两者结合效果的了解甚少。COMFI（联合疲劳干预）旨在整合这两种方法。在2026年伦敦EULAR大会上，Astrid Damgaard介绍了在丹麦和瑞典对29名炎症性关节炎患者进行的初步可行性研究结果。研究表明，COMFI在两国均能成功实施。招募情况可接受，各组依从性率有所不同，实施忠实度令人满意。在6个月的随访中，结果显示疲劳有减轻趋势，观察到的变化在两国所有疲劳测量指标（包括疲劳严重程度、影响和应对分量表）中均达到最小临床重要差异。作者总结认为，该方法对于患有炎症性关节炎并伴有疲劳的患者是可行的。未来研究需要考虑的方面包括：个人咨询、支持热线和亲属参与的使用率低于预期，但这个联合支持系统值得在更大规模的随机对照试验中进一步研究，以评估其有效性和成本效益。

对于RMD患者而言，能够进行自我管理非常重要，而患者教育在这方面可能很有用。一项旨在评估数字患者教育与标准面对面方法相比，对175名新诊断类风湿关节炎患者12个月成本效益的试验结果被分享。研究结果显示，数字方法平均医疗成本更低，QALY（质量调整生命年）增益略高，并且在调整和未调整模型中，数字干预均表现出主导性。医院护理是组间成本差异的主要驱动因素，数字患者教育组的平均住院成本较低。重要的是，自助抽样分析表明，在成本更低的情况下获得了更好的结果，这支持了医疗系统资源节约的假设。

一个次要结果是从基线到12个月，自我效能评分达到最小重要变化；为此，该干预措施具有成本节约效应，但其效果在调整和未调整分析中均较小且不一致。

Line Raunsbæk Knudsen在大会上表示：“虽然需要在更广泛的人群中进行确认，但这些发现表明，结构化的数字患者教育可以整合到常规护理中，以支持高效的资源利用，同时不影响质量。”

风湿性肌肉骨骼疾病（RMD）患者通常需要照护者的持续支持，以维持日常功能和疾病管理。然而，尽管照护者扮演着核心角色，但他们在风湿病研究中的经验和需求却未得到充分探索，他们常常被忽视。意大利的一个团队着手改变这一现状，他们通过探索一群家庭成员和主要非正式照护者的切身经历，并确定了针对性支持干预措施的需求。Valentina Bressan在介绍这项工作时，描述了这项定性现象学研究及其使用的方法。共有25名照护者参与，其中大部分是配偶和同居伴侣。疾病的发生被认为是主要的转折点，伴随着情感困扰以及无助和内疚感。许多人报告了艰难的选择和妥协，其中26.7%提到了重大的生活改变或牺牲，52%报告了主要是负面情绪，包括悲伤、焦虑、疲劳和情感超负荷，这常常限制了他们对未来的规划能力。

结果表明，照护者面临复杂且多方面的挑战，这些挑战严重影响了他们的福祉和日常生活，并强调了在风湿病护理路径中系统性地认可照护者的必要性。制定以照护者为中心的评估以及量身定制的社会心理和教育干预措施，可能会改善他们的福祉，提高患者的治疗效果，并支持更全面和以人为本的护理。

轴性脊柱关节炎（axSpA）的全球平均诊断延迟为7.4年。加拿大一个团队公布了FASTRAX研究的初步结果，这是一项旨在改善风湿病护理可及性并促进早期识别的跨学科模型。该方法独特地利用受过培训的扩展范围医疗服务提供者和风湿病研究员来快速筛查慢性腰痛患者。迄今为止，已有179名患者在三个研究地点完成了筛查过程，评估的中位等待时间为28天。其中，22.9%的患者被诊断出axSpA，他们的平均诊断时间为12.1年。总体而言，在全省范围内，筛查5名慢性腰痛患者即可识别1名axSpA患者，尽管这在不同地点略有差异；然而值得注意的是，历史上axSpA约占慢性腰痛患者的5%，这表明大约需要筛查20人才能发现一个axSpA病例。

主要作者Laura Passalent表示：“该模型是有效的，并提供了快速的护理途径，转诊等待时间远低于省级中位数。诊断时间仍然较长，但随着该模型在转诊医生中获得更多认知并随着时间推移而成熟，预计诊断时间将减少，从而实现更具针对性的风湿病转诊。”
