Hyperuricemia predicts mortality with chronic kidney disease

深度解析

该研究深入揭示了高尿酸血症在慢性肾脏病（CKD）患者中的预后重要性，超越了其传统上作为痛风病因的认知。核心机制在于，在肾功能受损的背景下，血清尿酸的升高并非简单的代谢异常，而是可能通过介导内皮功能障碍、氧化应激、炎症反应以及激活肾素-血管紧张素系统等多种途径，加剧CKD的进展和心血管并发症的发生，这些均是CKD进展和心血管事件发生发展的关键病理生理机制。研究发现的“剂量-反应关系”尤其关键，它表明血清尿酸是一个分级标志物，而非仅是二元风险因子，其水平的持续升高与死亡风险的线性增加相关。这一发现对临床实践具有深远影响：它强化了对CKD患者血清尿酸进行常规监测的必要性，并将其视为独立的风险评估指标。其次，它可能促使临床指南重新评估降尿酸治疗（ULT）在CKD患者管理中的角色，即便对于无症状高尿酸血症患者，也应考虑其对改善预后的潜在价值，尤其是在心血管事件和全因死亡率方面。未来，这可能推动更积极的尿酸管理策略和新型降尿酸药物的研发，以期在CKD患者中实现更全面的肾脏和心血管保护。

英文原文

Original
This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies . Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Hyperuricemia independently predicts mortality in chronic kidney disease (CKD), according to a study published online July 13 in the Journal of Clinical Medicine. Rasha Babiker, M.B.B.S., Ph.D., from Ras Al Khaimah Medical and Health Sciences University in the United Arab Emirates, and colleagues assessed whether hyperuricemia has prognostic significance for adverse outcomes in CKD. The analysis included 794 patients with CKD. The researchers found that hyperuricemia was present in 44.8% of patients. There was a trend toward an association between hyperuricemia and increased odds of cardiac events (odds ratio [OR], 1.49; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.98 to 2.27; P = 0.061). Hyperuricemia independently predicted mortality (OR, 1.98; 95% CI, 1.04 to 3.78; P = 0.039). There was also a dose-response relationship between serum uric acid and adverse outcomes, with each 100-μmol/L increase in serum uric acid associated with increased cardiac event risk (OR, 1.22; 95% CI, 1.05 to 1.42; P = 0.011) and increased mortality risk (OR, 1.29; 95% CI, 1.09 to 1.53; P = 0.004). Patients in the highest uric acid quartile had more than threefold higher mortality than those in the lowest quartile (OR, 3.13; 95% CI, 1.26 to 7.79; P = 0.014). "This study suggests that serum uric acid behaves as a graded marker of systemic vulnerability rather than a simple binary risk factor, with progressively higher concentrations associated with higher mortality risk," the authors write. More information Rasha Babiker et al, Serum Uric Acid and Mortality Risk in Chronic Kidney Disease: A Dose–Response Analysis, Journal of Clinical Medicine (2026).
