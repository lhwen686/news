深度解析

文章探讨了卡痛（Kratom）及其核心成分7-羟基帽柱木碱（7-OH）带来的公共卫生风险与监管应对。医学机制上，卡痛低剂量时具兴奋剂效应，高剂量则通过阿片受体激动作用呈现阿片样效应。7-OH作为高效阿片受体激动剂，其药理与传统阿片类药物相似，可导致严重生理依赖、戒断及过量死亡，因此被多州列为附表I管制物质，强调其高滥用潜力与缺乏公认医疗用途。 行业影响方面，各州政府正以超出联邦的速度和范围，将卡痛产品纳入严格管制，这反映了对新型精神活性物质（NPS）快速蔓延的紧急响应。由于7-OH可实验室合成并制成易获取的浓缩产品，其滥用潜力巨大，导致癫痫、幻觉等严重健康问题。严格管制旨在切断销售渠道，保护公众免受不负责任营销的危害。此趋势预示未来药物政策将更注重快速识别和分类新兴高风险化合物，以减轻社会医疗系统的负担。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: States are moving faster and further than federal regulators to get kratom off store shelves, with some banning the herb outright while a pending federal rule targets only one of its chemicals. Massachusetts acted Thursday. Its public health commissioner, Dr. Robbie Goldstein, issued a temporary emergency order placing all forms of kratom into Schedule I under state law. Schedule I substances are subject to the most stringent controls. The order also gives local health boards more authority to take action against stores that keep selling the products—sometimes referred to as "gas station heroin." North Dakota went farther on Aug. 5, when Gov. Kelly Armstrong declared a public health emergency and banned the sale, use and possession of all kratom products statewide. Kratom is a tropical tree native to Southeast Asia and related to the coffee plant. At low doses, it acts like a stimulant. At higher doses, it acts like an opioid. A separate emergency rule from the North Dakota Board of Pharmacy classified the kratom chemical 7-hydroxymitragynine, known as 7-OH, as a Schedule I substance for 180 days. Armstrong has called lawmakers into a special session Sept. 2 to write the restrictions into state law. Florida moved earlier. In June, its attorney general, James Uthmeier, signed an emergency rule that placed concentrated 7-OH and six related compounds in Schedule I. Products sold in Florida may now contain no more than 1 milligram of those chemicals per gram or per milliliter. Federal action is narrower. The U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) announced July 1 that it intends to temporarily place 7-OH and three related substances in Schedule I, according to the Congressional Research Service. The kratom plant itself would not be covered, as long as its 7-OH content stays below set limits. The leaves contain only trace amounts of 7-OH, but the compound can be made in a lab and packed into gummies, tablets, powders and dissolvable strips that are widely sold at gas stations, smoke shops and online. Health officials link the products to seizures, hallucinations, dependence and withdrawal symptoms. Since 2013, Florida medical examiners have tied at least 587 overdose deaths to these compounds, the attorney general's office said. "We are seeing this affect Floridians of all ages, from infants to the elderly, which speaks directly to how aggressively and irresponsibly this drug is marketed," said Dr. Cory Howard, an emergency physician and medical toxicologist at Tampa General Hospital. Dr. Thomas Nappe, an addiction medicine specialist at the same Florida hospital, said 7-OH causes severe dependence and withdrawal, "requiring intense support to foster recovery." Goldstein, the Massachusetts health commissioner, said many users do not understand the danger.
