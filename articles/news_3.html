Severe COVID-19 reveals distinct immune patterns tied to metabolism, not just antiviral response

Patients with COVID-19 can show several different antiviral immune response patterns, which may influence how the disease develops. This is shown in a new study from Karolinska Institutet, published in Genome Medicine, highlighting the importance of interactions between the immune system and metabolism.

Interferons are proteins that help the body fight viral infections by activating interferon-stimulated genes, also known as ISGs. In the present study, researchers at Karolinska Institutet investigated how this antiviral gene response pattern varies among patients hospitalized with COVID-19. By analyzing blood samples, they were able to group patients based on the expression of interferon-stimulated genes.

The findings show that the strength of this ISG response does not directly correspond to disease severity. Some patients with severe COVID-19 had low ISG expression, while others had high levels.

"A strong interferon-stimulated gene signature does not necessarily mean that the immune response is protective. Our results show that disease severity also depends on inflammation, metabolism and how well innate immune cells function," says Soham Gupta, associate professor at the Department of Laboratory Medicine, Karolinska Institutet.

Researchers also found that patients with high ISG expression often showed signs of inflammation and changes in innate immune cells, such as neutrophils and monocytes.

Interaction between immune response and metabolism

Among patients with both high ISG expression and severe disease, additional disturbances in metabolism were observed, particularly in lipid metabolism and energy pathways, which control how the body breaks down and uses fat and energy.

Experiments further showed that their plasma could reduce the activation of certain immune cells, suggesting that the immune system may not function optimally despite signs of inflammation and a strong antiviral gene response signature.

The study is based on analyses of blood and plasma samples from COVID-19 patients in Stockholm, comparisons with healthy and convalescent individuals, and advanced methods such as RNA sequencing, proteomics and metabolomics. The researchers also examined antibody reactivity against interferons, but this did not explain the differences between patient groups.

"This suggests that severe COVID-19 is not a single biological state, but may arise through several different mechanisms," says Gupta.

Next steps

The findings may contribute to more personalized treatment approaches, in which patients receive different therapies depending on how their immune response and metabolism are affected. The researchers now plan to investigate whether these immune-metabolic patterns are linked to long-term outcomes after infection.

The study was led by the Systems Virology Laboratory at the Division of Clinical Microbiology, Department of Laboratory Medicine, Karolinska Institutet, in collaboration with researchers from Karolinska Institutet, Uppsala University, SciLifeLab and Södersjukhuset/Karolinska University Hospital Huddinge.

COVID-19患者可能表现出几种不同的抗病毒免疫反应模式，这可能影响疾病的进展。卡罗林斯卡学院的一项新研究显示了这一点，该研究发表在《基因组医学》上，强调了免疫系统与代谢之间相互作用的重要性。

干扰素是蛋白质，通过激活干扰素刺激基因（也称为ISG）来帮助身体对抗病毒感染。在本研究中，卡罗林斯卡学院的研究人员调查了这种抗病毒基因反应模式在住院的COVID-19患者中如何变化。通过分析血样，他们能够根据干扰素刺激基因的表达将患者分组。

研究结果表明，这种ISG反应的强度并不直接与疾病严重程度相关。一些重症COVID-19患者的ISG表达水平低，而另一些患者则水平高。

“强大的干扰素刺激基因特征并不一定意味着免疫反应具有保护性。我们的结果表明，疾病严重程度还取决于炎症、代谢以及固有免疫细胞的功能状况，”卡罗林斯卡学院检验医学系副教授Soham Gupta说。

研究人员还发现，高ISG表达的患者通常表现出炎症迹象以及固有免疫细胞（如中性粒细胞和单核细胞）的变化。

免疫反应与代谢之间的相互作用

在高ISG表达和重症疾病的患者中，观察到额外的代谢紊乱，特别是在脂质代谢和能量通路方面，它们控制着身体如何分解和利用脂肪与能量。

实验进一步表明，他们的血浆可以降低某些免疫细胞的激活，这表明尽管有炎症迹象和强大的抗病毒基因反应特征，免疫系统可能未能发挥最佳功能。

这项研究基于对斯德哥尔摩COVID-19患者血液和血浆样本的分析，并与健康个体和康复期个体进行了比较，采用了RNA测序、蛋白质组学和代谢组学等先进方法。研究人员还检测了针对干扰素的抗体反应性，但这未能解释患者群体之间的差异。

“这表明重症COVID-19并非单一的生物学状态，而可能通过多种不同的机制产生，”Gupta说。

后续步骤

这些发现可能有助于更个性化的治疗方法，即患者根据其免疫反应和代谢受影响的方式接受不同的治疗。研究人员现在计划调查这些免疫代谢模式是否与感染后的长期预后相关。

该研究由卡罗林斯卡学院检验医学系临床微生物学部门系统病毒学实验室牵头，并与卡罗林斯卡学院、乌普萨拉大学、SciLifeLab和Södersjukhuset/卡罗林斯卡大学医院Huddinge的研究人员合作进行。
