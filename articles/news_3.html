深度解析

这项研究深入揭示了2型糖尿病（T2D）患者罹患慢性肾病（CKD）、心力衰竭（HF）及死亡风险随时间显著升高的严峻现实。其核心机制在于T2D引发的慢性高血糖、胰岛素抵抗和代谢紊乱，导致全身性微血管及大血管病变。高血糖通过非酶糖化和氧化应激等途径，直接损伤肾小球，进展为糖尿病肾病，最终发展为CKD。同时，长期高血糖、高血压、血脂异常和肥胖等共病，加速动脉粥样硬化，损害心肌功能，导致HF。研究强调CKD和HF紧密关联且可相互促进，凸显了糖尿病心肾综合征的病理生理学特征。 这些发现对行业具有深远影响。它强化了“早期、主动、全面”管理T2D的临床策略，需推广早期筛查并综合控制血糖、血压、血脂和体重。SGLT2抑制剂和GLP-1受体激动剂等具有心肾保护作用的新型降糖药物，其在T2D早期干预中的地位将更加凸显，可能改变现有治疗指南并推动相关药物市场增长。研究强调的多重共病负担，促使医疗系统和公共卫生政策向整合性照护模式转型，通过跨学科协作，有效管理T2D及其并发症，以期减轻全球医疗经济负担和患者痛苦。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain The risk of developing chronic kidney disease (CKD) or heart failure (HF), or dying, following a type 2 diabetes (T2D) diagnosis increases substantially over time, according to new research being presented at the annual meeting of the European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD) in Milan, Italy, Sept. 28–Oct. 2. The study, which analyzed data from more than 1.6 million patients across the U.S., found that the combined risk of developing CKD or HF, or dying, was 33% on average within the first five years of a T2D diagnosis, rising to 55% over 10 years. The researchers say their findings underscore the importance of early, proactive and more holistic approaches to managing type 2 diabetes to help reduce the risk of serious complications. T2D is projected to become the world's most prevalent epidemic disease, affecting an estimated 1.3 billion people by 2050. It frequently occurs alongside other conditions, including obesity, high blood pressure, HF, CKD and depression, contributing substantially to the global burden of multimorbidity. This causes considerable suffering for people living with T2D and economic stress for many countries, especially low- and middle-income countries. However, how quickly and in what order people newly diagnosed with T2D develop serious complications such as CKD and HF is not fully understood. To address these knowledge gaps, researchers analyzed real-world data from the CALOR observational study program to estimate the risk of developing CKD or HF following a new T2D diagnosis, the risk of developing the other complication within five years of the first, and the combined risk of developing CKD or HF, or dying, within five and 10 years of diagnosis. They included 1,617,508 patients newly diagnosed with T2D between Jan. 1, 2010, and March 31, 2024, from across the U.S. The average age of participants at enrollment was 59, and 51% were female. Among the 13% of patients who had a body mass index (BMI) recorded, the average (median) was 30.9 kg/m². Almost three-quarters (72%) of participants were also living with dyslipidemia (high levels of cholesterol and other fats in the blood), and more than two-thirds (68%) had high blood pressure. Within five years of a first T2D diagnosis, the risk of developing CKD first was more than twice that of developing HF first (19.0% vs. 8.3%). However, among people who developed HF first, there was a 42% risk of developing CKD within the following five years. Conversely, among those who developed CKD first, there was a 23% risk of developing HF within the following five years. "Our findings clearly show how the serious complications associated with type 2 diabetes rise sharply over time as the common combination of obesity, abnormal blood fats, high blood pressure and higher blood sugar damages blood vessels and vital organs," said professor Naveed Sattar of the University of Glasgow in Scotland, U.K., who co-led the study. "Our results emphasize the critical importance of detecting and treating type 2 diabetes thoroughly at the earliest opportunity to help reduce the risk of serious complications." The study has several limitations, including its observational design, which cannot establish causation. It also cannot rule out selection bias, a common limitation of real-world evidence. For example, differences in the risk of developing CKD or HF by race and ethnicity may have influenced the results. Provided by European Association for the Study of Diabetes
