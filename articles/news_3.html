热门AI聊天机器人正在自信地散布医疗错误信息，分析显示

Popular AI chatbots are confidently dispensing medical misinformation, analysis shows

💡 AI 深度解析 (AI Insight)

本研究揭示了当前生成式人工智能聊天机器人在提供医学信息方面存在的显著局限性与潜在风险。其核心专业概念在于理解AI的运行机制及其与医学知识体系的冲突。AI通过“推断统计模式”和“预测词序列”生成内容，而非进行逻辑推理、权衡证据或做出伦理判断。这意味着AI本质上是一个复杂的模式匹配器，无法真正理解医学概念的深层含义和临床复杂性。当其训练数据包含大量来自问答论坛、社交媒体和有限的开放获取科学文献（仅占已发表研究的30-50%）时，AI模型在追求对话流畅性的同时，其输出的科学准确性必然受损。

这种机制缺陷导致了“AI幻觉”和“虚构引用”等问题，使得AI在诸如干细胞、运动表现和营养等专业领域表现尤差，可能引导非专业用户采取无效甚至有害的治疗方案。其行业影响深远：在医疗领域，错误的医学信息不仅可能误导患者，延误正确诊断与治疗，甚至直接危害生命健康。因此，文章强调了对AI聊天机器人进行“公众教育、专业培训和监管监督”的迫切性。未来，AI在医疗健康领域的应用必须以辅助角色存在，需经由专业医务人员的严格审查和验证，以确保其作为工具能够“支持而非损害公众健康”，避免将AI的统计模式输出等同于专业的医学诊疗或建议。

A substantial amount of medical information provided by five popular chatbots is inaccurate and incomplete, with half (50%) of the responses problematic: 30% were somewhat, and 20% were highly problematic. These are the results of a study published in the journal BMJ Open.

Continued deployment of these chatbots without public education and oversight risks amplifying misinformation, warn the researchers.

Generative AI chatbots have been rapidly adopted across research, education, business, marketing and medicine, with many people using them like search engines, including for everyday health and medical queries, explain the researchers.

How the study was set up

To gauge the level of accuracy provided in areas of health and medicine already prone to misinformation, and therefore with consequences for everyday health behavior, the researchers probed five publicly available and popular generative AI chatbots in February 2025: Gemini (Google); DeepSeek (High-Flyer); Meta AI (Meta); ChatGPT (OpenAI); and Grok (xAI).

Each chatbot was prompted with 10 open-ended and closed questions in each of five categories of cancer, vaccines, stem cells, nutrition, and athletic performance. The prompts were designed to resemble common "information-seeking" health and medical queries and misinformation tropes online and in academic discourse.

And they were developed to "strain" models towards misinformation or contraindicated advice—a strategy increasingly used for stress testing AI chatbots and picking up behavioral vulnerabilities, note the researchers.

Closed prompts required chatbots to provide pre-defined responses, often with one correct answer, that aligned with the scientific consensus. Open-ended prompts typically required chatbots to generate multiple responses in list form.

How responses were judged and scored

Responses were categorized as non-, somewhat, or highly problematic, using objective pre-defined criteria. A problematic response was defined as one that could plausibly direct lay users to potentially ineffective treatment or come to harm if followed without professional guidance.

The information was scored for accuracy and completeness, and particular attention was given to whether a chatbot presented a false balance between science and non-science-based claims, regardless of the strength of the evidence.

Each response was also graded on readability, ranging from whether it was written in easy, plain English, to difficult, academic language, using the Flesch Reading Ease score.

Key findings on prompt type and models

Prompt type was influential: open-ended prompts, for example, produced 40 highly problematic responses—significantly more than expected—and 51 non-problematic responses—significantly fewer than expected. The opposite was true of closed prompts.

While the quality of responses didn't differ significantly among the five chatbots, Grok generated significantly more highly problematic responses than would be expected (29/50; 58%). Gemini generated the fewest highly problematic responses and the most non-problematic ones.

The chatbots performed best in the area of vaccines and cancer, and worst in the area of stem cells, athletic performance, and nutrition.

Answers were consistently expressed with confidence and certainty, with few caveats or disclaimers. Out of the total 250 questions, there were only two refusals to answer, both of which came from Meta AI in response to queries about anabolic steroids and alternative cancer treatments.

Reference quality was poor, with an average completeness score of 40%. Chatbot hallucinations and fabricated citations meant that no chatbot provided a fully accurate reference list.

All readability scores were graded as "difficult," equivalent in complexity to suitability for a college graduate.

Limits of the research and context

The researchers acknowledge that they only assessed five chatbots and that commercial AI is rapidly evolving, so their findings might not be universally applicable. And not all real-world queries are deliberately adversarial, an approach they took which may have overstated the prevalence of problematic content.

Nevertheless, "Our findings regarding scientific accuracy, reference quality, and response readability highlight important behavioral limitations and the need to re-evaluate how AI chatbots are deployed in public-facing health and medical communication," they point out.

"By default, chatbots do not access real-time data but instead generate outputs by inferring statistical patterns from their training data and predicting likely word sequences. They do not reason or weigh evidence, nor are they able to make ethical or value-based judgments," they explain.

"This behavioral limitation means that chatbots can reproduce authoritative-sounding but potentially flawed responses."

The data chatbots draw on also includes Q&A forums and social media, and scientific content is typically limited to open access or publicly available articles, which comprise only 30–50% of published studies. While this enhances conversational fluency, it may come at the cost of scientific accuracy, advise the researchers.

"As the use of AI chatbots continues to expand, our data highlight a need for public education, professional training, and regulatory oversight to ensure that generative AI supports, rather than erodes, public health," they conclude.

五款热门聊天机器人提供的大量医学信息不准确且不完整，其中一半（50%）的回答存在问题：30%有一定问题，20%有严重问题。这些是发表在《英国医学杂志开放版》（BMJ Open）上的一项研究结果。

研究人员警告称，若不进行公众教育和监管，持续部署这些聊天机器人恐会加剧错误信息的传播风险。

生成式人工智能聊天机器人已迅速被研究、教育、商业、营销和医学等领域采用，许多人将其用作搜索引擎，包括用于日常健康和医学查询，研究人员解释道。

研究是如何设置的

为了衡量在容易出现错误信息的健康和医学领域（从而对日常健康行为产生影响）提供的信息准确性水平，研究人员在2025年2月对五款公开可用且流行的生成式人工智能聊天机器人进行了探究：Gemini (Google)；DeepSeek (High-Flyer)；Meta AI (Meta)；ChatGPT (OpenAI)；以及Grok (xAI)。

每个聊天机器人都在癌症、疫苗、干细胞、营养和运动表现这五个类别中，分别被提问了10个开放式和封闭式问题。这些提示旨在模拟常见的“信息寻求”健康和医学查询以及在线和学术讨论中的错误信息“套路”。

研究人员指出，这些提示被设计成“迫使”模型产生错误信息或禁忌建议——这是一种越来越多地用于对人工智能聊天机器人进行压力测试并发现行为漏洞的策略。

封闭式提示要求聊天机器人提供预设的回答，通常只有一个正确答案，并且与科学共识一致。开放式提示通常要求聊天机器人以列表形式生成多个回答。

回答如何判断和评分

回答根据客观的预定义标准被分为无问题、有一定问题或有严重问题。一个有问题的回答被定义为可能合理地引导普通用户采取潜在无效治疗或在没有专业指导的情况下可能造成伤害的回答。

信息根据准确性和完整性进行评分，并特别关注聊天机器人是否在科学和非科学主张之间呈现虚假平衡，无论证据强度如何。

每个回答的易读性也得到了评分，范围从是否使用简单易懂的英语，到使用晦涩的学术语言，评分使用弗莱希阅读易度（Flesch Reading Ease）分数。

关于提示类型和模型的关键发现

提示类型具有影响力：例如，开放式提示产生了40个严重问题的回答——显著多于预期——和51个无问题的回答——显著少于预期。封闭式提示则相反。

虽然五款聊天机器人的回答质量没有显著差异，但Grok产生的严重问题的回答显著多于预期（29/50；58%）。Gemini产生的严重问题的回答最少，无问题的回答最多。

聊天机器人在疫苗和癌症领域的表现最好，在干细胞、运动表现和营养领域的表现最差。

回答始终表达自信和确定，很少有保留意见或免责声明。在总共250个问题中，只有两次拒绝回答，都来自Meta AI，涉及合成代谢类固醇和替代癌症治疗的查询。

参考文献质量差，平均完整性评分为40%。聊天机器人幻觉和虚构引用意味着没有一个聊天机器人提供完全准确的参考文献列表。

所有易读性分数都被评定为“困难”，复杂程度相当于适合大学毕业生。

研究的局限性和背景

研究人员承认，他们只评估了五款聊天机器人，并且商业人工智能正在迅速发展，因此他们的发现可能不具有普遍适用性。而且并非所有现实世界的查询都是故意对抗性的，他们采取的这种方法可能夸大了问题内容的普遍性。

然而，他们指出，“我们关于科学准确性、参考文献质量和回答易读性的发现，突显了重要的行为局限性以及重新评估人工智能聊天机器人在面向公众的健康和医疗通信中如何部署的必要性。”

他们解释说：“聊天机器人默认不访问实时数据，而是通过从其训练数据中推断统计模式并预测可能的词序列来生成输出。它们不进行推理或权衡证据，也无法做出伦理或基于价值的判断。”

“这种行为局限性意味着聊天机器人可以复现听起来权威但可能存在缺陷的回答。”

聊天机器人所依赖的数据还包括问答论坛和社交媒体，而科学内容通常仅限于开放获取或公开可用的文章，这仅占已发表研究的30-50%。研究人员建议，虽然这增强了对话流畅性，但可能以牺牲科学准确性为代价。

他们总结道：“随着人工智能聊天机器人使用的持续扩大，我们的数据强调了公众教育、专业培训和监管监督的必要性，以确保生成式人工智能能够支持而非损害公众健康。”
