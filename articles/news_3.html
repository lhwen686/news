Single iron infusion improved exercise capacity in people with the most common form of heart failure

深度解析

本研究揭示了静脉补铁在射血分数保留型心力衰竭（HFpEF）合并缺铁患者中的潜在治疗价值。HFpEF是一种特殊类型的心力衰竭，其特点是心脏收缩功能正常，但舒张功能受损，导致心室充盈受限，最终影响全身供血。由于其复杂的病理生理学，HFpEF目前缺乏有效的特异性治疗方案。缺铁，即使没有合并贫血，也会通过影响线粒体功能（能量生成）和氧气转运，进一步加重HFpEF患者的运动耐量限制。 本试验通过静脉注射去铁异麦芽糖，有效绕过了口服铁剂可能存在的胃肠道吸收问题，直接提高了患者体内铁的生物利用度。研究结果显示，静脉补铁显著改善了峰值摄氧量（VO₂），这是一个衡量心肺适能的金标准指标，其10%的提升对于严重运动受限的HFpEF患者具有重要的临床意义。此外，其他次要运动能力指标也得到改善。这项发现为HFpEF的管理提供了新的方向，提示通过纠正缺铁状态，可能改善患者的生活质量和功能状态。尽管需要更大规模、更广泛人群的长期研究来验证其对临床结局的长期影响，但该研究为静脉补铁在心力衰竭治疗领域开辟了新的视角，有望影响未来的临床实践指南。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain A new study by investigators from the Mass General Brigham Heart and Vascular Institute found that a single iron infusion improved exercise capacity in people with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) and iron deficiency. The findings suggest that replenishing iron could help address the physical limitations associated with this condition. Results were presented at the 2026 American Heart Association Scientific Sessions and published simultaneously in Circulation. The IRON-MET HFpEF trial enrolled 65 participants with symptomatic HFpEF and iron deficiency, which affects more than 50% of patients with HFpEF. Their median age was 68 years, and 72% of participants were women. Forty-four participants were randomized to receive one 1,000-mg IV dose of ferric derisomaltose and 21 to receive placebo. Iron was administered intravenously because previous studies have demonstrated that oral iron is not beneficial in patients with other forms of heart failure, likely due to poor gastrointestinal absorption. All participants underwent cardiopulmonary exercise testing before and 12 weeks after the infusion. At 12 weeks, peak oxygen uptake, or VO₂—which is a key indicator of cardiorespiratory fitness—improved by approximately 10% in the intervention group and was unchanged in the placebo group. "Patients living with HFpEF are burdened with severe limitations in exercise tolerance," said lead author Gregory D. Lewis, director of heart failure at the Mass General Brigham Heart and Vascular Institute and professor of medicine at Harvard Medical School. "In the first study to comprehensively evaluate exercise response patterns to iron repletion in patients with HFpEF and iron deficiency, we found that ferric derisomaltose improved multiple measures of exercise capacity." Measurements of the bioavailability of iron in the blood improved with intravenous iron, as did prespecified secondary measures of exercise capacity, including 6-minute walk distance, VO₂ at the anaerobic threshold and New York Heart Association functional class, compared with those who received placebo. However, the authors note that patient-reported health scores, based on a comprehensive questionnaire, did not differ significantly between groups. There were no serious adverse events associated with the iron supplementation. As the study was conducted in a small, predominantly white population and participants were assessed at 12 weeks, the authors advocate for further studies to determine whether IV iron has benefits in diverse populations and whether these benefits last over longer time periods. "The improvement we observed in peak VO₂ represents a clinically meaningful increment for patients with heart failure," said senior author Rajeev Malhotra, who is associate director of the Cardiopulmonary Exercise Laboratory at the Mass General Brigham Heart and Vascular Institute and associate professor of medicine at Harvard Medical School. "The next step is to determine whether IV iron can improve long-term clinical outcomes in patients with HFpEF." Publication details Iron Repletion in Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction: The IRONMET-HFpEF Randomized Trial, Circulation (2026).
