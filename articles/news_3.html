Grape consumption linked to changes in gene expression and improved skin health

Clinical trials have shown that consumption of grapes can increase the resilience of skin to UV irradiation in about 30%–50% of the subject population. A new study published in the current issue of the journal ACS Nutrition Science suggests the effect of consuming grapes on human skin health is much broader—most likely affecting everyone.

The investigation was performed with human volunteers who consumed the equivalent of three servings of whole grapes each day for two weeks. Gene expression in skin was determined before and after grape consumption, with and without exposure of skin to low doses of UV irradiation.

The results demonstrated inter- and intraindividual differences of gene expression in the skin. In other words, from the outset, gene expression in the skin of each individual was different. The expression changed when the subjects consumed grapes or when exposed to UV irradiation, but still each individual was different from one another. Further unique changes were observed when UV irradiation was combined with grape consumption.

Consequently, while gene expression is different when comparing one person to another person, the gene expression of every person changed after consuming grapes.

When searching for a functional commonality resulting from these changes in gene expression after consuming grapes, the investigators were able to construe the myriads of data as being indicative of enhanced skin keratinization and cornification, which are known to create a barrier against environmental damage. This idea was reinforced by exposing skin to a low dose of UV irradiation and measuring the generation of malondialdehyde, a marker of oxidative stress, which was reduced when the subjects consumed grapes.

"We are now certain that grapes act as a superfood and mediate a nutrigenomic response in humans," said John Pezzuto, Ph.D., Professor and Dean of the College of Pharmacy and Health Sciences at Western New England University. "We observed this with the largest organ of the body, the skin. The changes in gene expression indicated improvements in skin health.

"But beyond skin, it is nearly certain that grape consumption affects gene expression in other somatic tissues of the body, such as liver, muscle, kidney and even brain. This helps us to understand how consumption of a whole food, in this case grapes, affects our overall health. It's very exciting to be working in the post-genomics era where we can finally start to employ functional genomics and actually visualize complex matrices indicative of nutrigenomic responses."

The studies were performed by investigators at Western New England University, Springfield, MA, in collaboration with investigators at Oregon State University, Corvallis, OR. Financial support for the work was provided by the California Table Grape Commission.

临床试验表明，摄入葡萄可在约 30%–50% 的受试人群中提高皮肤对紫外线辐射的抵抗力。发表在最新一期《ACS营养科学》杂志上的一项新研究表明，摄入葡萄对人体皮肤健康的影响更为广泛——很可能影响所有人。

这项调查是在人体志愿者中进行的，他们每天摄入相当于三份整葡萄，持续两周。在摄入葡萄前后，以及在皮肤暴露于低剂量紫外线照射或未暴露的情况下，测定了皮肤中的基因表达。

结果显示皮肤基因表达存在个体间和个体内的差异。换句话说，从一开始，每个个体皮肤中的基因表达就各不相同。当受试者摄入葡萄或暴露于紫外线照射时，基因表达发生变化，但每个个体之间仍然存在差异。当紫外线照射与葡萄摄入相结合时，观察到了进一步独特的改变。

因此，尽管比较不同个体时基因表达存在差异，但每个人在摄入葡萄后其基因表达都发生了变化。

在寻找摄入葡萄后这些基因表达变化所产生的功能共性时，研究人员能够将大量数据解读为表明皮肤角质化和角化增强，而这已知能形成对抗环境损害的屏障。通过将皮肤暴露于低剂量紫外线照射并测量丙二醛（一种氧化应激标志物）的生成量来强化了这一观点，当受试者摄入葡萄时，丙二醛的生成量减少了。

“我们现在确定葡萄是一种超级食物，并在人体中介导营养基因组反应，”西新英格兰大学药学与健康科学学院院长兼教授约翰·佩祖托博士说。“我们在人体最大的器官——皮肤上观察到了这一点。基因表达的变化表明皮肤健康有所改善。”

“但除了皮肤，几乎可以肯定的是，摄入葡萄还会影响身体其他体细胞组织（如肝脏、肌肉、肾脏甚至大脑）的基因表达。这有助于我们理解全食物（在本例中是葡萄）的摄入如何影响我们的整体健康。在后基因组时代工作非常令人兴奋，我们终于可以开始利用功能基因组学，并实际可视化指示营养基因组反应的复杂矩阵。”

这项研究由马萨诸塞州斯普林菲尔德的西新英格兰大学研究人员与俄勒冈州科瓦利斯的俄勒冈州立大学研究人员合作进行。这项工作的财政支持由加州鲜食葡萄委员会提供。
