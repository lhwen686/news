深度解析

这篇新闻报道揭示了表观基因组学研究领域一项重要的技术性挑战及其创新解决方案。文章的核心概念围绕“表观基因组”及其关键的测序技术“CUT&Tag”。表观基因组是指在不改变DNA序列的前提下，通过DNA甲基化、组蛋白修饰等机制调控基因表达的遗传信息层，它在细胞分化、发育及疾病发生发展中扮演关键角色。CUT&Tag技术因其高灵敏度和微量样本适用性，成为绘制表观基因组图谱的流行方法，尤其在单细胞水平的应用中优势显著。然而，研究发现该技术存在“潜在偏倚”，即Tn5转座酶在进行DNA片段化时偏好结合基因组的开放区域，从而引入非生物学相关的假象信号，严重干扰了对真实基因调控机制的解读。 为解决此问题，弗吉尼亚大学团队开发了基于机器学习的PATTY工具。PATTY通过学习真实信号与技术假象之间的差异，能够有效校正Tn5转座酶引起的偏倚，显著提升了CUT&Tag数据的准确性和可靠性。这一创新对行业影响深远：它将极大地提高表观基因组学研究的可信度，避免研究人员基于错误数据得出结论，从而节省宝贵的时间和资源。更精准的表观基因组图谱将加速疾病诊断生物标志物的发现，并推动基于表观遗传机制的新型药物研发。此外，PATTY为其他高通量基因组技术中的类似偏差校正提供了概念框架，有望促进整个基因组学领域的数据质量提升和技术发展。

英文原文

Original
University of Virginia School of Medicine scientists have identified a widespread source of error in a popular method for studying the genome – our genetic instructions – and created a machine-learning tool to correct it. The free tool could improve the reliability of both conventional and single-cell data generated using this method, giving researchers a clearer view of how gene activity is controlled in health and disease and providing stronger foundations for future diagnostic and drug-development efforts. Although nearly every cell in the body contains the same DNA sequence, different cells use different sets of genes to maintain their identities and functions. Much of that control comes from the "epigenome" – chemical modifications and structural features of chromosomes that influence whether genes are turned on or off without changing the DNA sequence itself. The CUT&Tag (Cleavage Under Targets & Tagmentation) method can map these epigenomic features efficiently from very small samples and even from individual cells. However, the UVA researchers, led by Chongzhi Zang, PhD, found a "hidden bias" in the method that creates artifacts resembling genuine biological signals. The DNA sequence is like the sheet music. The epigenome determines which notes to play, when they are played and by what instruments. When a technical artifact looks like a real signal, researchers can be led toward the wrong biological mechanism. That risk is especially serious in single-cell data, where the true signals are already sparse." Chongzhi Zang, UVA's Department of Genome Sciences and director of computational genomics, UVA Comprehensive Cancer Center Finding and fixing hidden bias CUT&Tag has many advantages over prior methods and has quickly become popular among scientists. But it also has weaknesses that have gone unnoticed until recently. The assay relies on an enzyme called Tn5 transposase, which naturally favors open, accessible regions of the genome. Zang and colleagues found that this preference can create misleading and confusing research results that the scientists described as "severe" after examining nearly 300 published datasets. Having discovered the unexpected scope of the problem, Zang and his team developed PATTY (Propensity Analyzer for Tn5 Transposase Yielded bias), a tool that uses machine learning to correct the CUT&Tag bias. It is effective both in conventional data from many cells and in sparser single-cell data, the scientists report. "Detecting true signals in noisy data is like finding a needle in a haystack, and it is even more difficult when many pieces of hay look like real needles," Zang said. "PATTY does not detect signals simply by subtracting a background. It learns how a real needle differs from hay and uses the learned model to reduce the artifact while preserving real signals." Zang and his colleagues have made PATTY available as a free, open-source software package at Github (https://github.com/zang-lab/PATTY) and Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20078737). They hope their tool will improve genomic research and eventually help get new diagnostics and medicines to patients faster, a major mission of UVA's new Paul and Diane Manning Institute of Biotechnology. "We believe that bias correction should become a routine part of CUT&Tag analysis," Zang said. "Cleaner data can keep scientists from wasting time and resources pursuing technical artifacts and can make real biological differences easier to see. More broadly, PATTY provides a conceptual framework for correcting similar biases in other genomic technologies." Findings published The researchers have described the development of PATTY in an article in the scientific journal Nature Communications. The article is open access, meaning it is free to read. The UVA research team consisted of Shengen Shawn Hu, Qingying Chen, Megan C. Grieco, Mengxue Tian and Zang. Collaborators Zhangli Su and Anindya Dutta of the University of Alabama at Birmingham and Lin Liu of Shanghai Jiao Tong University also contributed. The scientists have no financial interest in the work. The research was supported by the National Institutes of Health, grants R35GM133712, R21HG012981, R00CA259526 and R01CA060499.

弗吉尼亚大学医学院的科学家们发现了一种在研究基因组（即我们的遗传指令）的常用方法中普遍存在的错误来源，并创建了一个机器学习工具来纠正它。 这个免费工具可以提高使用该方法生成的常规数据和单细胞数据的可靠性，使研究人员能够更清晰地了解基因活性在健康和疾病中如何受到调控，并为未来的诊断和药物开发工作奠定更坚实的基础。 尽管人体内几乎每个细胞都含有相同的DNA序列，但不同的细胞使用不同的基因集合来维持其身份和功能。这种调控大部分来自于“表观基因组”——染色体的化学修饰和结构特征，它们在不改变DNA序列本身的情况下影响基因的开启或关闭。CUT&Tag（靶点裂解与标签连接）方法能够高效地从非常小的样本甚至单个细胞中绘制这些表观基因组特征图谱。然而，由张崇志博士领导的弗吉尼亚大学研究人员发现该方法存在一种“潜在偏倚”，会产生类似于真实生物信号的假象。 “DNA序列就像乐谱。表观基因组决定了演奏哪些音符，何时演奏以及使用何种乐器。当技术性假象看起来像真实的信号时，研究人员可能会被误导到错误的生物学机制上。这种风险在单细胞数据中尤为严重，因为那里的真实信号本身就稀疏。”弗吉尼亚大学基因组科学系和弗吉尼亚大学综合癌症中心计算基因组学主任张崇志说。 发现并纠正潜在偏倚 CUT&Tag相较于早期方法有许多优势，并迅速在科学家中流行起来。但它也存在一些直到最近才被注意到的缺点。该检测依赖于一种名为Tn5转座酶的酶，这种酶天然偏爱基因组中开放、可及的区域。张崇志和同事们发现，这种偏好会产生误导性和混淆性的研究结果，在检查了近300个已发表的数据集后，科学家们将其描述为“严重”。 在发现了这个问题意想不到的范围后，张崇志和他的团队开发了PATTY（Tn5转座酶诱导偏差倾向分析器），这是一种利用机器学习来纠正CUT&Tag偏差的工具。科学家们报告称，它对来自许多细胞的常规数据和更稀疏的单细胞数据都有效。 “在嘈杂的数据中检测真实信号就像大海捞针，当许多干草看起来都像真针时，这会更加困难。”张崇志说，“PATTY并非简单地通过减去背景来检测信号。它学习真实针与干草的区别，并使用学到的模型来减少假象，同时保留真实信号。” 张崇志和他的同事们已将PATTY作为一个免费、开源的软件包在Github (https://github.com/zang-lab/PATTY) 和 Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20078737) 上提供。他们希望这个工具能改善基因组学研究，并最终帮助更快地将新的诊断方法和药物带给患者，这也是弗吉尼亚大学新成立的保罗和黛安·曼宁生物技术研究所的一项重要使命。 “我们相信偏差校正应该成为CUT&Tag分析的常规部分，”张崇志说，“更清洁的数据可以防止科学家们浪费时间和资源去追求技术性假象，并能让真正的生物学差异更容易被发现。更广泛地说，PATTY为纠正其他基因组技术中的类似偏差提供了一个概念框架。” 研究结果发表 研究人员已在科学期刊《自然—通讯》上发表了一篇文章，描述了PATTY的开发。该文章是开放获取的，意味着可以免费阅读。弗吉尼亚大学研究团队包括胡声根、陈庆颖、梅根·C·格里科、田梦雪和张崇志。阿拉巴马大学伯明翰分校的合作者苏章利和阿宁迪亚·杜塔以及上海交通大学的刘林也做出了贡献。科学家们在该工作中没有任何经济利益。 该研究得到了美国国立卫生研究院的资助，项目号分别为R35GM133712、R21HG012981、R00CA259526和R01CA060499。
