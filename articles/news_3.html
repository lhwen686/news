开心手术同期左心耳封堵仅对高危患者有益

Left atrial appendage closure during open-heart surgery only beneficial for high-risk patients

深度解析

LAACS-2试验为心脏外科领域带来了关于左心耳（LAA）闭合术在卒中预防中的作用的关键见解。LAA是心脏左心房的一个小囊袋，因其结构复杂且血流动力学特征，易于形成血栓，尤其是在心房颤动（AF）患者中，这些血栓脱落后可引发缺血性卒中。外科闭合LAA的核心机制在于物理性地隔离这一血栓高发区域，从而降低栓塞风险。 这项大规模临床试验的结果提示，LAA闭合术不应作为所有接受开心手术患者的常规干预手段。这一发现挑战了此前可能存在的普遍适用性假设，强调了医疗决策的精准化。然而，对于基线卒中风险较高的特定患者群体（通过CHA2DS2-VASc评分评估），LAA闭合术显示出显著的保护作用，可使卒中或短暂性脑缺血发作的风险降低56%。这凸显了风险分层和个体化治疗策略的重要性。 其行业影响是多方面的：首先，它将促使临床医生更加审慎地评估患者的卒中风险，并根据风险评估结果，而非普遍性的规则来决定是否进行LAA闭合术，从而优化临床实践。其次，未来相关的医学指南，如欧洲心脏病学会（ESC）指南，可能会更新其推荐，更明确地指出LAA闭合术的适应证和患者选择标准。最后，这项研究也为医疗器械研发和后续的机制研究指明了方向，即如何更精准地识别受益人群以及深入理解LAA闭合术在不同风险人群中发挥保护作用的具体生物学机制。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Jonathan Borba from Pexels Results do not support routine closure of the left atrial appendage in all patients undergoing open-heart surgery, but those at high stroke risk may benefit. This was the main conclusion of the LAACS-2 trial presented in a Hot Line session today at ESC Congress 2026. Following open-heart surgery, patients face a high long-term risk of stroke. The left atrial appendage (LAA) is a small muscular pouch in the heart's left atrium that is a common source of blood clots in patients who have atrial fibrillation (AF). Surgical closure of the LAA is recommended in ESC Guidelines as an adjunct to oral anticoagulation for patients with AF undergoing cardiac surgery. However, evidence is lacking in patients without a known history of AF. Associate Professor Helena Dominguez from Bispebjerg University Hospital, Copenhagen, Denmark, and colleagues previously conducted the LAACS trial in patients undergoing open-heart surgery. She noted, "With long-term follow-up, we observed a lower risk of stroke after LAA closure whether the patient had AF or not. We conducted the much larger LAACS-2 trial to further investigate whether LAA closure has a protective effect in patients with, but mostly without, prior AF." The LAACS-2 trial was conducted across four sites in Denmark, Spain and Sweden. Patients undergoing first-time planned open-heart surgery without AF and those with a previous diagnosis of paroxysmal or chronic AF were included. A total of 1,500 patients were randomized (1:1) to either concomitant LAA closure or standard care (open LAA). The mean age was 67 years, and 18.5% were female. Before surgery, 4.1% had AF, 6.1% had prior stroke and 2.5% had prior transient ischemic attack. Across a median 4.0 years of follow-up, there was no significant difference in the primary outcome of stroke or transient ischemic attack with LAA closure vs. standard care (4.28% vs. 5.04%, respectively; hazard ratio 0.85; 95% confidence interval 0.53 to 1.37; p = 0.517). Of note, patients who had the highest stroke risk at baseline (CHA2DS2-VASc score above the median) had a 56% reduction in the primary endpoint with LAA closure (p = 0.018), while no significant benefit was observed in those with lower stroke risk. Overall, there was no significant difference in mortality between the groups. "Our results do not support routine LAA closure in all patients undergoing planned cardiac surgery. However, closure appeared to confer protection in patients at high risk of stroke," concluded Dominguez. "As we saw in the LAACS trial, the primary endpoint curves diverged over time in LAACS-2, and follow-up will be continued." Dominguez highlighted an ongoing mechanistic study that aims to further understand stroke risk following LAA closure in patients undergoing open-heart surgery. Clinical categories Cardiology
