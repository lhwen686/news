深度解析

该医学新闻揭示了NEXAF试验的重要成果，证实了结构化运动干预对心房颤动（AF）患者的显著益处。AF是一种常见的心律失常，其特点是心房电活动紊乱，导致心跳不规则且通常过快。长期以来，生活方式干预被认为是AF管理的关键，但运动的系统性应用仍有待加强。NEXAF试验采用混合模式，结合了短期监督下的高强度训练与长期居家远程监测，这种创新模式展现出临床可行性和资源效率。研究数据显示，运动干预组的AF负担（心房颤动占总监测时间的百分比）显著降低45%，同时心肺适能和静息心率得到改善，并大幅减少了总住院率和AF相关住院率。尽管对AF特异性生活质量的直接改善不显著，但这些客观生理指标的积极变化及其对医疗资源利用的影响，已足以强调运动疗法在AF治疗中的核心地位。这一发现不仅弥补了现有知识和实施的差距，也为未来AF指南的修订和临床实践提供了强有力的循证依据，预示着基于数字技术支持的个性化运动康复将成为AF管理的新范式。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain An exercise program combining short-term supervision with long-term home-based exercise was associated with reduced atrial fibrillation burden and other meaningful benefits, according to results presented in a Hot Line session at ESC Congress 2026. While the incidence of some cardiovascular diseases is stable or decreasing, atrial fibrillation (AF) prevalence is rising. Although lifestyle changes and risk factor modification are acknowledged as central components of AF management, tailored exercise remains underused. Principal investigator of the NEXAF trial, Dr. Bjarne Nes from the Norwegian University of Science and Technology, Trondheim, Norway, noted, "Despite recommendations in ESC Guidelines, there is currently an implementation gap—only around 1 in 10 eligible patients are being referred for exercise-based rehabilitation according to a survey from the European Heart Rhythm Association." Nes also pointed out a knowledge gap: "The few trials that have been published evaluating exercise are mostly short-term, small-scale studies where AF burden has not been continuously monitored." The NEXAF trial was designed to evaluate the effects of a one-year exercise intervention in reducing total AF burden and improving AF-specific quality of life (QoL) in symptomatic patients with AF. The trial was conducted at three centers in Norway and enrolled patients with nonpermanent AF and physical activity levels below general recommendations. Patients were randomized (1:1) to a one-year exercise intervention or usual care. The exercise intervention consisted of eight supervised high-intensity sessions during the first four to six weeks, followed mainly by remote exercise monitoring and guidance using a smartwatch, app and web-based platform. Usual care involved no structured or supervised exercise programs. An insertable cardiac monitor was implanted in all participants. The co-primary endpoints were time in AF as a percentage of total time monitored (AF burden) during the one-year follow-up and change in the Atrial Fibrillation Effect on Quality of Life (AFEQT) questionnaire overall score at one year. The trial population included 295 patients who had a mean age of 64 years, and 30% were women. The researchers found a 45% relative reduction in the co-primary endpoint of AF burden in patients assigned to the exercise intervention vs. usual care at one year (3.9% vs. 7.1%, respectively; p = 0.016). Despite the improvement in AF burden, there was no significant difference in AF-specific QoL with the exercise intervention at one year (AFEQT score: +5.6 vs. +4.4; p = 0.43). Regarding secondary endpoints, improvements were observed in measures of cardiorespiratory fitness and resting heart rate with the exercise intervention. The researchers also observed a 37% reduction in total hospitalizations and a 46% reduction in AF-related hospitalizations. Summing up the findings, Nes said, "In this large, randomized trial, a one-year exercise intervention induced a meaningful reduction in total AF burden, improved fitness and led to fewer AF-related hospitalizations compared to usual care." He noted that the hybrid model combining short-term supervision with long-term home-based exercise and digital support was delivered with modest personnel resources and was clinically feasible. "The trial strengthens the arguments for broader implementation of structured, long-term exercise in AF care," he concluded. The first dedicated ESC Guidelines on cardiac rehabilitation were published recently in the European Heart Journal. Key medical concepts Atrial Fibrillation
