Scientists discover BRCA links to head and neck cancer risks

Original Text
An international group led by researchers from the RIKEN Center for Integrative Medical Sciences (IMS) in Japan have discovered associations between pathogenic variants of the BRCA 1 and 2 genes and four types of cancer. Published in ESMO Open, the findings expand the potential for personalized medicine to several cancer types that currently have limited treatment options and poor prognoses.

Pathogenic variants of the BRCA genes are well known to be associated with the risk of breast cancer, as well as ovarian, pancreatic, and prostate cancer. As a result, personalized medicine for these types of cancer using PARP inhibitors—a type of drug that kills cancer cells by preventing them from repairing their DNA—or certain chemotherapeutic drugs has become routine in clinical practice. For most other less common cancers, the BRCA association has yet to be studied. The goal of the new study was to fill in this missing information gap and determine whether the potential for BRCA-based personalized medicine exists for other types of cancer.

To start with, the researchers performed a case-control analysis of 3,489 patients whose data was taken from BioBank Japan, a multi-institutional, hospital-based registry of blood samples from all over Japan taken between 2003 and 2018. This type of analysis involves looking at people with and without a particular disease and identifying factors that could have led to different clinical outcomes. In this case they focused on nine types of cancer that have yet to be tested for a BRCA-gene association, namely bladder, bone, brain, head and neck, sarcoma, skin, testis, thyroid, and ureteral cancer. They compared the BRCA variants of the patients with those of 38,842 individuals from the same database who did not have cancer.

From the analysis, they found that pathogenic variants in BRCA1 increase the risk of thyroid cancer, while pathogenic variants in BRCA2 increase the risk of bladder cancer, head and neck cancer, and skin cancer. For bladder cancer, they found that the impact of BRCA2 pathogenic variants on cancer risk was greater in women than in men.

In the world of medical research, resources, both funding and manpower, are often skewed toward the most common and most deadly diseases. As a result, people with less common diseases are often out of luck when it comes to clinical trials or new treatment options. According to Hajime Sasagawa, the first author of the paper, "While previous studies have expanded the cancer risk profile associated with BRCA1/2 pathogenic variants to ovarian, pancreatic, and prostate cancer, we felt that relatively less common cancer types would also benefit from an expansion of genetic evidence, particularly because of their limited treatment options and poor prognoses. I am happy that we were able to gain these new insights."

Although this study will not lead to immediate recommendations for active surveillance of those cancer types, we hope that these findings will contribute to the development of personalized medicine guidelines for these four types of cancer." 由日本理化学研究所综合医学科学中心（IMS）研究人员领导的一个国际研究小组发现，BRCA1和BRCA2基因的致病性变异与四种癌症存在关联。该研究成果发表在《欧洲肿瘤内科学会开放期刊》（ESMO Open）上，这些发现扩展了精准医疗在目前治疗选择有限且预后不良的多种癌症类型中的潜力。

BRCA基因的致病性变异众所周知与乳腺癌以及卵巢癌、胰腺癌和前列腺癌的风险相关。因此，对于这些类型的癌症，使用PARP抑制剂（一种通过阻止癌细胞修复其DNA来杀死癌细胞的药物）或某些化疗药物进行精准医疗已成为临床实践中的常规。对于大多数其他相对不常见的癌症，BRCA关联尚未得到研究。这项新研究的目标是填补这一信息空白，确定基于BRCA的精准医疗对其他类型的癌症是否存在潜力。

首先，研究人员对3,489名患者进行了病例对照分析，这些患者的数据来源于日本生物样本库（BioBank Japan），这是一个多机构、基于医院的注册系统，收集了2003年至2018年间来自日本各地的血液样本。这种分析涉及观察患有和未患特定疾病的人群，并识别可能导致不同临床结果的因素。在本研究中，他们重点关注了九种尚未进行BRCA基因关联测试的癌症，包括膀胱癌、骨癌、脑癌、头颈癌、肉瘤、皮肤癌、睾丸癌、甲状腺癌和输尿管癌。他们将这些患者的BRCA变异与来自同一数据库的38,842名非癌症个体的BRCA变异进行了比较。

分析结果显示，BRCA1的致病性变异增加了甲状腺癌的风险，而BRCA2的致病性变异则增加了膀胱癌、头颈癌和皮肤癌的风险。对于膀胱癌，他们发现BRCA2致病性变异对癌症风险的影响在女性中大于男性。

在医学研究领域，资金和人力等资源往往倾向于最常见和最致命的疾病。因此，患有相对不常见疾病的人在临床试验或新治疗方案方面往往运气不佳。正如该论文的第一作者笹川肇（Hajime Sasagawa）所说：“尽管之前的研究已将BRCA1/2致病性变异相关的癌症风险图谱扩展到卵巢癌、胰腺癌和前列腺癌，但我们认为相对不常见的癌症类型也将从基因证据的拓展中受益，特别是因为它们的治疗选择有限且预后不良。我很高兴我们能够获得这些新见解。”

“我很高兴我们能够获得这些新见解。尽管这项研究不会立即导致对这些癌症类型采取积极监测的建议，但我们希望这些发现能为这四种癌症的精准医疗指南的制定做出贡献。” 日本理化学研究所的桃泽行秀（Yukihide Momozawa）表示。

在此次研究之前，该团队曾在2022年发表于《美国医学会肿瘤学杂志》（JAMA Oncology）的一项研究中揭示了多项新的BRCA相关癌症，并在2023年发表于《新英格兰医学杂志》（New England Journal of Medicine）的研究中探讨了基因组与环境的相互作用。
