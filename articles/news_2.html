Immune changes could signal inflammatory bowel disease up to 10 years before diagnosis

深度解析

炎症性肠病（IBD）是一组以消化道慢性炎症为特征的自身免疫性疾病，主要包括克罗恩病（CD）和溃疡性结肠炎（UC）。本研究的核心突破在于揭示了IBD诊断前长达十年的“前驱期”内，患者血清中出现的独特抗体模式。这些抗体模式是对多种抗原（如微生物、病毒、食物和自身抗原）的免疫应答谱，反映了疾病早期免疫系统的异常活动。例如，CD患者表现出针对疱疹病毒和鞭毛蛋白抗体的升高，以及对荚膜细菌应答的下降；UC患者则显示出针对疱疹病毒和自身抗原的升高，同时对荚膜病原体反应性降低。这些特定抗体反应的失衡揭示了IBD发病机制中宿主-微生物相互作用和免疫耐受性丧失的关键环节。 这些早期免疫特征的识别具有深远的临床和行业影响。首先，通过机器学习整合这些复杂的抗体谱，能够以高准确率预测IBD的发生，甚至区分不同的疾病亚型（如复杂型CD），这为IBD的超早期诊断和风险分层提供了前所未有的窗口。这意味着在患者出现明显临床症状之前，医生有可能通过血液检测识别高风险个体，从而实现早期干预，潜在地阻止或延缓疾病进展，并避免肠道不可逆损伤。其次，早期识别复杂型疾病患者，使得医生能够更早地实施强化治疗策略，以期改善长期预后。对于制药行业而言，这些早期生物标志物有助于精准药物的开发，可能催生针对IBD前驱期的预防性疗法。然而，研究强调了在临床应用前，需要在独立、多样化人群中进行大规模验证，并证明其诊断价值和改善临床结局的有效性，这是未来转化的关键步骤。

Credit: Samir/Wikipedia A large-scale study, presented at UEG Week 2026, has identified distinct antibody patterns up to 10 years before the diagnosis of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), potentially opening new avenues for earlier risk detection and providing insights into future disease course. IBD is a group of chronic conditions, including Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC), in which abnormal immune responses contribute to ongoing inflammation and damage in the digestive system. Although IBD is increasingly recognized to develop over a prolonged period before diagnosis, relatively little is known about the immune changes that occur during this time. To investigate these changes, researchers analyzed 2,000 serum samples from 500 individuals, including 200 who developed CD, 200 who developed UC and 100 age- and sex-matched healthy controls. Samples were collected at diagnosis and approximately two, four and 10 years beforehand. The researchers profiled antibody responses against around 357,000 microbial-, viral-, food- and immune-associated antigens, allowing them to examine antibody patterns in the years leading up to diagnosis. Antibody changes emerge a decade early Across the 2,000 samples, researchers identified 123,980 unique antibodies. Compared with healthy controls, antibody diversity was significantly lower in people who later developed CD or UC, with distinct antibody responses evident in both conditions up to 10 years before diagnosis. In CD, elevated responses against herpesviruses and anti-flagellin antibodies were observed, particularly in people who later developed complicated or ileal disease, while responses to encapsulated bacteria progressively declined toward diagnosis. In UC, rising antibody responses against herpesviruses and autoantigens were accompanied by reduced reactivity to encapsulated pathogens. Models distinguish future disease and complications When these early antibody signatures were integrated using machine-learning models, they discriminated people who would later develop CD or UC from healthy controls up to 10 years before diagnosis, with area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.90 and 0.84, respectively. The profiles also differentiated people who later developed complicated from noncomplicated CD up to approximately 10 years before diagnosis, with an AUC of 0.85. Commenting on the findings, lead author Dr. Arno Bourgonje said, "It is fascinating to see that complicated CD at diagnosis is associated with a distinct antibody profile years before the first clinical symptoms and is not simply the consequence of disease progression after diagnosis. "Finding these patterns so early points to the potential of antibody profiling to support earlier risk detection, distinguish between disease subtypes and help identify patients who may benefit from more intensive therapy as early as possible." Validation needed before clinical screening The authors note that while the findings provide an important proof of principle, further research is needed before they can be translated into a clinical screening test. "The next priority is to validate these signatures in independent, more diverse and prospectively followed populations," concluded Bourgonje. "We also need to determine their added value alongside genetic, clinical and other blood-based markers, translate the findings into a reproducible and affordable targeted assay and, ultimately, demonstrate that using such a test actually leads to better clinical outcomes." More information Bourgonje, A.R. et al. Deciphering the prodrome of inflammatory bowel disease up to 10 years before disease onset by massively parallel serology.
