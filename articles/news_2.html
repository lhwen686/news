感觉神经信号被发现能阻断肺癌免疫疗法

Sensory nerve signals found to block lung cancer immunotherapy

Original Text
Researchers at the Francis Crick Institute have revealed that sensory nerve signals interfere with the immune system's response to lung cancer. This previously unrecognized neuroimmune connection could be targeted to improve responses to immunotherapy.

In the new study, published in Cell, scientists also found that exposure to cigarette smoke further exploits this neuroimmune interaction, accelerating tumor growth in mice.

The effectiveness of the immune response against cancer depends not just on immune cells being present, but how they are organised within the tumor microenvironment – the surrounding network of cells and signals that can either support or suppress immune activity.

Researchers have been investigating how different components of this environment shape immune responses, but the role of nerves has remained largely unexplored.

In this study, the research team focussed on the nervous system's early warning mechanism, specialised nerves that identify extreme thermal, physical, or chemical threats and trigger protective responses. By activating and deactivating these sensory nerves in mice, they demonstrated that their presence in the microenvironment was actively supporting tumor growth.

In turn, they also found that lung tumors increase the growth and activity of these nerves, triggering the release of a chemical messenger called calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP).

Using cell and mouse models, they showed how CGRP interacted with immune cells called macrophages in the tumor microenvironment. Through this interaction CGRP prevented the formation of clusters of immune cells called tertiary lymphoid structures, that are linked to better outcomes for people with lung cancer.

When the researchers disrupted local sensory nerve activity, or directly blocked CGRP signalling, the formation of these immune structures was increased. This was accompanied by stronger immune responses and reduced tumor growth.

Because drugs that target CGRP signalling, by inhibiting CGRP receptors, are already used clinically in other conditions, including for migraines, the researchers hope this could be a new approach for improving the effectiveness of cancer immunotherapy.

Finally, because smoking is the biggest risk factor for developing lung cancer, the team then decided to investigate whether cigarette smoke affects this neuroimmune pathway. They showed that cigarette smoke extract increases neuronal activity and accelerates tumor progression. This reveals a mechanism by which smoking can promote cancer progression beyond its role in causing DNA damage.

Leanne Li, Head of the Crick's Cancer-Neuroscience Laboratory, said: "We're starting to see the tumor microenvironment in all its complexity and what this means for disease progression. We've shown that nerves are not just present in cancers but actively shaping the tumor microenvironment and how cells talk to one another. This opens a new direction for cancer research, one that brings neuroscience and immunology together, and could reveal entirely new therapeutic opportunities."

Ya-Hsuan Ho, co-first author and postdoctoral fellow at the Crick, said: "It's fascinating to observe how sensitive the immune response is to changes in its local environment. We've shown that increased neuronal activity is enough to reorganise the immune landscape of the tumor and this changes how we think about immune evasion. It's not just about the presence or absence of immune cells, it's also how they respond to their surroundings."

Even with the amazing progress we've seen in people benefitting from immunotherapy, a large proportion of lung cancers don't respond. This unique insight into tumor biology could offer a completely new angle to improve those responses. And because drugs targeting elements of this pathway already exist, it's an especially promising route to explore further." Giacomo Bregni, co-first author and doctoral clinical fellow, Francis Crick Institute

This study will inform the future work of team InteroCANCEption, a Cancer Grand Challenges team led by Leanne Li, which was awarded up to £20 million, in March 2026 to take on the nervous system and cancer challenge. Cancer Grand Challenges is a global research initiative, co-founded by Cancer Research UK and the National Cancer Institute in the US, that brings together world-class interdisciplinary teams to tackle some of cancer's toughest challenges.

Dr David Scott, Director of Cancer Grand Challenges, said "Understanding how the brain might detect tumors and even influence how they grow is an emerging frontier in cancer research. This is why Cancer Grand Challenges is enabling team InteroCANCEption, led by Leanne Li, to explore this bi-directional tumor-nervous system connection and potentially develop innovative approaches that target the nervous system, expanding what is possible in cancer treatment."

Team InteroCANCEption is funded by Cancer Research UK and the National Cancer Institute through Cancer Grand Challenges.

弗朗西斯·克里克研究所的研究人员发现，感觉神经信号会干扰免疫系统对肺癌的反应。这种此前未被识别的神经免疫连接可能成为改善免疫疗法反应的靶点。

这项发表在《细胞》杂志上的新研究中，科学家们还发现，接触香烟烟雾会进一步利用这种神经免疫相互作用，加速小鼠体内的肿瘤生长。

免疫系统对抗癌症的有效性不仅取决于免疫细胞的存在，还取决于它们在肿瘤微环境中的组织方式——肿瘤微环境是周围的细胞和信号网络，它既能支持也能抑制免疫活动。

研究人员一直在调查环境的不同组成部分如何影响免疫反应，但神经的作用在很大程度上仍未被探索。

在这项研究中，研究团队专注于神经系统的早期预警机制，即识别极端热、物理或化学威胁并触发保护性反应的特化神经。通过激活和去激活小鼠体内的这些感觉神经，他们证明了这些神经在微环境中的存在主动支持了肿瘤生长。

反过来，他们也发现肺肿瘤会增加这些神经的生长和活性，从而触发一种名为降钙素基因相关肽（CGRP）的化学信使的释放。

利用细胞和小鼠模型，他们展示了CGRP如何在肿瘤微环境中与被称为巨噬细胞的免疫细胞相互作用。通过这种相互作用，CGRP阻止了被称为三级淋巴结构（tertiary lymphoid structures）的免疫细胞簇的形成，而三级淋巴结构与肺癌患者的更好预后相关。

当研究人员干扰局部感觉神经活动，或直接阻断CGRP信号传导时，这些免疫结构的形成增加了。这伴随着更强的免疫反应和肿瘤生长的减少。

由于靶向CGRP信号传导（通过抑制CGRP受体）的药物已在包括偏头痛在内的其他疾病中临床使用，研究人员希望这可能成为改善癌症免疫疗法有效性的一种新方法。

最后，由于吸烟是肺癌最大的风险因素，该团队随后决定调查香烟烟雾是否影响这种神经免疫通路。他们发现香烟烟雾提取物增加了神经元活动并加速了肿瘤进展。这揭示了吸烟除了引起DNA损伤外，还可以促进癌症进展的一种机制。

克里克研究所癌症神经科学实验室主任Leanne Li表示：“我们正开始全面了解肿瘤微环境的复杂性及其对疾病进展的意义。我们已经表明，神经不仅存在于癌症中，而且积极塑造肿瘤微环境以及细胞间如何相互交流。这为癌症研究开辟了一个新方向，它将神经科学和免疫学结合在一起，并可能揭示全新的治疗机会。”

克里克研究所的共同第一作者兼博士后研究员Ya-Hsuan Ho表示：“观察免疫反应对其局部环境变化的敏感程度令人着迷。我们已经证明，增加的神经元活动足以重塑肿瘤的免疫格局，这改变了我们对免疫逃逸的看法。这不仅仅是免疫细胞是否存在的问题，还在于它们如何响应周围环境。”

“尽管我们看到免疫疗法已取得惊人进展并使患者受益，但仍有很大一部分肺癌对治疗无反应。这种对肿瘤生物学的独特见解可能提供一个全新的角度来改善这些反应。而且由于靶向该通路元素的药物已经存在，这是一个特别有前景的深入探索途径。”弗朗西斯·克里克研究所共同第一作者兼临床博士研究员Giacomo Bregni说。

这项研究将为InteroCANCEption团队的未来工作提供信息，该癌症大挑战团队由Leanne Li领导，于2026年3月获得高达2000万英镑的资助，以应对神经系统与癌症的挑战。癌症大挑战是一项全球研究倡议，由英国癌症研究中心和美国国家癌症研究所共同创立，它汇集了世界一流的跨学科团队，以解决癌症中最艰难的一些挑战。

癌症大挑战主任David Scott博士表示：“了解大脑如何检测肿瘤甚至影响其生长是癌症研究的一个新兴前沿。这就是为什么癌症大挑战正在支持由Leanne Li领导的InteroCANCEption团队，探索这种肿瘤与神经系统的双向连接，并可能开发针对神经系统的创新方法，从而拓展癌症治疗的可能性。”

InteroCANCEption团队由英国癌症研究中心和美国国家癌症研究所通过癌症大挑战资助。
