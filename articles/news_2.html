深度解析

该文章深入探讨了英国国家医疗服务体系（NHS）乃至全球医疗系统面临的关键挑战——如何留住经验丰富的护士。研究运用大规模调查和离散选择实验（DCE）方法，揭示了影响护士留职的关键非经济因素，包括支持性的工作场所文化、富有同情心的领导力、灵活的工作安排以及通过安全人员配置和减少行政负担实现的可管理工作量。这标志着策略重心从单纯关注招聘和薪酬，转向全面理解工作环境对职业生涯可持续性的决定作用。文章阐明的核心专业概念是影响护士留职的社会组织机制，它直接关乎临床服务质量。经验丰富的护士凭借数十年的临床专长，对于维护安全、高质量的患者护理和指导初级员工至关重要。他们因职业倦怠或不满而过早离职，将导致宝贵机构知识的流失、现有员工压力增大，最终损害患者安全和医疗人力资源的长期可持续性。这项研究为政策制定者提供了强有力的证据，以投资于以人为本的工作场所改革，强调积极的工作环境不仅是员工福利问题，更是有效且有韧性的医疗服务提供的基本组成部分。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: RDNE Stock project from Pexels A new large-scale survey of NHS nurses by the University of Southampton and the University of Portsmouth has revealed what matters most to them. The study found that workplace culture and day-to-day working conditions were among the strongest factors influencing whether nurses in the later stages of their careers chose to stay in the NHS. The findings are published in the journal Health Policy. The findings suggest that improving the work environment should be a central part of efforts to keep experienced staff alongside wider workforce (and financial) measures. Retaining experienced nurses is becoming increasingly important as around half of NHS nurses are 45 or older, while those with more than 20 years of experience are among the most likely to leave or retire early. The research highlights that keeping these highly skilled professionals in the workforce is essential to maintaining safe patient care, preserving valuable expertise and supporting the next generation of nurses. Example of a DCE job choice for later-career NHS nurses. Credit: Health Policy (2026). DOI: 10.1016/j.healthpol.2026.105719 The study, involving almost 2,000 NHS nurses ages 45 to 54, is one of the largest of its kind to explore what later-career nurses value most in their jobs. Using a large-scale survey, researchers presented participants with different hypothetical job packages to understand which workplace characteristics mattered most when making career decisions. The results offer guidance for NHS employers looking to retain experienced staff. They recommend strengthening team collaboration, investing in compassionate leadership training, expanding flexible working opportunities that reflect the needs of an aging workforce and reducing workload pressures through safe staffing levels and fewer administrative demands. Turnbull said, "Experienced nurses are the backbone of safe patient care in the NHS and beyond and are essential for mentoring the next generation coming through." Lead author Dr. Zoé Ejebu, also from the University of Southampton, added, "Our findings show that if we want to prevent those with most experience from leaving the workforce early, we must invest in improving team culture, developing compassionate leadership and managing workloads to respect the immense value they bring to our health care services." Professor Shabbar Jaffry of the School of Accounting, Economics and Finance at the University of Portsmouth said, "Retaining experienced nurses is one of the biggest challenges facing global health care systems. NHS England is no exception. Our findings show that creating workplaces where nurses feel supported, respected and able to deliver the best possible care is a vital part of the solution. "This research provides evidence that investment in workplace culture, leadership, flexible working and manageable workloads should sit alongside recruitment, fair pay and broader workforce strategy. Keeping experienced nurses in the NHS benefits not only staff but also patients and the health service as a whole." Experienced nurses play a vital role within health care teams, bringing decades of clinical expertise while mentoring younger colleagues and supporting the delivery of safe, high-quality care. However, workforce shortages have increased pressure on remaining staff, creating a cycle of heavier workloads, burnout and further departures. The research underscores the importance of focusing on the everyday experience of work, alongside wider workforce policies, to help more experienced nurses remain in the profession, prevent early retirement and ensure the long-term sustainability of the NHS workforce.
