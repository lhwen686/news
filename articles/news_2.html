人工智能陪伴可能会重塑青少年学习冲突、界限和同理心的方式

AI companionship may reshape how teens learn conflict, boundaries and empathy

As teenagers increasingly turn to artificial intelligence chatbots for advice about friendships, family conflicts and romantic ties, researchers are raising concerns that the technology could disrupt how young people learn to navigate human relationships.

AI-powered conversational tools such as ChatGPT, Replika, Claude and Character.AI are becoming a common source of emotional support for teenagers. Writing in The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health, researchers from Arizona State University say the technology offers immediate, nonjudgmental guidance and has the potential to benefit emotional development. But without safeguards and careful design, the authors warn that reliance on these systems may bypass opportunities for young people to develop critical relationship skills through person-to-person interactions.

"The technologies are developing super-fast, faster than we can keep up with as scientists, faster than governance and policy can keep up with," said lead author Thao Ha, an associate professor in the Department of Psychology at ASU. Her co-authors are psychology doctoral student Jennifer Figueroa, postdoctoral research scholar Taren McGray, and youth advisory board members Jessica Ramirez and Susana Ortega, who are 16- and 17-year-old high school students in Tucson.

Students who met with the researchers described how they and their peers often use AI to seek advice about personal and often sensitive relationship challenges.

"I don't think I really expected so many teens to have the same concerns or thoughts when it came to AI," said Ortega, a high school senior. "We all mostly had concerns about how AI was replacing actual human connection and how it limits a lot of those needs that humans have that cannot be replaced with a computer artificial intelligence."

Adolescence is a crucial period for learning skills such as emotional regulation, conflict resolution, perspective-taking and boundary-setting, Ha said. Those competencies are typically developed through emotionally charged interactions with peers, romantic partners and family members, she said.

"People don't realize that relational learning happens during the teenage years and that these moments of social connection are little building blocks that become bigger things that will benefit you throughout life," Ha said. "You really need those building blocks, so you actually learn the skills that you need to thrive in your relationships."

Rampant use of AI

The researchers point to survey data showing that AI use among teens is widespread. A Pew Research Center study found that 64% of U.S. adolescents use interactive AI, while research from the Center for Democracy & Technology found that 42% have used AI chatbots for friendship-related purposes and 19% for romantic relationships.

Teenagers told the ASU researchers that current approaches to regulating AI, such as age verification, are ineffective and do not reflect their needs. Others described how AI use is becoming difficult to avoid, with one teen explaining that "there is almost no way not to use it anymore," limiting the ability to use it intentionally.

Ha and colleagues highlighted two significant risks:

The first, what they call "relational displacement," occurs when adolescents substitute AI interactions for conversations with other people. The authors argue that avoiding difficult discussions with friends, family members or romantic partners may limit opportunities to develop relationship skills that help protect against depression, anxiety and loneliness.

Youth participants cited examples ranging from seeking chatbot validation after arguments with partners to using AI for homework help instead of reaching out to classmates, potentially reducing everyday opportunities for social connection.

The second concern, termed "maladaptive relational learning," involves adolescents developing unrealistic expectations about human relationships. Because AI systems often provide immediate responses and consistent validation, it may reinforce youths' unhealthy, fixed ideas about relationships.

At the same time, young users may come to expect similar behavior from friends and romantic partners, the authors said. Over time, that could reinforce unhealthy relationship patterns and increase vulnerability to rejection, dating violence and mental health problems.

"With artificial intelligence, it's programmed to like you and it knows what to say to satisfy what you're feeding it," Ortega said. "If you're given full satisfaction on everything, you don't have learning experience with challenges or obstacles."

Gleaning benefits

To understand more fully how digital technologies are reshaping young minds, Ha is leading a major study recruiting 300 adolescents and their romantic partners to follow over 18 months to understand when, how and in which contexts digital interactions benefit or harm their relationships, mental health and academic achievement.

Shared data from teens' mobile devices will give the researchers real-time digital interactions to analyze and gain insights into the role of technology in teen relationships and mental health.

In the Lancet article, Ha and co-authors acknowledge that AI can provide meaningful benefits, particularly for adolescents who face barriers to traditional support systems. Teens who are rural, disabled, LGBTQIA+, or have limited access to counseling may find AI offers accessible information and guidance when other resources are unavailable.

"AI is cheaper than a therapist, it makes information more accessible and readily available for those who may not seek support," one teen told the ASU researchers.

When designed with developmental considerations, AI could scaffold self-reflection and redirect adolescents toward human engagement rather than substitution, the researchers said.

Rather than discouraging AI use altogether, the authors call for more research into how interactions with AI affect adolescent development over time. They also urge schools, communities and policymakers to invest in relationship education, counseling services and opportunities for young people to discuss relationships openly.

"Supporting adolescent mental health will require ensuring that AI systems are used in ways that support relational learning," the authors wrote, "while also protecting the real-world experiences through which young people learn to love and care for others."

随着青少年日益转向人工智能聊天机器人寻求关于友谊、家庭冲突和浪漫关系的建议，研究人员担忧这项技术可能会扰乱年轻人学习驾驭人际关系的方式。

人工智能驱动的对话工具，如ChatGPT、Replika、Claude和Character.AI，正成为青少年情感支持的常见来源。亚利桑那州立大学的研究人员在《柳叶刀·儿童与青少年健康》杂志上撰文指出，这项技术提供了即时、非评判性的指导，并有可能有益于情感发展。但作者们警告说，如果没有保障机制和审慎设计，对这些系统的依赖可能会让年轻人错失通过人际互动发展关键人际关系技能的机会。

“这些技术发展得超级快，快于我们科学家所能跟上的速度，也快于治理和政策所能跟上的速度，”亚利桑那州立大学心理学系副教授、主要作者Thao Ha说。她的合著者包括心理学博士生Jennifer Figueroa、博士后研究员Taren McGray，以及青年顾问委员会成员Jessica Ramirez和Susana Ortega，她们是图森市16岁和17岁的高中生。

与研究人员会面的学生们描述了他们和同伴如何经常使用人工智能来寻求关于个人且通常敏感的关系挑战的建议。

“我没想到有这么多青少年在谈到人工智能时有相同的担忧或想法，”高中生Ortega说。“我们大家主要都担心人工智能正在取代真正的人际连接，以及它如何限制了人类许多无法被计算机人工智能替代的需求。”

Ha表示，青春期是学习情绪调节、冲突解决、换位思考和边界设定等技能的关键时期。她说，这些能力通常通过与同伴、恋爱伴侣和家庭成员充满情感的互动来发展。

“人们没有意识到关系学习发生在青少年时期，这些社交连接的瞬间是小小的基石，它们会变成更大的、让你一生受益的事物，”Ha说。“你确实需要这些基石，这样你才能真正学到在人际关系中蓬勃发展所需的技能。”

人工智能的广泛使用

研究人员指出，调查数据显示人工智能在青少年中的使用非常普遍。皮尤研究中心的一项研究发现，64%的美国青少年使用交互式人工智能，而民主与技术中心的研究发现，42%的青少年曾使用人工智能聊天机器人处理与友谊相关的事情，19%用于恋爱关系。

青少年告诉亚利桑那州立大学的研究人员，当前监管人工智能的方法，例如年龄验证，是无效的，并且未能反映他们的需求。其他人则描述了人工智能的使用变得难以避免，一位青少年解释说，“现在几乎不可能不使用它了”，这限制了有意识地使用它的能力。

Ha及其同事强调了两个重大风险：

第一个，他们称之为“关系置换”，发生于青少年用人工智能互动替代与他人的对话时。作者们认为，避免与朋友、家人或恋爱伴侣进行艰难的讨论可能会限制发展人际关系技能的机会，而这些技能有助于预防抑郁、焦虑和孤独。

青少年参与者列举的例子包括在与伴侣争吵后寻求聊天机器人验证，以及使用人工智能寻求作业帮助而非向同学求助，这可能会减少日常社交连接的机会。

第二个担忧，被称为“适应不良的关系学习”，涉及青少年对人际关系形成不切实际的期望。由于人工智能系统通常提供即时回应和持续肯定，这可能会强化青少年对人际关系不健康、固化的观念。

作者们表示，同时，年轻用户可能会期望朋友和恋爱伴侣表现出类似的行为。久而久之，这可能会强化不健康的关系模式，并增加遭受拒绝、约会暴力和心理健康问题的脆弱性。

“人工智能被编程为喜欢你，它知道说什么能满足你所提供的信息，”Ortega说。“如果你在所有方面都得到充分满足，你就没有应对挑战或障碍的学习经验。”

获取益处

为了更全面地了解数字技术如何重塑年轻人的思想，Ha正在领导一项大型研究，招募300名青少年及其恋爱伴侣，追踪18个月，以了解数字互动在何时、如何以及何种情境下有益或有害于他们的关系、心理健康和学业成就。

来自青少年移动设备的共享数据将为研究人员提供实时数字互动，以供分析并深入了解技术在青少年关系和心理健康中的作用。

在《柳叶刀》文章中，Ha及其合著者承认人工智能可以提供有意义的益处，特别是对于那些面临传统支持系统障碍的青少年。农村地区、残障人士、LGBTQIA+群体，或咨询服务受限者，在其他资源不可用时，可能会发现人工智能提供了可获取的信息和指导。

“人工智能比治疗师便宜，它让信息更容易获取，随时可供那些可能不寻求支持的人使用，”一位青少年告诉亚利桑那州立大学的研究人员。

研究人员表示，当考虑到发展因素进行设计时，人工智能可以促进自我反思，并将青少年引导至人际互动而非替代人际互动。

作者们并未完全阻止人工智能的使用，而是呼吁进行更多研究，探讨人工智能互动如何随时间推移影响青少年发展。他们还敦促学校、社区和政策制定者投资于关系教育、咨询服务以及为年轻人提供公开讨论关系的机会。

作者写道：“支持青少年心理健康将需要确保人工智能系统以支持关系学习的方式使用，同时保护年轻人学习爱与关怀他人的真实世界经验。”
