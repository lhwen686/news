A ban won't stop abortion pill access, telehealth providers say

Two developments often get lost in the public's perception of the abortion wars. One is that the number of abortions in the U.S. has increased dramatically year over year since the U.S. Supreme Court overturned Roe v. Wade in 2022, according to the Society of Family Planning's latest #WeCount report. By December, 29% of abortions were through telehealth. The second is that, because the mailing of abortion pills has become so widespread in the post-Dobbs era, abortion opponents may simply be unable to stop it.

Massive legal battles are being waged by states with abortion bans, seeking to block the shipment of mifepristone across their borders.

In a suit brought by Louisiana, the U.S. Supreme Court recently restored telehealth access to mifepristone while a lower court hears the case. And abortion opponents continue their push to get the Trump administration to crack down on the pills' availability, either through a U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) safety review or more drastic measures.

But many telehealth experts who help patients get abortions aren't too worried.

"We like to say the genie is out of the bottle," said Elisa Wells, who co-founded and is the access director at Plan C, which provides information about accessing abortion pills online.

"Now that people know that they can get safe, fast, effective, affordable care through the mail, there's no going back," Wells said.

If mifepristone is restricted, many telehealth groups will immediately switch to using only misoprostol instead, they say.

Misoprostol is approved by the FDA to treat ulcers and is also widely used off-label to manage miscarriages, induce labor and end pregnancies. And while states could individually ban misoprostol, the FDA doesn't typically regulate how a drug is used off-label, according to David Cohen, a law professor at Drexel University and national expert on abortion law.

"There would have to be some finding that it is not safe or effective for ulcer treatment, something that there's no argument anyone could possibly show," Cohen said.

Some telehealth companies already used that alternative back in 2021, when the Supreme Court temporarily reinstated a requirement that mifepristone be dispensed in person. Carafem, a company that provides telehealth abortion, switched to using only misoprostol during that period "and has been prepared for this possibility ever since," said Melissa Grant, a co-founder of the company and its chief operating officer.

Other organizations are already directing patients to mifepristone from outside the U.S., through groups such as Aid Access and online pharmacies in places like India.

Even if the Trump administration tried to enforce the Comstock Act, an 1873 law that bans the mailing of obscene matter and anything intended to produce an abortion, it would be extremely difficult to stop the flow of pills, Cohen said.

"We've had the 'War on Drugs' for what, half a century, maybe longer?" he said. "And everyone, if they wanted, could find illegal drugs within minutes, and have it probably delivered to their doorstep within hours."

Abortion pills were called "the single greatest threat to unborn children in a post-Roe world" in the Project 2025 blueprint for the second Trump administration produced by the conservative Heritage Foundation, and abortion opponents are increasingly vocal about their frustration with the Trump administration over them.

"People's patience is at an end on this point," said Kristi Hamrick, vice president of media and policy for Students for Life of America. The group recently met with the U.S. Department of Justice to discuss priorities of the anti-abortion movement, such as enforcement of the Comstock Act.

The ability to use telehealth to circumvent state abortion bans is a key target of Louisiana's lawsuit, which a federal appeals court is currently weighing. The suit, which has the backing of 21 other states, argues that the FDA under the Biden administration acted illegally when it allowed mifepristone to be prescribed without an in-person visit to a doctor. Without that change, "activists in New York and California could not blanket pro-life states like Louisiana with mifepristone by mail," the suit argues.

But because medication abortion is now the most common form of abortion in the U.S., restricting mifepristone would have a wide impact even in states without abortion bans.

"Stopping the FDA's unlawful mail-order mifepristone scheme will surely decrease the number of chemical abortions across the country," said Gabriella McIntyre, a lawyer for Alliance Defending Freedom, which partnered with Louisiana in its suit.

And there are signs the administration may be feeling the pressure, even if it's tempered by worries that further abortion restrictions could hurt Republicans in this fall's midterm elections.

For one, it looks like the FDA's safety evaluation of mifepristone—which abortion opponents have long pushed for—may be moving forward, potentially allowing the administration to restrict the drug's use. It's too soon to tell, said Hamrick, who refers to the long-promised evaluation as a "unicorn."

"It feels mythical," she said. "I look forward to seeing it. But it's taking too long."

And if mifepristone is restricted and providers switch to misoprostol-only regimens, abortion opponents say they're ready to highlight the risks.

"We call it the new coat hanger," Hamrick said. Providers will prescribe it, she said, "knowing it fails more often, knowing that the complications are worse, because they're so committed to online distribution of pills for the purpose of abortion."

Telehealth providers agree that patients need to be informed about what to expect with any medication abortion.

在公众对堕胎争议的认知中，两项进展常被忽视。其一，根据家庭计划学会最新发布的《#WeCount》报告，自美国最高法院于2022年推翻“罗诉韦德案”裁决以来，美国堕胎数量逐年显著增加。截至去年12月，29%的堕胎通过远程医疗进行。其二，由于在多布斯案裁决后的时代，堕胎药的邮寄已变得如此普遍，堕胎反对者可能根本无法阻止它。

实行堕胎禁令的州正在发起大规模法律战，试图阻止米非司酮跨越其州界运输。

在路易斯安那州提起的一项诉讼中，美国最高法院最近恢复了通过远程医疗获取米非司酮的途径，同时等待下级法院审理此案。堕胎反对者继续推动特朗普政府严厉打击堕胎药的可及性，无论是通过美国食品药品监督管理局（FDA）的安全审查，还是更激烈的措施。

但许多帮助患者获得堕胎服务的远程医疗专家并不太担心。

“我们喜欢说，潘多拉的盒子已经打开了，”Plan C的联合创始人兼可及性总监伊莉莎·威尔斯说，该组织提供在线获取堕胎药的信息。

威尔斯说：“既然人们知道可以通过邮寄获得安全、快速、有效且负担得起的医疗服务，就没有回头路了。”

他们表示，如果米非司酮受到限制，许多远程医疗团体将立即转而仅使用米索前列醇。

米索前列醇已获得FDA批准用于治疗溃疡，并被广泛用于超说明书使用，以处理流产、引产和终止妊娠。德雷塞尔大学法学教授、堕胎法国家专家大卫·科恩表示，虽然各州可以单独禁止米索前列醇，但FDA通常不监管药物的超说明书使用方式。

科恩说：“必须有某种发现证明它对溃疡治疗不安全或无效，而这是任何人都无法提供的论据。”

早在2021年，当最高法院暂时恢复米非司酮必须当面配发的规定时，一些远程医疗公司就已采用这种替代方案。提供远程医疗堕胎服务的Carafem公司在那段时期改用仅米索前列醇方案，“并且从那时起就一直为此做好准备，”该公司联合创始人兼首席运营官梅丽莎·格兰特说。

其他组织则已经指导患者通过Aid Access等团体和印度等地的在线药房，从美国境外获取米非司酮。

科恩说，即使特朗普政府试图执行《康斯托克法案》——一项1873年禁止邮寄淫秽物品和任何旨在导致堕胎物品的法律，也极难阻止药物的流通。

他说：“我们打了‘禁毒战争’多久了？半个世纪，或许更久？”“如果每个人都想，他们可以在几分钟内找到非法药物，并可能在几小时内送到家门口。”

在保守派传统基金会为第二届特朗普政府制作的《2025计划蓝图》中，堕胎药被称为“罗诉韦德案后世界中对未出生儿童的最大威胁”，堕胎反对者对特朗普政府在这一问题上的不作为越来越不满。

“人们在这方面的耐心已经耗尽，”美国生命学生组织媒体和政策副总裁克里斯蒂·哈姆里克说。该组织最近会见了美国司法部，讨论反堕胎运动的优先事项，例如《康斯托克法案》的执行。

利用远程医疗规避州级堕胎禁令是路易斯安那州诉讼案（由其他21个州支持）的关键目标，目前联邦上诉法院正在审理此案。该诉讼辩称，拜登政府下的FDA在允许米非司酮无需医生当面问诊即可开具处方时，行为违法。该诉讼认为，如果没有这项改变，“纽约和加州的积极分子就无法通过邮寄方式，将米非司酮大规模送到路易斯安那等支持生命权的州。”

但由于药物流产现在是美国最常见的堕胎形式，限制米非司酮将产生广泛影响，即使是在没有堕胎禁令的州也是如此。

“阻止FDA非法的邮购米非司酮计划，必将减少全国范围内的药物流产数量，”与路易斯安那州合作提起诉讼的“捍卫自由联盟”律师加布里埃拉·麦金太尔说。

有迹象表明，政府可能正感受到压力，尽管这种压力因担心进一步限制堕胎可能会损害共和党在今年秋季中期选举中的表现而有所缓和。

其一，FDA对米非司酮的安全评估——堕胎反对者长期以来一直推动的工作——似乎正在推进，这可能允许政府限制该药物的使用。哈姆里克称这项长期承诺的评估为“独角兽”，她说现在判断还为时过早。

“感觉像是神话，”她说。“我期待着看到它。但这需要太长时间了。”

如果米非司酮受到限制，并且提供者转向仅米索前列醇的方案，堕胎反对者表示他们已准备好强调其中的风险。

“我们称之为新时代的衣架，”哈姆里克说。她表示，提供者将开具处方，“明知其失败率更高，明知并发症更严重，因为他们如此致力于在线分发堕胎药。”

远程医疗提供者同意，患者需要被告知任何药物流产的预期情况。
