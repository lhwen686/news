深度解析

该研究揭示了氯胺酮在剖宫产后早期产后抑郁症状缓解方面的潜在应用，具有重要的临床意义。核心专业概念在于氯胺酮的双重机制：它既是广谱麻醉剂和镇痛剂，又具有快速抗抑郁作用。作为一种N-甲基-D-天冬氨酸（NMDA）受体拮抗剂，氯胺酮能够迅速调节神经回路，促进突触可塑性，从而快速改善情绪障碍，这与传统抗抑郁药物数周才能起效的机制截然不同。 从行业影响来看，若进一步研究证实其疗效和安全性，氯胺酮有望成为剖宫产术后预防或早期干预产后抑郁症的一种创新策略。这不仅能为高危产妇提供更及时的支持，改善母婴健康结局，也可能改变现有产后心理健康管理模式。制药行业可能因此加大对快速起效抗抑郁药物的研发投入。然而，当前研究仍属早期阶段，尚需大规模前瞻性随机对照试验以明确最佳剂量、给药时机、长期疗效及潜在副作用，确保其临床应用的严谨性和安全性。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain Ketamine, a medication used in operating rooms for anesthesia and pain control, may offer another benefit for some new mothers: easing early symptoms of postpartum depression after cesarean delivery (C-section), according to a study presented at the ANESTHESIOLOGY 2026 annual meeting. A new meta-analysis found that administering ketamine or esketamine (a ketamine derivative) during or immediately after a C-section was associated with a statistically significant reduction in depressive symptoms within the first two weeks after birth, a vulnerable period for women as they recover from surgery and care for their infants. "Ketamine is known for its rapid antidepressant effects in psychiatric settings, and our findings suggest it may offer benefits beyond anesthesia and pain relief for women having a C-section," said Peter Hsin, B.S., lead author of the study and a medical student at FIU Herbert Wertheim College of Medicine, Miami. "This is promising, but it is too early to say that ketamine prevents clinically diagnosed postpartum depression or that it should be used routinely." Postpartum depression affects about one in five women after childbirth and can have serious consequences for mothers, infants and families. C-sections have been associated with a higher risk of postpartum depressive symptoms, such as sadness, anxiety, irritability, loss of interest and difficulty bonding with the baby, prompting researchers to explore whether medications used during surgery could help reduce symptom burden in the postpartum period. The study authors reviewed six randomized controlled trials published from January 2015 through September 2025, involving 1,832 women undergoing elective C-sections. Patients received ketamine or esketamine during or after surgery and were compared to those who didn't receive either medication. The analysis used scores from the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale, a widely used screening questionnaire that helps identify postpartum depressive symptoms, measured between postoperative days three and 14. Women who received ketamine or esketamine had a small-to-moderate reduction in depressive symptom scores compared to those who did not (standardized mean difference of -0.36), which was statistically significant. "While our findings suggest ketamine may modestly ease early depressive symptoms, larger studies with longer follow-up are needed to determine the best dose and timing, and whether ketamine can help prevent postpartum depression," said Hsin. "We plan to conduct a prospective, randomized study in the Department of Anesthesiology at the University at Buffalo to further evaluate the potential role of ketamine administered during cesarean delivery in reducing the incidence and severity of postpartum depression," said study co-author Rose Berkun, M.D., FASA, associate professor of anesthesiology, Jacobs School of Medicine and Biomedical Sciences, University at Buffalo, New York.
