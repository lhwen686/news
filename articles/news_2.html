Flu vaccine effectiveness often weaker than advertised, study finds

深度解析

本文揭示了流感疫苗效力（VE）在不同流感季节的显著变异性及其背后复杂的医学机制。流感病毒以其高度的抗原漂移特性而闻名，每年都会产生新的循环毒株。当前流感疫苗的制备是基于对当年流行毒株的预测，若实际流行的毒株与疫苗预测株不匹配，即发生疫苗错配，疫苗的保护效力便会下降。本研究通过回顾性分析，指出流感疫苗的中位效力低于普遍宣称的 40%-60% 区间，且在多数季节存在疫苗与流行毒株不匹配的情况。 尽管流感疫苗效力存在变数，但文章强调其在公共卫生中的关键作用。即使是中等效力，疫苗也能显著降低流感相关的住院率和死亡率，体现了疫苗在群体层面预防重症和挽救生命的巨大价值。这促使行业加速研发“广谱流感疫苗”或“通用流感疫苗”，旨在通过靶向病毒中更保守的抗原表位，提供更稳定、持久且不受年度毒株变异影响的保护。此类疫苗的成功将彻底改变流感预防策略，提高疫苗的可预测性和公共信任度，从而对全球公共卫生安全产生深远影响。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: https://kaboompics.com/ from Pexels New research suggests health officials may be overselling the protective power of the annual flu shot—but that doesn't mean folks should skip it, researchers added. The new analysis finds the influenza vaccine is often less protective than the 40% to 60% effectiveness against infection that's routinely stated, according to researchers from the University of Maryland. In many seasons, it may not even match current circulating strains, they noted. "The 40% to 60% range may describe some seasons, but it doesn't adequately reflect the variability we see over time," said study senior author Dr. Justin Ortiz, a researcher at the university's Center for Vaccine Development and Global Health. The trick, he said, is "to communicate that uncertainty without losing sight of the substantial benefits of vaccination." The findings were recently published in JAMA Network Open. In the study, Ortiz's team looked at end-of-season estimates of flu vaccine effectiveness for flu seasons from 2004 to 2005 through 2023 to 2024. They also looked at whether a particular season's vaccine was well matched to the flu strains that actually appeared. The team found that influenza vaccines had a median effectiveness of 39.5% in preventing flu infection. That's below the 40% to 60% effectiveness range typically cited by public health officials and media, researchers noted. (Median means some years' protection was greater and some years' less.) They noted that higher estimates of effectiveness are often predicated on the notion that the flu vaccine is a good match for circulating strains of the virus. However, the study showed it often wasn't the case: In 11 of the 18 tracked seasons, vaccines did not match circulating strains. None of this means flu shots are useless, and Ortiz's team stressed that shots are still the most effective means Americans have of preventing influenza, which kills between 6,300 and 52,000 people each year, according to the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). And this year, flu is spreading early, accounting for 2,100 deaths so far this season. The CDC estimates that, during the 2023 to 2024 flu season, vaccination saved 7,900 lives. It also prevented 120,000 flu-related hospitalizations and 9.8 million flu-related illnesses. It's hoped the new data will help spur research into improving influenza vaccines so they offer broader protection against the ever-mutating virus. "The message is clear that annual flu shots remain our best option for now as we continue to develop vaccines that provide more predictable and lasting protection," said study lead author Dr. Matthew Wolpert, an internal medicine resident at the University of Maryland Medical Center. Publication details Matthew D. Wolpert et al., Testing the 40% to 60% Influenza Vaccine Effectiveness Heuristic, JAMA Network Open (2026).
