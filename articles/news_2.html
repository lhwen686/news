这项研究提供了有力证据，反驳了流产（无论是药物流产还是手术流产）会增加心理健康问题的普遍误解。其核心专业概念在于通过严谨的流行病学方法和客观的临床指标，而非主观报告，评估流产对心理健康的影响。研究团队利用丹麦国家健康登记系统的大规模纵向数据，追踪了首次流产后精神健康处方（如抗抑郁药、抗焦虑药）的首次开具情况，以及精神科诊断的发生率。这种基于真实世界数据的回顾性队列研究设计，结合了多重统计调整，显著增强了结果的可靠性与临床可信度。 其行业影响深远。首先，研究结果为临床医生在提供流产咨询时提供了重要的循证依据，有助于消除患者的顾虑，并反驳公众中关于流产导致心理健康损害的错误观念。其次，它支持了公共卫生政策的制定，强调了将流产纳入常规医疗保健服务的重要性，而非将其视为一种独特的、高风险的心理健康事件。尽管研究指出丹麦的文化和医疗体系与美国等国存在差异，可能影响结果的泛化，但其核心发现——即流产方式本身不增加精神健康药物使用或精神障碍诊断的风险——仍是全球范围内关于生殖健康政策和临床实践的重要参考。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain While more women globally are using medication to have abortions, a University of Maryland-led study published today in JAMA Psychiatry found no evidence that a first medication or procedural abortion caused an increase in mental health medication use. Using records from the Danish health registry, researchers looked to see whether a person's first medication or procedural abortion was related to filling a mental health prescription for the first time as a way to measure whether abortion method affects mental health. The study group had never previously filled a mental health prescription. The study tracked prescriptions filled for a variety of psychiatric conditions, such as depression and anxiety, following a person's first abortion. From one year after an abortion and beyond, the researchers found no statistically significant increase in mental health medication use when adjusting for multiple tests. More than five years afterward, filling such prescriptions decreased when adjusting for multiple tests. "Counter to a common perception around abortion, the results showed neither medication nor procedural abortion caused the use of psychotropic medications, which suggests that neither method increases risk of mental health issues," said Dr. Julia Steinberg, lead author of the study and an associate professor in the Department of Family Science at UMD's School of Public Health. Steinberg and her Danish colleagues analyzed thousands of Danish health records of women and girls ages 12 to 38 who had an abortion by medication or procedure in the first trimester of pregnancy between 2000 and 2018. Denmark has provided abortions through its nationalized health care system since legalization in 1973. Researchers compared prescriptions from one year before a person's abortion with prescriptions claimed in the first year afterward, one to two years afterward, two to five years afterward and more than five years afterward. The study looked at all mental health medications treating a wide variety of conditions, including sleep disorders and ADHD, as well as medications that specifically target anxiety and depression. "This research helps us better understand the association between abortion method and mental health. We did not investigate social factors like cultural stigmas around abortion, which could impact the mental health of a person after having an abortion and are likely to vary by place and culture," Steinberg said. Within the year after a first abortion, the data showed a slight increase in psychiatric prescription use. However, after applying a common statistical adjustment necessary when analyzing a high number of variables and comparisons, researchers found the risks in that first year were no longer statistically significant. The study used data from the Danish population, where more than 94% of people are white and where the national culture, legal framework and access to abortion and health care differ from those in the United States. "While the study focused on Danish data, the results are an important rebuttal to claims that abortion—particularly medication abortion—harms mental health," Steinberg said. Steinberg co-led a 2024 study that examined whether mental health disorders, measured by psychiatric diagnoses, increased after a medication or procedural abortion and found that neither abortion method increased the risk of psychiatric diagnoses in the short or long term. Publication details Julia R. Steinberg et al, Medication and Procedural Abortion and Risk of Psychotropic Medication Use, JAMA Psychiatry (2026). DOI: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2025.3698 Journal information: JAMA Psychiatry
