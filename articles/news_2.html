双PET成像检测癌症治疗期间的肿瘤进展和心肌炎症

Dual PET imaging detects tumor progression and heart inflammation during cancer treatment

Representative PET images of cardio-oncology mice treated with anti-PD1 alone or in combination with JAK1i. The PET uptake was normalized by subtracting blood retention. Credit: SNMMI.

A novel PET imaging approach enables simultaneous visualization of tumor progression and cardiac inflammatory responses during cancer treatment. Using this strategy, researchers demonstrated the effectiveness of a new combination therapy that enhances tumor response while reducing cardiac inflammation.

This new paradigm, presented at the Society of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging 2026 Annual Meeting, offers a more comprehensive assessment of therapeutic impact, supporting more personalized clinical decision-making.

Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-based therapies have revolutionized cancer treatment and substantially improved patients' survival. These treatments, however, can also lead to serious heart and vascular adverse effects in a subset of patients. Management of these cardiac complications may require interruption or discontinuation of ICI therapy, potentially resulting in cancer progression.

"Currently, there is no established molecular imaging approach to identify the development of these cardiovascular immune-related adverse events in patients receiving ICI treatments," said Jaume Otaegui, Ph.D., postdoctoral researcher at Washington University in St. Louis.

"The CCR2 protein is expressed in both atherosclerotic plaques (which are driven by inflammation) and tumors, making it a promising biomarker for monitoring both. In this study, we evaluated a PET imaging approach to noninvasively detect immune-related adverse events in a cardio-oncology model using a CCR2-targeted radiotracer, as well as a therapeutic strategy to reduce cardiac risk during treatment."

Two cohorts of atherosclerotic mice were implanted with mouse oral carcinoma cells as a cardio-oncology model. The first group received ICI treatment or a control treatment twice a week. PET imaging using the CCR2-targeted radiotracer 64Cu-DOTA-ECL1i was performed after five and 10 doses; 18F-FDG PET/CT was also performed for comparison.

The second group of mice received ICI treatment for three weeks, and during the last two weeks of treatment, itacitinib (a Janus kinase 1 inhibitor) was given twice a day. This cohort of mice underwent CCR2 PET imaging at the end of the treatment.

64Cu-DOTA-ECL1i was found to effectively bind to CCR2 cells, allowing for better PET/CT imaging of plaque and tumors compared to 18F-FDG. ICI treatment slowed down tumor progression but did not affect CCR2 PET tumor signal; CCR2 cardiac signal, however, was significantly increased. Co-treatment with itacitinib showed better tumor response, reduced CCR2 PET signal, and fewer inflammatory cells in the aorta compared to ICI treatment alone.

"CCR2 PET imaging could potentially allow clinicians to detect harmful cardiovascular changes that occur during cancer treatment before they result in heart attack, myocarditis, or other serious complications," said Otaegui. "Additionally, our therapeutic approach of combining itacitinib ICI treatment could be used to both reduce cardiovascular inflammation and enhance tumor control."

He continued, "Overall, for patients, this work points toward more personalized and safer immunotherapy—where we can image, anticipate, and possibly mitigate cardiovascular complications without compromising cancer treatment."

64Cu-DOTA-ECL1i is currently being evaluated in clinical trials for imaging across multiple disease indications, and ICI therapy has received U.S. Food and Drug Administration approval for several stages of head and neck cancer.

Consequently, the imaging component of this work may be implemented in the near-term. Further studies on the combination therapy, however, are still needed to provide broader validation of the study's findings.

一种新型PET成像方法能够在癌症治疗期间同时可视化肿瘤进展和心脏炎症反应。利用这一策略，研究人员证实了一种新型联合疗法的有效性，该疗法在增强肿瘤反应的同时减少了心脏炎症。

这一在2026年核医学与分子影像学会年会上提出的新范式，提供了对治疗效果更全面的评估，支持更个性化的临床决策。

基于免疫检查点抑制剂（ICI）的疗法彻底改变了癌症治疗，并显著提高了患者的生存率。然而，这些治疗也可能导致部分患者出现严重的心脏和血管不良事件。这些心脏并发症的治疗可能需要中断或停止ICI疗法，从而可能导致癌症进展。

“目前，还没有成熟的分子成像方法来识别接受ICI治疗的患者中这些心血管免疫相关不良事件的发生，”圣路易斯华盛顿大学的博士后研究员Jaume Otaegui博士说。

“CCR2蛋白在动脉粥样硬化斑块（由炎症驱动）和肿瘤中均有表达，使其成为监测这两者的有前景的生物标志物。在这项研究中，我们评估了一种PET成像方法，通过使用CCR2靶向放射性示踪剂，在心脏肿瘤学模型中无创检测免疫相关不良事件，以及一种在治疗期间降低心脏风险的治疗策略。”

两组动脉粥样硬化小鼠被植入小鼠口腔癌细胞，作为心脏肿瘤学模型。第一组每周两次接受ICI治疗或对照治疗。在施用五到十剂后，使用CCR2靶向放射性示踪剂64Cu-DOTA-ECL1i进行PET成像；同时还进行了18F-FDG PET/CT以进行比较。

第二组小鼠接受了三周的ICI治疗，在治疗的最后两周，每天两次给予伊他西替尼（一种Janus激酶1抑制剂）。这组小鼠在治疗结束时进行了CCR2 PET成像。

研究发现，64Cu-DOTA-ECL1i能有效结合CCR2细胞，与18F-FDG相比，可以更好地进行斑块和肿瘤的PET/CT成像。ICI治疗减缓了肿瘤进展，但未影响CCR2 PET肿瘤信号；然而，CCR2心脏信号显著增加。与单独使用ICI治疗相比，伊他西替尼联合治疗显示出更好的肿瘤反应，降低了CCR2 PET信号，并减少了主动脉中的炎症细胞。

“CCR2 PET成像可能使临床医生能够在心脏病发作、心肌炎或其他严重并发症发生之前，检测到癌症治疗期间出现有害的心血管变化，”Otaegui说。“此外，我们将伊他西替尼与ICI治疗相结合的治疗方法，可以同时减少心血管炎症并增强肿瘤控制。”

他继续说道：“总的来说，对于患者而言，这项工作指明了更个性化和更安全的免疫疗法——我们可以在不影响癌症治疗的情况下，通过成像预判并可能减轻心血管并发症。”

64Cu-DOTA-ECL1i目前正在多项疾病适应症的临床试验中进行成像评估，ICI疗法已获得美国食品药品监督管理局批准用于头颈癌的多个阶段。

因此，这项工作的成像部分可能在近期实施。然而，关于联合疗法仍需进一步研究，以提供对研究结果更广泛的验证。
