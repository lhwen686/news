综述评估了肌酸作为抑郁症辅助治疗的疗效

Review assesses the efficacy of creatine as an add-on therapy for depression

本系统综述对肌酸作为抑郁症辅助治疗的潜力进行了批判性评估，其核心专业概念聚焦于细胞生物能量学。肌酸通过肌酸激酶系统，促进三磷酸腺苷（ATP）从二磷酸腺苷（ADP）快速再生，这对大脑等高能量需求组织至关重要。研究已发现脑部肌酸代谢异常和线粒体功能障碍可能是重度抑郁症的潜在病理生理基础。除能量代谢外，肌酸还可能调节多巴胺和血清素等神经递质系统，这些是传统抗抑郁药的关键靶点。

随机对照试验的结果呈现出喜忧参半的局面：部分研究，尤其针对重度抑郁症女性，显示肌酸与依他普仑或认知行为疗法联用时，能显著减轻症状并提高缓解率，且效应量可观（如汉密尔顿抑郁量表上的科恩d值为1.13）。然而，其他针对难治性抑郁症或双相情感障碍抑郁发作期的试验未发现益处，更值得注意的是，部分双相患者出现了轻躁狂/躁狂症状，凸显了诊断特异性作用和患者安全的重要性。临床上，尽管肌酸的安全性概况总体良好（轻微胃肠道不适），但由于方法学异质性、小样本量和结果不一致，目前的证据尚不足以改变标准临床实践。本综述呼吁开展更大规模、更严谨、更长期的随机对照试验，并可能探索性别特异性反应和联合疗法，以最终确定肌酸在精神科护理中的治疗价值。
Original Text
Walk into any gym supplement aisle and creatine is there, sold by the tub to people chasing bigger muscles. It is one of the most studied compounds in sports science. What far fewer people know is that the brain runs on much the same chemistry the muscles do, and that the energy creatine helps supply may matter just as much above the neck as below it. A new systematic review, published today in Brain Medicine, takes that quiet possibility seriously and asks a hard question. Can creatine help treat depression?

What the researchers looked at

The team behind the review, led by Bassam Jeryous Fares of the University of Ottawa, did not run a new trial. They gathered the ones that already existed. After screening the literature, they settled on six published reports describing five randomized controlled trials, the kind of study in which neither patient nor doctor knows who received the real compound and who received a placebo.

Those trials had been conducted across five countries, in South Korea, the United States, Brazil, Israel, and India, and together they enrolled 238 participants at baseline, 126 on creatine and 112 on placebo. The average age was 36 years. Most participants were women. Two of the trials enrolled women only.

Four of the trials studied major depressive disorder. One studied people with bipolar disorder who were living through a depressive episode. Because the studies differed so widely in design, the authors did not pool the numbers into a single statistic. They summarized them in narrative instead, letting each trial speak for itself.

A split decision

Here is where the story refuses to resolve cleanly. Two of the five trials, both drawn from the same study of women with major depressive disorder, found real benefit. When five grams of creatine per day was added to the antidepressant escitalopram, depressive symptoms fell further than they did on placebo after eight weeks. The effect was large by the usual statistical yardsticks, with a Cohen's d of 1.13 on the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale, and more women reached remission.

A separate trial paired creatine with cognitive behavioral therapy and saw a steeper drop in symptoms on a standard questionnaire than therapy plus placebo produced.

Then the picture darkens. The remaining three trials found nothing. One saw no effect of creatine, at five or ten grams daily, in people who had already failed to respond to medication. Another, testing several doses in adolescent girls, found no difference from placebo.

The last looked at people with bipolar disorder in a depressive episode and again found no treatment benefit. Worse, two of those bipolar patients taking creatine developed hypomania or mania, a sober reminder that the same compound can behave very differently depending on the diagnosis.

Why creatine might matter for the brain

The logic behind the experiments is not far fetched. The brain is an expensive organ, burning energy at a rate out of all proportion to its size, and creatine helps cells rebuild adenosine triphosphate, the molecule that pays for that work. Studies of people with mood disorders have found altered creatine metabolism in the brain, and impaired energy production has been proposed as one root of depression.

Creatine may also nudge the pathways that govern dopamine and serotonin, the neurotransmitters that most antidepressants target. The authors are careful here. They note that the link between brain creatine and mood remains correlational, not proven cause and effect, and that the biology of depression has many moving parts.

"The signal is interesting, but it is not a verdict," said Bassam Jeryous Fares, first author of the review and a student in the Faculty of Medicine at the University of Ottawa.

Two trials pointed one way and three pointed another. That is not the kind of evidence on which you change clinical practice. It is the kind that tells you the question is worth further exploration." Bassam Jeryous Fares, Faculty of Medicine, University of Ottawa

Nicholas Fabiano, corresponding author and a psychiatry resident at the University of Ottawa, framed the work as a starting point rather than a conclusion. "Creatine appears to be a safe intervention. The adverse events we found were limited to mild gastrointestinal discomfort. We cannot yet reliably say that creatine helps with depressive symptoms or if the findings are generalizable to everyone."

What comes next

The review does not pretend to settle the matter. The authors flag the obvious limitations. The trials were small. The sexes were unbalanced, with two studies enrolling only women. The quality varied, with two trials judged at low risk of bias and three carrying some concern, arising mostly from how patients were assigned and how missing data were handled. The findings, the authors stress, are not yet generalizable.

What they call for is more rigorous work. Larger trials. Longer ones, running past the eight week mark. Studies that test creatine alongside exercise, and studies that explore higher doses, while keeping in mind that more is not always better.

There is even a tantalizing clue from animal research, where creatine altered depression like behavior differently in male and female rodents, which may help explain why the human trials with more women showed more promise. For now, creatine remains a promising lead rather than a proven remedy. The molecule that builds muscle has earned a closer look from the people who study the mind.

走进任何一家健身补剂货架，肌酸随处可见，成桶出售给那些追求更大肌肉的人。它是运动科学中研究最多的化合物之一。然而，鲜为人知的是，大脑的化学运作方式与肌肉大致相同，肌酸所帮助供应的能量，对大脑（颈部以上）的重要性可能不亚于对身体（颈部以下）的重要性。今天发表在《脑医学》杂志上的一项新的系统综述，认真探讨了这种潜在的可能性，并提出了一个棘手的问题：肌酸能帮助治疗抑郁症吗？

研究人员关注的方面

由渥太华大学的Bassam Jeryous Fares领导的综述团队并未开展新的试验，而是收集了已有的试验。在筛选文献后，他们最终确定了六份已发表的报告，这些报告描述了五项随机对照试验，这种研究中，患者和医生都不知道谁接受了真正的化合物，谁接受了安慰剂。

这些试验在五个国家进行，包括韩国、美国、巴西、以色列和印度。基线时共招募了238名参与者，其中126人接受肌酸，112人接受安慰剂。平均年龄为36岁。大多数参与者是女性。其中两项试验仅招募了女性。

四项试验研究了重度抑郁症。一项研究了处于抑郁发作期的双相情感障碍患者。由于研究设计差异巨大，作者没有将数据合并成单一统计结果，而是采用叙述方式进行总结，让每项试验独立阐述其结果。

结果不一

故事在这里未能得出明确结论。五项试验中有两项（均来自同一项针对重度抑郁症女性的研究）发现肌酸确实有益。当每日五克肌酸被添加到抗抑郁药依他普仑中时，八周后抑郁症状的下降幅度大于安慰剂组。按照常规统计标准，其效应显著，汉密尔顿抑郁量表（Hamilton Depression Rating Scale）上的科恩d值为1.13，并且有更多女性达到缓解。

另一项试验将肌酸与认知行为疗法相结合，结果显示，与单独采用疗法加安慰剂相比，症状在标准化问卷上的下降幅度更大。

然而，情况变得不那么乐观。其余三项试验未发现任何效果。其中一项在对药物已无反应的患者中，每日五克或十克肌酸均未显示出效果。另一项在青春期女孩中测试不同剂量，也未发现与安慰剂有何差异。

最后一项研究着眼于处于抑郁发作期的双相情感障碍患者，同样未发现治疗益处。更糟的是，其中两名服用肌酸的双相情感障碍患者出现了轻躁狂或躁狂症状，这清醒地提醒我们，同一种化合物根据诊断的不同，其作用方式可能会大相径庭。

肌酸为何可能对大脑有益

这些实验背后的逻辑并非牵强附会。大脑是一个“昂贵”的器官，其能量消耗与其体积相比极不相称。肌酸帮助细胞重建三磷酸腺苷（ATP），即为该工作提供能量的分子。对情绪障碍患者的研究发现，其大脑中肌酸代谢异常，能量产生受损已被认为是抑郁症的一个潜在根源。

肌酸也可能影响调节多巴胺和血清素的通路，这些神经递质是大多数抗抑郁药的作用靶点。作者在此处持谨慎态度。他们指出，大脑肌酸与情绪之间的联系仍是相关性而非因果关系，并且抑郁症的生物学机制复杂，涉及多个环节。

“这个信号很有趣，但这还不是定论，”渥太华大学医学院的学生、该综述的第一作者Bassam Jeryous Fares表示。

“两项试验指向一个方向，三项指向另一个方向。这种证据不足以改变临床实践。它只是告诉我们，这个问题值得进一步探索。” 渥太华大学医学院的Bassam Jeryous Fares说。

渥太华大学精神病学住院医师、通讯作者Nicholas Fabiano将这项工作定性为起点而非结论。“肌酸似乎是一种安全的干预措施。我们发现的不良事件仅限于轻微的胃肠道不适。我们目前还不能可靠地断定肌酸有助于改善抑郁症状，也不能确定这些发现是否适用于所有人。”

后续展望

这篇综述并未声称解决了这个问题。作者指出了明显的局限性。试验规模较小。性别比例不平衡，其中两项研究只招募了女性。研究质量参差不齐，两项试验被评为偏倚风险较低，而三项则存在一定程度的担忧，这主要源于患者的分配方式和缺失数据的处理。作者强调，这些发现尚不具有普遍适用性。

他们呼吁开展更严谨的研究。包括更大规模的试验、更长期的试验（超过八周）。以及结合运动测试肌酸的研究，和探索更高剂量的研究，同时要牢记并非剂量越大越好。

甚至有来自动物研究的诱人线索，肌酸在雄性和雌性啮齿动物中对抑郁样行为产生了不同的影响，这或许有助于解释为何有更多女性参与的人类试验显示出更大的潜力。目前，肌酸仍是一个有前景的线索，而非已被证实的疗法。这种构建肌肉的分子，已引起了精神领域研究人员的密切关注。
