深度解析

这篇社论深刻揭示了核武器对全球公共卫生的严峻威胁，远超常规医学范畴。其核心专业概念在于，即使是有限的核战争，也能够通过核冬天效应引发全球性气候剧变，导致农业生产系统崩溃，进而造成大规模的全球饥荒。饥荒并非简单的食物短缺，而是一种复杂的病理生理状态，涉及多系统衰竭、免疫抑制和高死亡率。在核战争情境下，这种规模的饥荒将彻底摧毁所有医疗援助能力，引发次生疾病流行，导致人类社会结构崩溃。文章强调，传统的核威慑理论其基础薄弱，面临心理学和神经科学层面的质疑，指出人类在极端压力下的决策并非总是理性，这极大增加了核误判的风险。人工智能和无人机技术，以及错误信息的传播，进一步放大了这种固有风险，对全球健康安全构成新的挑战。在此背景下，医疗卫生部门的角色超越了疾病治疗，上升到预防人类文明灭绝的公共卫生最高伦理层面。其行业影响在于，它将核裁军从纯粹的政治议题提升为不可或缺的全球健康干预措施，推动国际社会重新审视核风险的公共卫生维度，将维护人类生存作为医学伦理的终极目标。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain In response to growing geopolitical tensions, editors of more than 100 international journals have jointly published an editorial urging health professionals to keep health at the center of public discussions on nuclear risk. The editorial appears in leading global publications, including The BMJ, The Lancet, the Medical Journal of Australia, the East African Medical Journal, the National Medical Journal of India and International Nursing Review. Arsenal spending and global consequences In 2025, the nuclear-armed states—China, France, India, Israel, North Korea, Pakistan, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States—spent just under $119 billion, or $3,768 per second, on their nuclear arsenals, an increase of 19% from the previous year, explain the authors. And with over 12,000 nuclear warheads worldwide along with high populations living in megacities, models show that even a "small" nuclear war using just 2%–3% of existing weapons could trigger global starvation and the collapse of modern civilization. International reviews and treaty efforts In response, the United Nations General Assembly initiated a comprehensive review of current knowledge about the consequences of nuclear war, which is due in 2027, and the World Health Organization will update its studies on the public health effects of nuclear war by 2029. These initiatives represent the first major international reassessments of the effects of nuclear war by these bodies in nearly 40 years, note the authors. Work on top-level international nuclear treaties has also intensified, they say, notably the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW), to which 75 countries have become legally bound, and a further 26 have signed it. However, they point out that, ahead of its first review conference at the end of 2026, none of the nine nuclear-armed states have signed or ratified it, despite rising pressure. Deterrence rests on fragile assumptions The principal argument for nuclear weapons is that they prevent nuclear war through deterrence, but the authors argue that this security "remains fragile, asymmetric, dependent on the permanent threat of catastrophe, and blind to the needs of non-nuclear states." Insights from psychology and neuroscience also suggest that decisions about nuclear weapons are not rational, while emerging AI and drone technologies, misinformation and disinformation further heighten risk. "We need a verifiable, deadline-driven agreement to eliminate all nuclear weapons," write the authors. To safeguard human survival, they say it is essential that the health sector continues to demand that every country, including nuclear-armed states, join the TPNW. Key medical concepts Starvation
