Alzheimer's drug diranersen shows clinical benefit in trial

深度解析

本文着重介绍了Diranersen作为一种反义寡核苷酸（ASO）疗法在早期阿尔茨海默病（AD）治疗中的初步二期临床试验结果。Diranersen的核心机制是“基因沉默”，其作用原理是特异性靶向信使RNA（mRNA）。与传统的小分子药物或抗体不同，ASO是短的合成核酸序列，通过与编码tau蛋白的mRNA结合，从而抑制tau蛋白的合成。tau蛋白在AD病理中扮演关键角色，其异常聚集形成神经纤维缠结，是神经元功能障碍和死亡的主要原因。通过减少tau蛋白的产生，Diranersen旨在从根本上干预AD的疾病进展。 此次试验报告的临床衰退减缓幅度显著，CDR-SB（临床痴呆评定量表总盒分）减缓38%，ADAS-Cog（阿尔茨海默病评估量表–认知分量表）改善54%。CDR-SB是衡量全球认知和日常功能障碍的综合性指标，而ADAS-Cog则侧重于认知表现。这些数据表明Diranersen对患者可能产生有意义的临床益处。如果后续三期临床试验能进一步证实其疗效和安全性，Diranersen有望成为AD领域首个针对病因的疾病修饰疗法，而非仅限于症状缓解。这无疑将对整个神经退行性疾病药物研发领域产生深远影响，预示着精准医学和基因靶向疗法在攻克复杂疾病方面的巨大潜力，为AD患者及其家庭带来新的希望。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: CC0 Public Domain Interim results of the CELIA trial, with data from the first two years of the trial, were presented this week at the Clinical Trials on Alzheimer's Disease (CTAD 2026) conference in Boston, U.S. Professor Cath Mummery (UCL Queen Square Institute of Neurology and UCLH) is one of the study's principal investigators. Diranersen is an antisense oligonucleotide therapy, or "gene-silencing" therapy, that targets tau, one of the two main harmful brain proteins involved in Alzheimer's disease. The research team has now reported that study participants with early Alzheimer's disease being treated with diranersen, delivered by injection twice annually, have continued to make progress relative to those in the comparison group. At the two-year mark, the scientists have reported a continued slowing of clinical decline across multiple measures of cognition, function and disease progression, across all doses studied, with the 60 mg dose group showing the most compelling results. Diranersen slowed clinical decline by 38% on the Clinical Dementia Rating Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB), a global measure of cognition and daily function, after two years (week 96) for study participants on the 60 mg twice-yearly regimen, relative to study participants who were initially given a placebo; this effect was up from 26% at week 76. The researchers found that the drug also yielded additional improvements at week 96 relative to 20 weeks earlier on four of the five other measures used in the trial. On the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale–Cognitive Subscale, 13-item version, the difference was even more pronounced, showing a 54% improvement. The scientists say these phase 2 findings support the continued development of diranersen and further evaluation in a phase 3 trial designed to confirm its efficacy and safety.
