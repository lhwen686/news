One-size-fits-all fetal growth charts used in the NHS to monitor babies' growth before birth often misclassify babies as being either too small or too large, which can lead to missed cases at risk of stillbirth or unnecessary interventions, finds a study of more than 3 million NHS births across England published by The BMJ.

Fetal growth restriction (when a baby's growth in the womb is slower than expected) increases the risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes, so spotting this during pregnancy and before the baby is born is essential for the safety of mother and baby. Its lack of recognition during pregnancy is the most frequent cause of avoidable stillbirth.

A variety of fetal weight charts are in use across hospitals in the NHS to define small and large for gestational age babies, including Hadlock, Intergrowth-21st (IG21), World Health Organization (WHO) and Fetal Medicine Foundation (FMF) charts, all of which are not adjustable, and the customized GROW charts, which can be personalized to each individual mother.

Customized charts produced steadier results

Researchers at the Perinatal Institute in Birmingham compared rates of babies identified as too small or too large according to different charts in 3.2 million births between 2015 and 2025 in 38 of the 42 NHS integrated care boards (ICBs) in England.

They found that using a customized growth chart that adjusts for the mother's characteristics, such as weight and ethnic origin, which affect the usual growth of the baby in the womb, provides more accurate and consistent data, improving safety and helping midwifery and medical staff better identify babies requiring extra care during pregnancy.

Without adjustment for such factors, the rates of babies classified as too small or too large varied widely between charts because of differing methods used to produce them. The "universal," unadjustable charts were mostly derived from other countries, while the GROW standard was based on a UK-wide population.

As an example, the average rate of babies identified as being small for gestational age (below the 10th centile) at term (37+ weeks) ranged from 4.8% with Intergrowth to 17.2% with WHO and FMF charts, and 12.3% with customized GROW charts.

With the unadjustable charts, wide variation was also observed with the same chart in different ICBs because of local population differences. This was not seen with GROW charts because they were adjusted for normal variation.

Call for NHS-wide oversight

These are observational findings, and the researchers acknowledge that they used birth weight rather than ultrasound-estimated weight to assess chart performance, but this allowed all cases to be included regardless of whether they had growth scans during pregnancy.

However, the study was based on routinely collected information covering 90% of NHS areas in England, providing greater confidence in the conclusions. The authors call for urgent standardization of growth charts used in the NHS.

The potential consequences of using inappropriate growth charts were illustrated in a recent BMJ Analysis by the Perinatal Institute on the need for the NHS to improve prevention of avoidable perinatal deaths.

The authors argue that individual NHS trusts tend to see rare but catastrophic outcomes that may result from local protocols and practice too late. They call for a coordinated and coherent program across the NHS that recognizes the importance of standardized growth assessment, and propose the establishment of real-time national oversight to monitor quality and safety in this essential area of maternity care.

《英国医学杂志》发表的一项针对英格兰国民医疗服务体系（NHS）超过300万例分娩的研究发现，NHS中用于监测胎儿出生前生长情况的“一刀切”胎儿生长图表常将胎儿错误地归类为过小或过大，这可能导致漏诊有死产风险的病例或进行不必要的干预。

胎儿生长受限（指胎儿在子宫内生长速度低于预期）会增加不良妊娠结局的风险，因此在怀孕期间、胎儿出生前及时发现这一点对于母婴安全至关重要。妊娠期间未能识别胎儿生长受限是可避免死产最常见的原因。

NHS各医院目前使用多种胎儿体重图表来定义小于胎龄儿和大于胎龄儿，包括Hadlock、Intergrowth-21st (IG21)、世界卫生组织（WHO）和胎儿医学基金会（FMF）图表，这些图表均不可调整；还有可根据每位母亲情况进行个性化定制的GROW图表。

定制图表产生更稳定的结果

伯明翰围产期研究所的研究人员比较了2015年至2025年间英格兰42个NHS综合护理委员会（ICBs）中38个区域的320万例分娩数据，分析了不同图表识别出胎儿过小或过大的发生率。

他们发现，使用可根据母亲特征（如体重和民族血统，这些特征会影响胎儿在子宫内的正常生长）进行调整的定制化生长图表，能够提供更准确、更一致的数据，从而提高安全性，并帮助助产士和医务人员更好地识别在怀孕期间需要额外护理的胎儿。

由于缺乏对这些因素的调整，不同图表所识别的胎儿过小或过大的比例差异很大，这归因于其制作方法的不同。“通用”的不可调整图表大多源自其他国家的人群数据，而GROW标准则基于英国全国人群。

例如，足月（37周以上）小于胎龄儿（低于第10百分位）的平均发生率，在使用Intergrowth图表时为4.8%，而使用WHO和FMF图表时为17.2%，使用定制化GROW图表时为12.3%。

对于不可调整的图表，即使是同一张图表，在不同的综合护理委员会（ICBs）中也观察到显著差异，这归因于当地人群的差异。而GROW图表则未出现这种情况，因为它已针对正常变异进行了调整。

呼吁NHS进行全系统监督

这些是观察性研究结果，研究人员承认他们使用出生体重而非超声估算体重来评估图表性能，但这使得所有病例（无论是否在怀孕期间进行过生长扫描）都得以纳入研究。

然而，该研究基于覆盖英格兰90%的NHS区域的常规收集信息，为研究结论提供了更高的可信度。作者们呼吁NHS紧急标准化所使用的生长图表。

最近，围产期研究所在《英国医学杂志》的一项分析中阐述了使用不当生长图表的潜在后果，并强调NHS需要改进可避免围产期死亡的预防工作。

作者们认为，单个NHS信托机构往往直到为时过晚才发现由当地规程和实践导致的罕见但灾难性的结局。他们呼吁NHS实施一项协调一致的全国性计划，认识到标准化生长评估的重要性，并提议建立实时国家监督机制，以监测这一基本产科护理领域的质量和安全。
