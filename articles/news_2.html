临床医生自行解读影像时，开具影像检查医嘱的几率更高

Odds of imaging order higher when clinicians self-interpret

深度解析

这项研究揭示了非放射科医生自行解读影像与更高影像检查开具率之间的显著关联，触及了医疗影像诊断的核心专业性问题。放射科医生通过多年专业训练，具备识别细微病变、鉴别诊断和评估图像质量的深厚知识。相比之下，其他专科医生在影像解读方面可能缺乏同等深度，这可能导致不确定性增加，从而倾向于开具更多不必要的后续影像检查，甚至可能导致误诊或漏诊，影响患者诊疗。 这种现象对医疗行业影响巨大。经济上，不当影像检查直接推高医疗成本和患者负担；研究指出若无自行解读，门诊影像量可潜在减少17%，显示出显著的节约潜力。患者安全方面，不必要的检查（如CT辐射）增加了风险。此外，过度使用影像资源也导致设备和专业人员的紧张。因此，研究结果强调了制定更严格的非放射科医生影像解读标准的重要性，旨在优化医疗资源配置，确保诊断准确性，并提升患者安全与护理质量。

Original
This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies . Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Providers who self-interpret imaging are significantly more likely to order outpatient imaging, according to a study published online Aug. 31 in the Journal of the American College of Radiology. Eric W. Christensen, Ph.D., from the Harvey L. Neiman Health Policy Institute in Reston, Virginia, and colleagues examined the association between imaging ordering rates after office-based evaluation and management (E&M) visits and the percentage of a practice's imaging studies interpreted by a radiologist versus the ordering provider. The analysis included 24.2 million E&M visits identified from the U.S. Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services 5% Research Identifiable Files (2021–2023). The researchers found that 13.6% of identified visits had imaging, and 33.2% of those involved ordering providers' self-interpretation. Practices with 100% radiologist interpretation, versus 0%, had lower odds of 30-day imaging for all modalities except computed tomography (adjusted odds ratios [aORs] ranging from 0.59 for ultrasound to 0.95 for magnetic resonance). Among ordering providers with 100% self-interpretation, versus 0%, there were higher odds of visit-associated imaging, both overall (aOR, 2.88) and for each modality (ranging from an aOR of 1.54 for magnetic resonance to an aOR of 3.51 for ultrasound). There was variation by ordering provider subspecialty and patient diagnosis. "A policy that establishes more stringent standards for nonradiologist interpretation may reduce inappropriate imaging that increases economic burdens and poses patient-safety risks," Christensen said in a statement. "In fact, our analyses indicate that office-based imaging could potentially be reduced by 17% in the absence of self-interpretation." Publication details Eric W. Christensen et al, Association of Office-Visit Imaging Utilization with Radiologist Versus Ordering Provider Imaging Interpretation, Journal of the American College of Radiology (2026).
