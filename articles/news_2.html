深度解析

这项研究极大地深化了我们对蛛网膜下腔出血（SAH）这一严重卒中病理生理机制的理解。SAH不仅是单纯的出血事件，其早期进展性脑损伤涉及复杂的时序性病理过程。本研究的核心突破在于揭示了中性粒细胞RAGE（晚期糖基化终产物受体）在SAH后早期脑血管损伤中的关键作用。研究团队描绘了一个时间依赖性的病理生理序列：SAH后即刻发生交感神经过度激活及α1受体介导的脑动脉收缩，导致循环障碍；随后，在超急性期至急性期，损伤相关分子模式（DAMPs）如HMGB1激活中性粒细胞上的RAGE，引发RAGE依赖性炎症反应，导致中性粒细胞在脑动脉周围聚集，进而加剧脑血管痉挛、皮层循环受损及神经功能障碍。 这一发现具有显著的临床和行业影响。目前SAH的治疗主要关注预防再出血和管理脑血管痉挛等下游并发症。本研究将中性粒细胞RAGE确定为一种“上游”病理机制，为开发新型、基于机制的早期治疗策略提供了重要靶点。通过针对RAGE信号通路或中性粒细胞相关炎症进行药理学干预，有望在疾病进展的早期阶段即进行有效阻断，从而减轻脑损伤，显著改善患者的神经功能预后。这预示着SAH的临床诊疗策略可能从被动应对并发症转向主动精准干预核心病理过程，有望革新未来的治疗范式。

The group's studies suggest a temporal sequence in which sympathetic stimulation and alpha1-mediated cerebral arterial constriction occur immediately after SAH, followed by neutrophil RAGE-dependent inflammatory cerebrovascular injury during the hyperacute-to-acute phase. Credit: Kanazawa University An international research team from Japan and the United States, led by Assistant Professor Hiroshi Ishii and Professor Osamu Hori, together with Professors Yasuhiko Yamamoto and Mitsutoshi Nakada of Kanazawa University, has identified neutrophil RAGE (receptor for advanced glycation end products) as a key driver of early cerebrovascular injury after subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH). The study, published in the Journal of Cerebral Blood Flow & Metabolism, shows that neutrophil RAGE promotes neutrophil accumulation around cerebral arteries, cerebral vasospasm, impaired cortical circulation and neurological dysfunction. SAH is a severe form of stroke most often caused by rupture of a cerebral aneurysm. Even after the bleeding source is secured, reductions in cerebral blood flow, vasospasm, inflammation and brain edema can continue to worsen neurological outcome. The mechanisms linking these early events to later brain injury, however, remain incompletely understood. In 2023, the group reported in Stroke that sympathetic hyperactivity immediately after SAH impairs cerebral circulation and worsens neurological outcome. The new study extends this work by identifying a subsequent inflammatory cerebrovascular phase driven by neutrophil RAGE. Key findings In wild-type mice, SAH caused neurological deterioration, marked vasospasm of major cerebral arteries and a substantial loss of cortical vascular perfusion. In RAGE-knockout mice, neurological function and cortical perfusion were markedly improved, while vasospasm was reduced. The researchers also found that MPO-positive neutrophils accumulated around cerebral arteries early after SAH, with a prominent increase by six hours. This perivascular neutrophil accumulation was strongly suppressed in RAGE-knockout mice. Analyses using bone marrow–derived cells and myeloid-cell-specific RAGE deletion further supported a central role for neutrophil RAGE in the development of cerebrovascular injury. Mechanistically, damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), including HMGB1 released from the hematoma and injured tissue, activated RAGE-dependent inflammatory responses in neutrophils and promoted their migration. Pharmacological suppression of RAGE signaling or neutrophil-derived inflammatory responses also improved vasospasm and neurological deficits. "Subarachnoid hemorrhage is not a single event that ends when the bleeding is controlled," said Ishii, the corresponding author. "Our studies suggest that an immediate sympathetic vascular response is followed by a neutrophil RAGE-dependent inflammatory phase. Understanding how these responses evolve in space and time may help us identify earlier, mechanism-based therapeutic strategies for SAH." A time-dependent sequence of brain injury Taken together with the group's 2023 findings, the results support a time-dependent pathophysiological sequence after SAH: an immediate sympathetic response with alpha1 receptor-mediated cerebral arterial constriction and circulatory impairment, followed during the hyperacute-to-acute phase by neutrophil RAGE-dependent inflammatory cerebrovascular injury. How the sympathetic response and the neutrophil RAGE response interact mechanistically remains to be determined. Current SAH treatment focuses on preventing rebleeding and limiting downstream complications such as cerebral vasospasm and impaired cerebral blood flow. The present study identifies neutrophil RAGE as an upstream pathological mechanism that may provide a new point of therapeutic intervention. Future work will examine how neural, vascular and immune responses evolve spatially and temporally after SAH and how they collectively lead to brain injury.
