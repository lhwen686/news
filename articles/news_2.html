DNA修复蛋白基因失控或有望开启癌症治疗新方法

DNA repair protein gene gone rogue may unlock new cancer treatments

这篇医学新闻报道揭示了EXO1基因在癌症治疗中的重要潜力。其核心专业概念在于，EXO1蛋白的异常高表达会导致基因组不稳定性，具体机制是EXO1像“失控的剪刀”一样，过度剪切并降解新合成的DNA，形成单链DNA间隙和逆转复制叉降解，进而产生有毒的双链断裂。这一过程意外地模拟了BRCA基因突变导致的DNA修复缺陷。BRCA基因通常负责DNA修复，其功能缺失使得细胞更容易积累DNA损伤，从而对特定的靶向疗法（如PARP抑制剂奥拉帕利）敏感。

When it comes to cancer, tumor suppressor genes are usually thought of as the "good guys." These genes make proteins that protect and repair DNA in cells. If they stop functioning or there's not enough, cancer risk goes up. But there can be too much of a good thing: when cells overexpress the gene EXO1—meaning that they make more of the protein than they should—it can degrade the DNA it's supposed to repair. This causes damage that can disrupt the genome, which is a hallmark of cancer, according to a team of researchers from Penn State College of Medicine.

In a study published in Nature Communications, the researchers found that the EXO1 gene is overexpressed in 20% to 30% of breast and ovarian cancers as well as in melanoma, testicular, cervical, and hepatobiliary cancers, which develop in the liver, gall bladder, and bile duct. Tumor cells with high levels of EXO1 protein exhibit characteristics similar to cells with a BRCA mutation, a genetic code change known for its link to hereditary breast and ovarian cancers. That means that these tumor cells behave like BRCA-mutant cells—including their response to the chemotherapies and other drugs—even when there is no BRCA mutation present, a finding the researchers said hasn't previously been established.

"EXO1 doesn't predict cancer risk, but it could potentially serve as a biomarker to help predict which patients are more likely to respond to certain chemotherapy treatments, leading to more personalized therapies," said George-Lucian Moldovan, professor of molecular and precision medicine and senior author on the study.

"The same drugs that are reserved for treating BRCA-mutant tumors and that have fewer side effects could potentially be used to treat EXO1 overexpressing tumors, which don't have BRCA mutations. It would expand the applicability of those drugs."

Digging into genomic data and models

For this study, the research team analyzed The Cancer Genome Atlas, a cancer genomic program of the National Cancer Institute, for EXO1 alterations in tumor samples. They found that EXO1 was overproduced in several tumors, including those in the breast, skin, liver, and cervix, which aligns with previous studies in the field. Specifically, high levels of EXO1 were linked to basal-like breast cancers, an aggressive subtype of breast cancer.

The research team conducted laboratory studies with commercially available human cancer cells. The researchers overexpressed the EXO1 gene in the cells to see how an overabundance affected the cell's DNA. They also overexpressed a biochemically disabled version of the EXO1 gene, meaning the proteins it produced were present but not interacting with other cells, to confirm that any DNA damage observed was specifically caused by protein activity and not just the presence of the protein.

How too much EXO1 harms DNA

In normal cells, the EXO1 protein acts like a pair of molecular scissors, trimming and repairing damaged DNA. When there's too much EXO1 in cells, the researchers found that it started cutting things that it shouldn't. They observed that EXO1 destabilizes newly synthesized DNA in two primary ways—by expanding single-stranded DNA gaps and degrading reversed replication forks. Both processes chew up DNA and lead to localized loss of DNA sequences, Moldovan explained.

"Regardless of which pathway, EXO1 overexpression leads to the generation and accumulation of toxic lesions in DNA, such as double strand breaks, which we ultimately think is what makes the tumor more sensitive to chemotherapy and increases cell death," said Alexandra Nusawardhana, the lead author of the study and who earned her doctorate in biomedical sciences this year from Penn State College of Medicine.

Parallels with BRCA-mutant tumor biology

Normally, BRCA genes produce proteins that protect these genomic structures; when there's a mutation in the BRCA genes, cells can't protect their DNA during replication, which can eventually lead to cancer development. However, in this study, high levels of EXO1 protein bypassed BRCA's defenses, even in cells where BRCA is fully functional and there is no mutation. The researchers found that EXO1 worked in concert with another protein called MRE11 to expand gaps in DNA and create dangerous breaks.

"Mechanistically, this overexpression does exactly what the loss of the BRCA pathway does in BRCA-mutant tumor cells," Moldovan said. But unlike BRCA mutations, EXO1 overexpression isn't inherited and it's not known if it causes cancer, he explained.

Testing sensitivity to cancer drugs

Because of the similar behavior, the researchers wanted to know if tumor cells that overexpressed EXO1 would also respond to cancer treatments the same way that BRCA-mutant tumors do, too. They tested how EXO1 overexpressing tumors responded to olaparib, an existing medication used to treat BRCA-mutant cancers by targeting the cellular repair system. They found that the tumors were hypersensitive to the drug, responding like the BRCA-mutant cancers.

The finding suggests that tumors that overexpress EXO1, but don't have a BRCA mutation, might also benefit from the same kind of cellular repair-targeted therapy. The researchers also found that EXO1-overexpressing tumors responded to cisplatin, a widely used chemotherapy drug, suggesting that lower doses of cisplatin could potentially achieve the same tumor shrinkage with fewer side effects.

Potential biomarker for future therapies

Since EXO1 overexpression is more common across tumors than the BRCA mutation, its presence in cancer cells could potentially be a useful biomarker to identify more personalized treatment options, according to Moldovan.

"We shouldn't treat cancers based on what tissue they come from but based on the landscape of the genetic mutations present in the tumors," Moldovan said. "That would result in high efficiency treatment. That's the future of cancer treatment."

The research team plans to continue this line of research with the goal of eventually conducting clinical trials with cancer patients with tumors that exhibit EXO1 overexpression.

Claudia Nicolae, assistant professor of molecular and precision medicine at Penn State College of Medicine, also contributed to the paper.

谈到癌症，肿瘤抑制基因通常被认为是“好基因”。这些基因制造的蛋白质可以保护和修复细胞中的DNA。如果它们停止发挥功能或数量不足，癌症风险就会增加。但“好东西”也可能过量：当细胞过度表达EXO1基因——意味着它们制造的蛋白质超出了应有的量——它可能会降解本应修复的DNA。根据宾夕法尼亚州立大学医学院的一个研究团队的说法，这会导致损害，扰乱基因组，而基因组扰乱是癌症的一个标志。

在《自然通讯》杂志上发表的一项研究中，研究人员发现EXO1基因在20%到30%的乳腺癌和卵巢癌中过度表达，在黑色素瘤、睾丸癌、宫颈癌以及肝胆癌（发生在肝脏、胆囊和胆管）中也存在过度表达。高水平EXO1蛋白的肿瘤细胞表现出与BRCA突变细胞相似的特征，BRCA突变是一种以其与遗传性乳腺癌和卵巢癌的关联而闻名的基因编码改变。这意味着这些肿瘤细胞表现得像BRCA突变细胞一样——包括它们对化疗和其他药物的反应——即使没有BRCA突变存在，研究人员表示这一发现以前从未被确立过。

“EXO1不预测癌症风险，但它可能作为一种生物标志物，帮助预测哪些患者更有可能对某些化疗治疗产生反应，从而实现更个性化的治疗，”分子与精准医学教授兼该研究的资深作者乔治-卢西安·莫尔多万（George-Lucian Moldovan）说。

“那些专门用于治疗BRCA突变肿瘤且副作用较少的药物，有可能被用于治疗EXO1过度表达但没有BRCA突变的肿瘤。这将扩大这些药物的适用范围。”

深入挖掘基因组数据和模型

为了这项研究，研究团队分析了美国国家癌症研究所的癌症基因组项目——癌症基因组图谱（The Cancer Genome Atlas），以寻找肿瘤样本中的EXO1改变。他们发现EXO1在多种肿瘤中过度产生，包括乳腺、皮肤、肝脏和宫颈肿瘤，这与该领域先前的研究结果一致。具体而言，高水平的EXO1与基底样乳腺癌（一种侵袭性乳腺癌亚型）相关联。

研究团队利用市售的人类癌细胞进行了实验室研究。研究人员在细胞中过度表达EXO1基因，以观察过量表达如何影响细胞的DNA。他们还过度表达了一个生化失活的EXO1基因版本，这意味着它产生的蛋白质存在但不会与其他细胞相互作用，以确认观察到的任何DNA损伤是专门由蛋白质活性引起的，而不仅仅是蛋白质的存在。

过多的EXO1如何损害DNA

在正常细胞中，EXO1蛋白就像一把分子剪刀，修剪和修复受损的DNA。研究人员发现，当细胞中EXO1过多时，它开始剪切不该剪切的东西。他们观察到EXO1以两种主要方式破坏新合成的DNA——通过扩大单链DNA间隙和降解逆转复制叉。莫尔多万解释说，这两个过程都会“咀嚼”DNA，并导致DNA序列的局部丢失。

“无论通过哪种途径，EXO1的过度表达都会导致DNA中产生和积累有毒损伤，例如双链断裂，我们最终认为这正是使肿瘤对化疗更敏感并增加细胞死亡的原因，”本研究的主要作者、今年从宾夕法尼亚州立大学医学院获得生物医学博士学位的亚历山德拉·努萨瓦达纳说。

与BRCA突变肿瘤生物学的异同

通常，BRCA基因产生的蛋白质可以保护这些基因组结构；当BRCA基因发生突变时，细胞在复制过程中无法保护其DNA，这最终可能导致癌症的发展。然而，在这项研究中，高水平的EXO1蛋白绕过了BRCA的防御，即使在BRCA功能完全正常且没有突变的细胞中也是如此。研究人员发现EXO1与另一种名为MRE11的蛋白质协同作用，扩大DNA中的间隙并产生危险的断裂。

“从机制上讲，这种过度表达与BRCA突变肿瘤细胞中BRCA通路缺失所产生的效果完全相同，”莫尔多万说。但他解释说，与BRCA突变不同，EXO1的过度表达并非遗传，目前尚不清楚它是否导致癌症。

测试对癌症药物的敏感性

由于行为相似，研究人员想知道过度表达EXO1的肿瘤细胞是否也会以与BRCA突变肿瘤相同的方式响应癌症治疗。他们测试了EXO1过度表达的肿瘤对奥拉帕利（一种通过靶向细胞修复系统治疗BRCA突变癌症的现有药物）的反应。他们发现这些肿瘤对该药物高度敏感，反应类似于BRCA突变癌症。

这一发现表明，过度表达EXO1但没有BRCA突变的肿瘤，也可能受益于相同类型的靶向细胞修复疗法。研究人员还发现，EXO1过度表达的肿瘤对广泛使用的化疗药物顺铂有反应，这表明较低剂量的顺铂可能达到相同的肿瘤缩小效果，同时减少副作用。

未来疗法的潜在生物标志物

莫尔多万表示，由于EXO1过度表达在肿瘤中比BRCA突变更常见，其在癌细胞中的存在可能成为识别更个性化治疗方案的有用生物标志物。

“我们不应该根据癌症的组织来源来治疗癌症，而应该根据肿瘤中存在的基因突变图谱来治疗，”莫尔多万说。“这将带来高效的治疗。这是癌症治疗的未来。”

研究团队计划继续这项研究，目标是最终对表现出EXO1过度表达的癌症患者进行临床试验。
