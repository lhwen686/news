无创产前检测结果与侵入性方法媲美，且安全性更高、成本更低

Results of non-invasive prenatal testing compare well to those from invasive methods, with better safety and cost

While noninvasive prenatal testing (NIPT) has revolutionized prenatal diagnostics by allowing the detection of a number of genetic problems in a fetus, it is currently limited and thus misses many genetic causes of abnormalities. But a new technique, to be presented at the annual conference of the European Society of Human Genetics, introduces a technology called noninvasive fetal sequencing (NIFS) that will simultaneously screen almost 23,000 genes, as well as all of the conditions currently captured by NIPT, in pregnancies both with and without a previously detected fetal anomaly.

Presenting the research, Dr. Christopher Whelan—a senior computational scientist working in the laboratory of Dr. Michael Talkowski at the Broad Institute of Massachusetts Institute of Technology and Harvard, and at the Center of Genomic Medicine at Massachusetts General Hospital, Boston—says the new technique was able to identify a very high proportion of the clinically relevant genetic variants that are currently detectable only by invasive genome sequencing (GS). The finding suggests that NIFS may be used as a safer, equally accurate screening tool in all pregnancies, he says.

Most current NIPT methods are low-resolution and focus on only a small number of genetic abnormalities, and there is limited standardization among them. Comprehensive testing of all genes relevant to prenatal diagnostics is accessible only through invasive testing methods.

"Currently, many women refuse the invasive sequencing methods—amniocentesis and chorionic villus sampling (CVS)—because of the risk to the fetus, related stress, difficulties of access and cost, even though their diagnostic capacity is high," says Dr. Whelan. "We were trying to develop a test with similar diagnostic value, but without the risks and other downsides."

The researchers tested NIFS on 565 pregnancies at an average of 17 weeks of gestation. They applied deep cell-free fetal DNA (cffDNA) sequencing to the analysis of maternal blood samples and used advanced computing methods to identify genetic variants across nearly 23,000 genes (the exome) in each fetus.

Checking their findings against those from direct sequencing of the fetus following amniocentesis or CVS allowed them to verify their accuracy. They found that NIFS detected around 95%–99% of the genetic variants found by the invasive methods, depending on variant type and inheritance pattern, and—importantly—97.2% of the genetic variants responsible for clinically important conditions in the study.

"The test performed really well in capturing all of the clinically relevant variants found by invasive GS that would have been missed by all current noninvasive tests, and accurately genotyping over 97% of them. There were also some unexpected discoveries, such as twin pregnancies with abnormal tissue, and evidence that some mothers had received a bone marrow transplant from a male donor that confounded NIPT results," says Whelan. "This provided further evidence of the strength of the technique."

NIFS is estimated to be considerably cheaper than the current gold standard of invasive GS, since it is largely built on capabilities that already exist and are widely available in commercial diagnostic labs and does not require a medical procedure. The technique uses only a slightly greater number of sequencing reads than are needed for invasive GS, and it can be used at an earlier stage in pregnancy than that at which most fetal abnormalities are detectable by imaging.

By providing earlier access to genetic information and diagnoses, NIFS can reduce overall costs by allowing more informed management of a pregnancy. The test has already been shown to be accurate in samples from pregnancies as early as 10 weeks of gestation, with the proportion of cfDNA in maternal blood that comes from the placenta (the fetal fraction) as low as 3%.

"At those fractions, we still saw a very high concordance with clinical GS performed on DNA from invasive testing," says Whelan.

The researchers now intend to keep improving the capabilities of NIFS to identify additional clinically relevant genetic variants that are not assessed by standard exome sequencing. They are also expanding and scaling their studies in order to enable NIFS screening for all pregnancies in the future.

"While the diagnostic yields and overall performance of the test were not a surprise, it was remarkable that we were able to access and sequence as much of the fetal genome as we did from a simple maternal blood draw during pregnancy. In the future, there will be a lot of exciting work happening in the field of prenatal treatment for genetic disease. Together with NIFS, this could be transformative by allowing a treatment to be used at an earlier and more effective stage.

"NIFS also allows us to begin to capture, months before birth, the clinically relevant information that is currently assessed by newborn screening, allowing early preparation for postnatal management," says Whelan. "This is an exciting paradigm shift and inflection point for prenatal diagnostics."

Chair of the conference, Professor Alexandre Reymond, who was not involved in the research, said, "Sequencing the entire genome of a fetus without even getting a sample from that fetus is a tour de force. It immediately opens up treatment and prevention opportunities and means that reproductive medicine will be changed forever."

虽然无创产前检测（NIPT）通过检测胎儿的一些遗传问题，彻底改变了产前诊断，但其目前存在局限性，因此会遗漏许多导致异常的遗传原因。然而，一项将在欧洲人类遗传学学会年会上发布的新技术，引入了一种名为无创胎儿测序（NIFS）的技术，该技术将能够同时筛查近23,000个基因，以及目前NIPT所能捕捉到的所有疾病，适用于此前已检测到胎儿异常和未检测到胎儿异常的妊娠。

在介绍这项研究时，麻省理工学院和哈佛大学布罗德研究所Michael Talkowski博士实验室以及波士顿麻省总医院基因组医学中心的高级计算科学家Christopher Whelan博士表示，这项新技术能够识别出目前只有通过侵入性基因组测序（GS）才能检测到的绝大部分临床相关遗传变异。他指出，这一发现表明NIFS有望成为所有妊娠中一种更安全、同样准确的筛查工具。

目前大多数NIPT方法分辨率较低，仅关注少数遗传异常，且标准化程度有限。对所有与产前诊断相关的基因进行全面检测，目前只能通过侵入性检测方法实现。

Whelan博士表示：“目前，许多女性拒绝侵入性测序方法——羊膜穿刺术和绒毛膜取样（CVS），因为存在胎儿风险、相关压力、获取困难和成本高等问题，尽管它们的诊断能力很高。我们试图开发一种具有相似诊断价值，但没有风险和其他缺点的检测方法。”

研究人员对565例平均妊娠17周的孕妇进行了NIFS测试。他们将深度无细胞胎儿DNA（cffDNA）测序应用于母体血液样本分析，并使用先进的计算方法识别每个胎儿近23,000个基因（外显子组）的遗传变异。

通过将NIFS的发现与羊膜穿刺术或CVS后胎儿直接测序的结果进行比对，研究人员验证了其准确性。他们发现，NIFS检测到了侵入性方法所发现的约95%–99%的遗传变异（具体取决于变异类型和遗传模式），更重要的是，该研究中NIFS检测到了导致临床重要疾病的97.2%的遗传变异。

Whelan表示：“该测试在捕获所有侵入性基因组测序（GS）发现的、而目前所有无创检测都会遗漏的临床相关变异方面表现出色，并准确地对其中超过97%的变异进行了基因分型。此外，还有一些意想不到的发现，例如伴有异常组织的双胎妊娠，以及一些母亲曾接受男性供者骨髓移植的证据，这些情况曾干扰了NIPT的结果。这进一步证明了该技术的强大之处。”

NIFS预计比目前侵入性基因组测序的黄金标准便宜得多，因为它主要基于现有且在商业诊断实验室中广泛可用的能力，并且不需要医疗操作。这项技术所需的测序读数仅略多于侵入性基因组测序，而且可以在比大多数胎儿异常通过影像学检测更早的妊娠阶段使用。

通过提供更早获取遗传信息和诊断的机会，NIFS可以通过实现更明智的妊娠管理来降低总体成本。该测试已证明在妊娠10周（胎儿游离DNA在母体血液中的比例，即胎儿分数，低至3%）的样本中也能准确无误。

Whelan表示：“在这些低胎儿分数下，我们仍然观察到NIFS结果与侵入性检测DNA进行的临床基因组测序（GS）具有非常高的一致性。”

研究人员目前计划继续改进NIFS的能力，以识别标准外显子组测序未评估的、更多临床相关的遗传变异。他们还在扩大和推广他们的研究，以期未来能为所有妊娠提供NIFS筛查。

Whelan表示：“尽管该测试的诊断效能和总体表现并不令人意外，但我们能够通过简单的孕期母体采血获取并测序如此多的胎儿基因组信息，这确实非凡。未来，在遗传疾病的产前治疗领域将会有许多令人兴奋的工作。结合NIFS，这将是变革性的，因为它能使治疗在更早、更有效的阶段实施。

NIFS还使我们能够在出生前数月就开始捕获目前通过新生儿筛查评估的临床相关信息，从而为产后管理提供早期准备。这对于产前诊断来说，是一个令人振奋的范式转变和转折点。”

未参与这项研究的大会主席Alexandre Reymond教授表示：“在不获取胎儿样本的情况下测序整个胎儿基因组，这是一项壮举。它立即开启了治疗和预防的机会，意味着生殖医学将永远改变。”
