New AI tool detects early psychosis risk in youth speech

深度解析

文章的核心专业概念在于LEMon-AID框架，它开创性地利用大型语言模型（LLMs）识别精神病超高风险（UHR）青年。其技术原理突破了传统LLM难以从青少年“非披露性言语”中直接识别症状的局限。LEMon-AID并非直接诊断，而是将LLMs作为情感特征提取器，从非正式对话中捕捉诸如喜悦、信任、恐惧和悲伤等细微情感信号，再结合传统机器学习进行风险预测。这种间接但高效的识别方式，使识别敏感度显著提高至93%。 从医学机制来看，该方法通过解读言语中的情感“指纹”来间接推断潜在的心理健康风险，这与临床医生评估患者时依赖非语言线索和细微行为模式的逻辑相契合。其行业影响深远：首先，它极大提升了精神病早期干预的可能性，有望改善UHR青年的预后，预防长期残疾。其次，LEMon-AID的透明推理机制，即提供自然语言解释而非“黑箱”操作，增强了临床医生对AI工具的信任和采纳意愿，促进了人机协作。最后，这项技术能赋能基层医疗和社区工作者，扩大精神健康筛查的覆盖面，实现从被动治疗到主动风险识别的转变，为精神健康领域带来革命性的变革。

英文原文

Original
Nanyang Technological University, Singapore (NTU Singapore) and the Institute of Mental Health (IMH) have developed a novel artificial intelligence (AI) framework capable of identifying youths at high risk of psychosis - a severe mental health condition that typically emerges in adolescence to early adulthood where a person may experience hallucinations, delusions, and disordered thinking - from short samples of casual, spontaneous speech. This is the first reported study in Singapore to focus specifically on early psychosis risk among youths using large language models (LLMs), a form of artificial intelligence trained to analyse and generate human language. The team of researchers believes the technology could eventually support primary care providers and community workers in identifying vulnerable youths before clinical symptoms escalate. Early identification of individuals at ultra-high risk (UHR) of psychosis is challenging because youths in everyday life rarely articulate mental health struggles directly. Ultra-high risk of psychosis refers to a clinical state in which an individual shows early warning signs or risk factors that significantly increase the likelihood of developing psychosis. For example, a depressed person may not explicitly state that they feel depressed. This is known as "non-disclosing speech," meaning language that does not contain any explicit mention of the mental health condition experienced by the speaker. Therefore, evaluating whether AI can detect potential risk purely from casual, spontaneous conversation addresses a key hurdle in early intervention. Transforming casual conversation into clinical insight Published in the scientific journal npj Mental Health Research, the joint NTU-IMH study evaluated transcribed text samples from 429 participants aged 14 to 29 enrolled in Singapore's Longitudinal Youth-at-Risk Study (LYRIKS), a landmark Singapore study initiated in 2008 to comprehensively assess the social, clinical, and biological risk factors associated with youths at ultra-high risk of developing psychosis. Each participant provided a 45-second non-self-disclosing speech sample in response to a neutral prompt, such as making a case for why they should win an award for being the 'most interesting person'. The researchers found that standard LLMs struggled when prompted directly to predict clinical risk from raw transcripts, achieving performance no better than a random guess and missing most at-risk individuals. The researchers believe standard models struggled due to a lack of suitable training datasets, as well as the nature of spontaneous everyday speech, which rarely contains structured clinical information or explicit symptom descriptions. To overcome this limitation, the team developed LEMon-AID (Latent Emotion Proxy for Mental Health Detection with AI-integrated Decision-making), which leverages LLMs to extract subtle emotional profiles such as joy, trust, fear, and sadness from the text, then combines these emotional signals with traditional machine learning predictions. The study demonstrated that LEMon-AID performs significantly better than conventional approaches. It boosted overall predictive accuracy from 55 per cent under conventional direct LLM prompting to 67 per cent, while dramatically increasing detection sensitivity such that up to 93 per cent of at-risk youths can be identified. Human-AI collaboration and transparent reasoning Co-lead investigator of the study, Wilson Goh, Assistant Professor of Biomedical Informatics from LKCMedicine and the NTU Centre of AI in Medicine, said: "Standard AI models struggle to pick up early mental health risks from everyday conversation because people rarely talk about their symptoms openly. Instead of asking AI to give a direct diagnosis, LEMon-AID uses language models to read underlying emotional cues. This system connects emotional patterns with mental health risks while keeping patient data private and providing clear, understandable explanations." Unlike traditional machine learning models, which may operate as 'black boxes', LEMon-AID generates natural-language explanations alongside its risk assessments. This allows clinicians to understand the clear reasoning behind each prediction, making AI a safer and more transparent tool for doctors to use when evaluating patients. Dr Jimmy Lee, Group Chief Research and Innovation Officer, NHG Health and Senior Consultant, IMH, and an Associate Professor at NTU's LKCMedicine, who led the LYRIKS study said, "Early detection is one of our most powerful tools in preventing long-term disability and restoring the health and futures of young people with mental illness. This research brings us a meaningful step closer to that goal, a future where frontline community workers and healthcare providers are better equipped to identify at-risk youths early, supported by AI tools that help surface those who might otherwise be missed. The aim is to reach young people sooner and encourage them to seek the care they need before their condition worsens." When we directly asked standard AI models to identify at-risk individuals from casual conversation transcripts, they missed almost everyone. By shifting the AI's focus to mapping emotional signals such as joy, sadness, or trust across a conversation, we found a way to uncover meaningful patterns that were previously hidden in everyday speech." Matheus Calvin Lokadjaja, first author of the study, Research Assistant, LKCMedicine Next steps towards clinical validation The newly developed LEMon-AID framework is currently being explored for various clinical deployments in future work. The researchers will tap into multimodal approaches, incorporating different data types and modalities - such as acoustic cues, vocal pitch, speech rhythm and facial expressions - to improve system reliability while also helping us understand how discovered patterns across speech, behaviours and acoustics inform on mental health progression. Further extensive research and real-world validation will be needed before practical implementation can occur. This research is supported by the Singapore Ministry of Health through the National Medical Research Council (NMRC) Office, MOH Holdings Pte Ltd under the NMRC Translational and Clinical Research Flagship Programme (grant number NMRC/TCR/003/2008).

AI Translation
新加坡南洋理工大学（NTU Singapore）与心理健康研究所（IMH）共同开发了一种新型人工智能（AI）框架，该框架能够通过简短的非正式、自发性言语样本，识别出有精神病高风险的青少年——精神病是一种通常在青少年期至成年早期发作的严重精神疾病，患者可能经历幻觉、妄想和思维紊乱。 这是新加坡首个专门针对使用大型语言模型（LLMs）识别青少年早期精神病风险的研究报告，LLMs是一种经过训练以分析和生成人类语言的人工智能形式。研究团队认为，这项技术最终可以支持基层医疗服务提供者和社区工作者在临床症状恶化前识别脆弱青少年。 早期识别精神病超高风险（UHR）个体具有挑战性，因为青少年在日常生活中很少直接表达心理健康困扰。精神病超高风险是指个体表现出早期预警信号或风险因素，这些因素显著增加了罹患精神病的可能性的一种临床状态。 例如，一个抑郁的人可能不会明确地说他们感到抑郁。这被称为“非披露性言语”，指说话者所使用的语言中不包含任何对其所经历的心理健康状况的明确提及。因此，评估人工智能是否能够纯粹从非正式、自发性对话中检测出潜在风险，解决了早期干预的一个关键障碍。 将日常对话转化为临床洞察 这项由南洋理工大学和心理健康研究所联合开展的研究发表在科学期刊《npj精神健康研究》上，评估了新加坡纵向高危青年研究（LYRIKS）中429名14至29岁参与者的转录文本样本。LYRIKS是新加坡一项于2008年启动的里程碑式研究，旨在全面评估与罹患精神病超高风险青年相关的社会、临床和生物学风险因素。 每位参与者根据一个中立提示提供了一段45秒的非自我披露性言语样本，例如，说明他们为什么应该赢得“最有趣的人”奖项。 研究人员发现，当直接要求标准大型语言模型（LLMs）从原始转录文本中预测临床风险时，它们表现不佳，其表现不优于随机猜测，并且漏掉了大多数高风险个体。 研究人员认为，标准模型表现不佳是由于缺乏合适的训练数据集，以及自发性日常言语的特性——这类言语很少包含结构化的临床信息或明确的症状描述。 为了克服这一局限性，该团队开发了LEMon-AID（结合人工智能决策的心理健康检测潜在情感代理），该系统利用大型语言模型（LLMs）从文本中提取微妙的情感特征，例如喜悦、信任、恐惧和悲伤，然后将这些情感信号与传统机器学习预测相结合。 研究表明，LEMon-AID的表现显著优于传统方法。它将传统直接大型语言模型（LLM）提示下的总体预测准确率从55%提升至67%，同时显著提高了检测敏感度，使得高达93%的高风险青年能够被识别出来。 人机协作与透明推理 该研究的共同主要研究员、南洋理工大学李光前医学院生物医学信息学助理教授兼人工智能医学中心研究员吴伟兴（Wilson Goh）表示：“标准人工智能模型难以从日常对话中捕捉早期心理健康风险，因为人们很少公开谈论他们的症状。LEMon-AID没有要求人工智能直接给出诊断，而是利用语言模型解读潜在的情感线索。该系统将情感模式与心理健康风险关联起来，同时保护患者数据隐私，并提供清晰、易懂的解释。” 与传统机器学习模型可能作为‘黑箱’运行不同，LEMon-AID在其风险评估的同时生成自然语言解释。这使得临床医生能够理解每次预测背后的明确推理，使人工智能成为医生评估患者时更安全、更透明的工具。 领导LYRIKS研究的、新加坡国立健保集团（NHG Health）集团首席研究与创新官兼心理健康研究所（IMH）高级顾问、南洋理工大学李光前医学院副教授李金（Jimmy Lee）博士表示：“早期发现是我们预防长期残疾、恢复精神疾病青年健康和未来的最强大工具之一。这项研究使我们向这一目标迈出了有意义的一步，展望未来，一线社区工作者和医疗服务提供者将更好地识别高危青年，并得到人工智能工具的支持，帮助发现那些原本可能被遗漏的个体。我们的目标是更早地接触到年轻人，并鼓励他们在病情恶化之前寻求所需的护理。” “当我们直接要求标准人工智能模型从非正式对话转录文本中识别高风险个体时，它们几乎遗漏了所有人。通过将人工智能的重点转移到绘制对话中的情感信号，如喜悦、悲伤或信任，我们找到了一种方法来揭示以前隐藏在日常言语中的有意义模式。”该研究的第一作者、李光前医学院研究助理马修斯·卡尔文·洛卡贾贾（Matheus Calvin Lokadjaja）说。 临床验证的下一步 新开发的LEMon-AID框架目前正在未来的工作中探索各种临床部署可能性。研究人员将采用多模态方法，整合不同类型的数据和模态——例如声学线索、语调、言语节奏和面部表情——以提高系统可靠性，同时帮助我们理解言语、行为和声学中发现的模式如何揭示心理健康的进展。 在实际应用之前，还需要进行更广泛的研究和真实世界验证。 这项研究由新加坡卫生部通过国家医学研究理事会（NMRC）办公室、MOH Holdings Pte Ltd在NMRC转化与临床研究旗舰项目（项目编号NMRC/TCR/003/2008）下提供支持。
