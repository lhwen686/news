Exercise therapy reduces knee osteoarthritis risk after ACL surgery

本研究揭示了前交叉韧带（ACL）重建术后，基于运动的康复和教育对预防早发性膝骨关节炎（OA）的关键作用。ACL重建旨在恢复膝关节稳定性，但术后约一半患者面临不满意的膝关节健康状况，甚至在40岁前发展为OA。核心机制在于，科学、渐进的运动疗法，如物理治疗师指导的方案，能有效强化膝关节周围肌肉群，改善关节生物力学，从而降低对关节软骨的异常应力，延缓或预防软骨退变。这不仅提升了关节的稳定性和功能，也直接改善了患者的疼痛和生活质量。该研究挑战了运动有害软骨的传统观念，为年轻患者的长期膝关节健康提供了有力的临床证据。其行业影响深远，有望更新国际临床指南，推广低成本、易实施的康复方案，惠及全球ACL术后高风险人群，减少早发性膝骨关节炎的社会和经济负担。

英文原文

Original
Exercise-based rehabilitation and education can reduce the chance of knee osteoarthritis following ACL reconstruction in people aged under 40, as well as improve pain and quality of life, a study by La Trobe University researchers has found. Around half of people are dissatisfied with their knee health two years after ACL reconstruction, and within a decade half develop early-onset knee osteoarthritis before the age of 40. Clinical guidelines recommend exercise-based rehabilitation with education after an ACL operation, but patients are often provided with care that does not follow these guidelines. Many patients and healthcare providers also believe that exercise is harmful for knee cartilage. The La Trobe SUPER-Knee clinical trial was the first to assess the effectiveness of exercise-based rehabilitation and education beyond nine months of an ACL operation – when a full recovery is expected but often not achieved. Researchers from the La Trobe Sport and Exercise Medicine Research Center studied 184 people between the ages of 18 and 40 with persistent knee problems between one and three years after ACL reconstruction, comparing a progressive exercise therapy program to a single education session. The results have now been published in the Annals of Internal Medicine. Lead researcher Associate Professor Adam Culvenor said that when participants followed the four-month physiotherapist-led exercise program they showed improvements in outcomes and muscle strength without any deterioration of knee cartilage and early osteoarthritis. Currently we don't have any high-quality evidence for what treatments should be offered to young people at risk of early-onset knee osteoarthritis. Our SUPER-Knee study changes this, and addresses a globally recognized research priority. Our study showed physiotherapist-supervised exercise-therapy and education is safe and should be recommended to improve the knee health of young people at high risk of early-onset osteoarthritis following ACL reconstruction. The evidence generated by the SUPER-Knee trial can be used to update international clinical practice guidelines for rehabilitation following ACL reconstruction and could be a lifeline for young people struggling after serious knee injuries. The intervention is ready to be used by local health professionals with minimal specialized equipment or staff training." Adam Culvenor, Associate Professor, La Trobe University Ruby Cumming tore her ACL when she was 24, playing AFL in 2019. She had a reconstruction within six weeks as she wanted to get back on the footy field as quickly as possible. However, rehabilitation has lasted years and she never returned to footy. "I thought if I had the surgery and did the rehab I'd be able to get back to normal. After surgery impacted my entire life – not just footy – I changed my outlook and decided I wanted to avoid re-injuring my knee ever again so haven't played contact sport since," Ruby said. "Experiencing regular pain, not being able to walk, getting public transport to work and living independently all became really challenging for me." Ruby said she was diligent with her rehab, visiting a physio regularly and doing her exercises daily and then weekly, but she continued to experience daily pain for 3-4 years at varying levels. "When I joined the SUPER-Knee program and did weekly physio-supervised rehab, this improved my pain significantly," Ruby said.
