深度解析

此项研究深入揭示了网络欺凌与青少年心理健康问题之间的核心病理生理学机制，即“纵向平行中介模型”。它超越了单纯的因果关联，阐明了网络欺凌如何通过引发“孤独感”和“情绪调节困难”这两种关键的心理中介变量，进而导致青少年出现焦虑等“情绪问题”以及“品行问题”。具体而言，遭遇网络欺凌显著增加了青少年的孤独感，并削弱了其有效管理情绪的能力。这些心理障碍作为“桥梁”，将网络欺凌的负面体验转化为长期的情绪和行为困扰。这种机制的揭示具有重要的临床和公共卫生意义。在临床实践中，它指导精神卫生专业人员和心理咨询师在干预网络欺凌受害者时，应将重点放在减轻其孤独感和提升其情绪调节技能上，而非仅关注自尊问题。在公共卫生和教育领域，它强调了在网络欺凌事件发生后，学校、家庭和社会应提供有针对性的心理支持和技能培训，以阻断或减弱这些关键的中介路径，从而有效预防和缓解青少年长期心理健康问题的发生。该研究为开发更精准、有效的干预策略提供了科学依据。

Young people who experienced cyberbullying were more likely to develop emotional and behavioral problems over the following six months if the experience led to feelings of loneliness and difficulties managing their emotions, a study shows. The findings, published in the Journal of Affective Disorders, highlight the importance of supporting young people's mental health after cyberbullying by helping them feel less isolated and supporting their emotional well-being. The work is The University of Edinburgh study is among the first to systematically examine the mechanisms linking online bullying and teenage mental health. Around two-thirds of children worldwide report an increase in cyberbullying, with one in two saying they don't know how to get the right support. Longitudinal Parallel Mediation Model for Emotional Problems. Credit: Journal of Affective Disorders (2026). DOI: 10.1016/j.jad.2026.122151 Researchers surveyed 501 adolescents to test whether more severe cyberbullying was associated with greater emotional and conduct problems later and, if so, why. They examined the results to investigate which emotional responses had the most influence in linking online bullying with later emotional consequences. The team examined data from two Chinese senior high schools. China is particularly important as an example because of a high prevalence of cyberbullying, the researchers say. The researchers analyzed questionnaires that surveyed adolescents ages 15 to 18 about their online experiences. The research used the European Cyberbullying Intervention Project Questionnaire. The surveys, which were repeated after six months, also measured loneliness, emotion regulation difficulties, self-esteem and emotional and conduct problems. Findings showed that loneliness and difficulties managing emotions appeared to have more influence than self-esteem issues in explaining why teenagers who experience online bullying were more likely to report later emotional and behavioral difficulties. Problems regulating emotions were a significant pathway to other emotional issues such as anxiety, while not being able to control impulses had a significant effect on the risk of conduct problems. This was the case even when other factors such as age and gender were taken into account. Yixuan Li, doctoral candidate at the University of Edinburgh's School of Health in Social Science, who led the study, said, "Cyberbullying victimization can have serious consequences for young people's mental health, but our findings suggest that what happens afterwards also matters. Adolescents subjected to cyberbullying may feel increasingly lonely and may struggle to manage difficult emotions, which may help explain why some go on to experience emotional and behavioral difficulties. The clear message is that supporting young people after cyberbullying is important." The study builds on the team's previous research that brought together evidence from 125 studies involving more than 260,000 participants from 33 countries. The findings identified factors that influence the lasting impact of cyberbullying on teenagers' mental health. The current study identifies which of those pathways appear to play the most important role in linking cyberbullying with later emotional and behavioral difficulties. The findings provide a more nuanced understanding of where prevention and intervention efforts may be most effective, the researchers say.
