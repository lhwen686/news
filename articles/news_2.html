深度解析

这篇综述深入探讨了口腔鳞状细胞癌（OSCC）局部药物递送系统的发展与挑战，核心概念在于精准与个体化治疗。其医学机制旨在通过将药物直接作用于病灶，提高肿瘤局部药物浓度，降低全身毒副作用。然而，口腔环境的特殊性，如唾液清除、酶降解、机械应力（咀嚼、说话）以及放化疗导致的口干症和黏膜损伤，构成了巨大的“硬性限制”，严重影响药物的驻留时间和生物利用度。文章强调，未来的制剂开发必须克服这些挑战，确保药物在动态口腔环境下保持稳定性和有效递送。 行业影响方面，该研究推动了口腔癌治疗从“一刀切”模式向“精准医疗”方向发展。它不仅聚焦于生物材料与药剂学的创新（如黏膜黏附薄膜、可注射水凝胶），更强调了制剂的“转化成熟度”，即从实验室到临床应用的实用性、可生产性及患者耐受性。这促使药物研发更加注重临床可行性，而非单纯的技术先进性。对口干症等伴随病症的考量，也提升了对患者治疗全周期管理的关注。通过匹配递送系统与药物、肿瘤和患者具体情况，有望显著提高OSCC的治疗效果，减轻患者痛苦，并为全球癌症发病率和死亡率的降低做出贡献。

DOI: 10.1016/j.ejpb.2026.115114 A new review by a team of researchers from Indonesia, Japan and China, led by Professor Nurhasni Hasan from the Faculty of Pharmacy at Hasanuddin University in Indonesia, examines how emerging local drug delivery systems could be designed to overcome barriers to treating oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), which is one of the most common malignancies affecting the oral cavity and head and neck region and contributes significantly to the global burden of cancer-related morbidity and mortality. Treating oral cancer with drugs delivered directly to the lesion site is a promising approach. This requires that the medicine remain at the cancer site long enough to provide sustained treatment, increasing drug exposure to the tumor while reducing unwanted exposure throughout the body. But there is a fundamental challenge: The mouth is constantly working against the medicine, creating what the authors describe as stringent "hard constraints" for effective local drug delivery. Saliva can dilute and wash away medicines, while enzymes, chewing, speaking and tongue movement can degrade or dislodge drug-delivery systems used for treatment. The review was published in the European Journal of Pharmaceutics and Biopharmaceutics on Aug. 1, 2026. It brings together different approaches to local OSCC treatment, including mucoadhesive films and patches, oral sprays, in situ gels and injectable hydrogels. Rather than treating these technologies as one-size-fits-all solutions, the researchers emphasize that the best delivery system may depend on the characteristics of both the drug and the tumor. "There is a tendency to think of drug delivery as a problem that can be solved by developing a better formulation," Hasan said. "However, the bigger question is whether the formulation is appropriate for that particular clinical situation." Depending on its shape and location, a flat, accessible lesion would be suitable for mucoadhesive films that remain attached to the mucosa while delivering the drug. However, irregular or infiltrative lesions may benefit from gels or injectable hydrogels that can conform to complex tissue surfaces and provide more sustained drug exposure by delivering the drug to deeper layers. Sprays are comparatively convenient to apply but have shorter residence times because of salivary clearance. Moreover, the oral environment also undergoes changes during cancer treatment. Radiotherapy and chemotherapy often result in xerostomia, or dry mouth, and mucosal damage, affecting hydration, lubrication, drug release and adhesion. An increase in salivary flow leads to accelerated dilution and clearance, indicating that formulations tested under simplified laboratory conditions may not perform the same way in patients. The researchers state that a more realistic evaluation of local drug-delivery systems is required. They emphasize that future studies should focus on how well formulations remain attached under salivary flow and mechanical stress, maintain stability amid changes in pH and enzymatic activity, and deliver drugs consistently to tumor margins. Additionally, the review assessed the translational maturity of different types of formulations. "A sophisticated technology has limited clinical value if it is difficult to manufacture, administer or tolerate. These practical considerations must guide formulation development from the start," Hasan said. Mucoadhesive films and patches currently appear to have stronger near-term potential because similar buccal delivery formats already have regulatory and manufacturing precedents Injectable hydrogels, on the other hand, offer promising options for irregular lesions but face additional challenges involving manufacturing, gelation, degradation, clinical application and long-term biocompatibility. Notably, these technologies are not yet established treatments for OSCC. The review provides a framework for moving promising approaches toward clinical use by matching the delivery system to the drug, tumor and conditions experienced by each individual patient. Taken together, the researchers suggest that the next generation of local oral cancer therapies should be designed to keep the right amount of drug at the right place for the right length of time, not merely to deliver drugs. "The goal should be to make local treatment more predictable. We need delivery systems that researchers can evaluate consistently and clinicians can realistically use," Hasan concluded. More information Muhammad Ruslin et al, Local drug delivery for oral squamous cell carcinoma under oral cavity, European Journal of Pharmaceutics and Biopharmaceutics (2026).
