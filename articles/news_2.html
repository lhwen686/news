深度解析

文章介绍的PinkDx阴道拭子检测代表了子宫内膜癌早期诊断领域的一项重要进展。当前子宫内膜癌的诊断主要依赖经阴道超声及侵入性的子宫内膜活检，后者不仅痛苦，且存在约30%的取样失败率。面对子宫内膜癌发病率和死亡率的上升，亟需更简便、高效的筛查方法。 PinkDx检测的核心医学机制在于利用癌细胞脱落的肿瘤游离DNA（ctDNA）或遗传物质片段。子宫内膜癌细胞死亡后，其携带特异性肿瘤“指纹”的DNA片段会释放到阴道分泌物中。该技术原理属于非侵入性分子诊断范畴，通过简单的阴道拭子采集这些微量DNA，再经由高敏感度的分子生物学技术进行检测和分析，从而识别癌症信号。 这项技术一旦验证成功，将对临床实践产生深远影响。它有望成为异常出血女性的首选筛查工具，显著减少不必要的侵入性活检，改善患者体验，降低并发症风险。其高敏感性（目标90%）将确保大部分癌症病例得以捕获，而合适的特异性则能有效控制假阳性。该方法促进了癌症早期诊断，并体现了精准医疗趋势下非侵入性生物标志物在肿瘤筛查中的巨大潜力，有望重塑子宫内膜癌的诊断临床路径，提升患者依从性和预后。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Pixabay/CC0 Public Domain A simple vaginal swab may one day help women with abnormal bleeding learn whether they have endometrial cancer without first going through a painful biopsy. The test, from California-based PinkDx, is being studied at 16 U.S. sites, The Washington Post reported. It's meant for women who already have symptoms, such as bleeding after menopause. Endometrial cancer starts in the lining of the uterus. Doctors often check for it with a transvaginal ultrasound, which uses a probe in the vagina to measure that lining. They may also do an endometrial biopsy, scraping out a small tissue sample through a thin tube passed into the uterus. Both can be painful. Biopsies also fail to collect enough tissue about 30% of the time in women past menopause, The Post reported. Sometimes the uterine lining is too thin, or the cervix is too narrow for the instrument. Meanwhile, endometrial cancer death rates are on the rise. That's partly because aggressive forms of the disease are becoming more common, The Post noted. "Of all the cancers, this is one that is getting more and more cases being diagnosed every year," Dr. June Hou, a gynecologic oncologist at Columbia University Irving Medical Center in New York City, told The Post. She's leading the PinkDx study there. Dying cancer cells in the uterus shed bits of genetic material that carry the tumor's fingerprint. These fragments can drift down into the vagina, where a swab taken by a clinician can collect them for lab testing. In an early test of 27 women having a hysterectomy, PinkDx found that signal even in swabs from the vaginal opening. Dr. Nicolas Wentzensen, deputy director of the Clinical Genetics Branch at the National Cancer Institute, told The Post that the first effort was very small. Its findings still need to be confirmed in a larger, independent group, he said. The current study plans to enroll about 3,000 women and is expected to wrap up this year. Company officials told The Post that early results are on target. The goal is for the swab to catch at least 90% of cancers. It also needs to correctly rule out cancer in at least half of women who don't have it, which limits false alarms. If those numbers hold, Wentzensen said, the swab could become a first step that spares "half of the population from doing the biopsy." Women who test positive would likely still need a biopsy. Those who test negative should still follow up with their doctor to evaluate other causes of their symptoms. Because PinkDx plans to run the test only in its own lab, it doesn't need U.S. Food and Drug Administration approval, The Post reported. The company expects to finish certification with the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services by early 2027. That would allow the swab to reach patients by mid-2027.
