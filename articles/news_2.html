研究表明，冒险行为先于饮酒量增加，而非反之

Research shows risk-taking behavior precedes increased alcohol consumption, not vice versa

深度解析

这项研究对酒精使用障碍（AUD）的核心病理生理学提供了新见解，发现特定的冒险决策模式，而非自我控制或奖赏驱动行为，在酒精消费量增加之前出现。这表明某些认知偏差，如混合赌博情境中冒险倾向升高及损失情境中冒险倾向降低，以及信息采样偏差减少，可能是AUD患者饮酒量波动的早期预警信号或潜在的神经认知脆弱性。 技术上，研究创新性地采用了基于智能手机的游戏化任务进行一年期纵向评估。这种方法实现了在自然环境中对认知功能和行为的生态瞬时评估（EMA），克服了传统自评或实验室测试的局限，提供了实时、客观且精细的数据，以准确捕捉现实生活中的波动。 临床意义在于，这些发现为未来开发精准、实时的AUD干预策略提供了方向。通过持续监测患者的决策模式，当识别出高风险行为时，可及时触发个性化预警和支持，从而在饮酒量显著增加之前进行预防性干预。这有望将AUD管理从被动治疗转向主动预防，显著改善患者预后，并为减少全球AUD相关的残疾和死亡率提供新途径。

(A) The task battery consisted of four tasks: a response inhibition task, a working memory task, a risk-taking task and an information sampling task. Alcohol consumption was measured using questionnaires. (B) N = 603 participants with mostly mild to moderate alcohol use disorder (AUD) completed each task every four weeks (s1-s13, 7.4 times on average) and questionnaires every two days for a period of one year. Credit: Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging / Zech et al. A study has found that increases in risk-taking behavior in people with mild to moderate alcohol use disorder (AUD) precede increases in alcohol consumption, not vice versa. This research is the first to predict changes in real-life drinking longitudinally using gamified smartphone-based decision-making tasks. The findings from the study, appearing in Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging, open up the possibility of future targeted and timely interventions for people with AUD. AUD is a major contributor to global disability and mortality. Cross-sectional studies have linked AUD to reduced cognitive control and heightened risky decision-making. However, whether these cognitive-behavioral alterations are a consequence or a precursor of changes in drinking was previously unknown. This study clarifies the temporal direction of these effects. The investigators conducted a one-year longitudinal study in individuals with mostly mild to moderate AUD who may fluctuate substantially in their drinking over time. Using smartphone-based assessments and gamified experiments, the researchers tracked participants' drinking behavior, cognitive control and decision-making. "We found that monthly fluctuations in aspects of decision-making predicted subsequent alcohol consumption," explains co-lead investigator Dr. Hilmar Zech, postdoctoral research fellow at the University Hospital Würzburg and Technische Universität Dresden, Germany. "Specifically, participants shifted to higher monthly alcohol consumption when their risk-taking was higher in a mixed gambling context and lower in a loss context in the preceding month. Further, when information sampling biases decreased, participants consumed more alcohol in the subsequent month." Co-lead investigator Dr. Lorenz Deserno, associate professor for neuroscience in developmental psychiatry and principal investigator of the Computational and Developmental Psychiatry Lab at the University Hospital Würzburg, Germany, adds, "This temporal direction was specific and was not observed vice versa, even after accounting for the amount of previous drinking. The findings also showed that fluctuations in self-control (i.e., response inhibition and working memory) and reward-driven behavior (i.e., risk-taking for rewards) were not associated with fluctuations in drinking." Editor-in-chief of Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging Cameron S. Carter, M.D., of the University of California Irvine School of Medicine, comments, "Decision-making and cognitive control cannot be accurately examined through interviews or self-reports and instead require experimental tasks. To address real-life fluctuations, the researchers of this study developed smartphone-based versions of experimental tasks. The findings of this longitudinal assessment suggest that smartphone-based gamified tasks show promise in identifying periods of heightened risk in people with AUD, paving the way for mechanism-based, real-time interventions." While there are no immediate treatment implications, these findings using smartphone-based and gamified measurement methods to link certain key aspects of risky decision-making to subsequent increases in real-life drinking could guide the development of early detection and preventive interventions such as warning signals for high-risk periods to deliver targeted and personalized support at just the right time. More information Hilmar Zech et al., Fluctuations in Real-Life Drinking in Alcohol Use Disorder: A 1-Year Longitudinal Study Using Gamified Tasks of Decision Making and Cognitive Control, Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging (2026). DOI: 10.1016/j.bpsc.2026.07.019
