该文章的核心专业概念在于强调了极端高温作为一种日益严峻的公共卫生挑战，已上升为医疗机构紧急准备工作的优先事项。其医学机制在于，热相关疾病（如中暑）可迅速导致机体多器官功能衰竭和患者病情急剧恶化，这种危重状态需要即时、资源密集型的高水平护理监测和严格遵循临床通用准则的标准护理治疗。护理人员在识别早期预警信号、实施降温措施、管理并发症以及维持生命体征方面发挥着不可替代的作用。从行业影响来看，文章提出，充分的护士配备和支持性的工作环境不再是可选项，而是构建医疗系统韧性的必要应急基础设施。这意味着医疗机构需将对护理人员的投资（包括人员招聘、培训和工作保障）直接整合到灾害准备规划中，以确保在高温导致的患者激增时，医疗系统仍能维持其组织能力和资源，保障患者安全和护理质量，从而避免因人员不足导致的不良患者结局，包括死亡率的上升。这种前瞻性的策略对于应对气候变化带来的日益频繁和严重的公共卫生威胁至关重要。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Unsplash/CC0 Public Domain Hospitals should make nurse staffing and supportive nurse work environments a core part of extreme heat emergency preparedness, according to a new editorial from Penn Nursing's Center for Health Outcomes and Policy Research (CHOPR), as record-breaking temperatures and heat-related emergency department visits continue to rise across the United States. The editorial appears in Public Health Nursing. Senior author Eileen T. Lake, Ph.D., RN, FAAN, the Edith Clemmer Steinbright professor in gerontology, professor of nursing and sociology, and CHOPR associate director, and co-authors Domenique Villani, MPH, RN, a CHOPR T32 predoctoral fellow, and Ysabella Perez, B.A., a recent Penn graduate and CHOPR research assistant, argue that building health system climate resilience requires ensuring bedside nurses have the organizational capacity and resources needed to deliver timely care and continuous surveillance during heat events. This research builds on CHOPR research that establishes the link between nurse staffing and the work environment and myriad patient outcomes, including mortality. The authors emphasize that heat-related illness management requires immediate, resource-intensive nursing surveillance to prevent complications including multiorgan failure and rapid patient deterioration. Insufficient staffing and inadequate support can severely limit a health system's ability to deliver standard-of-care treatments during sudden volume surges caused by heat waves. To prepare for increasing environmental public health emergencies, the editorial outlines actionable recommendations across research, education, practice and policy, urging health system leaders to incorporate nurse staffing and workplace environment investments directly into disaster preparedness planning. "Extreme heat is no longer just a public health issue—it is an immediate hospital preparedness priority that requires treating nurse staffing and supportive work environments as essential emergency infrastructure," the authors emphasize. More information Domenique Villani et al, How Nursing Can Strengthen Hospital Preparedness for Extreme Heat, Public Health Nursing (2026).
