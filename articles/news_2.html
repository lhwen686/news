辛辛那提科学家培养出具有自我发育神经细胞的更大肠道类器官

Cincinnati scientists grow larger gut organoids with self-developing nerve cells

这篇新闻报道的核心突破在于利用3D打印支架和“限制性培养系统”（CCS）技术，显著提升了功能性人体肠道类器官的生产效率、规模和复杂性。在医学机制上，CCS系统通过物理限制促使诱导多能干细胞（iPSCs）分化形成的球形类器官融合生长成厘米级的管状结构，并奇特地自发形成功能性神经系统，模拟了真实肠道的神经肌肉功能。这种自组织能力是生物工程与再生医学的关键进步。

其行业影响深远。首先，类器官的快速、大规模生产克服了以往研究中组织量不足的瓶颈，为再生医学提供了充足的“补丁”材料，有望用于修复小肠、胃或结肠的损伤，减少甚至避免婴幼儿和儿童对全器官移植的需求。其次，具有完整神经网络的类器官模型，能更准确地模拟人体生理和病理过程，极大促进了神经发育障碍等疾病的研究，并为新药筛选和口服药物毒性评估提供了更精确的体外平台。这项技术不仅是生物制造的飞跃，也为器官芯片和个性化医疗的未来奠定了基础，使其向人体临床试验又迈进了一大步。
Original Text
Thanks to special 3D-printed scaffolding trays designed by experts at Cincinnati Children's, researchers can now produce larger versions of functional human gut organoids twice as fast as previous methods-and these organoids grow their own nerve cells.

This improved technology could help accelerate production of human mini-organ tissues that are large enough to be useful in patching damage or restoring diminished functions of a person's small intestine, stomach or colon. Such tissues also would be valuable for future disease studies and to more accurately evaluate organ damage risks linked to oral medications.

Details of the project were published May 22, 2026, in Nature Biomedical Engineering. The new production system was designed and tested by a team led by staff investigator Holly Poling, PhD, senior author, Maxime Mahe, PhD, and a team of 17 other scientists from Cincinnati Children's and Nantes Université in France.

Using their new "confined culture system" (CCS), the team grew small intestine, colon, and stomach organoids from tiny spherical forms into centimeter-scale tubular forms nearly 10 times larger than previous methods. Also, unlike methods that require a complex effort to introduce nerve cells, these organoids develop a nervous system on their own.

By reaching transplantation maturity twice as fast and developing their own functional nerves, these organoids demonstrate how engineering principles can drive biological innovation. Our confined culture system is more than a production method; it's a scalable, flexible platform for building complex human tissues." Holly Poling, PhD, senior author

New production system prompts faster growth

Experts at Cincinnati Children's Center for Stem Cell & Organoid Medicine (CuSTOM) have been leaders at making miniature versions of digestive system organs for more than 15 years, steadily improving the sophistication of the lab-grown tissues. More recently, the team has been working on methods to make enough customized tissue to transplant into patients to help patch organ damage or restore diminished specialized functions.

The new method uses 3D printing technology to make tray-like scaffolding molds from surgical resin, then filling the molds with degassed polydimethylsiloxane-a flexible rubber-like type of silicone.

The new trays contain grooves designed to confine a collection of sphere-shaped organoids into a row, which encourages the spheroids to fuse together and mature. The fusions occur within a special mix of nutrients and other ingredients that support initial growth from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) into more complex organoids.

By day six, the discrete spheroids develop into unified constructs along the grooves of the trays. These are moved into another hydrogel medium for continued growth for another eight days.

By day 14, the organoid constructs have produced all the cell types and structures that previously required 28 days to achieve. These tissues are then transplanted into rodents that are genetically modified to minimize rejection risk.

All of the transplanted tissues engrafted successfully in the rodents, the co-authors state. After growing in the rodents, the team produced as much as 8 cm of functioning small intestine tissue, compared to approximately 1 cm of tissue using previous protocols. Not only were the structures much larger than previous methods, but now their neuromuscular function was also similar to native human tissue, representing a major advance.

"We are now able not only to generate complex gastrointestinal organoids at scale, but also to guide their differentiation into functional tissues with integrated enteric neuronal networks," Mahe says. "By leveraging a defined growth environment, the intrinsic self-organization capacity of the cells drives the formation of tissue structures that closely resemble the human gastrointestinal tract."

Jim Wells, PhD, a study co-author and chief scientific director at CuSTOM says the new technology overcomes key barriers to scale and function in organoid research and biomanufacturing.

"This platform's simplicity, reproducibility, and versatility make it accessible for widespread adoption," Wells says. "In addition, the emergence of a self-organized nervous system within these organoids is particularly important for further studies of neurodevelopmental disorders."

Another step closer to human clinical trials

Michael Helmrath, MD, a surgeon-scientist at Cincinnati Children's who co-directs CuSTOM, has been working for more than a decade to develop intestine organoids sophisticated enough for transplantation in human patients.

In 2017, Helmrath and colleagues demonstrated how to combine neural crest cells with intestinal tissue cells in a layered process to make the first human organoids with nerve function. His team also showed how intestine organoids could be grown larger by implanting them in a mouse to provide a blood supply. Ever since, intestine organoids have been getting more sophisticated, including versions with immune cells in addition to the specialized organ cells and nerves.

Now the new process-involving rats instead of mice-produces even larger amounts of tissue.

"It is still not possible to grow complete, full-sized human organs in some sort of tank, but research like this has produced significant amounts of tissue that can be matched directly to individual patients," Helmrath says. "We believe such tissues, once transplanted, would further grow and multiply as part of the patient's own organ to restore functions."

More research and development is needed before "CCS organoids" will be ready for human clinical trials, Helmrath says. But if successes continue, organoid medicine may allow more infants and children with dysfunctional organs to be treated without ever needing a full organ transplant.

About the study

In addition to Poling, Mahe, Wells and Helmrath, Cincinnati Children's co-authors included Akaljot Singh, Garrett Fisher, Konrad Thorner, Praneet Chaturvedi, Kalpana Nattamai, Kalpana Srivastava, Matthew Batie, Nicole Brown, Taylor Hausfeld, Amy Pitstick, Riccardo Barrile, Christopher Mayhew, and Takanori Takebe. Three experts with Inserm and Nantes Université also were co-authors.

Funding sources for the research included the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (U01 DK103117, P30 DK078392) and the Agence Nationale de la Recherche (ANR-17-CE14-0021, ANR-21-CE14-0017).

这项改进的技术有助于加速人体微型器官组织的生产，使其足够大以用于修补损伤或恢复小肠、胃或结肠的受损功能。此类组织对于未来的疾病研究以及更准确地评估口服药物相关的器官损伤风险也具有重要价值。

该项目的详细内容于2026年5月22日发表在《自然生物医学工程》杂志上。这个新的生产系统由资深作者、高级研究员Holly Poling博士、Maxime Mahe博士以及来自辛辛那提儿童医院和法国南特大学的17位其他科学家组成的团队设计并测试。

利用他们的新型“限制性培养系统”（CCS），该团队将小肠、结肠和胃类器官从微小的球形生长成厘米级的管状结构，其大小几乎是以前方法的10倍。此外，与需要复杂努力引入神经细胞的方法不同，这些类器官能够自行发展出神经系统。

资深作者Holly Poling博士表示：“通过以两倍的速度达到移植成熟度并自行发展出功能性神经，这些类器官展示了工程学原理如何推动生物创新。我们的限制性培养系统不仅仅是一种生产方法；它是一个可扩展、灵活的平台，用于构建复杂的人体组织。”

新的生产系统促进更快生长

辛辛那提儿童医院干细胞与类器官医学中心（CuSTOM）的专家在制造消化系统器官微型版本方面已经有超过15年的领先经验，并持续改进实验室培养组织的复杂性。最近，该团队一直在研究如何生产足够定制化的组织，以移植到患者体内，帮助修补器官损伤或恢复受损的特化功能。

新方法利用3D打印技术，使用手术树脂制作托盘状支架模具，然后将脱气的聚二甲基硅氧烷（一种柔性橡胶状硅酮）填充到模具中。

新托盘包含的凹槽旨在将一系列球形类器官限制成一排，这促使球体融合并成熟。融合发生在一个特殊的营养混合物和其他成分中，这些物质支持诱导多能干细胞（iPSCs）初步生长成更复杂的类器官。

到第六天，离散的球体沿着托盘的凹槽发展成统一的结构。这些结构被转移到另一种水凝胶培养基中，继续生长八天。

到第14天，类器官结构已经产生了以前需要28天才能实现的所有细胞类型和结构。然后将这些组织移植到经过基因改造以最大程度降低排斥风险的啮齿动物体内。

共同作者指出，所有移植的组织都在啮齿动物体内成功植入。在啮齿动物体内生长后，该团队生产了多达8厘米的功能性小肠组织，而以前的方案只能产生约1厘米的组织。这些结构不仅比以前的方法大得多，而且其神经肌肉功能也与天然人体组织相似，代表了一项重大进展。

Mahe表示：“我们现在不仅能够大规模生成复杂的胃肠道类器官，而且能够引导它们分化成具有整合肠神经元网络的功能性组织。通过利用明确定义的生长环境，细胞固有的自组织能力推动了与人类胃肠道密切相似的组织结构的形成。”

CuSTOM研究共同作者兼首席科学主任Jim Wells博士表示，这项新技术克服了类器官研究和生物制造中规模和功能的关键障碍。

Wells说：“这个平台的简洁性、可重复性和多功能性使其易于广泛采用。此外，这些类器官中自组织神经系统的出现对于进一步研究神经发育障碍尤为重要。”

距离人体临床试验又近了一步

辛辛那提儿童医院的外科医生兼科学家Michael Helmrath医学博士是CuSTOM的联席主任，他十多年来一直致力于开发足够复杂的肠道类器官，以便移植到人类患者体内。

2017年，Helmrath及其同事展示了如何通过分层过程将神经嵴细胞与肠道组织细胞结合，从而制造出第一个具有神经功能的人体类器官。他的团队还展示了如何通过将其植入小鼠体内以提供血液供应来使肠道类器官生长得更大。从那时起，肠道类器官变得越来越复杂，包括除了特化器官细胞和神经外还含有免疫细胞的版本。

现在，涉及大鼠而非小鼠的新工艺生产出更大数量的组织。

Helmrath说：“目前仍不可能在某种容器中培养出完整、全尺寸的人体器官，但像这样的研究已经产生了大量可以直接与个体患者匹配的组织。我们相信，这些组织一旦移植，将作为患者自身器官的一部分进一步生长和繁殖，以恢复功能。”

Helmrath表示，在“CCS类器官”准备好进行人体临床试验之前，还需要更多的研究和开发。但如果成功继续，类器官医学可能使更多患有功能障碍器官的婴幼儿和儿童得到治疗，而无需进行全面的器官移植。
