New genetic risk score uncovers hidden type 1 diabetes cases

深度解析

该研究解决了糖尿病诊断中的一个关键难题：当青年人成熟期发病型糖尿病（MODY）的初步基因检测结果呈阴性时，如何有效区分MODY与非典型1型糖尿病（T1D）。MODY通常由单一基因突变引起，而T1D则是一种复杂的、多基因介导的自身免疫性疾病。本研究的核心创新在于引入了“遗传风险评分”（Genetic Risk Score, GRS），其专业原理在于整合了所有已知的与1型糖尿病相关的多个遗传风险位点，从而对个体罹患T1D的综合遗传倾向进行量化评估。这种多基因分析方法弥补了传统单基因MODY检测的局限性，使得在MODY基因检测阴性患者中，能够识别出那些因多基因累积效应而实际患有非典型T1D的个体。 这项研究对临床实践产生了深远影响。首先，它显著提升了诊断的精确性，确保患者能够获得与其特定糖尿病类型相符的个性化治疗方案（例如，某些MODY亚型可能仅需口服降糖药，而T1D则必须依赖胰岛素治疗）。其次，GRS作为一种经济且操作简便的辅助诊断工具，能够有效减少不必要且昂贵的后续检查，从而优化医疗资源配置。更重要的是，它为长期处于诊断不确定性的患者和临床医生提供了明确的诊断依据，减轻了患者的心理负担，并促进了更精准、更高效的医疗护理。该方法被英国国家医疗服务体系（NHS）采纳，也充分证明了将前沿基因组学研究成果快速转化为临床应用，直接改善患者预后的巨大潜力。

英文原文

Original
Incorporating a genetic risk score into standard testing for a genetic form of diabetes which affects young people could identify hidden type 1 diabetes in around one in five patients with a negative genetic test. Researchers at the University of Exeter studied more than 1,100 people who were being treated with insulin and were referred for genetic testing for Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young (MODY). Often diagnosed before the age of 25, MODY is caused by a change in a single gene, and has a strong inherited component. To be diagnosed, a blood sample is sent off to a lab and tested against a panel of genes known to cause MODY. However, around 80% of patients have a negative test and the cause of their diabetes is uncertain. The study was funded by Diabetes UK and the Medical Research Council, and supported by the NIHR Exeter Biomedical Research Centre and the NIHR Exeter Clinical Research Facility. Published in Diabetes Care, the researchers found that applying the type 1 genetic risk score, developed at Exeter, to people whose genetic test came back negative identified that 16 per cent (around 180 patients) do in fact have atypical type 1 diabetes. The genetic risk score is a cheap, simple way to analyze risk of type 1 diabetes, which takes into account all known genetic risk factors. Incorporating the score into routine testing could enable genetic laboratories to provide a diagnosis of type 1 diabetes for patients whose MODY test would previously have returned without an explanation. The approach will now be adopted as part of NHS testing, demonstrating the direct impact of the research on clinical practice and providing answers that were not previously possible. The study was led by Kashyap Patel is an Associate Professor at the University of Exeter, and Consultant Physician in Diabetes and Endocrinology. He said: "In clinic, it's extremely challenging to distinguish between MODY and type 1 diabetes, yet getting the right diagnosis is crucial to getting the right treatment. We found that applying the type 1 genetic risk score to patients who tested negative for MODY is effective in identifying patients with type 1 diabetes, reducing the need for expensive further testing and providing answers to patients." The study was supported by Kevin Colclough, the lead clinical scientist for the Exeter MODY testing service at the Royal Devon University Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust. He said: "This study showcases the strong link between the University of Exeter and the Exeter Genomics Laboratory that enables rapid translation of research findings into improved patient care. We've demonstrated how information on genetic risk can be incorporated into an established NHS genetic testing pathway to provide additional diagnostic information when testing is negative. This will be a significant quality improvement to our service, providing clinicians with a more informative result and helping to reduce diagnostic uncertainty for their patients." The ability to differentiate between 'straight forward type 1' and other more uncommon forms of diabetes is invaluable in managing people with diabetes and to optimize and target their treatment on an individualized basis. Over the years I have been a clinician, there seem to be more grey areas and clinical uncertainties, and using risk scores is really helpful in focussing clinical care – this is a great example using results in the real world." Dr. Alison Evans, Consultant Physician, Diabetes and Endocrinology at Gloucestershire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust Anna Morris, Assistant Director of Research at Diabetes UK, said: "An accurate diagnosis is crucial to ensure that people with diabetes get the right treatment and support. Yet for some people, existing tests do not clearly define the type of diabetes they have. This can leave them without clear answers and affect their care. This research shows how genetic approaches could help identify people with type 1 diabetes, reducing uncertainty for patients and clinicians and helping inform the most appropriate care."

将遗传风险评分纳入对影响年轻人的遗传性糖尿病的标准检测中，可以在约五分之一的基因检测呈阴性的患者中识别出隐藏的1型糖尿病。 埃克塞特大学的研究人员对1100多名正在接受胰岛素治疗并被转诊进行青年人成熟期发病型糖尿病（MODY）基因检测的患者进行了研究。MODY通常在25岁之前被诊断，是由单一基因的改变引起的，并具有强大的遗传成分。要确诊MODY，需要采集血样并送往实验室，针对一系列已知导致MODY的基因进行检测。然而，大约80%的患者检测结果呈阴性，他们的糖尿病病因不确定。 这项研究由英国糖尿病协会和医学研究理事会资助，并得到了NIHR埃克塞特生物医学研究中心和NIHR埃克塞特临床研究设施的支持。研究成果发表在《糖尿病护理》杂志上，研究人员发现，将埃克塞特开发的1型糖尿病遗传风险评分应用于基因检测结果呈阴性的人群后，识别出16%（约180名患者）实际上患有非典型1型糖尿病。 遗传风险评分是一种廉价、简单的分析1型糖尿病风险的方法，它考虑了所有已知的遗传风险因素。 将该评分纳入常规检测可以使基因实验室为那些以前MODY检测结果未给出解释的患者提供1型糖尿病的诊断。该方法现在将被NHS（英国国家医疗服务体系）采纳作为其检测的一部分，这表明了该研究对临床实践的直接影响，并提供了以前无法获得的答案。 这项研究由埃克塞特大学副教授兼糖尿病和内分泌学顾问医师Kashyap Patel领导。他说：“在临床上，区分MODY和1型糖尿病极其具有挑战性，然而，获得正确诊断对于获得正确治疗至关重要。我们发现，将1型糖尿病遗传风险评分应用于MODY检测呈阴性的患者，可以有效识别出1型糖尿病患者，减少了昂贵的进一步检测的需要，并为患者提供了答案。” 这项研究得到了皇家德文大学医疗NHS基金会信托基金埃克塞特MODY检测服务首席临床科学家Kevin Colclough的支持。他说：“这项研究展示了埃克塞特大学与埃克塞特基因组实验室之间的紧密联系，这种联系使得研究成果能够快速转化为改进的患者护理。我们展示了如何将遗传风险信息整合到已有的NHS基因检测路径中，以便在检测结果为阴性时提供额外的诊断信息。这将是我们服务的一项重大质量改进，为临床医生提供更具信息量的结果，并有助于减少患者的诊断不确定性。” 格洛斯特郡医院NHS基金会信托基金的糖尿病和内分泌学顾问医师Alison Evans博士表示：“区分‘典型1型糖尿病’和其他更罕见的糖尿病形式的能力，对于管理糖尿病患者以及根据个体情况优化和针对性治疗是无价的。在我作为临床医生的这些年里，似乎存在更多的灰色地带和临床不确定性，而使用风险评分对于聚焦临床护理非常有帮助——这是一个在现实世界中应用研究成果的绝佳例子。” 英国糖尿病协会研究助理主任Anna Morris说：“准确的诊断对于确保糖尿病患者获得正确的治疗和支持至关重要。然而，对于某些人来说，现有的检测无法明确界定他们所患的糖尿病类型。这可能使他们得不到明确的答案，并影响他们的护理。这项研究表明，遗传学方法如何帮助识别1型糖尿病患者，减少患者和临床医生的不确定性，并有助于制定最合适的护理方案。”
