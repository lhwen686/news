Five research teams secure funding for frontotemporal dementia studies

深度解析

这篇新闻稿详细阐述了基西克家族基金会与米尔肯研究院SPARC合作，资助额颞叶痴呆（FTD）研究的举措。文章核心概念在于通过多学科、创新性的研究方法，共同攻克散发性FTD这一复杂神经退行性疾病的挑战。资助的项目涵盖了从细胞层面（神经元类型特异性脆弱性）、基因组层面（体细胞基因组不稳定性、遗传嵌合体），到免疫调节（干扰素驱动基因IFI44L），以及药物发现（化学蛋白质组学建立药物图谱）等多个关键维度。这些研究旨在深入理解FTD的发病机制，识别新型生物标志物和治疗靶点，进而加速开发有效的诊断和治疗方案。 该计划的行业影响深远。首先，它通过慈善投资，弥补了散发性FTD这一“资源不足”领域的研究空白，支持了高风险、早期但可能带来突破性进展的基础研究。其次，通过构建研究社区、促进跨学科合作和知识共享，加速了科学发现的转化。最终目标是为受FTD影响的患者提供更精准的诊断、更个性化的治疗和更积极的预后。这种模式展示了慈善资本在推动医学前沿、解决重大公共健康问题中的关键作用，特别是在神经退行性疾病领域，为全球相关研究提供了有益的范式。

英文原文

Original
The Kissick Family Foundation, in partnership with the Milken Institute Science Philanthropy's Accelerator for Research and Collaboration (SPARC), today announced five research teams selected to receive a total of $2.5 million through the third funding cycle of the Kissick Family Foundation Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD) Grant Program. The partners also launched the program's fourth funding cycle and are now accepting research proposals. Through this program, the Kissick Family Foundation is investing in research that accelerates foundational discoveries, advances novel treatment options, and ultimately improves outcomes for people living with sporadic FTD. The grant program has selected the following researchers to receive funding, subject to the execution of the relevant grant agreements: Institution: University of California, San Francisco Principal investigator: William W. Seeley, MD Co-investigator: Alex Pollen, PhD Project: Probing neuron type-specific vulnerability to reveal upstream FTLD-TDP pathogenesis Institution: University of Pennsylvania Principal investigator: Michael Guo, MD, PhD Co-investigators: Michael Haney, PhD; Hong Xu, PhD Project: Decoding somatic genome instability as a driver of selective neuronal vulnerability in FTD Institution: Massachusetts General Hospital and Harvard Medical School Principal investigator: Clotilde Lagier-Tourenne, MD, PhD Co-investigator: Liron Bar-Peled, PhD Project: Chemical proteomics to establish a pan-FTD DrugMap Institution: Ulm University Principal investigator: Jochen Weishaupt, MD Co-investigators: Luc Dupuis, PhD, INSERM; Lena Voith von Voithenberg, PhD, German Cancer Research Center Project: Genetic mosaics: The dark matter of FTD pathogenesis Institution: University of California, San Francisco Principal investigator: Jennifer Yokoyama, PhD Project: Interferon-driven gene IFI44L as a powerful modifier of sporadic FTD disease trajectory Together, these projects represent diverse approaches to understanding why FTD develops and progresses differently across individuals. They aim to uncover new biomarkers and therapeutic targets while expanding the scientific foundation for developing more effective treatments. This third cohort joins the research teams supported through the program's first two funding cycles. The Kissick Family Foundation and SPARC have also opened the program's fourth funding cycle, inviting independent researchers whose work is applicable to FTD to review the request for proposals. Letters of intent will be accepted through November 4, 2026. Individual researchers may receive up to $500,000 over two years, inclusive of indirect costs, with awardees expected to be notified in mid-2027. FTD is a complex disease, and we believe getting to treatments and cures will require scientists across disciplines coming together. We're proud to support so many incredible research teams around the world and, more importantly, to have helped build a community where researchers can share ideas and build on one another's successes and failures to move the field forward faster. While it's too late for my dad, who passed away from FTD in 2024, this work gives us hope that we're on the cusp of discoveries that will change the outlook for all the other families affected by this disease." Kasey Kissick Duarte, co-president, Kissick Family Foundation The Kissick Family Foundation's partnership with SPARC began in 2022 with an analysis of the FTD research and funding landscape. That work informed the Milken Institute's November 2023 report, Frontotemporal Dementia: A Giving Smarter Guide, which identified opportunities for focused philanthropic investment to accelerate scientific progress and enrich the FTD research ecosystem. The grant program was created to put those findings into action, with a particular focus on sporadic FTD-an area in which researchers still face significant gaps in understanding disease biology, identifying reliable biomarkers, and developing effective treatments. "Neurodegenerative diseases like FTD can be devastating for people living with the disorders and for their families, who deserve tailored treatments, clearer diagnostic journeys, and improved outlooks," said Sylvie Raver, PhD, managing director of SPARC. "At SPARC, we believe that philanthropic investment is a critical tool to advance these outcomes, through supporting communities of scientists and clinicians conducting high-risk, early-stage research that serves as the foundation for new breakthroughs. Grant programs like these are invaluable for under-resourced fields of study like FTD."
