大规模脑部扫描研究揭示抑郁症患者运动区和视觉区存在意想不到的差异

Large-scale brain scan study reveals unexpected differences in motor and visual regions linked to depression

深度解析

这项研究显著推进了我们对重度抑郁症（MDD）神经生物学基础的理解。通过对超过23,000份大脑扫描进行细致分析，研究人员采用全面的全脑方法，识别出额叶皮层、前扣带皮层和脑岛等关键区域灰质体积减少的一致模式。这些区域在情绪调节、认知加工和内感受方面至关重要，进一步巩固了它们在MDD病理生理学中的既定作用。 尤为重要的是，研究发现运动和视觉皮层较小区域与更高水平的抑郁症相关，这一新发现挑战了传统认知，表明MDD可能涉及更广泛的神经网络，而非仅限于先前认定的情绪和认知回路。这意味着MDD的表征可能包含感觉运动加工的改变，为探讨其多样化症状学开辟了新途径。而与早期研究在基底脑区域方面的不一致，则凸显了数据集特征（特别是患者严重程度）在神经影像学研究中的重要性。从行业角度来看，这些发现至关重要。它们可为开发更精确的神经影像学生物标志物提供信息，以用于早期诊断和严重程度分层。此外，通过阐明更广泛受累的大脑区域，这项研究有望指导靶向神经调控疗法或药物干预的开发，从而迈向更加个性化和有效的MDD患者治疗范式。

Major depressive disorder, commonly referred to as depression, is a debilitating psychiatric disorder estimated to affect roughly 5% of the global population. Depression is characterized by persistent sadness and hopelessness, difficulty concentrating on tasks, a lack of interest in everyday activities and changes in sleep and/or appetite. While past studies have shed light on some of the factors that can contribute to the emergence of depression, the neural processes and brain changes associated with this disorder have not yet been fully elucidated. Better understanding how depression affects the brain could potentially guide the development of new tools and strategies for diagnosing or treating it. Past research has linked depression with changes in various brain regions, including a reduction in the volume of gray matter in the hippocampus and frontal cortex. While earlier studies have provided some interesting insights, their findings were often inconsistent or inconclusive. Researchers at Washington University School of Medicine analyzed thousands of brain scans collected from a population sample, including people with subclinical symptoms. The results of their analyses, published in Nature Mental Health, identified various changes in the brain that appear to be associated with depression, some of which are inconsistent with earlier observations. "Determining how the brain changes in people with depression has been a longstanding scientific goal that many papers have tried to answer," Janine D. Bijsterbosch, the paper's senior author, told Medical Xpress. "Despite substantial existing work, studies designed to identify agreement between previous studies (so-called 'meta-analyses') sometimes struggle to find consistent evidence for depression-related brain changes. Furthermore, prior studies often focus on target brain systems likely to be involved in depression and may therefore overlook findings in other parts of the brain (e.g., as suggested in our prior work)." The team's study is part of an issue focused on Major Depressive Disorder published in Nature Mental Health. Credit: Springer Nature. The analysis of over 23,000 brain scans The main goal of the study by Bijsterbosch and her collaborators was to improve the current understanding of depression and how it is reflected in the brain. To do this, the researchers analyzed six available datasets individually and then compared the results they attained. "We wanted to see if a whole-brain approach using big datasets would help to find more consistent evidence of brain changes linked to depression," explained Bijsterbosch. "We used six big datasets covering the full lifespan that ranged from 185 to 11,000 participants per dataset. Each dataset was analyzed separately to relate summary indices of brain structure and function to depression measures (of severity and personality-based risk). The results were then combined across the six datasets." Consistent with earlier research, the researchers found that depression was linked to a reduction in gray matter in three brain regions: the frontal cortex, anterior cingulate cortex and insula. These areas of the brain are known to play a role in the regulation of emotions, decision-making, motivation and the internal processing of bodily sensations. "A key surprising finding was that smaller brain regions in motor and visual regions were linked to higher levels of depression," said Bijsterbosch. "It has been known that reduced brain size in other (e.g., frontal) parts of the brain is linked to depression, but the location of these new results was surprising because we don't usually link symptoms of depression to sensory processing or motor action." Earlier research had also linked depression to changes in regions in the basal brain, such as the amygdala and hippocampus. Bijsterbosch and her colleagues, however, observed little evidence of this. It is possible that this discrepancy is linked to the population datasets that were used, which do not tend to include patients with high levels of depression severity. Toward a better understanding of depression This study was one of the most detailed and comprehensive explorations of brain changes associated with depression to date. Overall, its findings suggest that depression affects a broad network of brain regions that have been implicated in important mental functions. The team's efforts could soon inform further studies exploring how the uncovered brain changes could relate to specific symptoms of depression. Eventually, they could help paint a clearer picture of this common mental health disorder, potentially also informing the development of promising new diagnostic tools or treatments. "One of the first authors of our paper, Kassandra Hamilton, is currently planning a follow-up study to test whether visual and motor regions encode specific symptoms of depression," added Bijsterbosch. Bijsterbosch）告诉《医学快报》（Medical Xpress）：“确定抑郁症患者大脑如何变化一直是一项长期的科学目标，许多论文都试图解答。” “尽管现有大量工作，但旨在识别先前研究之间一致性的研究（即所谓的‘荟萃分析’）有时难以找到与抑郁症相关的大脑变化的持续性证据。此外，先前的研究通常侧重于可能与抑郁症相关的目标大脑系统，因此可能会忽略大脑其他部分的发现（例如，正如我们先前工作所暗示的）。” 该团队的研究是《自然·精神健康》杂志上关于重度抑郁症的专题之一。图片来源：施普林格·自然（Springer Nature）。 超过23,000份大脑扫描的分析 比斯特博施及其合作者的研究主要目标是增进对抑郁症及其在大脑中反映方式的当前理解。为此，研究人员分别分析了六个可用的数据集，然后比较了他们获得的结果。 比斯特博施解释说：“我们想看看使用大数据集的全脑方法是否有助于找到更多与抑郁症相关的大脑变化的持续性证据。” “我们使用了六个涵盖全生命周期的大数据集，每个数据集的参与者从185到11,000人不等。每个数据集都进行了单独分析，以将大脑结构和功能的汇总指标与抑郁症测量（包括严重程度和基于人格的风险）联系起来。然后将六个数据集的结果进行整合。” 与早期研究一致，研究人员发现抑郁症与三个大脑区域的灰质减少有关：额叶皮层、前扣带皮层和脑岛。已知这些大脑区域在情绪调节、决策、动机以及躯体感觉的内部处理中发挥作用。 比斯特博施说：“一个关键的惊人发现是，运动和视觉区域中较小的大脑区域与更高水平的抑郁症相关。众所周知，大脑其他部分（例如，额叶）的脑容量减少与抑郁症有关，但这些新结果的定位令人惊讶，因为我们通常不将抑郁症症状与感觉处理或运动行为联系起来。” 早期研究也曾将抑郁症与基底脑区域的变化联系起来，例如杏仁核和海马体。然而，比斯特博施及其同事对此观察到的证据甚少。这种差异可能与所使用的人群数据集有关，这些数据集通常不包括重度抑郁症患者。 走向更深入的抑郁症理解 迄今为止，这项研究是对与抑郁症相关的大脑变化进行的最详细和最全面的探索之一。总的来说，其发现表明抑郁症影响着一个广泛的大脑区域网络，这些区域与重要的精神功能有关。 该团队的努力有望很快为进一步的研究提供信息，探讨所揭示的大脑变化如何与抑郁症的具体症状相关联。最终，这些发现可能有助于描绘出这种常见精神健康障碍的更清晰图景，并有可能指导有前景的新诊断工具或治疗方法的开发。 比斯特博施补充说：“我们论文的第一作者之一卡桑德拉·汉密尔顿（Kassandra Hamilton）目前正计划一项后续研究，以测试视觉和运动区域是否编码特定的抑郁症症状。” 本文由英格丽德·法代利（Ingrid Fadelli）撰写，萨迪·哈雷（Sadie Harley）编辑，并经罗伯特·伊根（Robert Egan）事实核查和审阅——是人类精心工作的成果。我们依靠像您这样的读者来维持独立科学新闻的生命力。如果这份报道对您很重要，请考虑捐款（特别是每月捐款）。作为感谢，您将获得一个无广告账户。 发表详情：Kassandra Miyoko Hamilton 等人，《在六个大型人群数据集中建立的抑郁症神经影像学关联》（The neuroimaging correlates of depression established across six large-scale population datasets），《自然·精神健康》（Nature Mental Health）（2026）。DOI: 10.1038/s44220-026-00680-y。期刊信息：《自然·精神健康》（Nature Mental Health）。 © 2026 科学X网络
