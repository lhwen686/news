深度解析

性发育差异（DSDs）是一组复杂的先天性疾病，揭示了人类生物学性别形成并非由单一基因或事件决定，而是一个多阶段、精细调控的级联过程。其核心机制涉及染色体性别、性腺分化、性激素合成与代谢，以及靶细胞对激素的反应。例如，先天性肾上腺皮质增生症（CAH）源于肾上腺皮质类固醇合成酶的缺陷，导致雄激素前体过度积累并转化为雄激素，可能引起46,XX胎儿外生殖器男性化。完全性雄激素不敏感综合征（CAIS）则因雄激素受体基因突变，导致46,XY个体尽管睾丸分泌睾酮，但身体无法识别并响应，从而表现为女性外表。5-α还原酶缺乏症则涉及睾酮向更强效的二氢睾酮（DHT）转化的酶受损，造成出生时生殖器模糊，青春期才出现男性化。此外，性染色体非整倍体（如克氏综合征的XXY、特纳综合征的XO）或Y染色体Sry基因突变（如斯威雅综合征）直接影响性腺的正确分化。 这些病症的深度解析对临床实践和医疗伦理具有深远影响。它强调了对DSDs患者进行早期、准确诊断的重要性，通常需结合基因检测、内分泌评估和影像学检查。同时，呼吁采用多学科团队协作模式，提供包括内分泌、遗传、泌尿生殖外科、心理和社会支持在内的全面、个性化护理。文章还强调了患者知情同意权，尤其是在非紧急情况下对儿童进行不可逆手术的谨慎态度，推动了从传统“矫正”观念向“支持”和“赋能”患者的转变，促进了更具包容性的社会认知和医疗实践。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Katie Rainbow 🏳️‍🌈 from Pexels I teach a university course called Hormones and Behaviour. What surprises students is that biological sex is not produced by a single switch. What surprises me is that many students have never been taught this before. It is basic biology, but it helps explain life. Sex emerges through a sequence of events before birth and continues at puberty. Chromosomes help direct the formation of gonads, the body parts that usually become ovaries or testes. Gonads make hormones. The body then has to respond to those hormones. In most people, these steps flow in the same direction. But in rare cases, they do not. These cases are known as disorders of sex development, more recently called differences of sex development (DSDs). They are not new, but they provide a useful way to understand how bodies develop. DSDs can affect hormone production. Some affect the body's response to hormones. Some affect chromosomes or gonad development. Two people can have a DSD yet have very different bodies, medical needs and life experiences. Each condition reveals what can happen when one step in the normal sequence is altered. Here are five ways sex development can take a different path. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia This DSD affects about 1 in 16,000 births. The adrenal glands, which normally produce small amounts of male hormones, even in females, are hyperactive in this condition. A baby with two X chromosomes and ovaries can therefore be born with external genitalia that look male. Complete androgen insensitivity syndrome In contrast, a child with complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (CAIS) can have XY chromosomes and internal testes that make testosterone, but their body cannot respond to that testosterone. This lack of a response to testosterone keeps the developing fetus in its "default" female state, leading to female external genitalia at birth. Someone with CAIS will develop breasts at puberty. Since their external anatomy is typically female, people with CAIS are usually raised as girls and may only be diagnosed later, often when their periods do not start. CAIS is estimated to affect 1 to 5 in 100,000 live-born females. 5-alpha-reductase deficiency Another condition, called 5-alpha-reductase deficiency, shows how sex development does not finish at birth. The body needs an enzyme called 5-alpha-reductase to turn testosterone into a stronger version of the hormone before birth. When a genetic mutation inactivates this enzyme, an XY baby may be born with genitalia that are read as female and may be raised as a girl. At puberty, however, the normal surge of conventional testosterone masculinizes the body. The condition is rare worldwide, and its exact incidence is unknown, partly because conditions affecting sex development have often been surrounded by silence. In parts of the Dominican Republic, where this condition is known as guevedoces, studies have reported surprisingly high local rates. A rare genetic mutation is more common in these isolated populations, affecting about 1 in 90 males. More recently, some of these communities have begun to recognize guevedoces at birth and raise them as boys. Sex chromosome disorders Usually, females receive one X chromosome from their father and one X from their mother and are therefore XX, while males receive one X chromosome from their mother and the Y chromosome from their father. However, a person with Klinefelter syndrome, affecting 1 in 500–1,000 male births, has XXY chromosomes, causing smaller testes, scant body hair, lower testosterone and reduced fertility. It is often never diagnosed. On the other hand, Turner syndrome, or the loss in females of one of the two X chromosomes, affects 1 in 2,500 female births and impairs growth, puberty and fertility. It can affect physical appearance, for instance causing a wide neck and broad chest, and often comes with health conditions such as hearing loss and kidney problems. Swyer syndrome is rarer: A person has XY chromosomes but a mutation in the Y chromosome disrupts the signal that normally drives testis formation. The gonads do not develop into testes, testosterone remains low and the external and internal anatomy is typically female. These are the clearer examples. Milder sex differences can result from mutations that reduce, rather than completely inactivate, an enzyme or hormone response. For example, non-classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, where some enzyme activity remains. Their effects may be less obvious, and their incidence is often unknown because many cause subtler changes or are never diagnosed. What this does and does not tell us None of this is new. These conditions have been known to scientists for decades. But now genetic testing can identify the mutation behind some cases. Specialist clinics and patient groups have pushed for clearer information for parents, better psychological support for patients and greater caution around irreversible childhood surgery when it is not medically urgent. Recent patient-centered research shows that clear information, good communication and psychological support are central to good care for people with DSDs. It's unclear whether increased awareness has improved people's lives, but the direction is clear: better information, specialist care and long-term support matter. The lesson is not that sex is meaningless or endlessly fluid. These rare conditions do not provide a simple answer to every argument about sex and gender. However, DSDs do show sex is not a single event. Just like the growth of our bodies, it is a process. When chromosomes, gonads, hormones and the body's response to them all follow the usual sequence, the result often looks simple. When one link changes, the body may no longer fit the binary pattern many of us were taught at school. For the people affected, this is not an abstract puzzle. Some need lifelong hormone treatment. Some face infertility. Some discover their condition only as teenagers or adults. Chromosomes or sex designation at birth may explain part of the biology behind DSDs. They do not explain what it feels like to grow up, go through puberty, seek fertility treatment or decide who needs to know. 性别的形成是一个始于出生前并延续至青春期的事件序列。染色体有助于指导性腺的形成，性腺是通常发育成卵巢或睾丸的身体器官。性腺产生激素。身体随后必须对这些激素作出反应。在大多数人中，这些步骤是顺畅一致的。但在极少数情况下，它们并非如此。 这些情况被称为性发育障碍，近期更名为性发育差异（DSDs）。它们并非新发现，但它们提供了一种理解身体如何发育的有用方式。DSDs可以影响激素的产生。有些影响身体对激素的反应。有些影响染色体或性腺发育。两个患有DSDs的人可以拥有截然不同的身体、医疗需求和生活经历。 每一种情况都揭示了当正常序列中的一个步骤发生改变时可能出现的结果。以下是性发育可能采取的五种不同路径。 先天性肾上腺皮质增生症 这种DSD影响大约每16,000例活产中的1例。 在这种情况下，肾上腺（即使在女性体内也会正常产生少量雄性激素）会过度活跃。 因此，一个拥有两条X染色体和卵巢的婴儿可能出生时外生殖器看起来像男性。 完全性雄激素不敏感综合征 相反，患有完全性雄激素不敏感综合征（CAIS）的儿童可能拥有XY染色体和产生睾酮的内部睾丸，但他们的身体无法对睾酮作出反应。 这种对睾酮缺乏反应使得发育中的胎儿保持在其“默认”的女性状态，导致出生时具有女性外生殖器。 CAIS患者在青春期会发育乳房。由于其外部解剖结构通常为女性，CAIS患者通常被抚养为女孩，并可能在晚些时候才被诊断出来，通常是因为月经未开始。 CAIS估计影响每100,000名活产女性中的1到5例。 5-α还原酶缺乏症 另一种名为5-α还原酶缺乏症的病症表明了性发育并非在出生时就完成。 身体需要一种名为5-α还原酶的酶，才能在出生前将睾酮转化为更强效的激素。当基因突变使这种酶失活时，一个XY染色体婴儿可能出生时生殖器被认为是女性，并可能被抚养为女孩。然而，在青春期，正常的常规睾酮激增会使身体男性化。 这种病症在全球范围内罕见，其确切发病率不明，部分原因是影响性发育的疾病常常被讳莫如深。 在多米尼加共和国的一些地区，这种病症被称为“guevedoces”，研究报告显示当地发病率出奇地高。一种罕见的基因突变在这些孤立人群中更为常见，影响大约每90名男性中的1名。 最近，其中一些社区已开始在出生时就识别出“guevedoces”儿童，并将其抚养为男孩。 性染色体疾病 通常，女性从父亲那里获得一个X染色体，从母亲那里获得一个X染色体，因此是XX；而男性从母亲那里获得一个X染色体，从父亲那里获得Y染色体。然而，患有克氏综合征（Klinefelter syndrome）的人，影响每500-1,000名男性活产中的1例，具有XXY染色体，导致睾丸较小、体毛稀疏、睾酮水平较低以及生育能力下降。这种疾病常常从未被诊断出来。 另一方面，特纳综合征（Turner syndrome），即女性缺失两条X染色体中的一条，影响每2,500名女性活产中的1例，并损害生长、青春期发育和生育能力。它可能影响身体外观，例如导致宽颈和宽胸，并常伴有听力损失和肾脏问题等健康状况。 斯威雅综合征（Swyer syndrome）更为罕见：患者拥有XY染色体，但Y染色体上的突变扰乱了通常驱动睾丸形成的信号。性腺不会发育成睾丸，睾酮水平保持在低位，外部和内部解剖结构通常为女性。 这些是更清晰的例子。更轻微的性别差异可能由仅是削弱而非完全使酶或激素反应失活的突变引起。例如，非经典型先天性肾上腺皮质增生症，其中一些酶活性仍然存在。它们的影响可能不那么明显，其发病率通常未知，因为许多只引起细微变化或从未被诊断出来。 这告诉了我们什么，又没有告诉我们什么 这些都不是新发现。这些病症几十年来一直为科学家所知。但现在，基因检测可以识别某些病例背后的突变。 专科诊所和患者团体一直在推动为父母提供更清晰的信息、为患者提供更好的心理支持，并对非紧急医疗情况下不可逆的儿童期手术采取更谨慎的态度。近期以患者为中心的研究表明，清晰的信息、良好的沟通和心理支持对于DSDs患者的良好护理至关重要。目前尚不清楚意识的提高是否改善了人们的生活，但方向是明确的：更好的信息、专科护理和长期支持至关重要。 由此得出的教训并非是性别毫无意义或无限流动。这些罕见病症并不能为所有关于生理性别和性别认同的争论提供简单的答案。 然而，DSDs确实表明生理性别并非单一事件。就像我们身体的成长一样，它是一个过程。当染色体、性腺、激素以及身体对它们的反应都遵循正常序列时，结果通常看起来很简单。当其中一个环节发生改变时，身体可能就不再符合我们许多人在学校里学到的二元模式。
