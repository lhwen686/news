深度解析

本研究的核心专业概念围绕替西帕肽（Tirzepatide）这一GLP-1受体激动剂在2型糖尿病合并心脏病患者中的应用及其心血管获益。替西帕肽独特之处在于其作为GLP-1（胰高血糖素样肽-1）和GIP（葡萄糖依赖性促胰岛素多肽）双重受体激动剂。这两种肠道激素通过促进葡萄糖依赖性胰岛素分泌、抑制胰高血糖素释放、延缓胃排空和增加饱腹感来改善血糖控制。与单一GLP-1激动剂相比，替西帕肽的双重作用使其在降糖和减重方面表现出更强的效力。 在心血管影响方面，替西帕肽通过改善血糖、体重和血压等代谢指标，进而降低主要不良心血管事件（MACE）的风险。研究结果表明，在标准治疗基础上添加替西帕肽，可显著降低心脏病发作、感染相关住院及死亡、以及全因死亡的风险。这一发现对医疗行业具有深远影响，意味着替西帕肽有望成为2型糖尿病合并心血管疾病患者的重要治疗选择，改变当前的治疗指南和临床实践。然而，社论也指出，药物的成本、可及性、供应及长期疗效数据仍是未来需要解决的关键问题，以确保其广泛的临床应用价值。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: Image generated by the editorial team using AI for illustrative purposes. Adding the GLP-1 receptor agonist drug tirzepatide (marketed as Mounjaro) to standard care for patients with type 2 diabetes and heart disease is associated with a lower risk of a major cardiovascular event, such as a heart attack or stroke, finds a study published by The BMJ today. Randomized trials and observational studies have shown noninferior effects of tirzepatide compared with another GLP-1 receptor agonist, dulaglutide, for major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE)—a combined measure of heart attack, stroke and death from any cause. But evidence on the effects of adding tirzepatide to standard care is more limited, resulting in uncertainty for both regulators and clinicians. To address this, researchers analyzed clinical practice data from two U.S. health insurance claims databases between May 2022 and May 2025. They aimed to estimate the cardiovascular effects of adding tirzepatide to standard care for patients with type 2 diabetes, a body mass index of at least 25, and established heart disease by comparing outcomes with sitagliptin, another diabetes drug. Sitagliptin was chosen as a neutral placebo proxy based on several studies showing no effect on cardiovascular outcomes. The main outcome of interest was a reduction in MACE, which was monitored from the first day of treatment up to one year, or until the individual stopped or switched treatment or disenrolled from the health plan. Factors including age, sex, race, body mass index, previous heart problems, other chronic conditions and medication use were taken into account, and a technique called propensity score overlap weighting was used to balance differences between the two groups and draw more reliable conclusions. Lower event rates over one year A total of 52,971 individuals were included in the analysis (average age 70 years; 51% female), of whom 35,353 started tirzepatide and 17,618 started sitagliptin. At one year, the risk of MACE was 2.9% in the tirzepatide group and 4.4% in the sitagliptin group (a 32% relative reduction), and the researchers estimate that for every 70 patients starting tirzepatide, one case of MACE would be prevented. For individual MACE components, tirzepatide was associated with a 33% lower risk of heart attack compared with sitagliptin, whereas ischemic stroke showed no meaningful difference. Infections requiring hospital admission were also lower with tirzepatide (one admission prevented for every 48 patients), as were infection-related death (one death prevented for every 200 patients) and death from any cause (one death prevented for every 122 patients). Limits on causal certainty This is an observational study, but the researchers previously benchmarked their design, data and analytics infrastructure against a randomized controlled trial before drawing conclusions about cause and effect. They also acknowledge several limitations, including a relatively short follow-up period, which may underestimate long-term cardiovascular and safety effects, possible misclassification of treatment duration or outcomes, and findings that may not apply to other health care systems or patients without established cardiovascular disease. However, they conclude, "This study shows how trial-anchored evidence from clinical practice can estimate the expected cardiovascular benefit of initiating tirzepatide beyond standard background treatment and inform shared decision-making." Questions about treatment access and sequencing A linked editorial notes that while this study provides an important, transparent estimate of what initiating tirzepatide might achieve in routine care, it does not establish a mortality indication or define the best sequence for cardiometabolic therapy. The authors say longer follow-up, randomized and pragmatic comparisons, and more studies of additive benefit on contemporary background treatment are needed. Furthermore, cardiovascular efficacy cannot benefit a population if cost, authorization barriers, supply and discontinuation prevent sustained treatment, they add. As such, they add, "The signal is compelling; the causal and clinical placement questions remain open." Publication details Tirzepatide and the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular events: population based cohort study, The BMJ (2026).
