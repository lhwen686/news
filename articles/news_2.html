研究揭示超二倍体如何在儿童体内形成罕见的白血病前期克隆。

Study reveals how hyperdiploidy creates rare pre-leukemic clones in children

该研究揭示了儿童B细胞急性淋巴细胞白血病（B-ALL）发病机制的关键洞察，核心概念是“超二倍体”和“染色体不稳定性”的双重作用。超二倍体指细胞染色体数量异常增多，导致染色体不稳定性，这是一种内在的遗传缺陷。研究首次提出，这种不稳定性在产前阶段反而会减缓白血病前期细胞的增殖并延迟分化，使其能作为“罕见、长寿命克隆”在骨髓中潜伏多年而不直接致病，解释了“非整倍体悖论”。然而，这些潜伏的克隆在产后阶段，可能在未知因素（如过度免疫反应、细胞因子刺激）诱发下，进一步累积协同突变，最终导致恶性转化。这种“两阶段模型”对于临床实践具有深远影响，它提示在发病高峰期（2-6岁）之前可能存在一个预防性干预的时间窗口。通过单细胞全基因组测序（scWGS）和异种移植模型等尖端技术，研究得以精确描绘细胞层面的遗传变异与功能变化，为未来儿童白血病的早期筛查、风险评估和精准预防策略的设计提供了坚实的科学依据，有望显著提升儿科肿瘤的预后水平。
Original Text
B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia is the most common form of childhood cancer. In this type of cancer, which affects blood cells, one of the most common abnormalities is the presence of cells with an excess of chromosomes (hyperdiploidy), a condition that leads to chromosomal instability. Now, a study published in Cell Reports reveals that this chromosomal instability caused by hyperdiploidy reduces the proliferation of the affected cells, delays their differentiation and allows some to persist as rare, long-lived clones in the bone marrow, but without triggering leukemia.

The study, conducted using animal models, is led by professors and researchers Òscar Molina and Pablo Menéndez from the Faculty of Medicine and Health Sciences of the University of Barcelona and the Josep Carreras leukemia Research Institute. The paper, whose lead author is Namitha Thampi, also a member of both institutions, is supported by the Spanish Association Against Cancer (AECC).

The study proposes a two-stage model to explain the origin of childhood B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL): an initial prenatal stage - hyperdiploidy - and a subsequent postnatal stage - triggered by unknown factors - which is necessary to initiate the malignant transformation of rare clones and lead to the development of the disease.

From the first phase (hyperdiploidy) to the second (malignant transformation), there may be a time window of between two and six years, which corresponds to the peak incidence of childhood lymphoblastic leukemia. It remains unclear how these rare clones evolve to cause the disease, and understanding this will be key to designing future strategies for the prevention of childhood leukemia.

Cells with more chromosomes than necessary

This type of lymphoblastic leukemia can develop when a child's immune system responds excessively to a common infection. This response involves the production of large amounts of cytokines and proliferation signals that stimulate the bone marrow cells to divide and produce new immune cells.

If the pre-leukemic clone with extra chromosomes is present among these cells, it also receives growth signals and can proliferate more than normal. Increased cell division raises the likelihood of further genetic errors. If the clone acquires synergistic mutations, it may eventually develop into leukemia." Òscar Molina, member of the UB's Department of Physiological Sciences

Between 35% and 40% of cases of the disease involve cells with a hyperdiploid chromosome count. In most patients, between 51 and 63 chromosomes are identified, whereas the normal chromosome count is 46.

"Chromosomal gains in hyperdiploid B-ALL are not random. The chromosomes most frequently found in excess are chromosomes 4, 6, 10, 14, 17, 18, 21 and the X chromosome," notes the expert. "Everything suggests that this excess of chromosomes arises in utero (before birth) during foetal development, in early hematopoietic progenitor stem cells, which are responsible for generating the various blood cells."

Extra chromosomes and the persistence of rare clones

The study reveals that hyperdiploidy causes chromosomal instability, which has effects at various levels. "At the cellular level, it reduces the proliferative capacity of cells and delays the differentiation of hematopoietic stem cells, which remain in an undifferentiated state for longer - a characteristic commonly found in cancer cells," says Pablo Menéndez, a researcher at the Josep Carreras Research Institute.

From a functional perspective, "hyperdiploid cells can persist for some time as rare clones in the bone marrow, but on their own they are not sufficient to trigger leukemia," says Òscar Molina. "These results," he continues, "help to explain what is known as the aneuploidy paradox: chromosomal changes can harm normal cells, but, in turn, facilitate tumor progression in certain contexts."

However, these hyperdiploid pre-leukemic clones can also persist for years without manifesting clinically. "The factors that trigger the malignant progression of these pre-leukemic clones are not precisely known," notes Menéndez.

"The chromosomal gains observed in these cells in animal models correspond to those most commonly found in B-ALL patients, which reinforces the clinical relevance of the model and suggests that these gains may contribute to the persistence of these preleukaemic cells," explains postdoctoral researcher Namitha Thampi.

Cutting-edge technology for studying lymphoblastic leukemia

Paediatric B-cell ALL is now one of the cancers with the best prognosis in paediatric oncology, with cure rates of between 80% and 90% thanks to combination chemotherapy administered in different phases (induction, consolidation and maintenance), hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (recommended for high-risk patients) and immunotherapy (in cases of relapse).

One of the most distinctive methodological aspects of this study has been the initial biological sample: human fetal hematopoietic stem cells, a material that is extremely difficult to obtain and which was provided by the UK Medical Research Council. "This institution has provided us with foetal material and enabled us to study directly the cells in which the first alterations associated with paediatric leukemia originate," the authors note.

Among other leading techniques, single-cell whole-genome sequencing (scWGS) has also been used to analyse the chromosomal content of each individual cell with great precision. Xenograft models in immunodeficient mice (NSG) have also been used to study how pre-leukemic clones persist and evolve in the bone marrow of a living organism. High-throughput confocal microscopy has enabled the automated examination of thousands of cells at high resolution. In this regard, the team has also developed its own computer macros to automate the analysis of microscopic images and process large volumes of cellular data.

B细胞急性淋巴细胞白血病是儿童期最常见的癌症。在这种影响血细胞的癌症中，最常见的异常之一是存在染色体过多的细胞（超二倍体），这是一种导致染色体不稳定性的状况。现在，发表在《细胞报告》杂志上的一项研究揭示，这种由超二倍体引起的染色体不稳定性会降低受影响细胞的增殖能力，延缓其分化，并允许一些细胞作为罕见的、长寿命的克隆存在于骨髓中，但不会引发白血病。

这项使用动物模型进行的研究由巴塞罗那大学医学与健康科学学院以及何塞普·卡雷拉斯白血病研究所的教授兼研究员奥斯卡·莫利纳（Òscar Molina）和巴勃罗·梅嫩德斯（Pablo Menéndez）领导。这篇论文的主要作者是同样来自这两个机构的娜米莎·坦皮（Namitha Thampi），并得到了西班牙抗癌协会（AECC）的支持。

该研究提出了一个两阶段模型来解释儿童B细胞急性淋巴细胞白血病（B-ALL）的起源：一个初始的产前阶段——超二倍体——以及一个随后的产后阶段——由未知因素触发——这是启动罕见克隆恶性转化并导致疾病发展的必要条件。

从第一阶段（超二倍体）到第二阶段（恶性转化）之间可能存在两到六年的时间窗口，这与儿童淋巴细胞白血病的发病高峰期相对应。目前尚不清楚这些罕见克隆如何演变为致病，而了解这一点将是设计未来儿童白血病预防策略的关键。

染色体多于所需数量的细胞

当儿童免疫系统对常见感染反应过度时，可能会发展出这种类型的淋巴细胞白血病。这种反应涉及产生大量细胞因子和增殖信号，刺激骨髓细胞分裂并产生新的免疫细胞。

如果这些细胞中存在带有额外染色体的白血病前期克隆，它也会接收到生长信号并可能比正常细胞增殖更多。细胞分裂增加会提高发生进一步基因错误的几率。如果克隆获得协同突变，它最终可能会发展成白血病。”巴塞罗那大学生理科学系的成员奥斯卡·莫利纳说。

35%到40%的病例涉及超二倍体染色体计数的细胞。大多数患者体内检测到51到63条染色体，而正常染色体计数为46条。

“超二倍体B-ALL中的染色体增益并非随机。最常过量的染色体是4、6、10、14、17、18、21号染色体和X染色体，”该专家指出。“一切迹象表明，这种染色体过量是在胎儿发育的子宫内（出生前）发生在早期造血祖干细胞中，这些细胞负责生成各种血细胞。”

额外染色体与罕见克隆的持久性

该研究揭示，超二倍体引起染色体不稳定性，这在各个层面都有影响。“在细胞层面，它降低了细胞的增殖能力，并延缓了造血干细胞的分化，使其在未分化状态下停留更长时间——这是癌细胞常见的特征，”何塞普·卡雷拉斯研究机构的研究员巴勃罗·梅嫩德斯说。

从功能角度来看，“超二倍体细胞可以作为罕见克隆在骨髓中持续一段时间，但它们本身不足以引发白血病，”奥斯卡·莫利纳说。“这些结果，”他继续说道，“有助于解释所谓的非整倍体悖论：染色体变化可能损害正常细胞，但在某些情况下反而促进肿瘤进展。”

然而，这些超二倍体白血病前期克隆也可能持续多年而无临床表现。“触发这些白血病前期克隆恶性进展的因素尚不明确，”梅嫩德斯指出。

“在动物模型中观察到的这些细胞的染色体增益与B-ALL患者中最常见的增益相对应，这增强了该模型的临床相关性，并表明这些增益可能有助于这些白血病前期细胞的持久性，”博士后研究员娜米莎·坦皮解释道。

用于研究淋巴细胞白血病的尖端技术

儿科B细胞急性淋巴细胞白血病现已成为儿科肿瘤学中预后最好的癌症之一，通过分阶段（诱导、巩固和维持）的联合化疗、造血干细胞移植（推荐用于高危患者）和免疫疗法（复发病例）治疗，治愈率可达80%到90%。

这项研究最具特色的方法学之一是其初始生物样本：人类胎儿造血干细胞，这是一种极难获得的材料，由英国医学研究委员会提供。“该机构为我们提供了胎儿材料，使我们能够直接研究与儿科白血病首次改变相关的细胞，”作者指出。

除了其他领先技术外，该研究还使用了单细胞全基因组测序（scWGS）来高精度分析每个单独细胞的染色体含量。此外，还使用了免疫缺陷小鼠（NSG）异种移植模型来研究白血病前期克隆如何在活体生物体的骨髓中持续和演变。高通量共聚焦显微镜实现了数千个细胞的高分辨率自动化检查。在这方面，该团队还开发了自己的计算机宏，以自动化显微图像的分析并处理大量细胞数据。
