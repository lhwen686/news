为什么我们在黑白陀螺中看到颜色？

Why do we see colors in a black-and-white top?

深度解析

本研究深入探讨了“主观颜色”这一经典的视觉神经科学现象，特别是本汉姆陀螺效应的内在机制。其核心专业概念在于利用先进的人工神经网络（AI）模型，通过“预测学习”范式，模拟人脑如何从无色刺激中构建色彩感知。传统观点可能倾向于将颜色感知局限于视网膜的光感受器活动，但此研究揭示了大脑高级认知功能在其中的关键作用。 AI模型通过学习自然视频中运动物体与颜色之间的关联，即使接收到纯黑白输入，也能“预测”并生成微弱的颜色。这暗示了人脑在视觉处理过程中，并非被动接收信息，而是主动利用过往经验和预测机制来解释和构建视觉现实。这种运动与颜色关联的学习机制，对于理解视觉通路中的多模态整合至关重要。 对神经病学领域而言，这项研究的行业影响深远。它为解释某些视觉错觉、幻觉以及特定神经系统疾病（如视网膜病变或皮层损伤）患者的异常视觉感知提供了新视角。理解大脑如何通过预测性机制产生主观感知，有助于开发更精准的神经诊断工具，并为神经康复（例如，通过训练大脑的预测网络来改善视觉功能）提供理论基础。同时，这也推动了AI在模拟人脑认知功能方面的应用，为未来的人工智能视觉系统设计提供了生物学灵感。

Credit: Laboratory of Neurophysiology, NIBB When a pattern drawn only in black and white is rotated, people sometimes perceive colors that are not actually present. This phenomenon, known as "subjective color," has been studied for approximately 200 years as one of the remarkable mysteries of vision. Among such phenomena, Benham's top is a well-known example in which colors are perceived simply by spinning a black-and-white disk. Researchers have now used an artificial neural network trained through predictive learning to investigate the mechanism underlying this subjective color phenomenon. The research group presented a black-and-white Benham's top to an AI model trained to predict "what kind of image will come next" after viewing natural videos. Although the input images contained no color information, faint colors appeared in the images predicted by the AI. Furthermore, the artificially generated colors changed depending on the colors of moving objects in the videos used to train the AI. Analyses using natural videos, 3D computer graphics and simple two-dimensional videos of moving red, green and blue squares suggested that learning the association between motion and color may be one factor that enables colors to emerge from black-and-white stimuli. Benham's top illusion. Credit: Laboratory of Neurophysiology, NIBB This study suggests that subjective color may not be a phenomenon completed solely within the retina but may also be related to the brain's function of predicting future visual input based on past experience. By using AI, the study provides a new clue to the classical mystery of why colors are perceived from a black-and-white spinning top. These findings are published in Scientific Reports. The first author of the study is Kyohei Ueda, a graduate student at the Laboratory of Neurophysiology, National Institute for Basic Biology, and SOKENDAI. Publication details Kyohei Ueda et al, Predictive networks generate motion-induced color illusions, Scientific Reports (2026).
