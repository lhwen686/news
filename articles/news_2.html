深度解析

这篇医学新闻揭示了伽马德尔塔T细胞在小细胞肺癌（SCLC）治疗中的突破性潜力。与主要通过T细胞受体（TCR）识别主要组织相容性复合体（MHC）呈递抗原的常规αβ T细胞不同，伽马德尔塔T细胞是独特的一群T淋巴细胞，它们通常以MHC非限制性方式识别肿瘤细胞，例如通过识别磷酸抗原或应激诱导配体。SCLC以高度侵袭性和免疫逃逸能力著称，其低MHC表达常使其对传统免疫疗法“隐形”。伽马德尔塔T细胞的这种非MHC限制性识别机制，使其能够“看穿”SCLC的伪装，直接识别并杀伤癌细胞，从而提供了一条绕过传统免疫屏障的全新抗肿瘤途径。研究中提及的两种药物——唑来膦酸和塔拉他马布，在机制上具有协同作用。唑来膦酸通过抑制法尼基焦磷酸合酶，导致细胞内磷酸抗原积累，这些抗原是伽马德尔塔T细胞的有效激活剂，从而增加其数量和肿瘤识别能力。塔拉他马布则是一种双特异性T细胞衔接器，一端结合T细胞表面的CD3分子，另一端结合SCLC细胞表面的DLL3，将伽马德尔塔T细胞“拉近”肿瘤细胞，增强其杀伤效果。这一发现为SCLC这种预后极差的癌症带来了新的希望，不仅为新型免疫疗法开发提供了靶点，也为现有药物的重新利用和伽马德尔塔T细胞过继转移疗法开辟了前景，预示着未来个性化精准医疗的新方向。

Credit: Walter and Eliza Hall Institute of Medical Research Researchers have uncovered a previously overlooked immune cell that can recognize and kill small cell lung cancer, the most aggressive form of the disease globally. Published in Cancer Cell, the WEHI study found patients with higher levels of these cells lived longer, uncovering a hidden immune defense that could be exploited to attack these hard-to-treat tumors. Researchers also showed existing drugs could be repurposed to boost the cancer-killing activity of these immune cells, opening new opportunities to strengthen current treatments and help more patients benefit from these therapies. Finding a way to boost treatment responses for lung cancer Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) is one of the world's deadliest diseases, with a five-year survival rate of less than 7%. While immunotherapy has transformed outcomes for other cancers, only a rare subset of SCLC patients will experience long-term benefits from the treatment. One major reason is that these cancers are often invisible to the conventional immune cells that immunotherapy treatments rely on, making them difficult to detect and treat. The new Cancer Cell study reveals a previously unrecognized immune pathway that can override these invisibility powers, unlocking a potential new therapeutic avenue for SCLC patients. Kate Sutherland, senior author on the paper and WEHI laboratory head, said the findings could help solve one of the field's most pressing questions: how to boost the number of lung cancer patients who respond to immunotherapy. "For decades, we've known that small cell lung cancer is exceptionally good at hiding from the immune system," Sutherland said. "We've found an immune cell that appears to be able to see through that disguise. "Strikingly, patients whose tumors contained more of these specialized immune cells often had better treatment outcomes. This is the strongest evidence yet that gamma delta T cells are present in small cell lung cancers and appear capable of fighting the disease. "It suggests these cells may offer an alternative immune attack route for tumors that are able to evade conventional cancer-fighting immune cells, opening up new possibilities for treating a cancer that has seen few advances over the past decades." Credit: Cancer Cell (2026). DOI: 10.1016/j.ccell.2026.08.015 Using mini tumors to uncover a new lung cancer treatment strategy To test whether gamma delta T cells could fight cancer, researchers created miniature tumors in the lab using samples donated by patients through the Victorian Cancer Biobank in collaboration with the Royal Melbourne Hospital. These "mini tumors" represent one of the most realistic laboratory models of SCLC developed to date. When the team combined the mini tumors with gamma delta T cells taken from the patients' own blood, they found the immune cells were highly effective at recognizing and killing SCLC cells. The researchers discovered they could amplify this immune response by using two existing drugs—tarlatamab (a cancer treatment approved for SCLC) and zoledronate (approved for osteoporosis and cancer-related bone disease). The team found zoledronate increased the number of gamma delta T cells available and enhanced their ability to recognize SCLC tumors. Tarlatamab acted like a magnetic force that brought the immune cells into closer contact with the cancer. Together, these drugs significantly enhanced the gamma delta T cells' ability to attack the cancer cells, suggesting a potential new way to strengthen anticancer immunity in SCLC. Dr. Jin Ng, first author on the paper, said the findings also raise the possibility of using existing gamma delta T cells for adoptive transfer therapies—where large numbers of these immune cells are grown outside the body and infused back into patients. "For patients with lower gamma delta T cell levels, therapies such as zoledronate may offer a way to boost their cancer-fighting immune cells and responsiveness to treatments," Ng said. "As therapies that expand and reinfuse gamma delta T cells already exist, our findings raise the exciting possibility of rapidly translating this discovery into new treatment strategies. "This could open an entirely new avenue for personalized treatment approaches in small cell lung cancer and other solid cancers that are often missed by conventional immune cells." Gathering clues from landmark immunotherapy clinical trials The team focused on gamma delta T cells after analyzing data from two practice-changing clinical trials—IMpower133 and CASPIAN—which established immunotherapy as a standard treatment for SCLC. Using advanced genomic and computational analyses led by WEHI's genomics and bioinformatics teams, researchers were able to link a strong gamma delta T cell signature to patients who experienced the greatest benefit from immunotherapy. The team now hopes to clinically translate the findings into a new class of immunotherapy drugs for small cell lung cancer, in an ongoing collaboration with researchers at the Doherty Institute. Publication details Jin Ng et al., γδ T cells modulate anti-tumor immunity in small cell lung cancer, Cancer Cell (2026). DOI: 10.1016/j.ccell.2026.08.015 Journal information: Cancer Cell
