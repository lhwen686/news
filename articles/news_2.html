研究人员敦促对大规模新生儿基因组筛查持谨慎态度

Researchers urge caution over large-scale newborn genome screening

这项研究深入探讨了新生儿基因组筛查中的核心挑战——过度诊断的风险，这主要源于对遗传变异外显率的误判。外显率是指携带特定基因变异的个体在特定年龄或条件下表现出相应疾病表型的概率。传统上，基因研究多在高风险或已患病人群中进行，导致这些研究报告的外显率往往高于普通人群中的实际水平。当将这些基于高风险群体的外显率直接应用于大规模新生儿筛查时，便可能出现过度诊断，即无病个体被错误地标识为高风险，从而引发不必要的焦虑、反复检查乃至干预措施，给家庭带来沉重的心理和经济负担。埃克塞特团队通过分析英国生物样本库等大型普通人群数据集，重新评估了遗传变异与疾病的关联，发现其在普通人群中的外显率显著低于以往研究。这一发现对基因组筛查的临床实践具有深远影响。它强调了在推广新生儿基因组筛查项目前，必须利用大规模、多样化的普通人群数据来校准疾病风险模型，以确保筛查结果的准确性和临床效用。这不仅有助于减少过度诊断及其带来的负面影响，还能优化医疗资源配置，并确保筛查真正惠及有需要的家庭，而非制造恐慌。对单拷贝基因缺失与双拷贝基因缺失导致疾病的风险差异分析，也进一步细化了风险评估的精准度，推动了基因组医学向更负责任、更精细化的方向发展。
Original Text
New research from large population studies provides invaluable evidence on genome screening of newborn babies to reduce risks from overdiagnosis. The authors conclude that further studies are needed before such screening is rolled out.

A team at the University of Exeter is publishing a series of papers which highlight the risks that screening can pose and call for action to minimize harms to families. Funded by the Medical Research Council and National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR) Exeter Biomedical Research Centre, the research is being presented at the European Human Genetics Conference (June 16), with three papers so far published in the European Journal of Human Genetics special issue on DNA in public health screening programs.

Newborn genome screening pilot studies are increasingly being rolled out in countries including the UK, and involve determining the entire sequence of a baby's genetic code and interrogating it for hundreds of potentially treatable conditions. The research team cautions that most genetic studies to date have been carried out in groups of people who already have a condition, or are in a high-risk family. This means that the known risk of a genetic variant causing a disease (known as penetrance) is often higher than the actual risk in the general population.

To put this to the test, the Exeter team has analyzed genetic variants previously found to cause disease, using large datasets of the general population, including nearly a million volunteers in either UK Biobank or All of Us. Focussing on more than 50 genes across 15 diseases included in largescale trials for newborn genome screening, the team looked for the variants, and whether people who carry them have the disease.

We found that in most cases, although there is a link between the genetic variants and the disease, it's weaker than previous research has suggested. This is because most research is conducted in people who already have a disease or are at high risk. The actual risk is often lower if you're looking at the general population, as would be the case if you applied this risk to newborn screening." Dr. Leigh Jackson, University of Exeter Medical School

The team's published research found that the biggest risk of overdiagnosis arose when the risk of disease stems from a loss of just one of the two copies of a gene. However, the picture was mixed and for other conditions, their upcoming research will show that where both copies of the gene need to be lost to cause disease, the evidence suggests a lower risk of overdiagnosis.

Professor Caroline Wright, of the University of Exeter Medical School, said: "The UK is leading the way on newborn genome screening and the world is watching. We urgently need the best evidence to ensure we get this right. The benefits of a successful programme will be profound, but we need to be aware of the risk of overdiagnosis based on current evidence. Being told your baby is at high risk of developing disease has a massive impact on families – we need to ensure we use the most accurate risk estimates possible in order to provide the best care."

The three papers published so far in the European Journal of Human Genetics are:

Revisiting penetrance in an era of genomic screening: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-026-02101-1 Reduced penetrance of COL1A1/2 pathogenic variants linked with osteogenesis imperfecta: analysis of a large population cohort: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-026-02118-6 Uncovering apparent incomplete penetrance of TSC1/TSC2 variants: Insights from multiple population cohorts and implications for newborn screening: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-026-02107-9

埃克塞特大学的一个团队正在发表一系列论文，强调筛查可能带来的风险，并呼吁采取行动将对家庭的危害降至最低。这项研究由医学研究理事会和国家健康与护理研究所（NIHR）埃克塞特生物医学研究中心资助，正在欧洲人类遗传学大会（6月16日）上进行展示，迄今已有三篇论文发表在《欧洲人类遗传学杂志》关于公共卫生筛查项目中DNA的特刊上。

新生儿基因组筛查试点研究正在包括英国在内的多个国家日益推广，涉及确定婴儿整个基因编码序列，并检测数百种潜在可治疗的疾病。研究团队警告说，迄今为止，大多数基因研究都是在已经患有某种疾病或属于高风险家庭的人群中进行的。这意味着某种遗传变异导致疾病的已知风险（即外显率）通常高于其在普通人群中的实际风险。

为了验证这一点，埃克塞特团队利用包括英国生物样本库或“我们所有人”计划中近百万名志愿者在内的大型普通人群数据集，分析了先前被发现会导致疾病的遗传变异。该团队聚焦于新生儿基因组筛查大规模试验中包含的15种疾病的50多个基因，寻找这些变异，并研究携带者是否患有该疾病。

我们发现在大多数情况下，尽管遗传变异与疾病之间存在关联，但这种关联比以往研究提示的要弱。这是因为大多数研究是在已患病或高风险人群中进行的。如果考察普通人群，就像将这种风险应用于新生儿筛查一样，实际风险往往会更低。—— 埃克塞特大学医学院的利·杰克逊博士。

该团队已发表的研究发现，当疾病风险仅源于基因两个拷贝中一个的缺失时，过度诊断的风险最大。然而，情况是复杂的，对于其他疾病，他们即将发表的研究将表明，如果需要两个基因拷贝都缺失才会导致疾病，那么过度诊断的风险会较低。

埃克塞特大学医学院的卡罗琳·赖特教授表示：“英国在新生儿基因组筛查方面处于领先地位，全世界都在关注。我们迫切需要最好的证据来确保我们能正确地进行这项工作。成功项目的益处将是深远的，但我们需要意识到基于现有证据的过度诊断风险。被告知您的孩子患疾病的风险很高，这对家庭会产生巨大的影响——我们需要确保使用尽可能最准确的风险估计，以提供最佳护理。”
