深度解析

这篇新闻报道的核心在于探讨口腔健康不平等问题，并提出了一种创新的教育与研究工具——桌面游戏。其专业概念基石是“健康社会决定因素”（Social Determinants of Health, SDH）。SDH指出，一个人的健康状况不仅受生物学因素影响，更受其生活、工作、社会和经济环境（如职业、贫困、生活成本危机、全球疫情等）的深刻塑造。文章通过性工作者口腔健康需求的案例，深刻揭示了社会污名、结构性障碍如何导致特定群体面临严重的口腔健康服务可及性不足和健康结果差异。 该研究及其衍生的桌面游戏，对牙医学和公共卫生领域具有显著影响。传统牙医学往往侧重于临床治疗，而该项目强调将社会学视角融入医疗教育和实践。通过游戏化教学，未来的医疗专业人员能够更直观地理解患者所处的复杂社会背景，培养同理心，从而在临床诊疗和公共卫生干预中，更全面地考虑社会经济因素，设计更具包容性和公平性的医疗策略。这不仅有助于提高特定弱势群体的口腔健康水平，也推动了整个医疗行业向更具人文关怀和系统性解决健康不平等问题的方向发展。这种跨学科的创新方法，为解决复杂社会健康问题提供了新的范式。

This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies . Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: University of Liverpool A new board game developed by University of Liverpool researchers is creating opportunities for students, health professionals and other stakeholders to explore the social factors that influence oral health and access to dental care. Informed by findings from more than 70 in-depth interviews, the prototype game offers an interactive way to understand the barriers people can face in maintaining their oral health and accessing dental services. It explores how wider social determinants of health, including occupation, poverty, the cost-of-living crisis and global events such as the COVID-19 pandemic, can shape people's experiences. The research informing the game draws on two national projects: a study published in the British Dental Journal exploring the oral health needs of sex workers and a study examining oral health stigma and shame. This wider work has been described as "bold and important" in an editorial in the British Dental Journal. The board game has significant potential as a teaching and training resource across a range of disciplines, including dentistry, criminology, biomedical sciences, public health and planning. By placing players in situations that encourage discussion and reflection, it provides an accessible way to engage with the complex realities behind oral health inequalities. It also offers opportunities to involve health professionals and other key stakeholders in conversations about the challenges people face in maintaining their oral health, accessing dental care and living with dignity and confidence. The game was recently showcased at three Liverpool venues as part of the ESRC Festival of Social Science, giving members of the public an opportunity to try the prototype. It builds on an art exhibition exploring the oral health experiences of sex workers, which is now set to tour U.K. dental schools. The project team, led by clinical lecturer in dentistry Dr. Janine Yazdi-Doughty and senior lecturer in criminology Dr. Gemma Ahearne, is keen to explore its potential for teaching, training and public engagement. Ahearne said, "The game provides an opportunity for students on our largest optional module in my department—Crime, Justice and the Sex Industry—to engage with our research through gamification." Yazdi-Doughty said, "We expect the project to impact teaching, training and public engagement as well as raising broader awareness of the inequalities many face. We are continuing to develop and refine the game and hope to expand to our other research."
