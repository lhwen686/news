Fake doctor reviews often trusted more than real ones, study finds

深度解析

本文揭示了虚假医护评论如何利用消费者对详细信息的渴望和真实患者的隐私顾虑，通过编造丰富的个人健康叙事来提升可信度。其核心机制在于“披露悖论”：患者在选择医疗服务时渴望了解细致的诊疗体验，但真实患者碍于隐私保护，往往不愿公开分享敏感的症状、诊断、药物及治疗细节。不法分子正是抓住这一信息不对称，通过虚构具体病程来模拟真实性，甚至借助生成式AI工具，大规模炮制此类具有高欺骗性的详细评论。 这种现象对医疗行业造成深远影响。首先，它严重损害了在线医疗评价系统的公信力，加剧了“信息茧房”效应，使患者难以辨别真伪，从而影响其做出明智的医疗决策。其次，生成式AI的滥用使得虚假信息生产成本大幅降低，进一步挑战了现有内容审核机制。这促使行业必须探索更前沿的验证技术，如结合区块链的分布式账本技术来确保评论溯源，或利用先进的自然语言处理（NLP）技术识别虚假叙事的模式和异常。同时，加强医患关系确认（如预约挂钩评论系统）和匿名性保护，重塑评价格式以平衡信息共享与隐私保护，是恢复信任、维护医疗信息生态健康的关键举措。这将推动医疗服务平台在技术创新和伦理规范方面进行双重升级，以应对未来AI带来的挑战。

Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Credit: CC0 Public Domain New research reveals fraudulent clinician reviews often seem more credible because they include richer personal health details that real patients are reluctant to share publicly. Researchers say these detailed narratives exploit a gap between the information people want to read and what real patients are willing to share. "Patients naturally want detailed information when they're choosing a health care provider. A real patient might simply say a doctor was helpful, while a fake review tells a detailed story about symptoms, diagnosis and treatment," says Aishwarya Deep Shukla, associate professor at the Beedie School of Business and lead author of the study. "People are drawn to stories. Unfortunately, these deceptive reviews have the kind of information real patients don't usually share publicly and can influence decisions about who people trust with their care." The study, published in Internet Research, looked at 35,000 reviews collected in India between 2015 and 2017. The reviews spanned medical specialties from family medicine to dentistry and dermatology. Researchers verified 8,313 fake reviews posted by health care providers and associated clinic staff, some of whom impersonated patients. They exploited a security flaw in an online platform that invited patients to leave reviews following appointments. The team further analyzed a representative random sample of 5,000 reviews and found fake reviews were longer than genuine ones and gave more detail about symptoms, diagnoses, medications and treatment. These fake reviews received more helpful votes and scored higher on a measure of perceived trustworthiness than genuine reviews. Shukla says the fake review tactics documented in the study persist today, aided by advances in generative artificial intelligence (AI). "When I look at reviews now, I see the same patterns of length and detail. The double-edged sword of AI is that it's helping real people write more complete and useful reviews, but it's also helping bad actors produce detailed fake reviews at scale." Shukla prescribes stronger safeguards, such as appointment-linked review systems, authenticated anonymous reviews and stronger moderation practices. Revamping review formats to help patients share more meaningful information without revealing sensitive personal details must be part of the solution, he adds. Longer-term solutions should include verified review systems managed by health care organizations or public agencies that confirm a patient relationship while preserving anonymity. Top tips for using clinician reviews Fake reviews are most prevalent where there's competition for patients or clients, says Shukla. This includes private clinics and services such as dentistry, psychology, counseling and physiotherapy. He says patients searching for a new clinician should: Read all the reviews: Look for consistent themes across many reviews rather than focusing on one or two standout stories. Be cautious of reviews that reveal unusually detailed medical information that most patients would be uncomfortable sharing publicly. Don't dismiss providers who have zero or very few reviews; most satisfied patients never leave reviews at all. Consider reviews alongside other sources of information, including professional credentials, personal recommendations and direct interactions with a clinic. More information Aishwarya Deep Shukla et al, The illusion of trust and the paradox of disclosure: how fake physician reviews exploit privacy concerns, Internet Research (2025).
