深度解析

Bharat Semi开发的AI图像传感器是边缘计算和嵌入式AI技术的重要突破。其核心创新是将图像捕获、信号处理和人工智能功能整合到单一可编程芯片上。这意味着传感器能直接进行数据处理和AI推理，无需将原始视觉数据传输到独立的ISP或SoC。这种“片上智能”设计显著降低了数据传输延迟和带宽需求，更关键的是，能将系统成本降低至少70%。这对于需要在设备端实时进行视觉分析的应用至关重要，如智能家居、工业自动化、无人机和监控系统。 从“传感器”转向“感知和理解解决方案”（sense-making solutions）标志着设备从单纯的数据收集者升级为能自主分析并采取行动的智能体。通过在边缘设备上实现高效、低成本的AI视觉，Bharat Semi有望推动AI在成本敏感或实时响应要求高的场景中普及。该公司采用无晶圆厂（fabless）模式，专注于设计和软件，利用全球代工厂能力加速产品迭代。其发展不仅符合印度构建本土半导体产业的战略，也响应了全球供应链多元化的趋势，预示着印度在全球AI芯片市场中可能占据一席之地，为各行业带来更智能、更经济的解决方案。

英文原文

Original
Bharat Semi, an Indian semiconductor startup, has raised $24 million in seed funding to develop AI-enabled image sensors, as it seeks to win customers in the United States and elsewhere. The investment comes as the nearly five-year-old, pre-revenue startup says it has secured design wins with U.S. defense technology company General Atomics and India’s Kaynes Semicon. Its image sensors, however, remain under development, with the startup targeting its initial commercial production in 2027. India has committed billions of dollars to build up a domestic semiconductor industry, with a hope to become an alternative manufacturing hub as U.S. companies look to diversify supply chains beyond China. The country, however, has yet to establish itself as a major chip manufacturing hub. Meanwhile, the AI boom is also giving Indian startups such as Bharat Semi an opportunity to develop specialized chips for global markets. The New Delhi-based startup is developing programmable image sensors that combine image capture, processing, and AI capabilities on a single chip, reducing the need to send visual data to separate processors. The move could cut system costs by at least 70% compared with conventional setups that use a separate image sensor, image signal processor, and a system-on-chip, co-founder Vrinda Kapoor (pictured above, right) said in an interview. “Our aim here is to move from sensors to sense-making solutions,” Kapoor told TechCrunch. Kapoor, a South Park Commons fellow who earlier worked in healthcare technology, co-founded Bharat Semi in late 2021 with Vinayak Dalmia (pictured above, left), the startup’s managing director, who previously worked at McKinsey and in private equity. Its chief technology officer, Boyd Fowler, previously held the same role at image-sensor maker OmniVision. Bharat Semi is targeting smart homes, industrial automation, surveillance, and aerospace. Without disclosing specifics, Kapoor pointed to air conditioners that could detect when a room is empty and adjust automatically and drones that could process visual information — even with the absence of more expensive computing hardware elsewhere in the system. The startup’s first-generation sensors are already in prototyping, while its second-generation sensors, which will incorporate more advanced AI capabilities, are expected to reach the prototype stage by mid-2027, Kapoor said. Moreover, the startup plans to get those newer sensors into customers’ hands by the end of 2027. The all-equity seed round was led by Info Edge Group, South Park Commons, and Indus Bridge Ventures, with participation from Valour Capital, Micromax, Gaingels, and individual investors. Bharat Semi, Kapoor said, plans to use the funding to expand its engineering team, complete product development, and bring its sensors to market. Bharat Semi has about 75 employees across India and the U.S., including 10 to 12 in its American operations, which span engineering and business development in Santa Clara. The startup plans to increase its U.S. headcount to around 20 by mid-2027, Kapoor said. It has also begun hiring engineers in Europe. “It’s a story of can we build a global technology company out of India,” Kapoor said. In 2024, Bharat Semi announced plans for a semiconductor fabrication project in India involving a technology partnership with the U.S. Space Force. The initiative, Kapoor said, remains in development and the startup is working to identify domestic partners that would build and operate the facility. The project is separate from Bharat Semi’s image-sensor business, Kapoor added. The startup plans to remain fabless, outsourcing chip manufacturing while focusing on semiconductor design and software. The new funding will not be used to finance the proposed fabrication facility.
