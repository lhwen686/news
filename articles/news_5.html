深度解析

"Phantom Blade Zero"从独立小品到3A巨制的演变，清晰展现了中国游戏产业在IP孵化与商业模式创新上的深度探索。灵游坊从最初的独立游戏起步，通过与网易合作开发手游《影之刃》实现了商业上的可持续性，再将IP回溯至高端单机市场，这一策略性布局不仅解决了生存问题，也为实现更大抱负积累了资源。这种多阶段、跨平台的IP运营模式，是当前游戏公司在激烈竞争中寻求突破的关键商业逻辑。 在技术和玩法层面，《影之刃零》放弃传统RPG数值堆砌，转而强调武器变化与技能树分支带来的“质变”成长，反映出一种更注重玩家操作技巧和策略深度的现代游戏设计理念。其对动作系统的高度投入，邀请甄子丹及其团队参与创意和战斗设计，以及对海量动捕动画的坚持，都旨在通过核心玩法创新来突破动作游戏领域的既有范式，挑战《忍者龙剑传》等经典作品，为武侠题材游戏在全球市场树立新的标杆，尽管初期反馈中出现的“画风割裂”问题，提示了大型项目在艺术风格统一性上面临的挑战。

源自原创 IP《雨血》的武侠单机之梦。 2026.10.29 / PS5、PC 由北京灵游坊（S-GAME）开发并自行发行，团队创始人梁其伟担任了本作制作人、总监，他向这个 IP 倾注了快 20 年的心血：2008 年左右，学生时代的梁其伟就用 RPG Maker 独立制作了《雨血：死镇》，后来拉起了队伍推出《雨血 2：烨城》《雨血前传：蜃楼》等；《蜃楼》虽取得约 30 万套的销量成绩，但还不足以支撑公司，最终网易入股结束了灵游坊的关门危机，双方开始合作经营脱胎于《雨血》的手游《影之刃》；一晃就是十年，直到今天《影之刃零》面世，灵游坊在这个 IP 上孵化出了 3A 级单机作品。 《影之刃零》采用虚幻引擎 5 开发，可以看作是原版《雨血》的重新演绎，不要求玩家接触过灵游坊的其他作品。本作最早在 2023 年索尼 PlayStation Showcase 上正式亮相，但灵游坊已内部酝酿了很久，2016 年提出了构想、2022 年启动全面开发，据称灵游坊还在开发过程中砍了很多项目，足见有股破釜沉舟的架势。 故事发生在名为「影境」的虚构舞台，设定了强风格化的视听、宏大的势力角力、复杂的武侠术语，玩家将扮演顶尖刺客「魂」，正面临所属组织的追杀，流程就随着通缉令的倒计时来推进。虽然主角叫「魂」，但梁其伟强调，他并不把本作视为类魂游戏，立体式的关卡设计算有致敬，但团队更多是在想，在动作游戏赛道上怎样不走老路、超越《忍者龙剑传》（忍者龍剣伝）或《鬼泣》（Devil May Cry）。 本作最深下功夫的无疑是动作方面，得到了着重的宣传。今年 8 月的预购启动宣传中，开发团队重磅介绍了甄子丹从 2023 年起担任了本作创意顾问、亲自出演了名为「魔渊」的游戏内角色，其麾下「甄家班」亦参与战斗设计。PC Gamer 打趣道，懂行的朋友或许早有预期，因为《影之刃零》刚开始宣传时就提到了谷垣健治担任动作指导，后者正是甄子丹三十年的老搭档。 就笔者的观察而言，星光熠熠的武指团队还真不太噱头，因为梁其伟在与 VICE、17173、Inverse 等的多篇访谈中提到，团队极其重视独特、丰富的动作，而且是实实在在基于捕捉得来的动作，团队的原则是，有海量且多变的动画才有流畅的战斗、类人单位尽量上动捕、一把武器对应一整套角色动作（不止主角），等等。根据宣传，游戏收录了三十多种主武器、二十多种副武器（小技能），没有偏传统 RPG 式的角色升级加点，成长就依赖武器变化和技能树分支，至少光看纸面，相比起重在加数值的「量变」成长，《影之刃零》的「质变」成长更令笔者倾心。 目前游戏刚刚发售，笔者尚未上手，翻看了一下社区，看到不少「低于预期」的评价。游玩者的简评中，我记忆最深刻的是「画风的割裂」：似乎游戏流程中插入了 2D 剧情动画，还有一些角色、物件的形象不够仿古。这让我想起了当年玩《河洛英雄传》的玩后感，俯视角的城市漫游、战棋战斗还没享受够，出了城、猛地切到 2D 仙侠风看板玩法，瞬间给我浇了盆冷水，所以我还挺能共情武侠作品「割裂」带来的失望。

AI Translation
A martial arts single-player dream originating from the original IP "Rainblood." October 29, 2026 / PS5, PC Developed and self-published by Beijing S-GAME (灵游坊), with team founder Soulframe (梁其伟) serving as producer and director for this title. He has dedicated nearly 20 years to this IP: around 2008, as a student, Soulframe independently developed "Rainblood: Town of Death" using RPG Maker, later gathering a team to release titles such as "Rainblood 2: City of Flames" and "Rainblood: Mirage"; although "Mirage" achieved sales of approximately 300,000 copies, it wasn't enough to sustain the company. Ultimately, NetEase's investment ended S-GAME's closure crisis, and both parties began cooperating on operating "Phantom Blade," a mobile game born from "Rainblood"; a decade passed in a flash, until today, with the advent of "Phantom Blade Zero," S-GAME has incubated a 3A single-player title from this IP. "Phantom Blade Zero" is developed using Unreal Engine 5 and can be seen as a reimagining of the original "Rainblood" series, not requiring players to have experienced S-GAME's other works. The game first officially debuted at the 2023 Sony PlayStation Showcase, but S-GAME had been internally conceiving it for a long time, with the concept proposed in 2016 and full development commencing in 2022. It is said that S-GAME also cut many projects during development, demonstrating a "burn the bridges" attitude. The story unfolds in a fictional setting called "The Phantom Realm," featuring strongly stylized visuals and audio, grand power struggles, and complex Wuxia terminology. Players will take on the role of the top assassin "Soul," who is being hunted by his own organization, with the gameplay progressing according to a countdown on a wanted poster. Although the protagonist is named "Soul," Soulframe emphasized that he does not consider this game a "Soulslike" title. While the three-dimensional level design pays homage, the team's greater focus is on how to break new ground in the action game genre, surpassing titles like "Ninja Gaiden" (忍者龍剣伝) or "Devil May Cry." Undoubtedly, the game's most significant effort has been poured into its action elements, which have received prominent promotion. In the pre-order launch promotion this August, the development team prominently announced that Donnie Yen has served as creative consultant for the game since 2023, personally portraying the in-game character named "Mo Yuan," with his "Donnie Yen Stunt Team" also participating in combat design. PC Gamer quipped that connoisseurs might have already anticipated this, as Kenji Tanigaki was mentioned as action director when "Phantom Blade Zero" first began its promotion, and he is precisely Donnie Yen's thirty-year long-time collaborator. From my observation, the star-studded martial arts choreography team is genuinely more than just a gimmick, because Soulframe has mentioned in multiple interviews with VICE, 17173, Inverse, and others, that the team places extreme importance on unique and rich actions, which are genuinely based on motion-captured movements. The team's principles include: a massive and varied animation library for fluid combat, using motion capture as much as possible for humanoid units, and a full set of character actions corresponding to each weapon (not just for the protagonist), and so on. According to promotions, the game features over thirty main weapons and more than twenty sub-weapons (minor skills), eschewing traditional RPG-style character leveling and stat allocation. Progression relies on weapon variations and skill tree branches. At least on paper, compared to "quantitative" growth focused on numerical increases, the "qualitative" growth in "Phantom Blade Zero" is more appealing to me. The game has just been released, and I haven't gotten my hands on it yet. Looking through the community, I've seen quite a few "lower than expected" reviews. Among players' brief comments, what struck me most was the "art style discontinuity": it seems 2D story animations are interspersed throughout the gameplay, and some character and object designs aren't sufficiently antique-styled. This reminded me of my experience playing "Hero's Legend" back then: I hadn't fully enjoyed the top-down city exploration and tactical combat when, upon leaving the city, it abruptly switched to a 2D Xianxia-style signboard gameplay, instantly dousing me with cold water. Therefore, I can quite empathize with the disappointment caused by "discontinuity" in Wuxia works.
